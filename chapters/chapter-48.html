Chapter 48

A bead of perspiration gathered upon Talia's forehead before slipping down to the corner of her eye, leaving behind a faint, stinging sensation.

What if it is something meant to cause me even greater suffering? What if it is poison?

Such dreadful suspicions continued to plague her thoughts. Yet despite her fear, Talia slowly parted her lips.

Varkas tilted the small vial, allowing the bitter medicine to flow into her mouth. The unpleasant taste made her recoil, and she turned her face aside with a dry, choking cough. But his fingers immediately caught her jaw, guiding her face back towards him as he pressed the glass once more against her lips.

Unable to resist, Talia looked up at him while reluctantly swallowing the liquid that trickled down her throat.

The flickering torchlight was reflected in his pale eyes, yet they seemed strangely dim, as though concealed beneath a heavy veil. She searched those cold, distant depths for even the smallest trace of emotion, hoping to discover something that might reassure her. But before she could find anything, the strength began to drain from her limbs.

Talia widened her eyes, desperately struggling to remain conscious. She fought against the approaching darkness with every remaining fragment of her will, but her thoughts gradually scattered, and the world before her dissolved into a shapeless haze.

After several slow, helpless blinks, her eyelids finally closed, and she sank into a silence so profound that it seemed almost like death.

She could not have said how much time had passed. Perhaps only a few moments had gone by, or perhaps an entire lifetime had slipped away.

When Talia next opened her eyes, she found herself staring blankly into an unfamiliar space. The steady pounding of horses' hooves and the rattling of iron-bound wheels reached her ears, making her wince. As the heavy fog clouding her mind slowly began to lift, the dim interior of a carriage came into focus.

For one fleeting, precious moment, she wondered whether everything she remembered had merely been a terrible dream.

Perhaps none of it truly happened.

The monstrous creatures emerging from the darkness, Varkas rushing away to protect Ayla while leaving her behind, the dreadful sensation of being seized by a wyvern's talons and carried into the sky—perhaps all of it had been nothing more than a nightmare.

But that fragile hope was shattered almost immediately.

A searing pain suddenly shot upwards from her knees towards her waist, so fierce that her breath caught in her throat. Talia bit down hard upon her lower lip and instinctively reached for her legs, which felt as though they had been pressed against burning iron.

Beneath the thin fabric of her chemise, her trembling fingertips encountered something rough and tightly woven. Her eyes widened as she carefully traced its shape. Thick layers of bandages had been wrapped around the entire length of her left leg.

With unsteady hands, she lifted the hem of her garment and looked down. The linen covering her thigh and hip was stained dark with blood and other fluids, clinging unpleasantly to her injured flesh.

For several moments, Talia could do nothing but stare. Then her hands fell limply to her sides, and she allowed the fabric to slip back into place.

She drew a shallow breath and attempted to sit upright.

The carriage was unusually spacious, its interior carefully padded with thick, quilted wool. Cushions and light blankets had been scattered untidily across the seats, apparently arranged to make the journey as comfortable as possible for an injured passenger.

Talia surveyed her surroundings with a vacant expression before reaching towards a leather strap fastened to the carriage wall. Gripping it tightly, she tried to pull herself to her feet.

But her legs would not support her.

She attempted to force strength into her heavy, unresponsive limbs, only for a dreadful pain to shoot through her spine. Her knees immediately gave way, and she collapsed back onto the cushioned bench with a dull thud.

A ragged scream escaped her lips as the impact sent a violent wave of agony through her entire body.

"Your Highness! Are you all right?"

The carriage came to an abrupt halt at the sound of her cry. A moment later, the door was flung open, allowing a sudden flood of daylight to spill into the dim interior.

Talia narrowed her eyes against the brightness and looked towards the figure standing in the doorway. An Imperial Knight peered anxiously inside, his unruly hair appearing even more dishevelled than usual.

Upon seeing her condition, he immediately climbed into the carriage and began searching through a wooden chest tucked into one corner.

"You must be in terrible pain," he said with evident concern. "The healer left some medicine to ease your suffering. If you drink this, it should—"

"Why..."

Talia's weak voice interrupted him.

"Why has no one used healing magic on me?"

The knight stiffened and turned towards her, visibly startled by the accusation in her voice.

Talia drew the blanket tightly around her chest and regarded him through narrowed, suspicious eyes.

"Was it my brother who ordered you to leave me untreated?"

"N-no! Nothing of the sort, Your Highness!"

The man immediately raised both hands, waving them in flustered protest.

"The healer has already set the broken bones and treated the most urgent wounds," he explained hurriedly. "However, your injuries were far too severe to be completely healed under such conditions. He warned that attempting to mend everything at once might cause the bones to heal improperly and leave lasting damage. It would be much safer to entrust the remainder of your treatment to the imperial physicians once we reach the capital."

Talia studied his hesitant expression with open distrust before lowering her gaze towards her bandaged leg.

Fragments of memory slowly returned to her. She remembered the enormous boulder pressing against her knee and the terrible weight crushing her thigh. If the torn flesh had been healed while the broken bones remained out of place, she might very well have lost the use of her leg entirely.

She could understand the reasoning behind the healer's decision. Unfortunately, understanding it did little to soothe her foul temper.

"So I am expected to lie here and suffer until we reach the capital?"

"I know the pain must be dreadful, Your Highness, but please try to endure it a little longer," the knight pleaded. "We are travelling by the quickest possible route towards Gillian."

Talia continued to regard him for another moment before turning away towards the carriage window.

Beyond the glass, the pale afternoon sunlight stretched across a vast, open plain. A long procession of knights rode alongside the carriage, their armour catching the light as their horses moved steadily across the grasslands.

Without quite realising what she was doing, Talia began searching the passing ranks for a familiar head of ash-blond hair.

The instant she became aware of her own actions, she flinched.

With a sharp movement, she seized the curtain and pulled it shut. Even that small exertion left her utterly exhausted, and she sank back against the cushions with a weary breath.

"I must admit, I am surprised my brother agreed to return to the palace at all."

The knight's expression darkened slightly.

"Far too many men lost their lives during the wyvern attack," he replied. "Even His Highness the Crown Prince could hardly insist upon continuing the journey when it was necessary to return and see the fallen properly laid to rest."

Talia turned towards him in surprise.

There had been an unmistakable bitterness in his voice, a faintly cynical edge that seemed entirely unlike his usual manner.

Apparently realising that he had spoken too freely, the knight flushed and hurriedly lowered his gaze. He then reached for the small cup he had prepared earlier, evidently eager to steer the conversation elsewhere.

"More importantly, Your Highness, you look terribly pale. Please, at least take the medicine first."

He extended the cup towards her.

Talia regarded it in silence for a moment before dismissing the offer with an impatient flick of her hand.

"I have no desire to drink it. Take it away. I wish to rest."

The knight hesitated.

"...If you do not trust me, Your Highness, I could ask Lord Sierkhan to come."

Talia had already begun lowering herself amongst the cushions, but those words made her expression turn cold.

For one brief instant, a strange emptiness opened within her chest, as though she had been struck by an unseen hand. The feeling was so sudden and unexpected that she nearly forgot the pain in her leg.

Then her lips twisted into a sharp, contemptuous smile.

"Do I appear to you to be a woman who would place her trust in that man?"

"Your Highness, to the Commander, you are—"

"I trust no one."

Her voice cut through his unfinished sentence like a blade.

The knight fell silent.

Talia fixed him with a venomous glare, her patience exhausted by his stubbornly earnest expression.

"Least of all him."

A heavy silence settled between them.

Then she turned her face away and added with icy finality, "Now hold your tongue and leave me."

The knight opened his mouth as though he wished to argue further, but whatever words had risen to his lips remained unspoken. After a moment, he released a quiet sigh, lowered his head, and withdrew from the carriage.

A short while later, the wheels began to turn once more.

Talia pulled the coverlet up towards her shoulders and curled herself amongst the cushions as best she could. The pain, which had briefly subsided during their conversation, soon returned with merciless intensity. It spread through her injured flesh like fire, leaving her feeling as though she were being slowly burned alive.

She shifted restlessly, desperately searching for a position that might ease her suffering, but every movement only seemed to make matters worse. Biting back the groans threatening to escape her throat, she squeezed her eyes shut and endured the torment in silence.

As evening approached, a stout, round-bellied mage came to attend her.

Talia allowed the unfamiliar man to examine her injuries and cast restorative spells without offering a word of protest. The mere thought of another stranger laying hands upon her made her skin crawl, but she was far too weak to resist.

After completing his examination, the mage studied her pale face with concern.

"I shall burn some medicinal incense for you, Your Highness," he said gently. "It should dull your senses enough to make the pain more bearable."

He had evidently been informed of her refusal to take the sleeping draught. Rather than attempting to persuade her to drink anything, he placed a small incense burner near the carriage door and carefully lit it.

Within moments, a thick, sweet fragrance began to fill the enclosed space.

Talia opened her mouth, intending to tell him that she had no need of his interference. But before she could speak, the terrible tension gripping her nerves began to loosen.

Her rigid shoulders gradually relaxed, and the relentless pain buried deep within her bones softened into a distant, dull throbbing. Even her thoughts began to grow pleasantly indistinct.

So that is what he intended...

The herbs burning within the little censer possessed a powerful sleep-inducing effect.

Talia could feel herself slipping towards unconsciousness, but this time, she lacked both the strength and the desire to resist. Her eyelids grew heavy, and she finally surrendered to the darkness gathering around her.

Unfortunately, the relief did not last.

Before long, the familiar throbbing returned, slowly intensifying until it became sharp enough to drag her from sleep.

A muffled groan escaped Talia's lips as she reluctantly opened her eyes. Her entire body felt heavy and exhausted, and the pain in her leg had once again become almost unbearable.

I shall have to call for that mage again. Perhaps he can burn another stick of incense.

Pressing one hand against her pounding temple, she reached for the cushions with the other and attempted to push herself upright.

But just as she began to rise, her breath suddenly caught in her throat.

Talia went utterly still.

Her eyes widened as she stared into the pitch-black darkness surrounding her.

She could not have said what had frightened her.

Yet something was terribly wrong.
