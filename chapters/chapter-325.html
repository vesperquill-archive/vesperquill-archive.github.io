Chapter 325

The shock of finding that sentence in the diary was still vivid. If he had been even a little late, she wouldn't have been of this world anymore. Every time that fact came to mind, the blood in his entire body felt like it was turning cold

He gently buried his face in the nape of her neck, where her hair lay scattered, and let out a muffled groan.

How could he save her?

The question he had asked himself countless times once again tormented his mind.

No matter what he did, the future that had already been decided.

If he could not change the future, perhaps it would be better to take her away and flee somewhere far from here.

But even that plan had ended in failure. She had chosen to remain here.

More painful than seeing the escape plan he had painstakingly prepared over such a long time come to nothing was the realization that she still wished to return to Raedgo Castle, even though she bore such deep resentment toward him.

Varkas Raedgo Sierkhan had treated her cruelly.

He knew that. He knew that he had deliberately spoken the most merciless words imaginable, intending to wound her.

He had neither tried to understand her nor made any effort to believe her. He had convinced himself that he was protecting her, without ever realizing that he had, in truth, driven her to the very edge of a precipice.

He had never even tried to understand just how cruel her life had been to her.

And so, he would lose everything he had once possessed. Only after slowly suffocating in despair, unable to see even the faintest glimmer of hope, and watching everything he had built up like an illusion crumble completely, would he finally understand the true meaning of suffering.

Such a foolish wife… Why could she never bring herself to let go of him?

He gazed at her pale face with anguished eyes.

Just then, a faint voice, mingled with tears, pierced his ears like a song.

“I'm sorry…… Varkas…… I…… was wrong.”

For an instant, an agonizing pain swept through him, as though a red-hot iron spike had pierced his chest.

He let out a stifled groan, his limbs trembling uncontrollably.

He had spent nearly his entire life in a state of emotional numbness. Whenever he was with Talia, these unfamiliar feelings would come rushing over him.

Yet even then, those emotions had only stirred within him for a fleeting moment, as though he were merely an observer watching someone else's life unfold.

But now, the few words that escaped her lips made him feel as though his entire body were being torn apart by pain. It was as though he were being punished for all the years he had spent in indifference.

He opened his mouth.

Please, don't apologize.

Don't blame yourself.

Don't cry.

I'm the one who should be begging for forgiveness.

I should never have said those things to you, even if it cost me my life.

I should never have hurt you.

I should never have abandoned you and walked away.

Thousands of words surged up to his throat. Yet all that escaped his mouth was a pitiful.

All that escaped his lips was the sorrowful whimper of a beast. Overwhelmed by that despairing realization, he wrapped himself around her as though to shield her with his entire body.

Before he knew it, his gaze had turned toward the western sky, where the moon hung high. Watching the darkness, thick as a curtain, gradually begin to lift, he curled his body even more tightly around her.

When daylight came, her nightmares would finally end. Yet his own nightmare would continue.

The fear that had taken root deep within him only grew stronger with each passing day. Was this how a condemned prisoner felt, bound hand and foot by fate, waiting for the day he would be brought to the executioner's block?

Even though he knew that the time remaining to him was diminishing with each passing day, the realization that there was nothing he could do drove him to the brink of madness.

As he waited for the approaching dawn, he repeated to himself the words he had already recited hundreds of times, until they seemed engraved upon his mind.

I will never attack Lucas again. No matter how much his gaze upon her provokes me. Even if he touches her as though she belongs to him, and the urge to tear him apart overwhelms me every time he lays a hand on her, I must endure it.

Otherwise, I will lose my last chance to save her forever. I cannot allow that to happen again.

He repeated these words to himself like a prayer. He could not afford to repeat the same mistake, blinded by something as foolish as jealousy.

Even if Lucas approaches her, I must pretend not to notice. If Lucas truly cares for her, he will try to protect her from danger. Let me be grateful for that.

Varkas Raedgo Sierkhan cannot protect her. Perhaps Lucas can.

He turned his head and gazed at his own body, now concealed beneath the fur of a beast, before closing his eyes wearily.

What she needed was not a beast that constantly caused trouble.

Nor did she need a husband so emotionally broken that he could no longer be trusted. Lucas, too, was impulsive and quick-tempered, but at the very least, he would prove more useful than himself.

Suppressing the jealousy and possessiveness that threatened to overwhelm him, he made a solemn vow.

What matters is protecting her.

That is all that matters.

✦   ✦   ✦

Ever since Lucas had promised to let her take Khan outside, he had begun visiting her frequently.

Although Talia found her brother-in-law's presence uncomfortable and unwelcome, she endured it for Khan's sake. She wanted the wolf, who had been confined by her side, to enjoy even a little freedom.

For that to happen, however, Lucas's protection was essential.

Although the ban on her movements had been lifted, people still regarded her with suspicion and distrust.

Under such circumstances, if she were to take the direwolf out as she pleased and wander through the surrounding territory, there was no telling what accusations might arise against her.

It might not end with malicious rumors alone. She could even find herself dragged before a court.

To avoid such misfortune, she needed someone to vouch for her.

And with Varkas currently absent.

Lucas was the only person who possessed enough influence within the Sierkhan family to provide such protection.

Although she was not particularly pleased about accepting his help, Talia had no other choice. For Khan's sake, she could endure a certain amount of reluctance and discomfort.

As she slowly made her way along the forest path with Khan, she stole a glance at Lucas, who was following behind them.

He stood a few steps away, receiving a report from his subordinates. It seemed that his offer to let her take Khan outside while he was on patrol had not been an entirely empty excuse.

Talia watched him for a moment as he issued instructions with a rather stern expression. Then, sensing Khan's peculiar gaze as he looked back and forth between her and Lucas, she gave him a puzzled look.

"Why are you looking at me like that?"

Khan stared at her for a moment before calmly turning his head away, as though nothing had happened.

However, his ears, which had been drawn back, and his tail, which hung straight down, betrayed the displeasure he was quietly harboring.

Talia let out a bitter smile.

Ever since they had secretly slipped out of the castle and returned, Khan had been visibly dejected.

Previously, he would growl fiercely whenever Lucas so much as approached her. Now, however, he would either turn his head away while keeping his distance or simply avoid him altogether. He had even become noticeably less insistent about climbing onto her bed, as though trying to avoid troubling her.

The change weighed heavily on her heart.

He had once been a wolf who followed her around so persistently that she found him bothersome.

Could it be that she had disappointed him in more ways than one?

She had left him confined within the room throughout Tiuran's acupuncture treatment. As though that were not enough, she had ruined the escape plan he had worked so hard to prepare.

Perhaps Khan had every reason to feel disappointed in her.

Talia slowed her steps and crouched down beside Khan.

“Are you still angry with me?”

Khan's ears perked up.

Watching his bushy tail sway gently, almost as though he were denying her words, Talia let out a small laugh.

The wolf's blue eyes, gazing up at her, were still filled with an unwavering light of affection.

As though he were looking upon the one and only light in his world.

She let out a small sigh of relief beneath his gaze, only to hear a blunt voice from behind her.

“Sister-in-law.”

Startled, Talia turned her head. She had been so absorbed in Khan that she had failed to notice Lucas approaching her.

Lucas, who had been looking down at her, suddenly held something out.

She narrowed her eyes.

A cluster of bright red berries hung from a slender branch.

“……What is this?”

“They're hawthorn berries. My men discovered them, so I had them break off a few branches and bring them back.”

Talia gazed curiously at the small, round berries hanging from the branches.

“Are they edible?”

“Of course.”

Lucas replied with a smile so gentle that it almost startled her.
