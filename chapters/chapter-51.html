Chapter 51

How foolish she had once been.

There was a time when even the most ordinary remark from Varkas had seemed worthy of remembrance. Talia had cherished his words with an almost embarrassing devotion, repeating them silently to herself long after he had forgotten speaking them. She had searched for tenderness in his briefest replies and imagined hidden affection in gestures that had likely meant nothing at all.

Even his cruelty had become something she could not bear to forget. A harsh remark from him would wound her deeply, yet she would return to it again and again, as though examining the very blade that had pierced her heart might somehow lessen the pain.

She could no longer endure such misery.

For years, she had exhausted herself trying to discover meaning in every glance, every careless word, and every fleeting act of consideration. Each time she allowed herself to hope, disappointment followed, leaving her more miserable than before.

I have spent quite enough of my life making a fool of myself over him.

The girl who had once looked upon Varkas with such blind devotion was gone. Whatever innocence had remained within her had been worn away by years of humiliation and unanswered affection.

She understood her place in his life now.

To Varkas Sierkhan, I am of no consequence whatsoever.

Thus, when he gathered her carefully into his arms, Talia refused to surrender to the familiar comfort of his embrace. Her body instinctively longed to rest against his chest, but she resisted the temptation, extinguishing every foolish hope before it could take shape.

There was no affection in his consideration. She would not deceive herself into believing otherwise.

She had spent more than a decade observing him. She knew his habits, his convictions, and the rigid principles that governed his conduct. If there was one thing she understood about Varkas, it was that he would sooner endure any personal inconvenience than neglect a responsibility entrusted to him.

And she was precisely that—a responsibility.

However much he disliked her, she remained a princess under his protection. He might reserve his loyalty and devotion for Gareth and Ayla, but his oath did not permit him to abandon Talia merely because he found her troublesome.

He had failed to keep her from harm. A man such as Varkas would naturally consider himself obliged to compensate for that failure.

He is merely settling a debt to his own conscience.

The thought brought her little comfort, but at least it spared her the humiliation of expecting more.

"Someone will bring your supper shortly."

His voice reached her as though from a great distance.

Talia blinked and discovered that they had already entered his tent. Varkas was lowering her onto a narrow bed, taking care not to disturb her injured leg.

As soon as she settled against the mattress, she looked towards the thick bandages beneath her gown. The medicinal haze that had dulled her senses was beginning to lift, and with its departure came the familiar torment. A sharp ache stirred near her ankle before spreading steadily upwards towards her hip.

She closed her eyes in irritation.

"I have no appetite. Have the incense lit instead."

"You may have it when you have eaten something."

The answer was immediate and infuriatingly firm.

Talia lifted her head, gathering enough concentration to direct a proper glare at him. Unfortunately, Varkas had already turned towards the entrance, where he was instructing a young attendant to fetch her meal.

Must he always behave as though his word were law?

She would dearly have liked to throw something at him. A pillow would have served wonderfully, particularly if it struck the back of that intolerably proud head.

But her arms felt impossibly heavy, and the mere thought of moving exhausted her.

Abandoning the idea, she sank into the bedding and pressed her cheek against the cool fabric. A faint fragrance of cedar and mint lingered upon the blankets.

Some time later, Varkas returned with a bowl of porridge.

Talia eyed it with open displeasure. Its dull green colour was hardly inviting, and the bitter scent of medicinal herbs rising from the bowl made her stomach turn.

Nevertheless, she reached for the spoon.

There was little point in arguing. She knew perfectly well that he would withhold the incense until she obeyed him, and the pain spreading through her leg was rapidly becoming intolerable.

She forced down one spoonful, then another, swallowing the thick, unpleasant mixture with considerable effort.

Varkas remained beside the bed throughout the ordeal, watching her with the solemn attention of a guard overseeing a prisoner.

At last, having consumed roughly half the contents, Talia put the bowl aside with an impatient clatter.

"There. I have eaten your dreadful porridge. Will that satisfy you?"

Instead of answering, he glanced at the bowl.

His expression suggested that he was seriously considering whether the amount she had eaten was sufficient.

Talia nearly lost her temper.

"Good heavens, must you inspect every mouthful? I have done as you asked. What else do you expect of me?"

He raised his eyes towards her face.

Perspiration had gathered along her brow, and her complexion had grown noticeably paler. Though she was doing her utmost to conceal it, the increasing pain was plainly taking its toll.

After observing her for another moment, Varkas turned towards the waiting servant.

"Bring the incense burner."

Talia finally allowed herself to relax.

When the medicinal stick was lit, a pale ribbon of smoke rose from the burner and slowly spread through the tent. She breathed in the familiar fragrance, waiting impatiently for its effects.

Gradually, the dreadful tightness in her limbs began to ease. The sharp pain faded into something distant and indistinct, while her thoughts grew pleasantly sluggish.

She sank deeper into the mattress, feeling as though her body were being carried away upon a cool, weightless mist.

Even Varkas, whose presence had irritated her only moments before, seemed to retreat beyond the reach of her senses.

She welcomed the distance.

For a while, she floated in that strange condition between sleep and waking, neither fully conscious nor entirely unaware of her surroundings.

Then a woman's voice disturbed her peace.

Talia opened her eyes with considerable reluctance.

A slender figure stood near the entrance, framed by the fading light of evening. The woman's posture was elegant, her clothing immaculate, and every movement possessed that effortless grace which Talia had always found so thoroughly disagreeable.

It was Ayla.

Talia regarded her half-sister with the detached interest one might bestow upon an expensive ornament.

Yet something about Ayla was different.

Her beautiful features retained their usual refinement, but the perfect tranquillity of her expression had been disturbed. There was a stiffness about her mouth and an unmistakable tension in her eyes.

Well, this is unexpected.

Talia felt a faint stirring of amusement.

Ayla was ordinarily impossible to provoke. Even the most deliberate insults rarely succeeded in drawing more than a measured response from her.

Whatever has happened to make our perfect princess look so displeased?

"I do not deny that you must feel some obligation towards her," Ayla was saying. "Nevertheless, Varkas, surely you understand the position in which this places me. We are engaged to be married. It is hardly appropriate for you to keep my younger sister in your private quarters night after night."

Her voice remained gentle, though the displeasure beneath it was unmistakable.

Talia frowned.

Even now, Ayla spoke with that infuriating elegance of hers.

Does she rehearse her grievances before a mirror? Must even her anger sound so graceful?

Talia could never have managed such a performance. When she was furious, she wanted the entire world to know it. She would rather scream until her throat ached than disguise her resentment beneath a pleasant expression.

Perhaps that was why Ayla's composure offended her so deeply.

Her half-sister possessed everything Talia had been denied: admiration, legitimacy, refinement, and the effortless affection of those around her.

Worse still, Talia had spent her entire life being measured against those virtues.

Had Ayla been an unrelated noblewoman, perhaps Talia might have regarded her with nothing more than ordinary dislike.

But fate had made them sisters, and Talia had been forced to grow up beneath the shadow of a woman whom everyone seemed determined to praise.

Ayla continued her appeal.

"If it is her condition that concerns you, I shall personally arrange for her to be cared for. There is sufficient room amongst my attendants, and she may remain under my supervision until we reach the capital. Surely that would relieve you of this unnecessary burden."

Varkas answered before she could elaborate further.

"Would any sensible person place a snake and a wildcat within the same cage?"

His dry remark brought Ayla's carefully measured speech to an abrupt halt.

Talia turned her languid gaze towards him.

He stood beside one of the tent poles, his shoulder resting against the wooden frame, looking remarkably unconcerned by the offence he had just given.

The posture was unusual for a man who generally carried himself with the rigid bearing of a soldier standing inspection.

Has he been here the entire time?

The possibility puzzled her.

Had he truly remained in the tent while she lay half-conscious beneath the influence of the incense?

If so, he certainly concealed his fatigue well. She could scarcely hold her eyes open, whereas he seemed entirely capable of conducting an unpleasant conversation without the slightest difficulty.

"Are you suggesting that I am some vile, slithering serpent?"

For once, Ayla's gentle voice had lost its customary sweetness.

Talia strained to see her half-sister's expression. How delightful it would be to witness that flawless composure finally crumble!

Unfortunately, Varkas straightened, his broad shoulders obscuring her view.

"Must I truly explain what would happen if the Second Princess were placed in your quarters?" he asked, his voice as cold as ever.

A weary sigh escaped him before he continued.

"Or would you rather discover that your precious maids have had their throats cut before morning?"

Ayla fell silent.

The stillness that followed was almost tangible.

Talia stared at Varkas's back, her fleeting amusement gradually fading.

So that is how he sees us.

Ayla is the snake, and I am the wildcat.

And he keeps me here because he fears what I might do if I am allowed near her.

The conclusion was perfectly reasonable.

After what had happened at the campfire, he undoubtedly considered her a danger to anyone foolish enough to provoke her.

He wishes to keep me where he can watch me.

She had known from the beginning that his attentions were motivated by duty. There was no reason to feel disappointed.

And yet an unpleasant emptiness opened within her chest.

Why should it matter?

She despised herself for even asking.

Why do I continue to hope for something I know he will never give me?

Talia closed her eyes before the answer could take shape.

The voices around her became faint and indistinct. She allowed the medicinal haze to draw her downwards, surrendering her remaining consciousness without a struggle.

It was rather like sinking beneath dark, cold water.

For once, she was grateful for the silence.

The days that followed brought a heat so oppressive that even the most seasoned soldiers struggled beneath it.

The sun beat mercilessly upon the barren countryside, and scarcely a breath of wind offered relief. Under ordinary circumstances, the weather would have made travelling unpleasant enough. For a procession burdened with the remains of thirty-four fallen men, it was nothing short of dreadful.

Every precaution had been taken to preserve the bodies until their return to the capital. The wounds and empty cavities had been filled with salt and dried herbs, while myrrh and resin had been spread over the lifeless flesh.

Yet no preparation could entirely withstand the relentless heat.

As the days passed, a foul, sickly sweetness began escaping from the wooden coffins. The odour clung to the wagons and drifted along the marching ranks, growing steadily worse despite every effort to contain it.

The soldiers endured the journey with grim expressions. Some pressed cloths against their noses, while others marched in miserable silence, their faces drawn with exhaustion and disgust.

From her carriage window, Talia watched the mournful procession creep across the scorched landscape.

It scarcely resembled the magnificent company that had departed from the Imperial Palace.

She remembered the splendour of that departure, the richly dressed attendants, the shining armour, and the proud banners carried beneath the bright morning sky.

She also remembered the bitterness that had consumed her that day.

May the whole miserable procession find its way into hell.

She had wished it with all her heart.

Now, as she watched the soldiers dragging themselves through the heat beside wagons filled with the dead, she wondered whether heaven had taken her words rather more seriously than she had intended.

Was my prayer answered after all?

Her fingers moved absently towards her injured knee, where a familiar ache pulsed beneath the bandages.

Or is this the punishment reserved for the one who made it?

She could not decide which possibility troubled her more.

Still lost in thought, Talia was startled by a sharp whistle sounding from the head of the procession.

She lifted her gaze towards the road ahead.

Beyond the crest of a gently rising hill, the sunlight fell upon a distant stretch of grey stone.

Talia leaned closer to the window and narrowed her eyes against the glare.

There, rising above the surrounding landscape, stood the immense outer walls of the Imperial Palace.

For a moment, she merely stared.

After all the suffering, the endless days upon the road, and the dreadful memories that had accompanied their return, the familiar sight seemed almost unreal.

But there could be no mistaking those towering walls.

The journey had finally reached its end.

They were home.
