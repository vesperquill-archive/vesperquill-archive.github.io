Chapter 19

Having driven away nearly every servant appointed to her by the Main Palace, Talia now had fewer than ten maids remaining in the Separate Palace. Even those few had been forbidden from approaching her without permission, leaving her with scarcely a handful of attendants she could trust to accompany her on the journey.

Thus, much to her displeasure, she found herself obliged to travel in the company of servants sent by Senevia.

Ordinarily, Talia would have dismissed them all without a second thought. Yet when she saw Gares and Ayla preparing to depart with hundreds of attendants at their disposal, she could not bring herself to do so.

She bit her lower lip and glared resentfully at the great crowd gathered around the carriages of the Crown Prince and the First Princess.

For years, she had permitted scarcely anyone besides Varkas and her old nurse to remain close to her. She despised the constant presence of servants, particularly when any one of them might seize an opportunity to slight or humiliate her.

Nevertheless, the thought of appearing before the court with merely three or four attendants, while her half-siblings displayed all the splendour befitting their imperial station, was more than her pride could endure.

In the end, she reluctantly accepted the maids her mother had provided. Yet she remained suspicious of their every movement, watching them closely as they carried her possessions towards the waiting carriages.

It was while she stood supervising the preparations that an exasperated voice interrupted her thoughts.

“Your Highness, do you truly intend to bring all these belongings?”

Talia turned sharply towards the speaker.

The man was the knight who had recently been assigned as her personal guard, following the dismissal of his foolish predecessor, who had been far too eager to grovel before the Crown Prince. Unfortunately, the new knight had already developed an irritating habit of speaking to her with an altogether excessive degree of familiarity.

She regarded him coldly as he scratched the back of his head.

“Have you some objection to my arrangements?”

“I understand that Your Highness must maintain the dignity of the imperial family, but surely this is rather excessive. Hundreds of gowns, costly jewels, and heaven knows what else. Unless you intend to change your attire five times a day, most of these things will serve no purpose.”

“You know nothing of the matter,” Talia replied disdainfully. “I intend to change ten times a day, if necessary. We shall be travelling in a carriage for hours, gathering dust upon our clothes. Do you imagine I would willingly remain in a soiled gown for even a moment?”

The knight gave an awkward laugh.

“Surely Your Highness is jesting…”

Talia merely cast him a look of contempt. Evidently, the man had yet to understand that she was perfectly serious.

Leaving him standing there in bewilderment, she made her way towards her carriage.

It stood near the rear of the lengthy procession and was no less magnificent than the one prepared for the Crown Prince. Its doors and roof were richly ornamented with gold and ivory, while the interior was furnished with broad seats that could serve as beds, covered in soft cushions of silk and thick wool.

Talia climbed inside and drew aside the curtains hanging behind the seats, revealing a spacious dressing compartment and a large cabinet intended for her personal belongings.

She opened the drawers connected to the luggage compartment and began examining their contents.

There were gowns fashioned from the finest fabrics in her possession, together with an abundance of ornaments and jewels. Yet the longer she inspected them, the more dissatisfied she became.

None of it seemed sufficient.

How was she to surpass Ayla with such ordinary finery?

She rummaged impatiently through the drawers, biting her lip in frustration.

Perhaps she ought to have taken the diamond necklace the Emperor had presented to Senevia. Better still, she should have brought her mother's entire jewel collection.

After all, had Senevia not practically encouraged her to interfere with Ayla's engagement? If Talia had requested fine gowns and precious jewels for that purpose, her mother would surely have lent them without hesitation.

Should I return to the Empress's Palace now?

She stood contemplating the matter for some time before finally stepping down from the carriage.

Yet just as she turned towards her mother's residence, her gaze fell upon a familiar figure standing among the soldiers in the courtyard.

Varkas.

He wore the uniform of the Roem Knights, indistinguishable in dress from the hundred and fifty or more men assembled around him. Yet to Talia, there was no mistaking him.

The moment she saw him, the rest of the courtyard seemed to fade from her sight.

Her eyes followed the straight line of his back, the breadth of his shoulders, and the pale ash-blond hair that caught the afternoon light.

Varkas strode across the courtyard with his customary discipline, issuing brief commands to his subordinates as he inspected the formation before their departure.

Talia swallowed with difficulty.

As he drew nearer, an unpleasant tightness seized her throat, as though she had swallowed fragments of broken glass.

His indifferent gaze moved along the procession of carriages stretching towards the palace gates before settling upon her own.

Even from a distance, she recognised the faint crease that appeared between his brows.

It was an expression she knew all too well.

Whenever Varkas looked upon her, that same displeasure seemed to darken his face.

And now, the cold countenance that had caused her so much anguish was approaching once more.

“Have the preparations for departure still not been completed?”

Varkas addressed the knight assigned to her protection without so much as glancing in Talia's direction.

The man scratched the back of his head, looking thoroughly troubled.

“As you can see, my lord, it appears we shall require another wagon for Her Highness's luggage.”

At this, Varkas turned his pale blue eyes towards the servants struggling to arrange an enormous quantity of trunks and chests. A brief expression of irritation crossed his otherwise impassive face.

At last, he looked at Talia.

“We are scheduled to pass through six major cities during this journey. Anything you require may be purchased along the way. Have the unnecessary baggage removed.”

Talia lifted her chin defiantly.

“I shall do no such thing. How can you possibly know what I may require?”

“It is nothing but gowns and jewels,” he replied dryly.

“There are several prosperous cities in the northwest, where commerce and industry flourish. You will have ample opportunity to purchase whatever pleases you. There is no reason to exhaust the servants before we have even departed.”

Talia gave a contemptuous snort.

“Do not take me for a fool. You merely wish to make me appear a frivolous princess, indulging in luxuries during a sacred pilgrimage, so that everyone may compare me unfavourably with Ayla. Do you truly believe I would be deceived by such a scheme?”

“Since when have you concerned yourself with what others think?”

The corner of his mouth moved faintly, as though he found her accusation absurd.

“Besides, no one would think to place you upon the same footing as the First Princess. You may spare yourself such needless anxieties.”

Of all the words he might have spoken, those were the very ones Talia could least endure.

Her face flushed crimson.

Before she could restrain herself, she raised her hand to strike him.

Yet Varkas was not a man who would meekly submit to a blow. With swift precision, he caught her wrist before her hand could reach his face.

Without paying the slightest attention to her fury, he inclined his head towards the servants.

“Leave only what is necessary and remove the rest. We depart in fifteen minutes. Make haste.”

“By whose authority?!”

Talia cried out and struggled violently to free her arm, but his grasp remained unyielding.

Driven nearly beside herself with rage, she kicked him hard in the shin.

“Who gave you the right to order my belongings removed? Who do you imagine yourself to be? Have you already begun to fancy yourself a person of great importance?”

Her voice rose to a shriek.

“You are not yet a Grand Duke! You are merely a knight in service to the imperial family! How dare a common knight presume to treat an Imperial Princess in such a manner!”

“What are you waiting for? Proceed.”

Varkas paid no heed to her outburst. Instead, he turned a chilling gaze upon the servants who stood hesitating nearby.

They immediately sprang into motion, hastily removing the trunks and chests from the carriage.

The sight was a bitter humiliation.

There could scarcely have been a clearer demonstration that the commands of Varkas, the future Grand Duke of the East and Commander of the Imperial Guard, carried greater authority than those of a princess whose title commanded little genuine respect.

Talia watched the servants with burning resentment.

Then, overcome by fury, she lunged towards one of the maids.

“Take your hands off my belongings this instant! If so much as a single ornament goes missing, I shall have every last one of you hanged—!”

She never finished the threat.

Without warning, Varkas seized her about the waist, lifted her effortlessly with one arm, and deposited her inside the carriage as though she were merely another troublesome piece of baggage to be put away.

Talia landed upon the cushioned seat, her face burning with indignation.

For several moments, she could do nothing but stare at him in disbelief.

Varkas was a man whose loyalty to the imperial family ran deeper than blood. His conduct was always governed by duty, discipline, and an almost excessive reverence for the imperial household.

He would never have dared to handle Ayla in such a manner.

Never would he have seized the First Princess so roughly, disregarded her protests, or cast her aside before the servants as though she were of no consequence.

Yet he had done precisely that to Talia.

And the reason, she thought bitterly, was painfully clear.

He does not consider me a true member of the imperial family.

The realisation wounded her more deeply than his harsh words or careless treatment ever could.

Her eyes began to sting, and she fought desperately against the tears gathering within them.

How cruel it was that the very man who treated her half-sister with unfailing courtesy should reserve such coldness and contempt for her alone.

And how unbearably sorrowful that, despite everything, he remained the one person whose regard she desired above all others.
