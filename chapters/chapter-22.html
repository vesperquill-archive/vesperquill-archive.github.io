Chapter 22

The sight of Varkas standing before her, his broad, powerful figure illuminated by the soft glow of the lamplight, left Talia momentarily speechless. Her tongue seemed to have fastened itself to the roof of her mouth, and no words would come.

She swallowed with difficulty, her gaze wandering helplessly over his bare upper body. He had evidently just washed away the dust of the day's march, for his pale blond hair was still damp, its colour darkened by the water. Tiny droplets glistened upon his sculpted shoulders and broad back, catching the light as they slowly descended.

Talia's eyes followed one such droplet as it travelled along the firm contours of his muscles. Then, suddenly conscious of where she was looking, she flushed crimson and hastily raised her gaze.

Even his loosely fitted trousers were wet, the fabric clinging to the strong lines of his long legs.

It had been years since she had seen him in such a state. The last occasion had been when she was fourteen, after she had spitefully commanded him to walk into a lake.

Talia moistened her dry lips and struggled to recover the words that had deserted her.

Before she could manage it, however, a low, humourless laugh reached her ears.

“To hear you speak of hierarchy,” Varkas remarked dryly. “Even the dogs would find it amusing.”

The mockery immediately dispelled her embarrassment. Irritation took its place, and Talia lifted her chin with an offended scoff.

“Hierarchy exists precisely so that those of superior rank may exercise authority over their inferiors. You knights are sworn to serve the imperial family, and I am an Imperial Princess. I suggest you remind your subordinates whose commands they are obliged to obey, unless they wish to be punished for lèse-majesté.”

Varkas, who had begun slipping his arms into the sleeves of his shirt, turned a gaze of such icy displeasure upon her that Talia instinctively stiffened.

Seven years in his company had taught her that, despite his habitual silence, he possessed a tongue capable of inflicting wounds far more painful than any blade. Once he chose to speak without restraint, his words could be merciless.

She watched his lips apprehensively, as though expecting a venomous serpent to strike.

Yet Varkas said nothing.

Instead, he reached for the robe hanging nearby, took it from its hook, and walked past her without so much as another glance.

Talia stood staring after him in disbelief.

Had he answered her with some cruel remark, or even looked upon her with open anger, she might have endured it. But to be ignored so completely, as though she were no more important than a stone lying beside the road, was an indignity she could scarcely bear.

She hurried after him and caught up just outside the tent.

“Must you always pretend that everything I say is beneath your notice?” she demanded.

Her raised voice drew the attention of several soldiers who had been carrying provisions across the encampment. They stopped briefly and turned towards the disturbance.

Varkas, however, continued walking without the slightest indication that he had heard her.

His indifference only increased her fury.

Talia seized his sleeve and gave it a sharp tug. Perhaps unwilling to have his clothing torn before the watching servants, Varkas finally halted.

She stepped before him, her face burning with resentment.

“How long have you been waiting for this?” she demanded, forcing each word through clenched teeth. “You must be delighted to be rid of the obligation to obey a girl you despise. Is that why you will not even pretend to listen to me anymore?”

“Your words would first have to possess some meaning before I could trouble myself to listen.”

His answer was cold and immediate.

He prised her fingers from his sleeve and brushed the fabric as though removing something unpleasant.

Talia's teeth clenched.

The small gesture wounded her pride more deeply than she wished to admit. To see him behave as though her touch had soiled him filled her with an almost unbearable humiliation.

For one bitter moment, she thought that perhaps it would be better if he simply ceased to exist. At least then she would no longer have to endure the misery of loving a man who regarded her with such contempt.

Her eyes flashed with hostility, and a harsh laugh escaped her lips.

“My words have no meaning to you? Then perhaps I ought to speak in the language of beasts, as your barbarian ancestors once did. Would you understand me better then?”

The servants who had been watching the quarrel turned pale.

Even among the imperial household, such an insult was intolerably offensive. Yet Varkas merely looked down at her, his expression cold and unreadable.

Encouraged by his silence, Talia continued recklessly.

“Perhaps I should imitate the neighing of a horse. I imagine that would be far easier for you to understand. After all, you have always preferred horses to human beings.”

“Indeed, I would much rather converse with a horse than with you,” Varkas replied.

“At least my stallion and I are capable of understanding one another. He does not weary me with endless complaints and childish whining.”

Talia's shoulders trembled with indignation.

Observing her expression, Varkas gave a faint, incredulous smile.

“One sharp reply is enough to make you flush with anger, yet you never hesitate to strike at another person's weaknesses. Tell me, do you behave so disgracefully because you imagine no one else capable of being as cruel as yourself?”

Talia glared at him, her throat tightening with the desperate desire to answer.

What could you possibly know of me?

Who in this world understands human cruelty better than I do?

She knew only too well how merciless people could be. It was precisely because she understood their wickedness that she had resolved to become cruel herself.

If I do not trample upon them first, they will surely trample upon me.

Yet to speak such thoughts aloud would be to expose the very weaknesses she had spent years concealing.

Talia swallowed the words and took a step backwards. With considerable effort, she composed her features into an expression of indifference, as though she had not been shouting at him only moments before.

“I did not come here to waste my time exchanging insults,” she declared. “As I have already explained, I wish to move my campsite elsewhere. Give the necessary orders to your knights and have the preparations made at once.”

Varkas drew a long breath, evidently summoning what remained of his patience.

“I have no intention of indulging another of your whims. Cease this foolishness and return to your quarters.”

“I am not demanding that the entire encampment be moved! Why should I not be permitted to establish my own quarters elsewhere?”

“I am under no obligation to explain every decision I make to you.”

“I am the Emperor's daughter! If I command you to do something, you are bound to obey—”

“That is enough.”

His voice cut through hers with such sudden authority that Talia fell silent.

A tall shadow descended over her as Varkas stepped closer. She instinctively drew back, her shoulders tightening beneath his imposing presence.

He bent slightly towards her, abandoning even the customary politeness with which he ordinarily addressed a member of the imperial family.

“I have already exhausted every measure of patience I possessed for you today,” he said in a low, chilling voice. “If you have come this far, you ought at least to know when to reserve your foolishness for tomorrow.”

Talia stared up at him, unable to speak.

Even now, with anger sharpening his features, he appeared every inch a nobleman. His bearing remained dignified, his expression composed, and his authority unquestionable.

He seemed incapable of losing the natural distinction that set him apart from other men.

And somehow, that only made her feel more wretched.

“Escort Her Highness back to her quarters.”

Straightening, Varkas addressed the knights who had gathered nearby.

Those who had been silently observing the confrontation immediately stepped forward.

“This way, Your Highness,” one of them said respectfully.

Talia cast the men a furious glance as they moved to block her path. Then she turned towards Varkas once more, intending to protest.

But he had already walked away.

She stood watching his retreating figure, her teeth grinding together as he moved through the encampment with his usual effortless dignity.

He had not even troubled himself to ask why she wished to move her campsite.

Not once had he shown the slightest curiosity about her reasons.

He does not care what I think. He never has.

A bitter resentment swelled within her.

How foolish she had been to concern herself with his safety. She had endured humiliation, quarrelled with her own guard, and sought him out in desperation, all because she feared that Senevia might be plotting something against him.

Yet he had dismissed her as though her very presence were an inconvenience.

I truly wish he would just die.

The thought rose unbidden, cruel and desperate.

Why should she trouble herself over his fate? Once the pilgrimage ended, he would belong to another woman. He would stand beside Ayla, offer her his loyalty, and perhaps bestow upon her the tenderness that Talia had spent seven miserable years longing to receive.

He would never be hers.

Then what difference would it make if I were to see his lifeless body tomorrow morning?

Talia clenched her hands tightly at her sides.

With one final, resentful glance in the direction he had gone, she turned away.
