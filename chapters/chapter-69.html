Chapter 69

Varkas stepped onto the carriage's mounting block and looked down at Talia. His eyes narrowed slightly as his gaze travelled over her figure.

A faint frown appeared between his brows.

Before Talia could understand what had displeased him, he entered the carriage, picked up the cloak that had fallen upon the floor, and placed it around her shoulders.

Then, as though it were the most natural thing in the world, he bent down to lift her into his arms.

Startled, Talia immediately pushed against his shoulders.

“I am perfectly capable of walking on my own!”

The effects of the medicine had begun to fade, and the pain in her knees was returning. Nevertheless, she believed she could manage a few steps without assistance.

She pushed the heavy cloak aside and carefully stepped down, taking great care that her scars remained hidden beneath her skirts.

But before she could move any farther, a firm hand caught her shoulder.

“Do you truly intend to go outside dressed in such a manner?”

His sharp gaze made her shrink back.

Confused, Talia lowered her eyes to examine herself.

Only then did she discover that the laces of her gown had loosened while she slept. Her thin shoulders and upper arms were exposed, and the neckline had slipped dangerously low, revealing more of her chest than was proper.

Her face immediately turned crimson.

She hurriedly pulled the bodice back into place, struggling with the loosened fabric.

Varkas watched her awkward efforts in silence.

At last, he released a quiet sigh and once again placed his coat around her shoulders.

This time, Talia offered no resistance.

He fastened the garment securely beneath her chin before lifting her into his arms and stepping out of the carriage.

Wrapped so completely in the heavy fabric, Talia felt rather like a doll being carried about.

No. I must not mistake this for tenderness.

He merely pities a cripple who cannot even walk without assistance.

She deliberately forced those cruel thoughts upon herself.

It was better to wound her own heart than allow him to do so.

If she destroyed her foolish hopes herself, perhaps there would be nothing left for him to break.

“My lord, what are your orders concerning the messenger who has arrived from the East?”

As Varkas approached the entrance, one of the men standing nearby stepped forward to address him.

Talia cautiously peered through a narrow opening in the cloak.

The man was broad and powerfully built, almost like a bear, with sharp eyes that reminded her of a hawk. He was casting curious glances in her direction.

“Must I personally attend to every insignificant matter?”

Varkas's reply was cold.

As he spoke, he drew the cloak farther over Talia's head, concealing her face completely.

“See that he is given suitable quarters and assign someone to attend to his needs. I shall receive him at first light.”

Without waiting for the man's response, Varkas carried her into the manor.

He crossed the spacious entrance hall and began ascending the staircase.

Hidden within his cloak, Talia quietly considered the fragments of conversation she had overheard.

It seemed that many people feared this marriage would weaken the relationship between the Crown Prince and House Sierkhan.

While Senevier had occupied herself with arranging the wedding, Varkas must have been dealing with the political consequences.

He would have needed to reassure Gareth, who surely regarded the marriage as a betrayal, while convincing Marquis Oristein and the conservative nobles that his loyalty remained unchanged.

Yet none of them appeared willing to accept the sudden replacement of the bride.

I suppose I cannot blame them.

Even now, I can scarcely believe this marriage has truly taken place.

A part of her still feared that the entire affair was some elaborate and cruel deception.

“I shall have someone come and attend to you.”

Varkas spoke as he carried her into a spacious bedroom.

He gently lowered her onto a long velvet sofa.

Talia looked around the comfortably furnished room before turning her attention back to him.

Varkas was removing his formal coat with one hand while reaching towards the bellpull beside the bed with the other.

The sight immediately filled her with unease.

“I have no need of an attendant!”

Her voice came out sharper than she had intended.

Varkas glanced over his shoulder, a slight frown appearing upon his forehead.

Talia quickly looked away.

“Just bring Nanny to me.”

“It is already late. I shall have someone fetch her at dawn. For tonight, you must make do with another attendant.”

Talia immediately glared at him.

“I will not allow anyone except Nanny to touch me! You were the one who brought me here without asking what I wished. The least you can do is take responsibility and bring her to me at once!”

A cold expression settled over Varkas's blue eyes.

Talia clenched her fists so tightly that her knuckles began to ache.

She knew how little patience he had for her demands.

Indeed, she half expected him to turn away in disgust and leave her alone.

Yet, to her surprise, he merely walked towards the door and summoned a servant.

“Send someone to the Separate Palace immediately. The Quarter Dwarf is to be brought here.”

The servant stared at him in confusion.

“Forgive me, my lord, but do you mean tonight?”

“Yes. Send your fastest rider. I expect the matter to be attended to without delay.”

Varkas closed the door before the bewildered servant could ask another question.

Then he turned towards Talia, giving her a sharp look that seemed to ask whether she was finally satisfied.

Talia lowered her eyes without answering.

After releasing a long breath, Varkas took a robe from a nearby shelf and held it out to her.

“At least put this on until someone arrives to help you change.”

Talia immediately accepted the robe and pulled it over her wedding gown.

Then, after a moment's hesitation, she spoke in a strained voice.

“Now... please leave. I wish to remain alone until Nanny arrives.”

Varkas looked at her.

“This is my chamber as well.”

For a moment, Talia felt as though the room had begun to spin.

Cold sweat gathered upon her palms.

She pulled the robe tightly around her chest and nervously moistened her dry lips.

“Th-then I shall leave instead. Have another room prepared for me.”

“Your Highness.”

Varkas placed a hand upon her shoulder.

When Talia reluctantly raised her face, she found his blue eyes darkened by an expression she could not understand.

“You must remember that you agreed to this marriage.”

Talia stared at him, her eyes widening.

Does he truly intend to remain here?

In the same room as me?

Her chest tightened with fear.

She lowered her gaze towards her aching knees.

Suddenly, she remembered the way Senevier had looked upon her scars.

Her mother's cold, disgusted expression returned so vividly that, for an instant, Talia imagined seeing the same revulsion reflected in Varkas's silver-blue eyes.

Before she could restrain herself, the words came rushing from her mouth.

“Then... then perhaps we ought to put an end to this marriage! How can you expect me to share a room with you? I only agreed to marry you because I wished to make you miserable. And you only married me because the Emperor commanded it!”

Her voice trembled as she continued.

“You have never wanted to be near me. I know that perfectly well. So why must we continue pretending? We could simply agree that none of this ever happened, and—”

“Talia.”

Varkas interrupted her quietly.

He lowered himself onto one knee before her and gently took her face between his hands.

Talia froze.

His silver-flecked blue eyes were now so close that she could see her own pale, frightened reflection within them.

His voice was low and slightly rough when he spoke.

“I give you my word that I shall do nothing to you.”

Talia stared at him without speaking.

He continued in a calm, measured tone.

“It is only for tonight. I have managed to silence the priest, but I cannot prevent every servant in the household from gossiping. At least on our wedding night, we must appear to share the same chamber.”

His steady explanation gradually eased the frantic beating of her heart.

Talia pressed her lips together.

After a moment, she gave a small nod.

Seeing that she had finally calmed herself, Varkas slowly rose to his feet.

Talia followed his movements with wary eyes.

He removed his damp coat and settled into a chair beside the window, wearing only his thin shirt.

His broad shoulders, usually held with such rigid discipline, sank back against the chair in obvious exhaustion.

A long, weary sigh escaped him.

For a while, silence filled the bedroom.

Then a group of servants arrived carrying a tray of food and wine.

Talia ate a small piece of bread, forcing herself to swallow it despite having little appetite.

Instead of asking for the medicinal herbs that ordinarily helped her sleep, she reached for the strong liquor.

She poured herself a glass and drank it quickly.

The burning spirit spread through her chest, bringing with it a welcome warmth.

Gradually, the tension in her muscles began to ease, and the persistent pain in her legs grew duller.

Encouraged by the relief, she poured herself another glass.

Then another.

By the time she had emptied several cups, Varkas, who had been watching her in silence, finally reached forward and took the bottle from her hand.

“That will be quite enough.”

Talia immediately pushed herself up from her chair.

She reached towards him, determined to take the bottle back.

But the moment she tried to stand, her weakened legs gave way beneath her.

The wine had robbed her of what little strength remained.

Unable to support herself, she collapsed.

Varkas caught her before she could strike the floor.

Without a word, he lifted her limp body and carried her towards the bed, where he carefully laid her down.

Even in her drunken, half-conscious state, Talia's first instinct was to reach for the hem of her gown.

Her trembling fingers hurriedly gathered the fabric, making certain that her injured legs remained concealed.

Varkas looked down at her.

His blue eyes darkened, though he said nothing.

Instead, he drew the thick duvet over her body and carefully pulled it up to her shoulders.

Then he walked towards the window and began closing the heavy curtains.

Outside, the setting sun had stained the sky in shades of burnt orange and deep violet.

Its fading light fell across his broad back, bathing his shoulders in a warm, bronze glow.

Talia watched him through heavy eyelids.

Even surrounded by that beautiful, fiery light, Varkas seemed impossibly distant.

So cold.

So utterly beyond her reach.

She blinked slowly, feeling a faint shiver pass through her.

How strange...

Even beneath the warmth of the setting sun, he still looks so terribly cold.
