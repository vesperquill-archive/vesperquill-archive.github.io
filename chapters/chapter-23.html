Chapter 23

Contrary to Talia's fears, the journey continued without incident.

Though the nights descended upon the encampment like a thick veil of darkness, each morning arrived without bloodshed. Before the sun reached its highest point, the travellers would gather their belongings and resume their march.

The oppressive heat occasionally delayed their progress, leaving exhausted soldiers struggling to keep pace. Nevertheless, the pilgrimage proceeded without any serious disturbance.

Five days after departing the Imperial Palace, the procession, led by Crown Prince Gares and his personal guard, arrived in Sortika, a small city in the northwest. After resting there for a day, they continued their journey northward.

Throughout this time, Talia kept a watchful eye upon the attendants Senevia had assigned to accompany her.

They conducted themselves with such exaggerated devotion that she found their loyalty almost repulsive. Not for a moment did she allow herself to trust them.

They are merely waiting for the proper opportunity.

She was certain that something dreadful would happen before long. Wherever Senevia turned her attention, some sinister design was sure to follow.

If not today, then tomorrow. If not tomorrow, then perhaps the day after.

Sooner or later, a terrible scene would unfold before her eyes.

Yet Talia could scarcely decide whether she dreaded that moment or secretly longed for it.

Whenever she witnessed Varkas, who treated her with such cold indifference, showing endless tenderness towards Ayla, she found herself wishing that some dreadful calamity would strike them both.

At such moments, she thought she would rather see their bodies lying lifeless before her than endure another sight of him standing affectionately beside her half-sister.

But when darkness fell, those cruel wishes gave way to an almost suffocating terror.

No matter how often she told herself that she cared nothing for his fate, her heart refused to believe it.

Night after night, she lay awake, trembling with apprehension.

At last, unable to endure her anxiety any longer, Talia left her tent before the first light of dawn had fully broken.

Guided by the faint grey light of morning, she went in search of Varkas. It seemed that she would not be able to draw a peaceful breath until she had seen with her own eyes that he was still alive and unharmed.

She hurried along a narrow path bordered by thick undergrowth, only to stop abruptly at the sound of a horse snorting nearby.

Parting the branches, she caught sight of a familiar grey stallion with a long, flowing black mane.

It was Torque.

Varkas had led the great horse towards a small spring and was now kneeling upon one knee beside the water. Holding the reins, he gently encouraged the animal to lower its head and drink, while his free hand gathered cool spring water and poured it over the stallion's strong neck.

The first rays of sunlight filtered through the leaves above, bathing his pale hair in a soft, silvery radiance.

Talia stood motionless, watching him.

For a few moments, she scarcely dared to breathe.

Then she closed her eyes in despair.

No matter how desperately she struggled to uproot her feelings, her love for Varkas continued to grow within her like some dreadful disease, consuming her little by little.

She had tried to hate him. She had wished him gone. She had told herself countless times that he meant nothing to her.

Yet the mere sight of him alive and unharmed was enough to ease the terror that had tormented her throughout the night.

How am I ever to free myself from this wretched feeling?

Talia leaned against the trunk of a large tree and gazed helplessly towards the pale morning sky.

After a while, she turned to leave.

But before she could take more than a few steps, she noticed Ayla approaching along the path.

Talia immediately concealed herself behind the tree.

Her half-sister appeared to have come directly from her bed. She wore only a light robe over a thin dress, and her long hair hung loosely about her shoulders.

Her appearance was scarcely more orderly than Talia's own. Indeed, she looked even less prepared to be seen in public.

And yet, somehow, Ayla remained graceful and dignified.

Talia wondered bitterly whether there was something in her half-sister's very blood that she herself could never possess, no matter how earnestly she might desire it.

“So this is where you have been.”

Ayla spoke softly, a delicate flush colouring her cheeks as she approached Varkas.

She seated herself upon a flat stone beside the spring.

Varkas turned his gaze towards her.

That simple glance seemed enough to fill Ayla with happiness, for her eyes curved into a gentle smile.

She carefully removed her shoes and lowered her bare feet into the clear water, sending little ripples across its surface.

The stallion snorted softly nearby. Water splashed against the stones, and Ayla's bright, birdlike laughter mingled with the cool morning air.

Hidden behind the tree, Talia clenched her hands.

She was seized by the violent desire to rush forward and seize her half-sister by the hair.

She wanted to tear away the smile that Ayla bestowed so freely upon him and silence the cheerful voice that seemed to delight him.

Yet even more unbearable was the thought that Varkas would immediately step forward to protect her.

Talia remained hidden, forcing herself to endure the sight.

After amusing herself in the spring for some time, Ayla extended a hand towards Varkas.

Talia expected him to take it and help her rise.

Instead, he bent down before her.

With quiet care, he dried her wet feet and then placed her shoes upon them, one after the other, as though he were a devoted attendant serving his mistress.

The sight pierced Talia's heart like a dagger.

She could bear no more.

Turning away, she fled.

Branches caught at her sleeves, and sharp leaves scratched her arms and calves as she forced her way through the undergrowth. Yet she scarcely felt them.

It was as though every part of her body had become numb, leaving only the pain within her chest.

She ran along the winding forest path, breathing heavily, her steps growing increasingly reckless.

Then her foot caught upon a tree root protruding from the earth.

Talia stumbled and fell heavily into the bushes.

For several moments, she lay where she had fallen, her chest rising and falling with each ragged breath.

Then, quite suddenly, she began to laugh.

What would Senevia say if she could see her now?

Talia could almost imagine her mother's elegant brows drawing together in disapproval, followed by a slow shake of her head.

The Empress's mocking voice seemed to echo in her ears.

“You have two choices. Either obtain the man you desire by whatever means are necessary, or resign yourself to being a somewhat less miserable failure.”

Talia laughed bitterly.

Senevia would undoubtedly have preferred her to become a temptress, to employ every charm and deception until Varkas was hers.

The Empress had never hesitated to seize whatever she desired, regardless of the methods required.

But Talia could never become like her mother.

She did not possess Senevia's cunning, nor did she know how to turn another person's affections to her advantage.

All she could do was endure the misery and pray that the painful days would pass as swiftly as possible.

For a long while, she lay gazing at the fragments of pale sky visible between the branches overhead.

At last, she forced herself to rise.

With weary steps, Talia made her way out of the shadowed forest.

Upon returning to the encampment, she discovered several knights wandering about with troubled expressions. Their evident confusion suggested that they had been searching for someone.

She paid them little attention and proceeded towards her carriage.

But just as she reached it, a member of the Imperial Guard hurried into her path.

Rubon, or Ruben, or whatever his name might have been.

“Where in heaven's name have you been, Your Highness?” the knight demanded. “You disappeared without a word! How many times must I remind you that you are not permitted to wander about without an escort?”

He appeared prepared to continue his reprimand, but his words abruptly faltered.

His gaze travelled over her dishevelled clothing, the scratches upon her skin, and the leaves clinging to her skirts.

For a moment, he looked genuinely alarmed.

“Your Highness… what has happened to you? Have you encountered some danger?”

Talia offered no explanation.

She merely stepped past him and placed her foot upon the carriage step.

The knight, however, seemed unwilling to abandon the matter.

Taking hold of the doorframe, he continued in a firm voice.

“I am charged with ensuring Your Highness's safety. Therefore, I must insist that—”

“One might almost believe you were truly concerned for me.”

Talia looked down at him with a mocking smile.

“I suppose you were instructed never to let that troublesome princess out of your sight, even for a moment. But if you have been given the duty of watching me, then you ought to pay attention.”

Her expression grew colder.

“Why should I be blamed merely because you were foolish enough to lose sight of me while standing about like an idiot?”

The knight fell silent.

His lips pressed tightly together, and for once, he seemed unable to find a suitable reply.

Talia turned away and slammed the carriage door shut.

Unfortunately, the man's fingers had remained upon the doorframe.

A sharp curse rang out from outside.

His gauntlets had evidently spared him any serious injury, but his indignant complaints continued for some time, suggesting that the blow had been painful enough.

Talia paid him no attention.

As always, she allowed the resentful murmurs outside to pass unheard.

Had she taken every cruel word and whispered complaint to heart, she would surely have lost her senses long ago.

Indeed, one of the first lessons she had learned upon becoming an Imperial Princess was the necessity of pretending not to hear.

She drew the heavy curtains across the carriage windows, shutting out the pale morning light.

Then she curled herself into a small, defensive figure, like a hedgehog withdrawing beneath its spines.
