Chapter 41

The veins along Gareth's neck stood out as he struggled to contain his fury. Edric instinctively moved closer to Talia, preparing himself in case the Crown Prince attempted to strike her again. He was not the only one who sensed the danger. Varkas, who had remained silent throughout the confrontation, finally stepped forward and positioned himself between the two siblings.

“We have already fallen considerably behind schedule,” Varkas said firmly. “Let us put an end to this and proceed with the ceremony.”

For the briefest moment, Talia went still. Her eyes shifted towards Varkas, and her entire body seemed to stiffen. The change was so slight that most people would have overlooked it, but Edric, whose nerves were already strained, noticed immediately. So even Her Highness knows when to hold her tongue. It appeared that the Second Princess, reckless as she was, could still be made to retreat when Lord Sierkhan spoke in that cold, commanding manner.

Talia pressed her lips together and turned her face away. Gareth, meanwhile, looked as though his anger might consume him. His jaw tightened, and he ground his teeth before reluctantly returning to his position before the altar.

The head priest, who had been watching the confrontation with his head lowered and his shoulders drawn in, finally dared to breathe again. He cleared his throat and resumed the sacred rites. Standing a short distance behind the imperial family, Edric watched as the ceremony proceeded.

The Crown Prince was the first to approach the altar. He bowed his head, and the priest lifted a silver chalice filled with holy water. As he poured the blessed water over Gareth, he recited an ancient prayer in a solemn voice. Next came the First Princess, accompanied by her betrothed, Lord Sierkhan. They received the blessing in turn before stepping aside.

At last, Talia approached the altar. She lowered her head respectfully, her usual arrogance replaced by an unexpected stillness. Edric found himself staring at her. For some reason, the woman standing before the altar seemed entirely different from the troublesome princess he had come to know.

Hundreds of candles illuminated the sanctuary, bathing her in a soft, almost otherworldly glow. Their flickering light caught the golden-brown strands of her hair and reflected gently against her pale skin. For a moment, she looked less like an ordinary mortal and more like some beautiful creature from an ancient tale.

Edric watched in quiet wonder as the priest raised the silver chalice and poured holy water over Talia's bowed head. The clear liquid slipped through her golden hair, then flowed down her smooth forehead and across her cheeks. A few delicate streams traced the bridge of her nose before gathering beneath her chin. Talia remained perfectly still as the priest rested one hand gently upon her head and offered the final blessing.

“May the grace of the Almighty remain with you, now and for all eternity...”

His solemn voice echoed through the great sanctuary. At last, the lengthy prayer came to an end, and the priest rang a small silver bell to announce the completion of the consecration.

Gareth was the first to rise. Without waiting for anyone else, he turned and strode through the gathered crowd, his expression as unpleasant as ever. Ayla and Varkas followed shortly afterwards, leaving Talia to rise last.

She slowly straightened, lifted her chin, and walked away from the altar with the quiet dignity of an empress. Her ceremonial gown flowed gracefully around her, and every eye seemed to follow her as she passed through the sanctuary.

For years, people had whispered about the Second Princess with fear and contempt. They called her a bringer of misfortune, a woman whose presence invited conflict and suffering. Some even regarded her as an ill omen upon the imperial family. Yet as they watched her pass, dressed in all the splendour befitting her birth, those familiar accusations seemed strangely distant. For the first time, many of them appeared to recognise something they had long chosen to overlook.

Whatever her reputation, Talia Roem Guirta was still a princess of the Empire.

✦ ✦ ✦

The air had grown noticeably cooler as the procession climbed higher into the mountains. The oppressive heat of the lower roads had given way to a dry, refreshing chill, and as evening approached, the wind carried a sharpness that made the travellers draw their cloaks more tightly around themselves.

Inside her carriage, Talia sat beside the window and watched the fading sky. The sun was slowly disappearing beyond the distant hills, while deep shades of indigo spread across the eastern horizon. Another day was coming to an end, and once again, nothing had changed.

Talia swallowed uneasily. Her throat felt painfully dry, as though she had swallowed a burning coal. After sitting motionless for some time, she turned towards the small storage compartment in the corner of the carriage and withdrew a silver knife. It was the same blade she had secretly taken from the monastery.

The polished metal gleamed faintly in the fading light, its sharp edge looking almost impatient after so many days of being concealed. Talia stared at it for a long moment before slipping it into her pocket and rising from her seat.

When she stepped outside, the campsite was already alive with activity. Servants hurried between the tents, carrying trays of food and pitchers of wine. Laughter and music drifted through the evening air, mingling with the voices of soldiers who had already begun drinking. It appeared that someone had arranged a grand feast in an effort to improve the Crown Prince's foul temper.

Talia drew the hood of her cloak over her head and surveyed the busy camp. Her fingers closed around the cold metal hidden in her pocket. This may be my chance. With so many people moving about, it would be easier to pass unnoticed. The noise and confusion might provide precisely the distraction she needed. Moistening her dry lips, she lowered her head and quietly made her way into the camp.

She had not gone far when a familiar voice called out behind her.

“Your Highness!”

Talia's brow immediately furrowed. She turned and saw Edric approaching at a brisk pace, his expression brightening as though he were genuinely pleased to see her. That tiresome hound again. She could not understand why he insisted upon following her about with such enthusiasm. No matter how rudely she treated him, he always returned with the same foolishly cheerful expression, and his persistence was beginning to irritate her beyond endurance.

Talia gave him a cold look and attempted to walk past, but Edric, apparently undiscouraged, fell into step beside her.

“It must have been rather uncomfortable remaining inside the carriage all this time,” he remarked pleasantly. “Come, Your Highness. The servants are preparing supper nearby. If you wish, you may watch them prepare your meal yourself. That way, you can be certain that no one has tampered with it. Surely you would feel more comfortable eating something you have seen cooked with your own eyes?”

His suggestion was well meant, but Talia had little patience for it. She ignored his continued chatter and followed the sound of music drifting from the centre of the camp. Before long, she spotted Gareth seated before an elaborately decorated tent, where a large fire burned brightly.

Talia stopped at a distance and observed the gathering. Gareth lounged in his chair, a goblet of wine resting in his hand. His expression remained dark and unpleasant despite the entertainment arranged around him. Servants dressed as grotesque jesters performed tricks before the fire, tossing sharp daggers into the air and catching them with practised ease. Nearby, musicians played slow, indulgent melodies chosen to suit the Crown Prince's tastes, while several young maids hovered around his chair, pouring his wine and laughing rather too eagerly at his occasional remarks.

Their flirtatious behaviour was hardly subtle, and Talia watched the spectacle with a contemptuous smile. How very fitting.

Before she could continue, Edric stepped into her path.

“Your Highness, perhaps it would be wiser to go this way instead.”

Talia looked up at him. His expression was unusually earnest, the same concerned look he had worn while watching over her at the temple, as though he truly wished to prevent her from coming to harm. Talia almost laughed. Does he truly expect me to believe that? She had no intention of trusting a knight who served the very people responsible for her suffering.

“Attend to your own affairs,” she snapped. “I have no need of your advice.”

Without waiting for a reply, she turned away and headed towards the tents lining the riverbank. Gareth had never been her intended target. Her attention was fixed upon someone else entirely.

Ayla.

Keeping her movements as natural as possible, Talia began examining the area surrounding her half-sister's pavilion. It did not take long to find her. Ayla was seated comfortably among her attendants, enjoying her evening meal while several handmaidens gathered around her. She appeared perfectly at ease, smiling and conversing as though she had not a single worry in the world.

Talia watched her from a distance, but for a moment, she hesitated. Several knights stood guard around Ayla's pavilion. They knew Talia well enough to regard her with suspicion, and she had little doubt that any sudden attempt to approach her sister would be stopped immediately. If she behaved recklessly, she would be seized before she could accomplish anything.

She bit her lower lip. How am I supposed to get close to her without attracting attention? She studied the guards, searching for some opening in their formation, but before she could settle upon a plan, she noticed that Edric had fallen silent.

Talia glanced towards him. The knight was looking at her with a faintly puzzled expression. His earlier cheerfulness had faded, replaced by something more cautious. He had evidently begun to notice her unusual behaviour.

Talia quickly composed herself. She walked towards a nearby bench and sat down, pretending that she had merely been looking for somewhere to rest. Then she turned sharply towards Edric.

“I'm hungry. Go and fetch me something to eat.”

The change in his expression was immediate. His face brightened with such obvious relief that Talia nearly rolled her eyes. What an extraordinarily simple-minded man.

“Of course, Your Highness!” Edric replied eagerly. “Please wait here. I'll bring something at once!”

He hurried away before she could say another word. Talia watched him go, then pulled her hood a little farther over her face. At last, she was alone, and she could turn her attention back towards Ayla.

Her half-sister was laughing softly at something one of her maids had said, apparently enjoying herself. Talia stared at her for a long moment.

Could I truly take that woman's life?

Her throat tightened, and the knife felt strangely heavy in her pocket. She had imagined this moment countless times, yet now that the opportunity was before her, the reality seemed far more frightening. There were guards everywhere. If even one of them noticed her intentions, she would be restrained before she could reach Ayla.

And if she were caught attempting such a crime, there would be no forgiveness. It would not be dismissed as another petty quarrel between sisters. This time, the consequences would be final.

Talia slowly traced the outline of the knife through the fabric of her pocket. Is this truly worth dying for?

Even if Ayla were gone, Varkas would not suddenly belong to her. He would continue living, perhaps grieving for the woman he loved, while Talia herself would be dragged before an executioner. Her life would end beneath the blade, leaving behind nothing but disgrace.

What a miserable end that would be.

She knew it was foolish. She knew that destroying Ayla would not grant her the happiness she desired. And yet, the thought of living without Varkas seemed no less unbearable.

Would living be any less miserable?

If Ayla married Varkas, she would become the Grand Duchess of Sierkhan. She would stand beside him as his wife, share his home, and eventually bear his children. Talia would be forced to watch from afar as the life she had desperately longed for became another woman's happiness.

The thought was unbearable. Perhaps she would spend the rest of her days consumed by jealousy and regret, slowly wasting away while pretending that she had never wanted him. Or perhaps Empress Senevia would arrange a marriage for her, handing her over to some dreadful nobleman chosen solely for political convenience.

Talia could easily imagine being expected to smile, obey, and endure the touch of a stranger she could never love. I would rather die. The thought came with frightening certainty. She could already imagine herself driven to such despair that she would seek an end to her own suffering.

That is all the future holds for me.

Whether she died now or years later, she could see no happiness waiting at the end of either path. If her life was already destined to end in misery, perhaps it would be better to bring everything to a close tonight. And if she must fall, she would take Ayla with her.

The decision settled within her with a terrible calm. Talia rose from the bench, keeping her expression composed as she prepared to approach her half-sister as casually as possible.

But just as she was about to take her first step, someone caught her attention. A familiar figure had appeared nearby, and Talia stopped, her eyes narrowing as she stared at the woman.

That woman...
