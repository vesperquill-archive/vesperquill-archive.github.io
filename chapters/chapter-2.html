THE FORGOTTEN FIELD · CHAPTER 02

Chapter 2

Talia regarded her reflection in the looking glass. There, before her, was the countenance she had known since the very hour of her birth—a face so painfully familiar that the mere sight of it had long since become wearisome.

Yet there were certain distinctions. Where there ought to have been an elegant smile and eyes aglow with a mysterious, almost intoxicating allure, the young woman reflected in the glass possessed only parched lips and a troubled gaze, her eyes darkened by an agitation she could scarcely conceal.

Contemplating her own features with evident displeasure, Talia turned towards her nurse and demanded,

“How do I appear?”

“Why, Your Highness, you look positively angelic!”

The nurse, who had been attending tirelessly to her mistress's luxuriant tresses, delivered this assurance with a smile so delighted that her eyes narrowed into little crescents.

“Indeed, Your Highness bears the most extraordinary resemblance to Lady Senevia! Now that you have been adorned with such care, I could almost believe myself gazing upon Her Ladyship as she appeared at eighteen years of age.”

Talia impatiently struck aside the plump hand that continued to fuss over her hair. How insufferably thoughtless the woman was, to suppose that likening her to another person could possibly be received as a compliment!

“That will suffice. Leave my hair be, and fetch my garments.”

With the wounded countenance of a chastised puppy, the nurse shuffled towards the great chest.

Talia followed her with a look of undisguised disapproval.

Good heavens, could any creature be quite so obtuse?

After much rummaging, accompanied by a succession of laborious grunts, the woman withdrew a gown of crimson satin and cast a tentative glance towards her mistress.

“Pray, look upon this, Your Highness. It is the very gown Lady Senevia wore upon first entering the Imperial Palace. I daresay Her Ladyship had it preserved especially for you.”

At these words, Talia's expression darkened.

“Nurse, do you truly profess to remember every garment my mother wore so many years ago?”

“Why, certainly! How could I ever forget such a day? Lady Senevia seemed scarcely a creature of this mortal world. Such was her beauty that the mere knowledge of her existence was enough to bring tears to one's eyes. Even His Imperial Majesty himself could scarcely tear his gaze away from her!”

The nurse heaved a wistful sigh, her expression assuming the distant, enraptured air of one who had surrendered herself entirely to some cherished recollection.

Talia suppressed a mirthless laugh.

Did this foolish woman truly imagine that their first encounter had been some magnificent romance, worthy of being celebrated throughout the ages?

At the time, the Emperor had already been wed to his Empress for six years, and that unfortunate lady had been on the very brink of childbirth. His encounter with Senevia had been nothing more than a sordid affair, stripped of honour and wholly undeserving of romantic embellishment.

Even after the death of the former Empress Bernadette, when Senevia had at last ascended to the position of Empress, the circumstances of that disgraceful union had never been forgotten.

Indeed, so long as Talia herself continued to draw breath, the world would never be permitted to forget the shameless transgression from which she had sprung.

Swallowing the bitter retort that threatened to escape her lips, Talia snatched the gown from the nurse's hands.

“If you possess such an abundance of leisure for idle chatter, I suggest you devote it to completing my toilette.”

“Certainly, Your Highness. I shall attend to everything at once.”

The nurse proceeded to dress her mistress in a magnificent velvet bliaut.

When at last her attire had been arranged, Talia smoothed her hair and once more turned towards the looking glass.

Clothed in her mother's gown, she resembled Senevia more closely than ever before.

Had that ominous, almost oppressive air of sensuality begun to awaken within her as well?

Her gaze descended towards the gentle swell of her bosom, where the rounded curves of her breasts rose above the deeply cut, square neckline of the gown.

It was hardly an appearance that propriety would have sanctioned; nevertheless, none could have denied its extraordinary power to captivate.

Almost unconsciously, Talia raised a finger towards her lips, only to withdraw her hand with sudden haste, lest she disturb the careful application of her cosmetics.

On this particular evening, more than on any other, she was determined to surpass every woman present in beauty.

If it were possible, she would surpass even her mother.

I want every eye to be upon me. Let them look at me, and me alone, so that not a single person may spare so much as a passing glance for someone like Aila.

An insatiable hunger flickered within the deep blue eyes that stared back at her from the glass.

It was not the gaze befitting an Imperial Princess.

Rather, it resembled the desperate, ravenous look of some wretched beggar, driven half-mad by starvation upon the streets.

Without warning, Talia seized a candlestick from the dressing table and struck the woman in the mirror with merciless force.

A tremendous crash shattered the stillness of the chamber.

Across the glass, a multitude of delicate fractures spread like the threads of a monstrous spider's web.

The nurse, who had been occupied with arranging the hem of her mistress's gown, sank to the floor in terror.

Talia cast the candlestick aside and spoke with chilling indifference.

“I have grown quite weary of that looking glass. See that it is replaced.”

The nurse raised her pallid face towards her mistress. Pressing her lips tightly together, she struggled to her feet and, with the practised composure of one accustomed to such outbursts, behaved as though nothing whatsoever had occurred.

She merely lifted a sumptuous mantle, richly trimmed with fur, and settled it upon Talia's shoulders.

For one final moment, Talia regarded the fractured image before her, her countenance now scattered into countless distorted fragments.

Then, with an abrupt turn, she departed.

Upon entering the corridor, she observed the escort knight whom Senevia had dispatched standing rigidly at attention beside the wall.

The fellow stared at her with a conspicuously flushed countenance.

Talia paid him not the slightest heed and descended the staircase.

Before the Separate Palace stood an elegant four-wheeled carriage embellished with gold, accompanied by no fewer than eight guards.

Evidently, Senevia had no intention of allowing her eldest daughter to present a pitiable spectacle before the Imperial Court.

‘You would have me unsettle your political adversaries, I suppose.’

A cynical smile twisted Talia's lips as she entered the carriage.

At that very instant, a young palace guard stepped forward to close the door. Yet before he could accomplish his duty, he spoke in a voice so strained and fervent that the words seemed almost painfully forced from his throat.

“Your Highness... I must confess, you are exceedingly beautiful this evening.”

There was such naked longing in his voice that, for one fleeting instant, an unpleasant shiver travelled across her entire body.

Talia fixed him with a cutting glance.

She had no desire to receive the admiration of a man of his station.

“Spare me your foolish remarks and see that we depart without further delay.”

The unfortunate guard, his countenance coloured with embarrassment, hastily shut the carriage door.

Presently, the carriage lurched into motion.

Talia reclined against the cushioned seat and raised her eyes towards the heavens, visible through the gently swaying curtains.

The evening sky had assumed a deep, sanguine hue.

How delightful it would be if this evening's banquet were stained in that very same colour. Would that some dreadful commotion might arise and bring the whole wretched affair to ruin.

Absently, she began to worry at her lips, but lowered her hand at once upon discovering traces of crimson pigment upon the tips of her nails.

Her nerves seemed to burn beneath her skin, as though subjected to a slow and merciless flame.

Yet, in cruel contrast to the turmoil within her breast, the Main Palace shone with dazzling splendour, its illuminated halls overflowing with the strains of exquisite music.

Upon alighting from the carriage, Talia surveyed the broad avenue leading towards the banquet hall, together with the gardens that had been adorned with such extravagant care.

A frown settled upon her brow.

Hundreds of noblemen and ladies, resplendent in their silks, were ascending the marble staircase in orderly succession, making their way into the grand halls of the Main Palace.

Disregarding the knight who hastened after her to offer his escort, Talia strode directly towards the entrance.

Those who recognised her immediately withdrew to make way.

And why should they not?

The Imperial Palace was her own home.

She had not the faintest intention of waiting in line like some ordinary guest.

Approaching the attendant stationed at the entrance, whose expression betrayed considerable bewilderment, Talia addressed him with studied nonchalance.

“I have come to offer my felicitations upon the birthdays of my siblings.”

The servant's eyes widened in astonishment.

Talia's patience immediately deserted her.

“Well? What are you standing there for? Announce my arrival this instant!”

Only then did the unfortunate fellow scramble towards the pillar beside the entrance and, drawing himself upright, proclaim at the very height of his voice,

“Her Imperial Highness, the Second Princess, Talia Roem Guirta, makes her entrance!”

In the space of a single heartbeat, a deathly silence descended upon the magnificent hall.

With her chin raised and her bearing unyieldingly proud, Talia crossed the threshold into the splendid banquet chamber, where every surface seemed bathed in gold and light.

She could feel the scrutiny of hundreds of eyes, their gazes passing over her from the crown of her head to the very hem of her gown.

Yet she neither faltered nor lowered her own.

Instead, she savoured their astonishment, their resentment, their bewilderment, and, above all, the reluctant admiration they could not wholly suppress.

With each graceful step she took, the assembled guests retreated before her, parting like the waters of a receding tide.

It is as though I were some dreadful plague.

The thought passed through Talia's mind, and a smile of quiet malice curved her lips.

It was then that a figure suddenly stepped into her path.

“What business have you here?”

Talia lifted her gaze and regarded the man's face directly.

She recognised him at once as one of those foolish gentlemen who were forever trailing after her esteemed half-sister, Aila.

Deliberately, she allowed a mysterious smile to blossom upon her lips.

She had witnessed her mother ensnare the hearts of men with just such a smile on countless occasions.

“Pray, sir, am I to understand that my presence is forbidden here?”

The man's countenance, which had been rigid with suspicion only moments before, became suffused with colour.

In evident embarrassment, he retreated a step.

Talia advanced towards him, closing the distance by more than he had dared withdraw, and raised her chin with an air of imperious dignity.

“This is my father's palace, and the banquet held this evening is in honour of my siblings. Pray, what is there so extraordinary about my presence here?”

As her gaze met his unflinchingly, the man appeared to turn to stone, scarcely daring to stir so much as an inch.

She observed the slight flaring of his broad nostrils.

Evidently, he had caught the delicate fragrance of the rose oil her nurse had so painstakingly bestowed upon every silken strand of her hair.

When she perceived the peculiar haze settling over his eyes, as though he had been rendered senseless by some potent spirit, two sentiments arose within her at once: a curious satisfaction and a profound, almost instinctive revulsion.

Without granting him another glance, Talia swept past the wretched fellow, who could do little more than quiver his lips in foolish bewilderment, and proceeded towards the centre of the grand hall.
