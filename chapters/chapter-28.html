Chapter 28

It was probably only a few months before Talia's sixteenth birthday.

As her coming-of-age ceremony drew closer, Talia became increasingly restless. She had started bothering Varkas more than usual, constantly asking him to help her choose dresses, jewellery, and other accessories. In truth, she was only looking for excuses to spend more time with him and keep his attention on her.

Normally, Varkas would have ignored her demands. However, perhaps worn down by her constant complaints, he had recently begun answering her questions and offering his opinions.

Talia suspected that he was only being patient because he knew he would finally be free of her in a few months. The thought made her furious, but beneath her anger was a desperate wish to keep him by her side. She felt as though she would do anything if it meant delaying their separation, even for a little while.

After all, the day she had always feared was approaching much faster than she had expected.

"Varkas, look at this one. Don't you think it would be lovely for my coming-of-age ceremony?"

Wearing an elaborate silk gown imported from the south, Talia stepped forward and turned slightly, making sure he could see the dress properly.

Varkas merely glanced at her with his usual indifferent expression. His calm gaze made her cheeks grow warm, and she quickly raised her voice to hide her nervousness.

He was nearly twenty now. The boy she had known for years had grown into a striking young man, with broader shoulders and sharper features. If not for those cold, emotionless eyes, Talia thought he might have looked like an angel from a painting.

"Well? Are you going to say something, or must I stand here all day waiting for your opinion?"

Varkas looked at her for another moment before finally answering.

"It looks as though you've borrowed a dress meant for someone much older."

Talia stared at him in disbelief.

Before she could respond, he added, "I don't think it suits you."

Her cheeks immediately turned red with anger. She wanted to argue, but since she had been the one demanding his opinion, she could hardly complain that he had answered honestly.

"You really have no sense of beauty at all!"

With an irritated stomp of her foot, Talia disappeared behind the changing screen. She began searching through the enormous pile of dresses scattered around her, determined to find something that would finally make that expressionless man react.

After some time, she selected a much bolder gown. Its design was more daring than anything she had tried on that afternoon, and she hesitated briefly, wondering whether it was too much for her coming-of-age ceremony. But she quickly pushed aside her doubts and changed into it.

When she looked in the mirror, she was pleased with what she saw.

She turned from side to side, carefully examining her reflection. Over the past two years, her appearance had gradually changed as she grew older. She was no longer the thin, awkward girl she had once been, and she took considerable pride in becoming more graceful and mature.

Talia had always admired Senevia's extraordinary beauty. She dreamed of becoming just as beautiful as her mother, perhaps even surpassing her one day. Surely, once Varkas realised how much she had changed, he would begin to see her differently.

Filled with renewed confidence, she hurried out from behind the screen.

"Then what do you think of this one? Surely you can't complain about this dress too."

Varkas had been staring out the window with a bored expression. At her question, he turned his head, and for the first time that afternoon, something resembling an emotion appeared upon his face.

Unfortunately, it was not the reaction she had hoped for.

He frowned slightly as he looked at the gown.

"Change back into the first dress."

Talia blinked, caught off guard by his answer.

"The first one? Why?"

Varkas let out a quiet breath before replying.

"Because that one would be a better choice. Wear it instead."

His voice remained as indifferent as ever. There was no admiration, no embarrassment, and certainly none of the excitement she had imagined.

Yet Talia's heart began beating faster.

He remembered the first dress.

Out of all the gowns she had shown him, he had actually paid enough attention to remember which one she had worn first.

Trying to hide her happiness, she gave him a playful smile.

"Oh? So you were paying attention after all. And here I thought you weren't even looking at me."

Varkas offered no reply. Judging by his expression, he merely wanted the exhausting dress selection to end as soon as possible.

His lack of enthusiasm irritated her, but Talia decided to overlook it. After all, she could not expect his attitude to change overnight. She had already promised herself that she would gradually show him how much she was growing up.

Humming happily, she returned behind the screen and searched for the first gown.

It was an elegant velvet dress decorated with delicate elven embroidery. Until that day, it had been merely another beautiful garment among the many she owned. Now, however, it had become something precious.

Talia silently decided that she would never part with it.

After all, it was the dress Varkas had chosen for her.

She quickly changed and stepped out once more, turning around with a delighted smile.

"Here! This is the one you meant, isn't it? Tell me if I'm right."

Varkas looked at her quietly.

He did not seem particularly surprised that the girl who had been throwing tantrums only moments earlier was now smiling as though she had received the greatest gift in the world. Perhaps, to him, her sudden changes in mood were simply another part of her troublesome personality.

But Talia wanted to believe that something between them had changed.

Lately, she had noticed that his gaze sometimes lingered upon her face a little longer than before. Even his manner of speaking seemed slightly gentler, though the difference was so small that someone else might never have noticed it.

Talia noticed everything.

Those tiny changes were enough to awaken a fragile hope within her heart.

Could it be that Varkas doesn't want to leave me either?

They had spent so many years together. Surely, even someone as indifferent as him must have grown a little attached to her. Perhaps he had become so accustomed to her presence that the thought of parting was unpleasant to him as well.

Talia knew it was foolish to cling to such a small possibility, but she could not help herself.

"If I wear this to the ceremony and dance in it, don't you think I'll look like a fairy queen?"

A faint crease appeared between Varkas's brows.

Was he growing tired of her questions again? Perhaps. But Talia chose to believe that he was merely considering his answer.

"Actually, why don't we try it now? Come here and practise dancing with me!"

Before he could object, she caught his arm and began pulling him towards the centre of the room.

In the past, Varkas would not have moved an inch no matter how insistently she tugged at him. This time, however, he allowed himself to be led along, although his expression suggested that he had little interest in dancing.

See? Something really has changed!

Talia felt secretly triumphant.

She began turning around as though they were already dancing, pulling at his arm in an attempt to make him follow her movements. Varkas remained rather stiff, offering little cooperation, but she was too delighted to care.

Then, without warning, her foot caught against a chest left on the floor.

"Ah! Wait—!"

She lost her balance and instinctively grabbed Varkas's arm.

He reacted at once, reaching around her waist to keep her from falling. Unfortunately, the room was in such disorder that there was scarcely any clear space to stand.

His foot became caught in the pile of discarded dresses, and before either of them could recover their balance, they both fell onto the carpet.

Talia let out an annoyed cry.

"See what happened? I told you someone should have cleaned up this mess earlier!"

Varkas raised an eyebrow.

He had only been trying to prevent her from falling, yet somehow he was the one being blamed. Besides, he was her guardian knight, not a servant responsible for tidying her belongings.

Talia knew this perfectly well, but embarrassment made her even more unreasonable.

"And if I find even one bruise, I'm holding you responsible! What kind of knight lets his princess fall like that?"

Still grumbling, she tried to sit up. However, a sudden pain at the back of her head made her stop.

"Ouch!"

She glared at him accusingly.

"Varkas! Why are you pulling my hair?"

He sighed.

"I'm not pulling it. Your hair has become tangled around the button on my sleeve."

He carefully moved the arm resting behind her back, attempting to free the tangled strands. Unfortunately, the movement pulled at her hair again, causing Talia to cry out.

"Ow! Stop moving! You're making it worse, you idiot!"

She struck his chest in frustration.

Varkas immediately stopped moving and looked down at her. Her eyes had begun to water from the pain, though she continued glaring at him as though he had deliberately caused it.

For a brief moment, Talia felt guilty. After all, he had only tried to help her. Nevertheless, she refused to apologise.

Varkas released another quiet sigh before returning his arm behind her back. This time, he moved much more carefully, patiently working to untangle her hair from the troublesome button.

Talia sat awkwardly between his knees, suddenly uncertain where to look.

Only now did she realise how close they were.

Her earlier anger faded, replaced by an unfamiliar nervousness. She could smell the faint scent of soap and mint from his clothes, and the simple closeness made her heart beat faster.

She lowered her eyes, afraid that he might notice her embarrassment.

Her heart was pounding so loudly that she wondered whether Varkas could hear it too.
