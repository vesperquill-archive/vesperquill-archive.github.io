Chapter 34

The door would not open. Talia tried turning the handle several times before realising it had been locked from the inside. She bit her already wounded lip, struggling to control the anger that had been building inside her all night.

Damn you, Ayla.

She stood glaring at the door, her frustration growing with every passing second. Eventually, she forced herself to step away. Perhaps it was better that she had been stopped. If she had acted without a proper plan and failed, she might have lost her life for nothing.

There would be other opportunities during their journey. She only needed to be patient and prepare more carefully. After calming her breathing, Talia turned towards the stairs, intending to return to her room.

Just then, a faint rattling sound made her stop. She quickly slipped behind a nearby pillar and listened. Dawn was beginning to brighten the corridor, revealing a slender woman standing outside one of the rooms near her own.

Talia narrowed her eyes. The woman had dark brown hair and an ordinary face, but there was something familiar about her. After studying her for a moment, Talia recognised her as one of the attendants Empress Senevia had assigned to her.

The woman quietly closed the door, pulled a dark hood over her head, and hurried away. Her movements were so careful and controlled that Talia immediately grew suspicious. Without making a sound, she decided to follow.

The attendant left the building and walked along the muddy path, carefully avoiding the puddles left by the storm. Despite the slippery ground, she moved quickly and confidently. Talia watched her from a distance, wondering whether the woman had received some kind of special training.

After checking her surroundings several times, the attendant headed towards a small garden behind the monastery's main hall. Talia hid behind a pillar along the covered walkway and watched as the woman stopped near a pavilion.

For several minutes, the attendant paced back and forth as though waiting for someone. Then she suddenly straightened and hurried towards a figure emerging from the shadows.

It was a priest dressed in black robes. He appeared to be in his forties, and Talia thought she recognised him from the banquet held at the monastery.

The two spoke briefly before the priest reached inside his robe and handed the woman a small glass container. She immediately opened it to examine the contents, then closed it again and concealed it inside her sleeve.

Once their exchange was finished, the attendant turned towards the lodging building. Talia hurried away from her hiding place and rushed back before the woman could discover her. By the time she reached her bedroom, she was breathing heavily and could barely feel her legs.

She closed the door and leaned against it, listening carefully. A short while later, she heard footsteps outside, followed by the sound of another door opening and closing. Then the corridor became silent again.

Talia swallowed nervously. She could not be certain what she had witnessed, but she knew it was not an ordinary meeting.

Who was that priest? Could he be one of Mother's secret supporters?

Her thoughts returned to the small container. If the priest had been helping the Empress, what exactly had he given her attendant?

Was it poison?

Talia frowned. Ordinary poisons were easy enough for someone with Senevia's resources to obtain. There would be little reason to arrange such a secret exchange unless the substance was unusual or difficult to bring into the monastery.

What is Mother planning this time?

She considered several possibilities but could not reach a definite conclusion. Still, one thing seemed certain: something important was about to happen.

Empress Senevia was not the kind of woman who abandoned her plans once she had made a decision. If she had finally chosen to eliminate those who opposed her, the Crown Prince might already be in danger. And perhaps Ayla would disappear along with him.

Talia felt her heart begin to race. The possibility filled her with a strange excitement, and a small smile appeared on her lips. Perhaps she would not have to act against Ayla herself after all.

The imperial procession resumed its long journey beneath the blazing sun. Hundreds of knights and infantrymen travelled along the dusty road, accompanying the carriages and supply wagons. If they continued at their present pace, they would reach the border of the former Kingdom of Osiria in approximately ten days.

Edric Rupen unfolded his map and checked the remaining distance. After studying their route, he turned towards the carriage travelling behind him. As usual, the Second Princess had shut herself inside, with thick curtains covering every window. There had been no movement from within for quite some time.

Edric frowned. Even the horses were struggling in the unbearable heat. He could only imagine how suffocating the air inside the closed carriage had become.

Is she trying to cook herself alive in there?

Concerned, he guided his horse alongside the carriage and leaned towards the window.

"Your Highness, wouldn't it be more comfortable if you opened the curtains? You should at least let some fresh air inside."

There was a long silence before a faint voice answered.

"...Just leave me alone."

Edric sighed in relief. Well, at least she's still alive. Knowing that further arguments would accomplish nothing, he urged his horse forward and rode towards the front of the procession.

The Crown Prince was travelling at the head of the group, riding his magnificent golden horse from Norneck. His face was red from the heat, and he appeared to be in a particularly unpleasant mood. Gares was complaining loudly to Lord Sierkhan, demanding to know how much longer they would have to travel before stopping.

Edric watched them with growing concern. This journey is going to be difficult. For the next several days, they would be crossing rough fields and mountainous terrain. There would be few comfortable places to rest, and the conditions would only become more exhausting.

He wondered whether the Crown Prince, who was accustomed to the comforts of the imperial palace, could endure such a demanding journey. Shaking his head, Edric approached Varkas.

His superior was riding calmly beside Gares, keeping his eyes on the road ahead. Despite the Crown Prince's endless complaints, Varkas showed no sign of irritation. Edric admired his composure. He had never understood how the man could remain so calm while someone shouted beside him.

After a moment of hesitation, he spoke.

"Lord Sierkhan, the soldiers are beginning to suffer from the heat. I know it's earlier than we planned, but perhaps we should consider stopping for a short rest."

Varkas turned towards him. Edric immediately straightened in his saddle. Although he had never seen his superior lose his temper, he always felt strangely nervous whenever Varkas looked directly at him.

He quickly continued, hoping he had not overstepped his position.

"Forgive me if I'm speaking out of turn, my lord. It's just that the men at the rear are starting to fall behind. I'm worried they won't be able to maintain this pace much longer."

"There's a lake about half a gack ahead," Varkas replied calmly. "We'll stop there and allow everyone to rest."

Edric blinked in surprise. He had expected a longer discussion, perhaps even a reprimand for questioning their schedule. Instead, Varkas had already decided where they would stop.

"Understood, my lord."

Before he could leave, however, the Crown Prince interrupted angrily.

"Are you serious? I've been telling you to stop for ages, and you wouldn't even respond! But the moment this inexperienced knight suggests it, you suddenly agree?"

Varkas turned towards him without changing his expression.

"I heard everything Your Highness said. I simply didn't believe another response was necessary."

Gares stared at him in disbelief.

"And who gave you the authority to decide whether my questions deserve an answer?"

His voice rose so loudly that Edric felt his ears ringing. He resisted the urge to shake his head. The Crown Prince was known for his terrible temper, but whenever he argued with Varkas, he seemed more like a spoiled child than the future Emperor.

What will happen when Lord Sierkhan finally leaves the Imperial Knights?

The thought troubled Edric. Varkas Raedgo Sierkhan would eventually leave for the East, and Princess Ayla would accompany him. Once they were gone, Gares and Talia Roem Guirta would be the only imperial siblings remaining at the palace.

Without Varkas to restrain the Crown Prince's temper, the knights would have to endure his endless demands. And dealing with the Second Princess would hardly make their duties any easier.

Those two together... What a nightmare.

Edric sighed heavily, already feeling sorry for the men who would remain behind. He returned to his position near Talia's carriage.

A short while later, a whistle sounded from the front of the procession, signalling that they had finally reached their resting place. Edric brought his horse to a halt and instructed his men to prepare a suitable area.

The knights quickly began setting up temporary tents on a level patch of ground near the lake, creating enough shade for the soldiers and members of the imperial household. Once the carriage had stopped, Edric dismounted and approached its window.

He knocked lightly.

"Your Highness, we've stopped for a break. You should come outside and get some fresh air."

There was no response. Edric waited a moment before knocking again.

"Princess Talia, how long are you planning to stay shut inside? It's unbearably hot today. If you continue like this, you're going to make yourself sick."

Still, the carriage remained silent. His patience began to wear thin. He deliberately raised his voice, hoping to provoke her into answering.

"Your Highness, this stubbornness is getting ridiculous. You've been inside that carriage all day. At least come out for a while before you collapse from the heat."

Normally, Talia would have responded with an angry insult or ordered him to leave immediately. But this time, nothing happened.

Edric frowned. Wait... did she actually faint? An uneasy feeling settled in his stomach.

Without waiting any longer, he grabbed the handle and pulled the carriage door open. A wave of suffocating heat immediately rushed towards him. The air inside was thick and strangely sweet, carrying a fragrance that reminded him of honey mixed with warm milk.

Edric recoiled slightly before leaning forward to look into the dim interior. His eyes gradually adjusted to the darkness. Then he saw her.

Princess Talia was lying motionless on the carriage floor. Edric froze.
