Chapter 42

The woman Talia had been observing so carefully over the past several days finally appeared, slipping quietly between the tents. Talia narrowed her eyes and watched her for a moment before cautiously following. The woman moved with remarkable skill, her footsteps so light and her movements so discreet that an ordinary observer would scarcely have noticed her passing. Had Talia not recognised the distinctive methods employed by those trained under House Taren, she would undoubtedly have lost sight of her.

Within moments, the mysterious figure crossed the campsite and disappeared behind a row of supply wagons. Talia hesitated briefly before hurrying after her, taking care not to attract attention. When she reached the heavy carts, she peered through the narrow space between them, but the woman was nowhere to be seen.

A faint uneasiness settled over her. Talia examined her surroundings before squeezing into the cramped passage between the enormous wagons. She had taken only a few steps when her foot struck something lying upon the ground.

What could this be?

Frowning, she bent down and discovered a small glass vial beneath one of the wagon wheels. She picked it up and examined it with curiosity. A little liquid remained around its opening, and as she turned the bottle, the slippery substance brushed against her fingertips.

Startled by the unexpected sensation, Talia immediately tossed the vial aside. She raised her fingers towards her face and cautiously sniffed them, only to recoil in disgust. A nauseating mixture of overly sweet flowers and the foul, animal-like stench of bodily secretions assaulted her senses. The odour was so revolting that her head spun and her stomach threatened to empty itself.

With a grimace, she hurriedly took out her handkerchief and scrubbed her fingers. Yet no matter how vigorously she wiped them, the unpleasant smell seemed determined to remain.

How utterly vile.

She threw the soiled handkerchief onto the ground and straightened, intending to leave the place at once. But the moment she raised her head, she found a tall shadow standing directly before her.

Talia gasped and stumbled backwards, losing her balance and falling onto the dirt. Looking up in alarm, she discovered Varkas standing over her, his expression as cold and unreadable as ever.

"What business have you in a place like this?" he asked.

For several moments, Talia could do nothing but stare. Then she hastily scrambled to her feet, brushing the dirt from her clothes.

How long has he been standing there?

Her heart began pounding against her ribs. Although she had not yet carried out anything suspicious, the mere sight of him filled her with alarm. Determined not to reveal her uneasiness, she lifted her chin and answered with her usual sharpness.

"You frightened me half to death! Must you creep about so silently, like some wretched rat? What do you think you are doing?"

"You have yet to answer my question."

Varkas paid no attention to her insults. He merely unfolded his arms and took a measured step towards her. Talia instinctively retreated, but quickly recovered herself and raised an eyebrow in defiance.

"And why, pray, should I be obliged to answer you? Since when has it become your concern where I go or what I choose to do?"

She attempted to pass him, but Varkas moved with effortless speed, blocking her path with a single long stride. With the heavy wagon behind her and his imposing figure before her, Talia suddenly found herself trapped. She looked up at him, her eyes trembling despite her efforts to remain composed.

"What... what is the meaning of this?"

"I have no intention of allowing you to wander about unattended," Varkas replied coldly. "Not when doing so may oblige me to witness another disgraceful spectacle."

Talia flinched at the harshness of his words. The contempt in his voice struck her more painfully than she wished to admit. Varkas regarded her in silence for a moment before releasing a faint sigh. He then inclined his head towards the path leading back to the tents.

"I shall escort you to your quarters. Come along."

"I have no need of an escort. I am perfectly capable of returning by myself—"

"Is there some particular reason you do not wish me to accompany you?"

At his question, Talia's hand moved instinctively towards the knife concealed in her pocket. Her fingers closed around its hilt before she realised what she had done.

Oh, no.

It was a dreadful mistake. By reaching for the hidden weapon, she had practically announced that she was concealing something. Varkas's eyes narrowed. Panic rose within her, and she immediately thrust both hands against his chest.

"That is none of your concern! Get out of my way!"

She pushed with all her strength, but he did not move so much as an inch. Beneath his lean appearance, his body was as unyielding as a wall of iron. Talia struggled to conceal her growing alarm. Straightening her back, she raised her chin with all the dignity she could muster.

"Are you deaf? I told you to move!"

Varkas studied her face for several moments. Then, without a word, he slowly stepped aside. Talia seized the opportunity and slipped through the narrow space between him and the wagon. She had barely taken a few hurried steps when a strong hand closed around her arm and pulled her back.

Before she could protest, Varkas reached into the pocket of her cloak and withdrew the concealed knife. The colour drained from Talia's face. Varkas examined the finely sharpened blade with the ease of a man thoroughly accustomed to handling weapons. He ran his thumb lightly along the steel, then raised his eyes towards her. His expression revealed nothing.

Talia knew she ought to offer some explanation. She could claim that she had brought the knife for her own protection, or that she had merely found it and intended to return it. Any ordinary excuse might have been enough to divert suspicion. Yet beneath his penetrating gaze, every carefully prepared lie deserted her. It felt as though those cold eyes could see straight through her and uncover the thoughts she had so desperately tried to conceal. Her composure crumbled.

"Give it back!"

She lunged towards him, reaching desperately for the weapon. Varkas immediately raised his arm, holding the knife far beyond her reach. Talia rose onto her toes and caught hold of his uniform, struggling to pull his hand down.

"Give it back to me! I said, give it back!"

Her voice grew increasingly frantic. She clawed at his clothes, stretching upwards with all her strength, but Varkas remained unmoved. His expression hardened as he watched her desperate attempts to reclaim the blade. At last, apparently losing patience, he threw the knife away.

Talia immediately turned and hurried after it. But before she could reach the fallen weapon, Varkas stepped forward and struck it with the golden sabaton of his armoured boot, sending it sliding beneath a nearby wagon. Without hesitation, Talia bent down, intending to crawl beneath the cart and retrieve it. She had scarcely lowered herself when a powerful arm wrapped around her waist and lifted her upright.

"Let me go!"

Her face twisted with fury as she struggled against his hold. She looked up at him with such hatred that, for a moment, she seemed ready to attack him herself. Yet Varkas appeared equally displeased. His expression remained cold, but beneath that restraint lay unmistakable exasperation.

"I shall not trouble myself by asking what you intended to do with that weapon."

He leaned closer, his voice dropping to a quiet but cutting severity.

"From this moment forward, you will be kept under strict supervision. Whatever foolish and disgraceful scheme you have been entertaining, you would do well to abandon it immediately."

Talia stared at him.

And who are you to speak to me in such a manner?

What right have you to take something that belongs to me?

Who do you imagine yourself to be?

Her thoughts surged with a bitterness she could scarcely contain.

You, who are soon to belong to another woman.

You, who will leave me behind forever.

You are the very reason I have come to hate her so much. You are the one who has driven me to this. And yet, after bringing me to such despair, you will not even allow me to do this one thing?

The accusations crowded her throat, desperate to escape. She wanted to scream at him, to hurl every cruel and hateful word she could summon. She wanted him to understand the misery that had consumed her and the unbearable resentment she had carried alone. Yet she could not bring herself to speak.

Talia pressed her teeth together so tightly that her jaw ached. She was terrified that if she opened her mouth, the anger would dissolve into tears and she would begin sobbing like a helpless child. She would rather endure anything than allow Varkas to witness such humiliation.

For a brief moment, he watched her in silence. Then he straightened and gestured towards the path. Talia answered him with a venomous glare before turning sharply and striding out from between the wagons. Varkas followed. Just as he had promised, he remained close behind her, watching her every movement like an ever-present shadow.

Once, she had longed desperately for his company. She had wished for him to remain beside her, to notice her, to concern himself with her existence. Now, however, his presence filled her with such bitterness that she could scarcely bear it. He had never been there when she truly needed him. Yet the moment she had finally resolved to take matters into her own hands, he had appeared as though determined to destroy her plans.

All for Ayla's sake...

Talia's lips tightened.

No. Perhaps he is merely protecting Gareth.

After all, Varkas would never understand why she had been carrying that knife. He could never comprehend why her hatred was directed towards the gentle half-sister who endured her hostility rather than the brother who had repeatedly raised his hand against her. He would never know what it meant to look upon Ayla and see everything she herself could never possess.

Talia lifted her eyes towards the campsite. Evening was settling over the grounds, and the last traces of daylight were slowly fading into the darkening sky. Somewhere among those brightly lit tents, Ayla was undoubtedly enjoying a peaceful evening. Surrounded by devoted attendants and the affection of those around her, she could look towards the future with nothing but happiness.

Soon she would marry Varkas. She would become his wife, share his home, and receive the love Talia had spent so many years longing for. The mere thought made Talia's stomach churn.

How I despise her.

Ayla possessed everything, yet she still had the audacity to look upon Talia with those sorrowful, pitying eyes. As though she were some unfortunate creature deserving of compassion. To Talia, that gentle expression was more unbearable than open contempt.

What a hateful woman.

She had everything Talia desired, and still she behaved as though she were the one who ought to be pitied. Talia lowered her gaze, her fingers unconsciously rising to her mouth. She began picking at the healing wound upon her lower lip, tearing at the scab until the tender skin stung.

I truly do want to kill her.

She was so deeply absorbed in the thought that she scarcely noticed Varkas moving beside her. Without warning, his hand seized her shoulder. Before Talia could react, he shoved her violently backwards. She stumbled, her eyes widening in shock. For one dreadful instant, she thought he had somehow discovered what she was thinking.

Does he know?

But Varkas was not looking at her. His gaze was fixed upon something above them. Talia followed the direction of his eyes and raised her head towards the sky. Her breath caught in her throat.

Something enormous was falling towards the campsite. It was as large as a house, its monstrous shape descending through the darkening air with terrifying speed. Talia barely had time to comprehend what she was seeing. A thunderous crash shook the ground. An immense cloud of dust erupted into the air, swallowing the nearby tents and sending debris scattering in every direction.

For one suspended heartbeat, the entire campsite seemed to fall silent. Then a terrified voice rang out.

"A monster!"

The cry shattered the evening stillness. And in the next instant, shrill screams erupted throughout the camp.
