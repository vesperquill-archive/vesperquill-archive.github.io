Chapter 68

Talia nervously picked at the skin beside her thumbnail.

Tiny beads of sweat gathered upon her forehead and slowly trickled down her temples.

Varkas placed his hands upon her shoulders and gently turned her to face him. His long, firm fingers moved to cradle her cheek.

Unable to meet his eyes, Talia kept her gaze lowered towards his chin.

Then Varkas bent his head.

His lips brushed against the corner of her mouth. They were unexpectedly warm and soft, yet the touch lasted no more than a moment before he drew away.

It was scarcely a kiss at all.

A brief, delicate touch, no more than what was necessary to satisfy the requirements of the ceremony.

Yet that fleeting gesture caused Talia's heart to ache as though it had been cut into a thousand pieces.

“With this, I declare the sacred marriage ceremony complete.”

The High Priest's solemn announcement echoed throughout the cathedral, followed by a wave of polite applause.

Talia slowly raised her trembling eyes towards Varkas.

He was looking down at her, his expression as unreadable as ever.

Why does he continue to look at me? What could he possibly be searching for?

His gaze lingered upon her face for several moments before he finally turned towards the assembled guests.

Only then did Talia release the breath she had unknowingly been holding.

Varkas placed an arm securely around her waist and guided her through the crowded hall.

They moved slowly, passing rows of familiar faces that seemed little more than shadows through the haze clouding her vision.

There was Gareth, watching them with a cold and poisonous gaze. The Emperor appeared troubled, his expression unusually grave, while Senevier sat with a faint smile of satisfaction upon her beautiful face.

One by one, those figures faded behind them.

Before long, they reached the cathedral entrance, where the dark sky beyond was veiled by falling rain.

“Bring me my overcoat.”

Varkas addressed a knight waiting near the doorway.

The man immediately stepped forward and presented the coat folded over his arm.

Varkas took it and carefully placed it around Talia's shoulders. Then, bending slightly, he lifted her into his arms with the same effortless ease as before.

Talia hurriedly wrapped her arms around his neck, afraid she might lose her balance.

Her nose brushed against his hair, and she caught the faint, pleasant scent of clean soap.

Supporting her back with one arm, Varkas stepped out into the rain.

Talia watched the fine silver droplets settle upon his face, making his already pale complexion appear even colder.

Beyond him, the cathedral gardens lay beneath the heavy shadows of the storm.

After a moment, she finally spoke.

“…Where are you taking me?”

“I have prepared a residence beyond the palace grounds,” Varkas replied without slowing his steps.

“We shall remain there until it is time for us to depart for the East.”

Talia looked at him in bewilderment.

Is this truly how a wedding is meant to end?

Surely Senevier had arranged a grand reception. For several days, the palace servants had been busy decorating the banquet hall and preparing an elaborate feast.

Many distinguished guests must have come especially to see Varkas.

Could he truly intend to leave without attending any of it?

As though he had guessed her thoughts, Varkas spoke again.

“We have fulfilled our obligations. There is no reason to remain merely to satisfy the curiosity of others.”

His cold words brought Talia back to reality.

Of course. He is right.

Their marriage would provide enough material for gossip without offering the court another opportunity to ridicule them.

A bastard princess with a ruined leg. A bride chosen to replace another. And a distinguished groom whose marriage had become a humiliating spectacle overnight.

Talia could almost imagine the cruel remarks being exchanged behind their backs.

There was no reason for Varkas to subject himself to such disgrace. Even the Emperor could hardly demand that the future ruler of the East endure such public humiliation.

Varkas continued through the rain, his boots sinking slightly into the muddy ground.

At last, he stopped before a carriage bearing the crest of the Grand Duchy of Sierkhan.

The coachman hurried down from his seat and opened the door.

Varkas stepped inside and carefully lowered Talia onto the thickly cushioned bench.

She looked up at him with wide eyes.

Despite the short distance they had travelled, his clothes were already soaked through.

Taking the seat opposite her, Varkas loosened the stiff collar of his formal attire and released a long, weary sigh.

Rainwater dripped from his dark hair, running down his forehead and gathering near the corners of his eyes.

Yet when he looked at her, his gaze remained as cool and distant as ever.

“Tell me, how is your leg? Has the pain grown worse?”

Talia's lips twitched.

She was beginning to resent how frequently he mentioned her injury.

Gareth's mocking words returned to her mind.

You have exchanged your leg for the title of Grand Duchess.

She bit the inside of her cheek.

I know. I know perfectly well what has happened to me. Why must you remind me of it at every opportunity?

“My leg is still attached to my body, if that is what concerns you. You need not trouble yourself over it.”

Her reply was sharp.

Varkas's eyes narrowed slightly.

Talia immediately turned towards the carriage window, hoping to escape his scrutiny.

But before she could look away completely, his damp fingers caught her chin and turned her face back towards him.

“That was not what I asked. I wished to know whether you were suffering.”

The firmness of his voice made her shoulders stiffen.

She pulled her face away from his hand.

“And what answer would please you, my lord? Would you feel more at ease if I assured you that everything was perfectly well?”

Varkas said nothing.

Talia gave a bitter smile.

“Then I fear I must disappoint you. There has not been a single day since my injury when I have been free of pain.”

She glared at his impassive face, allowing all the bitterness within her to spill into her words.

“So I would be grateful if you ceased asking such pointless questions. Your concern has become quite unbearable.”

Varkas's lips tightened.

Those same lips that had touched hers so gently before the altar were now pressed into a rigid line.

He did not answer.

Although Talia had deliberately wounded him with her words, the silence that followed felt even more suffocating to her.

She nervously bit her dry lips.

It is better this way. Better that he should despise me than look upon me with pity.

Ever since she had lost the use of her leg, people had begun treating her with a strange, hesitant kindness.

Their careful voices and sympathetic expressions frightened her far more than open hatred.

She could endure cruelty. But pity made her feel as though she had already become something broken beyond repair.

Desperate to conceal her trembling emotions, she spoke again.

“Well? What are we waiting for? Do you intend for us to remain sitting here until nightfall?”

Varkas regarded her in silence for another moment.

Then he turned and struck the carriage wall with his knuckles.

A moment later, the coachman's reins cracked through the air, and the carriage jolted into motion.

Talia turned towards the window.

Beyond the misted glass, the rain-soaked palace grounds slowly passed from view.

The buildings, gardens, and pathways that had surrounded her for so many years disappeared one after another.

Yet none of them had ever truly felt like home.

She watched the unfamiliar landscape slip away beneath the grey sky.

Then, without warning, someone lifted her from her seat.

Talia gasped.

Before she could protest, her back came to rest against the soft cushions.

Varkas had laid her down along the spacious bench.

He reached behind the seat and drew out a cloak bearing the crest of the Roem Knights.

Without a word, he spread it over her body.

“The journey will take some time. You ought to sleep until we arrive.”

Talia immediately reached up to push the cloak away.

But Varkas caught her wrist before she could remove it.

He held her hand against the cushion and looked down into her eyes, which were still clouded by the lingering effects of the medicinal incense.

His expression darkened.

When he spoke again, his voice carried the rough impatience she remembered from their younger days.

“Enough of this stubbornness. Close your eyes and get some rest. I cannot bear watching you struggle to remain awake while looking at me with such an empty gaze.”

Talia flinched at the harshness of his tone.

For a moment, she stared at him in silence.

Then, without another protest, she pulled the cloak up until it covered the lower half of her face.

Varkas watched her for a little while before releasing a quiet sigh and returning to the seat opposite her.

For reasons she could not explain, Talia suddenly felt tears burning behind her eyes.

She buried her face deeper into the heavy cloak.

It carried his familiar scent.

Closing her eyes, she drew the fabric more closely around herself.

Before she realised it, sleep had claimed her.

When Talia next stirred, the carriage had fallen strangely quiet.

The steady rattling of the wheels had ceased.

She pressed a hand against her aching forehead and rubbed her heavy eyelids.

Slowly, she forced her eyes open.

The seat opposite her was empty.

Talia blinked in confusion before suddenly sitting upright.

Where is he?

Her eyes darted around the carriage, but Varkas was nowhere to be seen.

Panic began to rise within her.

She was about to call out when a deep, unfamiliar voice reached her from outside.

“What are your intentions regarding the First Princess, my lord?”

Talia froze.

She leaned towards the window, pressing herself closer to the rain-streaked glass.

The storm had finally passed.

Outside, the evening sky glowed with a dark reddish light.

A great stone manor stood nearby, its imposing walls casting long shadows across the grounds.

About a dozen men had gathered before the building.

Among them stood Varkas.

Even from a distance, she recognised him immediately.

His back was turned towards the fading sunset as he answered the question in a cold, measured voice.

“What exactly do you mean by that question?”

The man hesitated before speaking again.

“Forgive me, my lord, but do you truly mean to leave Her Highness behind in this manner?”

Varkas gave a faint, humourless laugh.

“That is a rather peculiar concern.”

His voice carried a trace of mockery.

“Was our engagement ever intended for anything beyond keeping the Empress's influence in check?”

A brief silence followed.

Then Varkas continued.

“Surely you understand that marriage is not the only means by which a person may be protected.”

“Then, my lord, does that mean you intend to—”

The knight's voice faltered in confusion.

But Varkas had no patience for further questions.

He interrupted him sharply.

“Since when have you considered it your place to question my decisions?”

The man immediately lowered his head.

“M-My apologies, my lord. I have spoken beyond my station.”

Varkas regarded him with cold indifference.

“Nothing about our arrangements is to change. Continue observing the Empress's movements as you have done until now.”

He paused briefly before adding,

“And should the Crown Prince attempt anything reckless, I expect to be informed without delay.”

“Of course, my lord. I shall see to it personally.”

Having given his orders, Varkas turned away.

Talia's heart lurched.

He is coming back!

She hurriedly withdrew from the window and dropped herself against the cushions.

She reached for the cloak, intending to pull it over her face and pretend she had been asleep the entire time.

But she was too late.

The carriage door opened, and Varkas appeared in the doorway.

Talia looked up at him, her entire body stiffening.

Her eyes met his.

For a moment, she could do nothing but stare.
