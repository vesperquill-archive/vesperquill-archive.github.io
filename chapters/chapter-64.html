Chapter 64

“Do you know why I have agreed to become your wife?”

Varkas looked down at Talia without uttering a word. His expression remained as unreadable as ever.

Talia stared at that impassive face, struggling to contain the anger burning within her. When she spoke again, every word carried the weight of her bitterness.

“Because I wish to see you suffer. That is the very reason I have chosen to marry you.”

Her voice trembled faintly.

“I want you to know misery, Varkas. I want you to suffer terribly.”

A deeper shadow settled over his eyes.

Talia held her breath, waiting for his answer. She wanted that cold, indifferent expression to disappear. She wished he would finally lose his temper, turn upon her in anger, or push her away in disgust.

Tell me that this marriage is impossible.

Tell me that you could never bring yourself to take a woman such as me for your wife.

Even if those words shattered what little remained of her heart, perhaps they might still save themselves from the misery awaiting them. Perhaps there was still a chance for both of them to preserve what remained of their lives.

Yet Varkas, as he had done so many times before, disappointed her.

He merely released a quiet sigh, as though her cruel confession had been of no particular importance. Then he gently loosened her fingers from his shirt and straightened.

“We shall arrange the wedding without delay.”

He crossed the room and pushed the window wide open. A cool breeze swept inside, brushing past him before reaching Talia's face.

She watched him silently as the amber light of the setting sun fell across his figure. When he spoke again, his deep voice seemed strangely distant.

“It would be better that way. For both of us.”

What is that supposed to mean?

Does he merely wish to be rid of this troublesome affair as quickly as possible?

Talia searched his face, hoping to discover what lay behind those words. But exhaustion soon overcame her, and her eyelids began to grow heavy.

It no longer matters.

She would not continue tormenting herself in an attempt to understand his thoughts.

If Varkas had agreed to marry her merely out of duty, then she had accepted his proposal for an equally miserable reason. She intended to repay him for every wound she believed he had inflicted upon her. She would not be so foolish as to expect affection from him.

Once again, she repeated the promise she had made to herself countless times before.

This shall be the last night.

I shall finally put an end to this wretched love.

Yet somewhere deep within her heart, she already knew the truth.

The love I destroy today will only return to torment me tomorrow…

Preparations for the Second Princess's approaching wedding had thrown the Imperial Palace into a state of great excitement.

Under the Empress's instructions, the maids of the Main Palace hurried about decorating the ceremonial hall. Servants rushed between the wine cellars and kitchens, ensuring that sufficient food and drink would be prepared for the guests.

Everywhere, there was movement and noise. Yet beneath the hurried preparations, an uneasy atmosphere lingered throughout the palace.

As Edric emerged from the supply depot, he noticed several apprentice knights gathered in a secluded corner of the training grounds. They were speaking in hushed voices, occasionally breaking into laughter.

Edric narrowed his eyes. As he drew nearer, their conversation reached his ears.

“In the end, it seems both mother and daughter have suffered the same humiliation. Each has had her man stolen away.”

The speaker laughed at his own remark. Edric recognised him as the son of Viscount Kenden, an arrogant young troublemaker who had gathered a small circle of followers among the apprentice knights.

The boy continued speaking as he placed his practice sword upon the rack.

“They say the Second Princess bears a remarkable resemblance to Her Majesty the Empress. With such beauty, can anyone truly blame Sir Sierkhan for losing his senses?”

“Do you imagine the Commander is governed by the same shameful desires as you?”

A broad-shouldered apprentice shook his head in disapproval.

“This marriage would never have been arranged without His Majesty's decree. If anything, I feel sorry for the First Princess.”

The viscount's son clicked his tongue.

“You are far too innocent. Do you have any notion of the power House Sierkhan possesses? Do you truly believe they would surrender their chosen bride merely because the Emperor commanded it?”

He gave a knowing smile.

“The marriage is taking place because Sir Sierkhan himself is willing. Besides, was he not once the Second Princess's personal guard? Who can say what passed between them in those days? I heard from those who accompanied the pilgrimage that their behaviour towards one another was rather peculiar…”

“You speak as though you know everything, yet you understand nothing.”

Another apprentice, who had been arranging arrows for archery practice, gave a contemptuous snort.

“Have you forgotten how terribly the Second Princess treated the Commander when he served as her guard? What difference does her beauty make? He witnessed her dreadful temper with his own eyes. I cannot imagine him ever falling in love with such a woman.”

He paused before adding with a sneer,

“And after that dreadful accident, she has been left unable to walk properly. Why would any man willingly choose a woman in such a condition when the First Princess herself was to be his bride? I suspect the Second Princess must have devised some wicked scheme behind everyone's backs.”

“It would seem your tongues have been receiving far more practice than your swords.”

The sudden voice cut sharply through their conversation. The apprentices turned in alarm.

Edric stood behind them, his expression cold and severe. At once, the boys straightened and assumed their proper positions.

He regarded each of them with an icy stare.

“Do you understand that the words you have just spoken could be regarded as treason against the Imperial Family?”

The colour drained from their faces. Edric looked from one apprentice to another, making certain he would remember them all.

“The Roem Knights exist for one purpose alone: to defend and protect the Imperial Family. If you truly aspire to become knights of this order, you would do well to learn how to govern your tongues before you presume to wield your swords.”

“W-We beg your pardon, Sir Ruben!”

The boys bowed their heads hastily. None dared offer another word.

Edric studied them in stern silence for a moment longer before turning away.

He had scarcely begun walking towards the knights' quarters when a heavy arm suddenly settled across his shoulders.

Glancing sideways, he found Sir Theoric Hart standing beside him. The senior knight wore an amused smile.

“Well now, you certainly know how to put those young fools in their place.”

Edric looked at him in surprise.

“What brings you to the training grounds at such an hour, sir?”

He glanced towards the bell tower. It was not yet noon. At this time of day, the senior knights were ordinarily occupied with their duties throughout the Main Palace.

Seeing his confusion, Theoric chuckled. He reached out with his rough, calloused hand and affectionately ruffled Edric's hair.

“I have come to deliver some welcome news. Your period of probation has finally been brought to an end. Beginning tomorrow morning, you are to return to my squad.”

Edric stopped walking. For a moment, he could only stare at his superior.

It was undoubtedly the news he had been hoping to hear. Yet instead of feeling entirely relieved, he found himself troubled by conflicting emotions.

After some hesitation, he asked quietly,

“Then… who will be entrusted with the Second Princess's protection?”

Theoric's expression changed slightly. He withdrew his arm from Edric's shoulders and released a weary sigh.

“The Roem Knights have been removed from the Second Princess's guard entirely.”

Edric looked at him in disbelief. Theoric continued.

“Her Majesty the Empress has raised an objection, claiming that our order deliberately neglected the Princess's safety. She has even declared that she will no longer entrust the Second Prince to our protection.”

Edric's expression hardened. Such an accusation was a grave insult to the honour of the Roem Knights.

Since the founding of the Empire, their order had served as the steadfast shield of the Imperial Family. To be accused of deliberately failing in that duty was to strike at the very foundation of their existence.

Edric lowered his head in shame.

“Forgive me, sir. Because I failed to fulfil my duty, the entire order has been brought into disgrace…”

“You must cease blaming yourself. No one could have anticipated such a disaster. There is no reason for you to torment yourself over what happened.”

Theoric placed a reassuring hand upon his shoulder. Yet Edric's brow remained deeply furrowed.

Seeing that his words had done little to comfort the younger knight, Theoric rubbed the back of his neck. He appeared to be struggling with a difficult decision.

At length, he quietly guided Edric away from the training grounds. Edric followed him, uncertain of his intentions.

They walked in silence until they reached a secluded grove, far from the other knights. Only then did Theoric speak.

“I know better than most how seriously you regard your duties. But what happened that day was beyond your power to prevent. You must not continue carrying the blame alone.”

Edric frowned.

“I was the knight sworn to protect Her Highness. Whatever the circumstances, the responsibility for failing to keep her safe rests upon my shoulders. How could I possibly—”

“The attack that day was arranged by the Empress.”

Edric fell silent. His eyes widened in disbelief as he stared at his superior. For a moment, he could scarcely comprehend what he had heard.

Theoric kept his gaze lowered towards the ground. When he spoke again, his voice was grave.

“Did you never find the circumstances of that attack peculiar? A pack of wyverns suddenly descending upon an armed procession without warning…”

He paused before continuing.

“Wyverns are fiercely territorial creatures, but they do not ordinarily attack a large company of armed men unless their nesting grounds have been disturbed. Our camp was established well away from any known nesting sites.”

Edric swallowed.

“I did find it strange, of course… but to suggest that Her Majesty herself was responsible…”

“Before the attack, reports had already reached us concerning suspicious movements among the Second Princess's attendants.”

Theoric's expression grew stern.

“And there is more. Traces of powerful sorcery were discovered near the place where the attack occurred.”

He looked directly at Edric.

“Someone deliberately disturbed the wyverns and provoked them into attacking.”

Edric stood motionless, his lips slightly parted. As the meaning of those words began to settle upon him, a cold shiver travelled down his spine.

“But… the Second Princess herself was travelling with the procession.”

His voice trembled with disbelief.

“How could a mother arrange such an attack when her own daughter might be killed? How could she knowingly place her own flesh and blood in such danger?”

“That,” Theoric replied quietly, “is precisely why no one thought to suspect her.”

Edric stared at him. All colour had drained from his face. He could find no words.

Theoric continued in a voice heavy with resignation.

“The Empress is not a woman who acts carelessly. She takes great pains to ensure that nothing can be traced back to her.”

He paused.

“We began investigating the incident in secret almost immediately. Yet all we uncovered were suspicious circumstances and fragments of indirect evidence. Nothing that could establish her guilt beyond doubt.”

His expression darkened.

“And when the person who suffered most grievously was her own daughter, who among the Imperial Court would dare accuse her of arranging the attack?”

Edric slowly lowered his gaze. Memories of that dreadful day returned to him.

He remembered the Second Princess trapped beneath the fallen stones. He remembered her desperate cries as she lay buried among the rubble.

And he remembered the terrible agony she had endured whilst the healers struggled to treat her mangled legs. Her suffering had been so great that he could scarcely bear to recall it.

Could it truly have been her own mother who brought such misery upon her?

The thought was almost beyond comprehension.

To imagine that the woman who had given the Princess life might also have been responsible for condemning her to such suffering…

Edric felt his stomach turn. A sickening wave of nausea rose within him.
