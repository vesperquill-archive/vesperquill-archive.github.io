Chapter 20

Unable to restrain the bitterness rising within her, Talia raised her hand once more. A sharp slap rang through the carriage, leaving a painful sting across her palm.

Expecting Varkas to seize her wrist as he had done before, she instinctively drew back, her shoulders tensing. Yet the man she had struck remained perfectly composed, as though the blow had scarcely troubled him.

“I shall regard that as sufficient punishment for having laid hands upon Your Highness without permission,” he remarked, lightly touching his unmarked cheek with his gauntleted fingers.

His pale blue eyes remained cold.

“However, I have no intention of tolerating your outbursts any longer. I suggest you remember that I am no longer your personal knight.”

With those words, Varkas stepped down from the carriage and shut the door firmly behind him.

Talia remained motionless against the cushioned seat for a long while. At last, she gathered enough courage to peer through the window, only to discover that Varkas had already departed.

Outside, the servants were busily removing trunks from the baggage wagons, while her bewildered guard urged them to make haste.

For a moment, she longed to fling open the carriage door and punish every servant who had dared disregard her commands. Yet she knew that Varkas would never permit such cruelty. Whatever his faults towards her, he had always been intolerant of those who abused their authority over their subordinates.

Her fingers curled involuntarily as an unpleasant memory returned.

Once, she had discovered a maid pressing her face into a coat Varkas had left behind. Enraged by the sight, Talia had seized a blade and cut away the girl's hair. She could still remember the terrible coldness of Varkas's gaze when he learned what she had done.

The recollection made her shudder.

With an irritated movement, she drew the curtains shut and retreated into the depths of the carriage. Cradling her aching palm against her chest, she curled herself upon the seat, wishing to be left entirely alone.

Some time later, the distant call of a trumpet announced the departure of the imperial procession. The carriage gave a gentle lurch before beginning to move.

And so, the journey that Talia feared would become a lifelong nightmare had finally begun.

A narrow shaft of sunlight slipped through a gap in the curtains, falling across the darkened interior. She stared at it for several moments before reaching out and drawing the fabric tightly closed.

Surrounded by shadows, she surrendered herself to thoughts darker still.

If this procession were to lead us all into the depths of hell, perhaps that would be for the best.

If they could simply march towards a common grave, if every grievance and sorrow could be brought to an end at once, she could imagine no greater relief.

For in that moment, Talia wished for nothing so much as an end to everything.

The pilgrimage undertaken by the imperial family was intended to retrace the footsteps of the founding Emperor, Darian. Their route would follow the winding course of the Silviska River, travelling from the northern territories towards the west before turning northward once more.

In ancient times, before the establishment of the Empire, the continent had been divided into ten kingdoms: Weddon, Tristan, Balto, Guin, Osiria, Libadon, Arex, Valis, Dumnos, and Sierkhan.

Darian Roem Guirta had originally belonged to the royal house of Guin. When the armies of Balto invaded his homeland, he fled towards the central territories, where he was received by Duke Vallender, his maternal uncle and the leader of the Osirian people. In time, the Duke adopted him as his own son.

Having united the scattered tribes of Osiria, Darian gradually gathered powerful allies from the surrounding nations and began his great campaign to bring the ten kingdoms beneath a single sovereign.

The struggle endured for twenty years and was marked by countless battles. Yet through determination, political alliances, and military conquest, Darian finally accomplished what had once seemed impossible. The ten kingdoms were united, and from their former divisions arose a vast and powerful empire.

The present pilgrimage was therefore far more than a ceremonial journey. It was a sacred observance honouring the path taken by the Great Emperor, as well as an opportunity to display the majesty of his descendants before the people.

For that reason, no expense had been spared in arranging the procession.

At its head rode the Crown Prince upon an enormous golden horse, followed by nearly a hundred members of the Imperial Guard bearing banners embroidered with the imperial crest. Behind them came the magnificent carriage of the First Imperial Princess, Ayla Roem Guirta, accompanied by her own escort of knights.

As the procession passed through the heart of the capital, the streets erupted with enthusiastic cheers.

The Roem Knights, heirs to centuries of distinguished military tradition, marched with solemn expressions. Their pure white uniforms bore the imperial insignia, while beneath them gleamed armour fashioned from orihalcon, the precious metal often praised as divine.

Alongside them advanced the infantry, their footsteps falling in perfect rhythm. Silver shields engraved with the insignia of the Imperial Guard shone upon their backs, catching the sunlight as they moved.

The sight of such splendid discipline stirred the citizens to even greater excitement. Women gathered along the roadsides and scattered brightly coloured flower petals before the passing knights, while travelling minstrels raised their voices in songs of blessing for the descendants of Darian.

Then, as though answering the affection of the people, the First Imperial Princess drew aside the curtain of her carriage and appeared at the window.

A collective murmur of admiration swept through the crowd.

Indeed, it seemed difficult to imagine a young lady more perfectly suited to the dignity of an Imperial Princess than Ayla Roem Guirta.

Her bearing was as graceful and upright as a lily. Her complexion possessed the delicate freshness of a rose, while her lustrous dark brown hair framed a face made all the more lovely by a pair of large emerald eyes.

The people stretched their necks to catch another glimpse of her, and some were so captivated that they began following the carriage through the streets. Had the surrounding knights not maintained a careful distance between the procession and the spectators, the Princess might well have found herself overwhelmed by the eager crowd.

Blessings and praises followed her wherever she passed.

Yet the joyful atmosphere changed abruptly when the next carriage came into view.

Unlike the graceful splendour of Ayla's carriage, the vehicle that followed was decorated with such extravagant ornamentation that it appeared almost offensively ostentatious.

The cheers dwindled, and a strange uneasiness settled over the streets.

The knights immediately grew watchful, their eyes moving cautiously across the gathered spectators.

Those who had been shouting themselves hoarse only moments before now lowered their voices and whispered urgently among themselves. The crowds that had pressed eagerly towards the road began to retreat, regarding the approaching carriage with expressions of curiosity, resentment, and undisguised hostility.

Some made signs of religious protection, while others spat upon the ground.

The knights exchanged weary glances.

Such a reception was hardly surprising. There was scarcely a soul in the capital who had not heard of the notorious temperament of Talia Roem Guirta.

The Emperor's illegitimate daughter had been the subject of scandal from the very moment of her birth. Even after receiving official recognition as the Second Imperial Princess, she had continued to furnish the capital with rumours of increasingly dreadful incidents.

Servants had reportedly departed from her Separate Palace in miserable condition, while others were said to have suffered violent and untimely deaths.

Whether every tale was true mattered little to the citizens. The Princess's reputation had long since been ruined, and the coldness with which they regarded her was plain for all to see.

“Your Highness, might you consider drawing aside the curtains and greeting the people?”

Unable to remain silent any longer, Sir Edric Ruben approached the carriage and offered the suggestion as respectfully as he could.

No answer came from within.

Edric glanced at the heavily curtained window, his expression betraying a measure of dissatisfaction.

Ever since the procession had begun, the Second Imperial Princess had remained concealed inside her carriage, refusing to show even the slightest glimpse of her face. He suspected that she was still nursing her resentment over the earlier confrontation with Lord Sierkhan.

Suppressing a sigh, he continued riding beside the carriage.

If she would only show herself for a moment, perhaps the people might regard her differently. She is certainly beautiful enough.

In Edric's opinion, the Princess possessed an astonishing talent for making herself disagreeable.

With a face such as hers, a little sweetness of manner would have been sufficient to win the devotion of countless gentlemen. There would surely have been no shortage of men willing to surrender their hearts, their fortunes, and perhaps even their lives for the privilege of pleasing her.

Yet Talia Roem Guirta seemed determined to inspire hatred wherever she went.

Her sharp tongue and relentless hostility towards those around her were so excessive that even the remarkable beauty she had inherited from her mother seemed diminished by comparison.

How many knights had already abandoned their posts because they could no longer endure her temper?

When Edric considered the matter, Lord Sierkhan's seven years of service at her side seemed nothing short of saintly patience.

Though I suppose he did not spend all those years merely submitting to her whims…

The memory of his superior lifting the Second Princess and depositing her inside the carriage as though she were an inconvenient piece of luggage returned vividly to his mind.

Edric raised his gaze towards the front of the procession.

Among the knights marching in disciplined formation, he could just make out Varkas, his dark hood drawn low over his head.

It seemed that even that man had finally reached the limits of his endurance.

The thought was almost difficult to believe.

Lord Sierkhan was known throughout the Imperial Guard for his unwavering discipline and absolute loyalty to the imperial family. He was not a man who acted rashly, much less one who would willingly treat a member of the imperial household with such open disregard.

What manner of provocation must the Princess have inflicted upon him to drive even Varkas to such behaviour?

Had Edric not witnessed the incident himself, he would scarcely have believed it possible.

He glanced once more at the silent carriage, behind whose thick curtains the Second Princess remained hidden.

Perhaps Her Highness possesses a rare gift for turning even the most patient of men against her.

With that uncharitable conclusion, Edric returned his attention to the road ahead.
