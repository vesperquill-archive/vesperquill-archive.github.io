Chapter 35

Edric immediately climbed into the carriage and gathered the princess into his arms. Her body felt alarmingly light, and her clothes were damp with warm sweat. Worried that she had fainted from the heat, he quickly moved the golden strands of hair away from her face and gently tapped her pale cheek.

"Your Highness? Can you hear me?"

Her long eyelashes suddenly fluttered, and her eyes slowly opened. Edric froze when he found himself looking into a pair of deep blue eyes. They were so dark and striking that, for a brief moment, he forgot what he had been doing.

Then a sharp slap struck his right cheek.

Edric stared in disbelief, one hand flying to his stinging face. Talia had already pulled herself out of his arms and was glaring at him with open disgust, as though he had committed some terrible offence.

"How dare you touch me without my permission? Keep your filthy hands away from me!"

Edric's mouth fell open. "Your Highness, I thought you'd fainted! I was only trying to help you!"

Talia narrowed her eyes. "Oh? So because you believed I was unconscious, you thought you could take advantage of me?"

"What? How could you even accuse me of something like that?"

Outraged, Edric tried to stand up, forgetting that he was still inside the carriage. His head struck the low ceiling with a loud thud, forcing him to crouch down again.

"Argh!"

He grabbed the top of his head and groaned. Between the pain and the princess's insulting accusation, he was so frustrated that tears nearly came to his eyes.

Talia watched him struggle without the slightest sympathy. After a moment, she rubbed her forehead as though his presence alone was giving her a headache. "Enough of this nonsense. Just leave me alone and get out."

Edric continued rubbing his aching head, but he refused to leave without saying anything. "Wouldn't it be better if Your Highness came outside for some air? We still have several hours of travelling ahead of us. How long do you intend to stay locked inside this unbearably hot carriage?"

Talia stared at him in disbelief. Her expression suggested that she could not understand why he continued bothering her after everything she had said.

Edric clenched his jaw and held back another angry reply. It was not as though he enjoyed arguing with her. He had never volunteered to become her personal guard, yet her safety was now his responsibility.

If she collapsed from the heat or became seriously ill during the journey, he would be the one expected to answer for it. He could not simply ignore her condition because she was difficult to deal with.

Determined not to give in, Edric met her furious gaze and waited. If necessary, he was prepared to carry her outside himself, even if it meant receiving another slap.

Talia's eyebrows drew together in annoyance. Finally, as though she had grown tired of his persistence, she pushed herself to her feet.

Edric immediately brightened. "That's much better. Come, Your Highness. Let me help you down."

He quickly climbed out of the carriage and extended his hand towards her. Before he could offer any further assistance, however, the door slammed shut directly in front of him.

Edric stood motionless, staring at the closed door. For several moments, he could only blink in disbelief. Then he slowly lowered his hand and turned away with a defeated sigh.

What exactly is she so angry about all the time? I genuinely don't understand her.

Shaking his head, he walked towards the nearby lake. He washed his sweaty face with the cold water and rested beneath the shade of a tree. The refreshing breeze helped ease his irritation, but even after some time had passed, Talia still had not emerged from the carriage.

Edric glanced back at it. Is she afraid someone might hurt her?

The thought came unexpectedly. The way she refused to let anyone near her reminded him of a frightened animal caught in a trap, ready to attack anything that approached.

He watched the carriage for another moment before dismissing the idea. Why am I wasting my time trying to understand her?

He only needed to endure this assignment until the journey ended. The Second Princess was notorious for replacing her personal guards, so there was a good chance he would be relieved of his duties before long.

Once that happened, he would finally be free of her unreasonable behaviour.

Encouraged by the thought, Edric joined the other knights resting beneath the trees. He ate a simple meal of bread and wine, then returned to his horse when the order to continue travelling was given.

As the afternoon passed, the heat gradually became more bearable. The soldiers, refreshed by their break, resumed their march along the Sinai Mountain Range, which separated the western and northeastern regions of the Empire.

They made good progress through the rugged landscape and eventually reached the Sinai Plains by sunset. The wide stretch of land had once belonged to the Kingdom of Balto, but it was now part of the territory through which the imperial procession had to travel.

"We'll stop here for the night," Varkas announced, looking over the soldiers gathered behind him.

Edric glanced at his superior with quiet admiration. Despite having spent the entire day on horseback, Varkas looked almost exactly as he had that morning. His clothes were neat, his posture remained straight, and he showed no sign of exhaustion.

After dismounting, Varkas surveyed the surrounding plains with a sharp, watchful gaze. "Secure the campsite. I want a defensive perimeter established and guards stationed around it before nightfall."

The knights immediately began carrying out his orders.

Edric joined the men assigned to construct the temporary defences. At first, he wondered whether such extensive precautions were necessary, especially with so many armed soldiers accompanying the imperial family.

However, the Sinai Plains were known to be inhabited by dangerous magical beasts. Although large groups of soldiers were rarely attacked, there was no reason to take unnecessary risks.

Edric helped unload wooden boards fitted with iron spikes from the supply wagons. The men secured them firmly into the ground, then stretched ropes between the barriers and attached small bells that would ring if something disturbed them.

The work continued for several hours. By the time the protective fence was completed, darkness had settled over the plains, and campfires were burning throughout the campsite.

Edric brushed the dust from his hands and headed towards the fires where dinner was being prepared. The princess's attendants had already finished cooking while the knights were occupied with the defences.

His stomach growled as the smell of roasted meat reached him. He approached one of the fires and helped himself to a small piece of bacon. While chewing, he turned towards a young maid who was stirring a large cooking pot.

"Has Princess Talia been given her dinner yet?"

The maid looked up nervously. "Well, my lord... Her Highness said she wasn't hungry, so she refused to eat."

Edric stopped chewing. "She refused again?"

The maid nodded hesitantly. "Yes, my lord. She said she didn't want anything."

Edric frowned and wiped the grease from his fingers with a towel. The princess had barely eaten anything throughout the day, and now she intended to skip dinner as well.

Does she think she can survive this entire journey without eating?

His irritation returned. Talia behaved as though refusing food was simply another way to express her displeasure. Yet if she continued neglecting herself, she would eventually become too weak to travel.

Edric tossed the towel aside and picked up an empty food basket. "Put together a proper meal for Her Highness. Something filling, and make sure there's enough for her to eat."

"Of course, my lord."

The maid quickly filled the basket with a generous slice of pie, bacon stew, preserved fruit, and a small container of wine. Once everything had been packed, Edric took the basket and headed towards Talia's carriage.

He knew perfectly well that he did not have to force her to eat. If the princess insisted on starving herself, there was only so much he could reasonably do. Yet he could not forget how light she had felt when he lifted her earlier.

Her body had seemed almost weightless in his arms, and the memory continued to trouble him.

Why do I always end up getting involved in things that only cause me trouble?

Cursing his own inability to leave matters alone, Edric approached the carriage and knocked on the door. "Your Highness, I've brought your dinner."

A familiar, irritated voice answered from inside. "I already told the servants that I'm not hungry."

Edric took a slow breath. "You haven't eaten properly all day. We still have a long journey ahead, and you won't have enough strength to endure it if you keep skipping meals. Even if you don't feel hungry, please try to eat a little."

"I said I don't want anything! Now leave me alone!"

Edric's face tightened with frustration. Even a hedgehog isn't this difficult to approach.

He had never encountered anyone so determined to reject every attempt at helping them. Still, he forced himself to remain calm. Losing his temper would only make the situation worse.

"Very well, Your Highness. I'll leave the basket outside your door. If you change your mind later, you can—"

He bent down to place the food beside the carriage. Before he could finish speaking, the door suddenly swung open.

Edric immediately stepped back in surprise. Talia stood in the doorway, her pale face set in a fierce expression. The last light of sunset fell across her features, making her deep blue eyes appear even more striking.

Edric swallowed nervously. For a moment, he could only stare at the princess standing before him.
