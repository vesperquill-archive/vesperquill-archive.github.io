Chapter 13

The maid held the spoon in her trembling hand, yet no matter how desperately she tried, she could not bring herself to lower it into the bowl. At last, she squeezed her eyes shut, as though by refusing to look at the dreadful contents, she might somehow escape the punishment awaiting her.

Perhaps she still entertained the foolish hope that someone would intervene. If she could endure a little longer, surely another servant would come to her assistance, or someone of sufficient authority would put an end to this terrible ordeal.

Talia watched her hesitation with an expression of cold impatience. Without a word, she reached for the meat knife lying beside her plate and took it firmly into her hand.

“Hold her hand against the plate,” she instructed the guard in an icy voice. “Since she appears determined to insult my generosity, I fear I must make an example of her. Perhaps the loss of a finger will teach her to appreciate the kindness of her mistress.”

The man obeyed without hesitation. He seized the maid's wrist and pressed her trembling hand flat against the silver plate, spreading her fingers so that she could not withdraw them.

Talia caught the tip of the woman's forefinger and raised the knife.

At the sight of the blade, the maid uttered a piercing scream.

“I shall eat it! Your Highness, I swear I shall eat it! Every last spoonful! Please, put the knife away!”

Her desperate cries echoed throughout the dining hall. Talia lowered the knife, and the woman immediately snatched up her spoon, plunging it into the revolting broth.

She began swallowing the soup with frantic haste, scarcely allowing herself time to breathe. Perhaps she imagined that if she swallowed quickly enough, without permitting the food to touch her tongue, she might endure the ordeal.

But the dreadful taste and stench proved too much for her. Before she had managed five spoonfuls, her stomach rebelled, and she bent forward, violently bringing up everything she had swallowed.

Talia regarded the miserable spectacle without the slightest appearance of pity.

“You have scarcely begun,” she observed coolly. “I expect you to finish the entire bowl. Not a single drop is to remain, and I shall not be satisfied until I can see its bottom.”

The maid raised her head with difficulty. Between her retching breaths, she stared at the young princess with eyes widened by terror.

There was no longer any trace of the contempt with which she had once regarded Talia. In its place was the unmistakable horror of a person who believed herself to be in the presence of something dreadful.

Talia met her gaze steadily, giving a slight movement of her eyes towards the bowl. The command required no further explanation.

Sobbing bitterly, the woman resumed eating.

Again and again, she forced the foul broth between her lips, only to retch and bring it up moments later. Each attempt left her weaker than the last, yet the terrible fear of the knife compelled her to continue.

She could not bring herself to swallow the decaying bird, no matter how fiercely she was threatened. Instead, she attempted to consume the surrounding soup, enduring the same dreadful cycle of swallowing and vomiting until her body could scarcely bear another moment.

Her face had become a ghastly sight, smeared with blood from her injured scalp, tears from her eyes, and the remains of the food she had been forced to consume. Gradually, the colour drained from her cheeks, leaving her complexion ashen.

At last, her eyes rolled backwards, and her strength deserted her entirely. She slipped from the chair and collapsed upon the carpet with a heavy thud.

Her body began to convulse, and pale foam gathered at the corners of her mouth.

Talia looked down at the unconscious woman for a moment, her expression remaining perfectly composed. Then she raised her chin towards the servants, who stood frozen in horrified silence.

“See that this dreadful mess is cleared away.”

She pushed the soiled plate from the table, sending it clattering towards their feet.

“And bring me another breakfast,” she added, with the same imperious calm. “This time, I expect to be served something fit for a human being.”

From that day forward, the cruel treatment which Talia had endured within the Separate Palace ceased almost entirely.

The maids who had once delighted in humiliating her now approached with the utmost caution, as though their young mistress were some dangerous creature whose temper must never be provoked. Others appeared genuinely terrified whenever she entered a room.

The contemptuous glances disappeared, as did the cruel whispers which had so often followed her through the corridors. No servant dared to address her with improper familiarity, and whenever Talia passed, heads were lowered and mouths remained firmly closed.

It was as though the preceding months of torment had never occurred.

Yet peace within the Separate Palace came at a considerable price.

Before long, rumours concerning the Second Imperial Princess began spreading throughout the Imperial Court. Servants whispered that the young girl possessed a wicked disposition, and the account of her treatment of a maid who had served the imperial household for more than ten years was repeated with increasing horror.

Those who heard the tale shook their heads in disapproval. To them, the injured woman was an innocent and faithful servant who had fallen victim to the cruelty of a malicious child.

The circumstances which had driven Talia to such an extreme were scarcely mentioned.

Certain priests lamented that a serpent's offspring had found its way into the imperial bloodline. Others declared that such a cruel and wilful princess could bring nothing but disgrace upon the dignity of the throne.

Among the Emperor's loyal subjects, there were even those who expressed concern that the girl's growing tyranny might one day cast a shadow upon the honour of the Imperial Family.

Yet there was one person who regarded these dreadful rumours with satisfaction.

That person was Senevia.

It was shortly before the arrival of winter when the Empress once again visited the Separate Palace. She wore a magnificent gown of deep blue silk, its colour so closely resembling her eyes that it seemed to lend an even greater brilliance to her beauty.

Talia had been descending the grand staircase to receive her mother when she caught sight of the familiar figure crossing the entrance hall.

Her footsteps faltered.

For several moments, she could do nothing but stare.

This was the very woman who had abandoned her so coldly. Talia still remembered how Senevia had loosened her desperate grip upon her gown, finger by finger, before turning away without the slightest promise of protection.

On that dreadful day, Talia had sworn to herself that she would never again love her mother with such foolish devotion.

Yet the moment Senevia approached and pressed a gentle kiss upon her cheek, that carefully guarded resolution crumbled like a castle of sand before the tide.

“Good morning, my darling Talia. How very lovely you look today.”

The familiar fragrance surrounded her at once. There were roses and lilacs, mingled with a sweetness reminiscent of ripe summer fruit.

Talia had almost forgotten how much she adored that scent.

To discover that she had missed it so desperately, despite everything her mother had done, filled her with a misery she could scarcely explain.

She kept her expression carefully composed, refusing to reveal the longing which had so suddenly risen within her heart.

Senevia regarded her daughter's solemn face with an affectionate smile.

“Come now, my dear. Surely you are not still displeased with me for remaining away so long? You must forgive your mother. I have been occupied with preparing a rather special present for you, and such matters cannot always be arranged in haste.”

Talia looked at her uncertainly.

“A present…?”

“Why, certainly. I have heard all about the admirable manner in which you brought those insolent servants to heel. You have shown that you are capable of learning a most important lesson, and I confess that you have pleased me greatly.”

Her voice was as sweet and musical as the song of a canary.

“And since you have made your mother so proud, it is only fitting that you should receive a suitable reward.”

With these words, Senevia turned gracefully towards the entrance.

Only then did Talia notice the figure approaching from the far side of the hall.

Her breath caught in her throat.

A boy was walking towards them, dressed in the formal uniform of the Imperial Guard. Whether he had received his knighthood during the months since their last meeting, Talia could not say, but there was no mistaking the familiar face beneath that pale, ashen-blond hair.

It was Varkas.

Sunlight streamed through the tall windows and fell upon his hair, scattering pale reflections around his head. The brilliance was almost painful to behold, as though countless fragments of shattered glass had been cast directly into her eyes.

Senevia stepped beside him and extended one elegant hand towards the boy, displaying him with the satisfaction of a woman presenting some magnificent prize.

“Here he is, my darling. This handsome young knight shall be entrusted with your protection from this day forward.”

Varkas came to a halt before Talia and offered the formal respects required by her rank.

But there was nothing welcoming in his expression.

The eyes which had once seemed to possess the brilliance and promise of a future crown were now cold and guarded. Beneath their stillness lay a sharp anger, accompanied by the unmistakable humiliation of someone compelled to submit to a duty he bitterly resented.

Even a fool could have understood that he had not come of his own free will.

Varkas looked down at her with the indifference one might bestow upon an object of no particular importance.

“My name is Varkas Raedgo Sierkhan, Your Highness.”

His voice was so dry and devoid of warmth that a chill passed through Talia.

“By imperial arrangement, I have been assigned to attend upon Your Highness and ensure your safety until the day of your coming-of-age ceremony.”

He delivered the words with perfect formality, yet his manner suggested that he regarded the appointment as an intolerable humiliation.

Indeed, there was something in his cold expression which made it seem as though he wished the appointed years might pass in a single night, allowing him to escape this unwelcome obligation.

Talia looked up at him in silence.

The boy she had once admired so deeply stood before her with a face as beautiful and unyielding as a marble statue. His distant gaze, his lifeless courtesy, and the rigid manner in which he held himself all conveyed the same dreadful message.

To him, she was someone beneath his regard.

Once again, Talia felt herself becoming something small and contemptible beneath another person's gaze.

She struggled desperately against the instinct to lower her head. She reminded herself of the resolution she had made, of the courage she had forced herself to acquire, and of the humiliation she would never again willingly endure.

Yet she could do nothing to prevent the burning shame which crept along her neck and coloured her cheeks.

And in that moment, a painful certainty settled within her heart.

This beautiful boy will never be my salvation.

He will become another source of suffering.

And perhaps the cruellest one of all.

The rain which had fallen steadily for several days had finally ceased, and the sky had cleared to reveal a sun of almost oppressive brilliance. Its fierce rays poured across the Imperial Palace, as though announcing the approach of the season of fire.

Ayla crossed the bustling courtyard in search of her fiancé, raising one hand to wipe the beads of perspiration from her forehead. The sunlight was so strong that she was obliged to narrow her eyes as she looked across the grounds.

The broad courtyard, ordinarily reserved for military exercises, had been transformed into a scene of extraordinary activity.

Dozens of wagons stood in orderly rows, surrounded by merchants supplying harnesses and travelling equipment. Powerful draught horses, bred for the heavy labour of pulling loaded carts, stamped impatiently upon the ground, while soldiers hurried to and fro carrying provisions and other necessities for the approaching journey.

For a moment, Ayla surveyed the commotion with a faint frown. The courtyard resembled a busy marketplace more than the disciplined grounds of an imperial military establishment.

Then her expression brightened.

Near the outer castle wall, she spotted Varkas examining the condition of a warhorse.

He was not wearing the familiar white uniform which distinguished the Knights of Roem. Instead, he had chosen a black tunic adorned with intricate embroidery, over which rested a breastplate fashioned from dark iron.

The unusual attire lent him the appearance of a nobleman from the eastern territories rather than a knight in the service of the Imperial Family.

Ayla regarded him with a smile of quiet pride.

Once his present mission had been completed, Varkas would finally withdraw from the Imperial Guard and begin the formal proceedings required to inherit the title of Duke Sierkhan.

And she would be there beside him.

As his future wife, she would undertake the duties and studies necessary to prepare herself for the position of mistress of the Grand Duchy. She had imagined that future countless times, ever since the day Varkas had followed her mother into the gardens of the Empress's Palace.

It was a future which had seemed settled from the very beginning.

Yet there were moments when Ayla found herself troubled by doubts she could neither explain nor entirely dismiss.

Varkas had never treated her with open discourtesy. On the contrary, his manners were invariably proper, and there were occasions when he even displayed a quiet kindness towards her.

But beneath that faultless courtesy lay a distance she had never succeeded in crossing.

No matter how warmly she approached him, some invisible barrier always seemed to remain between them. She had suffered from that distance for so long that it had become an unspoken sorrow, one she scarcely dared to acknowledge even to herself.

And now, with their marriage only a few months away, Ayla found herself unable to believe that the distant young man before her would soon become her husband.
