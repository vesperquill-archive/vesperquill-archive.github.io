Chapter 328

Thalia flinched, hunching her shoulders as if she’d been slapped. Heat rushed up the back of her neck, as though her most shameful secret had been exposed.

When she couldn’t give any answer, Lucas went on, his voice even lower, as if reciting something.

“You told me once that you had no intention of being useful to anyone else. That you had no intention of shaping yourself to suit other people’s expectations...”

His gaze traveled up her flushed neck and settled on her trembling eyes.

“Does that principle not apply to my brother?”

Thalia hugged Khan tightly around the neck to hide how flustered she was.

It might be a foolish thought, but if her pathetic, foolish one-sided love was going to be exposed, she’d rather be branded the most wicked woman alive: a heretic who had tried to poison her own husband.

She forced her faintly trembling lips apart.

“Don’t be ridiculous. Why would I...”

She tried to laugh it off as if it were nothing, but her voice came out hopelessly hoarse. She couldn’t meet his eyes, which seemed to see straight through her, so she lowered her gaze awkwardly.

“I was only trying to make money.”

“But you poured every last penny of the profits back into the textile business. If making money was your goal, why didn’t you keep any of the profit?”

“Because I wanted to grow the business and earn even more, obviously! What would I do with a few measly coins?”

At the lame excuse she’d spat out in a temper, Lucas let out a small, hollow laugh.

Thalia bit her lip in humiliation. It was bad enough that he’d uncovered the feelings she had tried so desperately to hide. Now he was laughing at her, and the shame of it was more than she could bear.

She reached over the bed and snatched up a pillow. Then she flung it at the man who had barged into the most private part of her heart and torn it open.

“If you’re going to keep getting on my nerves, get out of my room right now!”

Thalia hurled the large, feather-stuffed cushion at him. It struck him in the chest and tumbled to the floor. Lucas looked down at it with a blank face, then roughly raked a hand through his hair.

“I didn’t mean to upset you. I only...”

“Enough. I don’t want to talk to you anymore.”

She cut him off coldly. She didn’t want to exchange another word with this man.

“I only let you stay because you said you’d keep quiet as a mouse. If you’re going to keep bothering me, just get out of my room.”

Lucas parted his lips as if to say something, then let out a long breath and slowly turned around.

“Understood. I’ll take my leave, then.”

He said it as he grabbed the doorknob. Thalia didn’t reply. Watching her with a bitter look in his eyes, Lucas added quietly,

“Rest well, then.”

Then came the sound of the door opening and closing, and heavy footsteps slowly faded away. Only then did Thalia let go of her tension and sink against Khan, as if she were collapsing. Cold sweat ran down her spine. Would Lucas tell Varkas what he had found out today?

Without realizing it, she clutched a handful of Khan's fur. How would he react if he learned the truth? Maybe he would feel guilty for treating her so coldly.

“I’ve always felt as though I owed you a debt.”

Thalia bit her lip. He might even see feelings he couldn’t return as just another debt to her.

I don’t need any of that anymore.

She burrowed deeper into Khan’s embrace. Even if Varkas learned the whole truth, nothing would change.

All the concern and consideration he had shown her came from nothing more than guilt and a sense of responsibility. She didn’t want to be humiliated, to have it laid bare just for that: how pathetic her love had been all this time, and how low she had stooped trying to win his heart.

Khan rubbed his face against her neck as if to comfort her. She held him close and gently closed her eyes.

* * *

In the end, Lucas’s marriage arrangements seemed to fizzle out without any progress. Once the visitors had all left the castle, Lucas came to her looking relieved, like a man who had finally gotten rid of a headache, and suggested they go out. He acted as nonchalant as ever, as if he had completely forgotten their uncomfortable conversation a few days earlier.

But Thalia couldn’t treat him the way she used to. The way Lucas treated her felt more and more awkward.

At some point he had started treating her with great courtesy, yet the uneasy feeling just wouldn’t go away. Maybe it was the way he looked at her.

In Lucas’s light brown eyes, she could read raw, unfiltered affection and longing. At first, she assumed he simply pitied her and felt sorry for her because she had been falsely accused.

But before long, she realized that her brother-in-law felt something more for her. The way he spoke, his expressions, his gestures, the way his neck and ears sometimes turned red... All of it laid his feelings bare. Once she understood his heart, she grew even colder toward him, but Lucas didn’t care in the slightest.

“I came because I have something for you.”

The man who had come all the way to her bedroom door smiled brightly and held something out to her.

Thalia frowned down at a rectangular bundle wrapped in linen.

For the past few days she had been avoiding him, even turning down his invitations to go out. But now he had come in person, gift in hand.

Still gripping the doorknob, she answered coldly.

“I don’t need it.”

“Couldn’t you at least look at what’s inside before you refuse?”

Without the slightest sign of offense, Lucas let out a soft chuckle and unwrapped the bundle. Thalia narrowed her eyes. Inside the linen was a thick book with an elegant leather binding.

Lucas waved it in front of her face and said playfully,

“I hear it’s a romance novel that’s all the rage among the ladies these days.”

Thalia looked down at the rose-patterned cover as if it disgusted her.

“I don’t read that sort of thing.”

“A light story like this might be a nice change of pace once in a while. You might find it’s more to your taste than you’d expect.”

She tried to shut the door again, but Lucas quickly wedged his foot in the gap and pushed the book into her arms.

“You read nothing but heavy, gloomy books every day. Isn’t that why you’re getting crankier by the day? Try reading something bright and cheerful for once.”

Thalia glared at him, irritated. A few days ago he had shown interest in the book she was reading. Apparently he had gone and looked it up in the library, and now he was even trying to meddle in what she read. She shoved the book back at him.

“What’s it to you whether I’m cranky or not? I don’t need this, so take it back!”

“What would I do with it? If you really don’t feel like reading it, just leave it on your desk as decoration.”

Having said that calmly, the man turned before she could stop him and strode off down the corridor. Thalia stared after him, dumbfounded, then slammed the door shut.

Even after that, Lucas kept turning up at her room with gifts. Mostly they were small things: cheap trinkets you’d find at a market, sweets, books of poetry. Every time, she threw out what he brought without a second thought or tossed it to the maids, but Lucas wasn’t the least bit discouraged. Each time, he showed up in front of her with a new gift and smiled as if nothing had happened.

His attitude weighed on Thalia more and more. She would rather have rejected his feelings outright, but Lucas never gave her the chance. He always kept a proper distance and acted nonchalant, and whenever she looked uncomfortable, he would brush it off with a light joke or some playful teasing. So Thalia couldn’t easily confront him about his feelings either.
