Chapter 30

After that day, Talia became determined to free herself from the feelings she had carried for Varkas for so many years. She had tried to forget him before, but never with such desperation. This time, she wanted to put an end to her foolish hopes once and for all.

She began by hiding the dresses he had chosen for her at the very back of her wardrobe. Instead, she deliberately wore the gowns he had disliked, particularly those that had made him frown whenever she appeared before him. She also started attending the evening banquets frequently held at the Empress's Palace.

As her escort knight, Varkas had no choice but to accompany her to these gatherings. The banquets were filled with drinking, flirtation, and entertainment that continued late into the night. For someone who had received a strict and disciplined education until the age of fourteen, such an atmosphere must have been deeply unpleasant.

Truthfully, Talia was not particularly comfortable there either. Most of the people attending were devoted admirers of her mother, Senevia. Rather than seeing Talia as a person in her own right, they treated her as a younger version of the Empress.

Because she resembled her mother so closely, many of the guests found her fascinating. Some openly expressed their admiration, while others went out of their way to attract her attention. Talia hated being treated as though she were merely a replacement for Senevia, but she forced herself to smile and pretend that she enjoyed their company.

There was only one reason she continued attending those gatherings.

She wanted Varkas to notice.

Look at me. There are plenty of people who want my attention. One day, I'll become even more beautiful than Ayla. I'll be just as beautiful as Mother, perhaps even more.

She wanted him to understand that she was someone other men admired. Perhaps, deep down, she was even hoping that he would become jealous or tell her to stop.

But Varkas did nothing of the sort.

He simply remained at her side, silently carrying out his duties as her escort. Whether she drank too much or entertained the attention of men much older than herself, his expression hardly changed. He watched over her with the same calm indifference he had always shown.

His lack of reaction only made Talia more reckless.

You've been beside me for seven years. Can't you show even a little concern? Would it really be so difficult to stop me?

Those childish thoughts continued troubling her. She knew she was behaving foolishly, but she could not bring herself to stop. Part of her believed that if she became completely absorbed in this new life, she might finally forget her feelings for him.

And so, she began spending more time with the unfamiliar men she met at the banquets. She allowed them to flatter her, hold her hands, and fuss over her appearance, even when their attention made her uncomfortable.

She disliked being treated like a beautiful ornament that everyone wanted to admire. Yet there was something strangely satisfying about the way they looked at her, as though she were the most wonderful person in the room.

Sometimes, their admiration made her feel almost powerful. In those moments, the fact that she could not win the affection of one particular man seemed less important.

Talia desperately held on to that feeling.

If I keep myself busy with all of this, perhaps I'll eventually stop thinking about Varkas.

She became increasingly familiar with the playful conversations, compliments, and flirtatious behaviour that surrounded her. Little by little, she learned how to respond to the attention she received, even though much of it still made her uneasy.

It was during this period that she became acquainted with a southern nobleman named Margus.

At first, Talia paid him little attention. She met so many people at the banquets that she rarely bothered remembering their names. Most of the men who approached her seemed interested only in her beauty or her connection to the Empress.

However, Margus gradually became different from the others.

Unlike the men who constantly showered her with compliments, he treated her almost like a troublesome younger relative. He teased her, occasionally scolded her, and sometimes offered advice as though he were genuinely concerned about her well-being.

Initially, Talia found his attitude irritating. She was an imperial princess, after all, and she was not accustomed to being spoken to so casually. But as time passed, she began to appreciate his company.

Margus never seemed eager to take advantage of her attention. He maintained a friendly distance and behaved as though he expected nothing from her. Rather than giving her expensive jewellery or fashionable dresses, he often brought amusing little gifts, such as toys, dolls, and unusual objects he had discovered during his travels.

He was also remarkably good at making her laugh.

Whenever Talia became irritated or upset, Margus would tell a ridiculous story or behave so foolishly that she could not help smiling. Eventually, she began looking forward to seeing him.

For the first time in a long while, she felt comfortable around someone without constantly worrying about what he thought of her.

Perhaps Margus had recognised how lonely she truly was. Beneath her pride and difficult temper, Talia was still a young girl who desperately wanted affection and kindness.

Whatever his reasons, he had managed to earn her trust with surprising ease.

And four days before her coming-of-age ceremony, that trust was cruelly betrayed.

That evening, Margus arrived unexpectedly at the Separate Palace. It was already quite late, and Varkas was away attending to other duties.

Talia was initially surprised by the visit. She had never invited Margus to come to her residence, and she wondered why he would appear without sending word beforehand. However, when she saw his familiar cheerful expression, her doubts quickly faded.

"Your Highness, you won't believe what I've managed to find! Remember that puzzle I told you about?"

Margus held up a large box with an excited smile, looking extremely pleased with himself.

"I finally got my hands on it! You have no idea how much trouble I went through. I don't think I've ever worked this hard to find a present, not even for His Majesty!"

He laughed proudly before suddenly seeming to remember how late it was. Scratching the back of his head, he gave Talia an apologetic look.

"I was planning to surprise you with it on your birthday, but something urgent has come up, and I have to leave the capital tomorrow. I thought I'd bring it to you before I went. I hope you won't be too upset with me for arriving without an invitation."

He looked so apologetic that Talia found it difficult to remain annoyed.

"Well, since you've already come all this way, I suppose there's no point sending you back. Come inside."

Margus immediately brightened and followed her into the palace.

It was the first time Talia had allowed a visitor other than her attendants or escort knights into the Separate Palace. As she led him inside, she suddenly became conscious of how poorly maintained some parts of the building were.

The drawing room had hardly been used and had gathered a considerable amount of dust. Feeling embarrassed, she decided not to receive him there. Instead, she led him upstairs to the study, which was one of the tidiest rooms in the palace apart from her bedroom.

Once inside, they arranged a table and opened the large puzzle box. Margus explained how the pieces fitted together, and before long, Talia became completely absorbed in the activity.

The puzzle was far more complicated than she had expected. It contained numerous small pieces that had to be carefully assembled into the shape of a castle, complete with towers, walls, and narrow pillars.

Talia concentrated so intently on the task that she hardly noticed Margus gradually moving closer to her chair.

She was determined to finish one of the pointed towers without making a mistake. Whenever a piece refused to stay in place, Margus leaned forward to steady it while she carefully attached another section.

After several attempts, they finally completed the tower.

"There! We did it!" Talia exclaimed happily, admiring their work.

She smiled with satisfaction, but her happiness faded when she suddenly realised how close Margus was sitting.

His face was only a short distance from hers, and she could smell wine on his breath. The closeness made her uncomfortable, especially since she had not noticed him moving nearer.

Talia shifted slightly in her chair.

She wanted to create some distance between them, but she was afraid that moving away too suddenly would seem rude. After all, Margus had always treated her kindly, and she did not want to embarrass him over something that might have been accidental.

Pretending to search for another puzzle piece, she turned towards the box beside her.

"Let's see… I think we still need another piece for this side."

However, before she could move any farther away, Margus shifted behind her chair. He reached around her on both sides, apparently searching through the box for the missing piece.

"Oh, I think I've found the one we need," he said.

Talia immediately went still.

His arms were now on either side of her, leaving her feeling trapped in her seat. When she tried to move, she realised he was standing far too close for her to do so comfortably.

A sudden feeling of uneasiness settled in her stomach.

She hunched her shoulders and tried to lean forward, hoping he would realise that she wanted more space. Her heart began beating rapidly, but for some reason, she found it difficult to speak.

Margus did not move away.

Instead, he remained close behind her, and Talia became painfully aware that something about his behaviour had changed.

The friendly, harmless man she had grown accustomed to suddenly felt like a stranger.

She gathered what little courage she could and finally managed to speak.

"Please… move back a little."

Margus hesitated, but he did not immediately release her.

"Just a moment, Princess," he murmured. "You always smell so lovely."

He leaned closer, making Talia recoil in alarm.

A cold feeling spread through her body. Her hands began trembling, and she could feel sweat forming against her skin. She no longer cared about the puzzle or the gift he had brought.

All she wanted was for him to step away.

But she remained frozen in her chair, frightened by the sudden realisation that the person she had trusted might never have been as kind as she believed.
