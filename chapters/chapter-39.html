Chapter 39

Varkas reached the carriage in a matter of moments and deposited Talia rather roughly upon the cushioned seat.

She landed awkwardly and rubbed her aching hip, glaring at him with all the hatred she could muster. Yet when she saw his expression, her anger faltered. His face was dark with fury, and it seemed that the last of his patience had finally deserted him.

"Have I not warned you already?" he demanded through clenched teeth. "How many times must I tell you to stop creating such disgraceful disturbances?"

Talia flinched, but only for an instant. Her pride would not permit her to appear frightened before him. She raised her chin and returned his glare.

"And why, pray tell, should I obey you? Who do you imagine yourself to be?"

Varkas's lips curved into a cold, contemptuous smile.

"At the very least, someone whose position carries more weight than that of a princess who possesses little beyond her title."

The insult struck her like a slap. Fury surged through Talia, and she sprang to her feet, determined to strike that arrogant face. But Varkas moved first. He leaned over her, caught both her arms, and forced her back against the seat.

His gaze travelled over her injured face, lingering upon the broken blood vessels beneath her skin before moving to the dark bruises forming around her neck. There was no tenderness in his expression. After examining the marks Gareth had left upon her, he released a long, weary sigh.

"Do you behave this recklessly because you truly lack the sense to understand your situation?" he asked. "Look around you, Your Highness. There is no one here who will come to your defence. If the Crown Prince decides to take your life, who do you suppose will stop him?"

Talia stared at him, trembling with anger.

"Why should that concern you?" she cried. "Would you not be happier than anyone if I simply disappeared from this world?"

Varkas answered without hesitation.

"I cannot say that I would regret it."

Talia went still. She had expected cruelty, perhaps even invited it, yet hearing the words spoken so plainly caused a pain far worse than she had imagined. It felt as though something deep within her chest had been torn apart.

Varkas continued in the same merciless tone.

"Nevertheless, I cannot permit the Crown Prince's reputation to suffer because of your foolishness. If you are so determined to bring about your own destruction, then do so without involving His Highness."

Talia's eyes burned. She forced them open wider, desperately fighting the tears threatening to spill. She would sooner have bitten through her own tongue than allow him to see her cry.

At last, Varkas released her arms and straightened. The anger that had darkened his face was already fading behind his usual stern composure.

"I shall send for a healer," he said, as though discussing an ordinary duty. "Have your injuries treated and remain here until you have recovered."

Talia said nothing. Instead, she snatched a silver goblet from the carriage floor and hurled it at him. It struck his chest before falling to the boards with a sharp metallic clang.

Varkas looked down at it, then back at her. The disgust in his eyes was unmistakable. Without another word, he turned and left.

The moment he disappeared, Talia slammed the carriage door shut and secured the bolt with trembling fingers. Only then did her composure finally collapse.

Tears streamed down her cheeks. She pressed a hand tightly over her mouth, terrified that someone outside might hear her sobbing. Her shoulders shook as she tried to swallow the cries rising in her throat. The harder she struggled to remain silent, the more painfully her chest tightened, until it seemed that her grief might suffocate her altogether.

At the summit of Ulgram Hill stood the White Temple, a magnificent sanctuary built by Emperor Dorian to commemorate Osiria's independence.

Its pale stone walls rose proudly against the sky. Though countless years had passed since its construction, the temple remained remarkably well preserved. The imperial procession halted before its towering entrance, and the pilgrims gazed upon the splendid building in reverent silence.

Prayers had been carved into the stone, while statues of the great heroes of the past lined the path towards the sanctuary. Many travellers paused to admire these sacred monuments, overcome by the thought of standing upon ground so deeply connected to their nation's history.

The Crown Prince, however, appeared thoroughly unimpressed. Gareth dismounted and cast an indifferent glance towards the great arched entrance.

"So this is the second sacred site?" he asked, sounding rather bored.

"Yes, Your Highness," an attendant answered quickly.

The servants had been particularly anxious around him since the previous evening. His temper had grown so unpleasant that even the smallest mistake might provoke an outburst, and everyone watched his expression with great care. Only Varkas appeared entirely unaffected.

He approached the Crown Prince and spoke in his usual composed manner.

"I have been informed that no priests are presently stationed at this temple. We shall therefore need to arrange the sacred rites ourselves."

Gareth turned towards him. For a moment, he regarded Varkas with a strange, lingering hostility, suggesting that the events of the previous evening had not been forgotten. At last, he looked away.

"Then see that everything is prepared at once. I have no intention of standing about here all day."

At his command, the attendants began their preparations. The carriages were brought closer to the temple, and servants unloaded the supplies. The personal attendants of the First and Second Princesses arrived shortly afterwards.

While soldiers erected the tents, servants opened crates containing censers, the Holy Plate, silver ewers, and ornate candlesticks. Each sacred vessel was polished until it shone. Once the principal preparations were complete, the maids turned their attention to dressing the Crown Prince and the First Princess for the ceremony.

Edric observed the activity from a short distance away. After a moment, he made his way towards the carriage where Talia had remained secluded since the previous evening.

If the Second Princess intended to participate in the sacred rites, she would need to begin preparing soon. As one of the knights responsible for her escort, Edric knew it was his duty to inform her. Yet when he reached the carriage, he found himself strangely reluctant to speak.

He stood before the firmly closed door, scratching the back of his neck. The memory of what he had witnessed the previous evening had not left him.

Edric raised his hand to knock, then hesitated. After a moment, he lowered his arm and glanced towards the Crown Prince's tent.

Several servants were preparing Gareth's bath. A large wooden tub had been placed outside, and water was being heated over a fire. Meanwhile, the Crown Prince lounged comfortably in a chair, enjoying a cup of wine as though he had not a single care in the world.

Edric felt his stomach turn.

He nearly killed his own sister yesterday, and yet he sits there drinking as though nothing happened.

He had heard the rumours, of course. There had long been whispers that the Crown Prince sometimes raised his hand against the Second Princess. Yet Edric had never imagined the violence could reach such horrifying extremes.

The image of Talia lying helpless beneath her brother returned vividly to his mind. She was a slight young woman, scarcely capable of defending herself against a man of Gareth's size. Yet the Crown Prince had struck her without restraint and wrapped his hands around her throat as though he meant to end her life.

Edric could still remember the desperate way she had struggled beneath him.

Had Lord Sierkhan not arrived when he did...

He did not care to finish the thought. The consequences would almost certainly have been dreadful.

Edric lowered his gaze towards his hands and realised that his palms had grown damp with cold sweat. He wiped them against his uniform and released a troubled sigh.

Still, there was little point in standing outside the carriage indefinitely. The Second Princess would most likely refuse to participate in the ceremony. Given her present condition and her usual unwillingness to cooperate, he could hardly imagine her agreeing to attend.

She'll only tell me to go away again.

With that thought, Edric turned towards the temple.

He had barely taken a step when he heard the carriage door open behind him.

"You there."

Edric stopped and turned around in surprise. Talia stood in the doorway with her arms folded across her chest.

For a moment, he could only stare. She looked remarkably composed, as though the dreadful events of the previous evening had never occurred. Her chin was raised with its usual arrogance, and the familiar impatience had returned to her expression.

She stepped down from the carriage and gave him a commanding look.

"Summon my maids."

Edric blinked.

"...I beg your pardon, Your Highness?"

Talia's eyes narrowed.

"Good heavens, must I repeat myself? Are you deaf as well as foolish? I said to fetch my attendants! I intend to dress for the ceremony, and I have no wish to stand here waiting while you stare at me like an idiot."

Edric remained motionless for another moment. He had received his fair share of insults from the Second Princess, but her ability to deliver them with such extraordinary venom never ceased to astonish him.

Nevertheless, he quickly bowed and turned away to summon the maids whom the Empress had assigned to attend her.

As he walked, however, he noticed something unexpected. He was relieved.

For all her sharp words and dreadful temper, seeing Talia behave with her usual fierce spirit had eased a worry he had scarcely admitted to himself. The healer had attended to her injuries the previous evening, but Edric had feared that the ordeal might leave her confined to bed for several days.

After all, she had already been weakened by hunger and exhaustion before Gareth attacked her.

Well, at least she seems to have recovered some of her strength.

Then again, perhaps he should not have been surprised. Even while lying helpless beneath the Crown Prince, with death only moments away, Talia had somehow found the courage—or foolishness—to continue mocking him.

A woman capable of such defiance was hardly likely to surrender to a little suffering.

Edric shook his head and gave a dry, almost disbelieving chuckle. He instructed the servants to prepare hot water for the Princess and ordered the knights under his command to ensure that their uniforms and equipment were in proper order.

Since the Second Princess had chosen to participate in the sacred ceremony after all, he intended to see that she was escorted with every courtesy befitting her imperial rank.

Whatever her temperament, she remained a princess of the Empire. And he was still a knight sworn to fulfil his duty.
