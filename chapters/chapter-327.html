Chapter 327

Talia's eyes widened. His words sounded almost like a threat—that unless she agreed to his request, he would no longer help her go outside.

When she pressed her lips tightly together without offering a reply, Lucas approached her with an even more pleading expression.

“I'll sit quietly and behave as though I'm not even here. Please, just this once, make an exception for me.”

“Why are you so determined to stay here?”

Talia frowned, unable to understand his insistence, and asked,

“Why don't you simply go downstairs, make a brief appearance, and be done with it?”

For a moment, the smile vanished from Lucas's face. He stared into the fireplace, his expression turning cold, before letting out a weary sigh.

“If I show my face now, they'll immediately try to settle the date of the official engagement ceremony. And by this time next year, I'll likely find myself standing before the altar in formal attire. I'm not yet prepared for such a commitment. If possible, I'd rather remain unmarried for the rest of my life.”

Talia studied his face, which had become clouded with gloom.

“Do you dislike your fiancée that much?”

“I hardly know her well enough to say whether I like her or dislike her.”

Lucas tossed another piece of firewood into the fireplace and continued with a bitter smile.

“To be perfectly honest, I have no interest in that woman whatsoever. And I have no intention of spending the rest of my life as husband and wife with someone I do not love. It would only bring unhappiness to her as well.”

Talia stiffened her shoulders.

Although she knew his words were not directed at her, they struck her like a sharp blow, reopening wounds that had long since healed.

For a moment, she stared blankly at her own shadow cast upon the floor, her eyes clouded with sorrow. Then, in a subdued voice, she murmured,

“Perhaps… you might come to love her someday. Who knows? Your feelings may change as you spend more time together.”

“That may well be true. But isn't it equally possible that we might spend the rest of our lives resenting one another?”

Lucas spoke with a slight shrug of his shoulders.

“I have no desire to gamble two people's lives on such an uncertain possibility. At the very least, I wish to marry someone to whom my heart is truly drawn.”

Talia lowered her gaze.

For a man born into a noble family, it was a remarkably naive sentiment. Wasn't marriage, after all, something decided by the interests and alliances of their respective families?

Yet even as she thought so, Talia found herself sympathizing deeply with his wish.

She, too, would never have wanted to marry someone she did not love. She would rather have died than marry anyone other than Varkas.

And yet, even after marrying the man she had so desperately longed for, she had been so utterly miserable that she had wished to cast her own life away.

Perhaps, rather than a relationship in which only one person loved the other, it would be better if neither of them loved the other. If they expected nothing from one another, they would never have to endure the pain of seeing those expectations shattered.

Rather than giving voice to such thoughts, Talia let out a heavy sigh and nodded.

“Exactly one hour. Not a moment longer.”

Without waiting for the man's reply, she walked toward the chest of drawers and resumed searching for the ointment.

Lucas, who had been watching her rummage through the drawers, asked with childlike curiosity,

“What are you looking for?”

“Khan has hurt himself. I'm trying to find some ointment for his wounds.”

“He's injured?”

Lucas turned his head, slowly examining the wolf sitting quietly beside the bed.

“He looks perfectly fine to me.”

“The pads of his paws are torn. Judging by the bleeding, I think even the roots of his claws may have been damaged. I'm going to apply some ointment and wrap his paws in bandages to keep the wounds from getting any worse.”

Talia, who had been rummaging through the drawer, pulled out a small bottle of medicine from the very back. She opened the lid and took a sniff of its contents. Just then, Lucas rose from his chair and retrieved something from his pocket.

“Here, try using this. It's a medicine for treating wounds.”

Talia regarded the small vial he held out to her with a doubtful expression.

“Why are you carrying something like that around?”

“Have you forgotten that I'm a knight? One never knows when an injury might occur, so I make it a habit of carrying medicine with me.”

Lucas casually tossed the medicine vial toward her. Talia caught it almost instinctively and immediately opened the lid to examine its contents. The small container, fashioned from animal bone, was filled with a milky-white ointment.

She scooped up a little with her fingertip and rubbed it between her fingers before kneeling in front of Khan.

Then, gently lifting his injured front paw, she carefully spread a generous layer of ointment over the tips of his claws, where blood had begun to seep through.

The wolf's toes curled ever so slightly, as though the medicine stung.

Talia immediately withdrew her hand and studied Khan's face.

“Bear with it just a little longer. I'm almost finished.”

At the sound of her soothing voice, the wolf's drooping ears gave a small twitch before standing upright.

Smiling at his endearing reaction, Talia began applying the ointment to his other paw.

Lucas, who had been quietly watching them, suddenly parted his lips to speak.

“Come to think of it, this fellow has exactly the same eye color as my elder brother.”

Talia looked up at him in surprise.

Everyone had been so occupied with avoiding Khan and fearing him that no one had ever taken the time to examine his appearance closely.

A few of the maids who attended to her had occasionally admired Khan's unusual and beautiful eyes, but none had ever thought to associate them with Varkas.

Then again, it was hardly surprising. Most servants were so busy lowering their heads whenever they encountered Varkas that they rarely dared to look him directly in the eyes.

Perhaps Lucas was one of the very few people in Raedgo Castle who had ever looked closely enough to recognize that distinctive color.

Lucas, who had been studying Khan's face with quiet curiosity, turned his gaze toward Talia and asked,

“Is that why you've chosen to keep this beast?”

Talia lowered her eyes, feeling a sudden pang in her chest.

An uncomfortable sense of shame washed over her, as though he had seen straight through her. She could feel heat rising from her neck to her cheeks.

Trying to conceal her embarrassment, Talia drew her shoulders inward and hurriedly denied his suggestion as she carefully wrapped the bandage around Khan's paw.

“Don't be ridiculous. How could they possibly resemble each other? They don't look alike in the slightest.”

She hastily finished securing the bandage, but Lucas caught hold of her shoulder.

Startled by the unexpected touch, Talia flinched and turned her head toward him. His amber eyes, which had darkened considerably, filled her vision.

After a long moment, he finally spoke.

"Do you remember what you said to me in the past?"

Talia looked at him in bewilderment.

“What do you mean?”

“I'm referring to what you once said about having no intention of becoming a kind and virtuous person.”

Talia frowned at his unexpected words.

Why was he behaving so strangely today?

As the unpleasant memory of that conversation long ago resurfaced, a faint sense of revulsion crept over her.

Brushing Lucas's hand away as though shaking off something unpleasant, Talia moved closer to Khan and regarded him warily.

“What does that have to do with anything?”

“While conducting further investigations into the Church, I discovered that you've done quite a number of things over the years, Sister-in-law.”

Lucas continued in a calm, measured voice.

“You provided employment for many people through your textile business. I also heard that, thanks to you, several businesses in the outskirts of the capital were able to earn considerable profits.”

Lucas paused for a moment, carefully observing Talia's reaction.

“At first, I assumed that you had suddenly ventured into business as a means of concealing your activities within the Church. But the more I investigated, the more I began to question that suspicion.”

Talia looked up at him, her expression growing stern.

She could not understand why he had suddenly brought up such matters. Was this some sort of scheme to test her? Or had his opinion of her genuinely begun to change?

“What exactly are you trying to say?”

“I began to wonder whether you had started those businesses out of a sincere desire to bring prosperity to Kalmor.”

He looked directly into her eyes, which had widened in surprise, and continued.

“Please, answer me. Did you do all those things for my brother's sake?”
