Chapter 16

Ayla regarded Senevia with open suspicion, wondering what the Empress could possibly hope to gain by forcing Talia to accompany them on the pilgrimage. The girl was little more than a nuisance, capable of causing petty disturbances but hardly possessing the influence to threaten anyone of importance. Yet Senevia was not a woman who troubled the Emperor with meaningless requests. There must surely be some hidden purpose behind this sudden arrangement.

“It has always been customary for a lady of the Imperial Family to undertake a pilgrimage when her marriage is approaching,” Ayla remarked, her voice noticeably stiff. “Why, then, must Talia accompany us at this particular time?”

“Why, my dear, Talia may soon be married herself,” Senevia replied pleasantly.

Ayla frowned. She had heard nothing of any gentleman seeking Talia's hand. Whatever the circumstances of her birth, Talia remained an Imperial Princess whose name was entered in the royal records. A marriage involving a daughter of the Emperor could scarcely be arranged without attracting notice. Surely this was nothing more than an excuse invented to secure the girl's place on the journey.

As Ayla studied the Empress with growing distrust, Varkas spoke in a cold, measured voice.

“How remarkably convenient that this marriage should arise at precisely such a moment.”

The sarcasm was unmistakable, yet Senevia appeared entirely untroubled. “Indeed, it seems we may soon have two happy occasions to celebrate. The proposal arrived only recently, so no formal engagement has yet been announced. Nevertheless, His Majesty and I are most favourably disposed towards the match.”

A faint, mocking smile touched Varkas's lips. “And may I inquire who this fortunate gentleman might be?”

“I believe his name will be familiar to you. It is Count Verdein Serian, the present head of House Serian. His formal proposal reached us only a few days ago.” Senevia smiled with the benevolence of a mother discussing her daughter's happiness. “Talia has long since reached a suitable age for marriage. I can see no reason why we should not consider so respectable an offer.”

Ayla could scarcely suppress a bitter laugh. Count Verdein Serian was among the Empress's most devoted supporters, and there had long been rumours that he had once been the most enduring lover of Duchess Taren. The very thought of such a marriage filled her with disgust. She remembered seeing Talia arrive at a banquet in the Count's company, and the memory now seemed more disturbing than ever.

Can she truly mean to marry her own daughter to such a man?

A mother who would willingly arrange such a match, and a daughter who appeared prepared to accept it—surely there was something deeply wrong with them both.

“Then the matter is settled,” the Emperor declared, putting an end to the discussion. “See that the necessary arrangements are made for Talia's journey. I expect no unnecessary delay.”

With a dismissive movement of his hand, he indicated that the audience was at an end. Varkas remained still for a moment, regarding his sovereign with an expression that revealed nothing of his thoughts. Then he bowed, murmured his obedience, and departed without another word.

Ayla watched him in astonishment. She had expected some objection, or at least an attempt to question the Emperor's decision. Instead, Varkas had accepted the command with an obedience that seemed almost indifferent. After hastily offering her respects to her father, she hurried after him.

“Varkas, wait! Do you truly intend to bring Talia with us? Surely you cannot believe this sudden marriage proposal is genuine. The Empress must be plotting something dreadful.”

“I should imagine so,” he replied calmly, continuing down the staircase.

Ayla stared at him in disbelief. Was that truly all he intended to say? Gathering her skirts, she hurried after him and caught hold of his arm.

“And yet you mean to do nothing? We have no notion what Senevia intends to accomplish through Talia, and you would simply obey without so much as—”

“What, precisely, would Your Highness have me do?”

Varkas stopped abruptly and turned towards her. The coldness in his expression made Ayla falter, for only then did she realise how deeply his temper had darkened. This was the same man who had smiled at her in the garden scarcely an hour earlier, yet now he seemed an utter stranger, looking down upon her with a gaze as distant and unyielding as winter frost.

“Would you prefer that I openly defy His Majesty's command?” he asked, his voice edged with bitterness.

“I only meant—”

“The Emperor is the sovereign of this realm, and I have sworn my loyalty to the Imperial Family. Obedience is therefore my duty, whether the command pleases me or not. Is that not what the Imperial Family has always required of nobles such as myself?”

Ayla fell silent, wounded by the unexpected sharpness of his words. Before she could find an answer, Varkas turned away and resumed walking without so much as a backward glance. Watching his retreating figure, she was suddenly seized by an old and familiar fear. For one dreadful moment, she felt herself a little girl again, terrified that the person upon whom she depended would leave her behind.

She hurried after him and threw her arms around his waist.

“Forgive me! I was frightened, and I allowed my fears to make me unreasonable. I ought not to have spoken to you in such a manner. Please, do not turn away from me so coldly.”

Varkas stood motionless for several moments. Then she felt the tension gradually leave his body, and he released a quiet sigh before turning towards her. As he had done when they were children, he placed a hand upon her head and gently smoothed her hair.

“There is no need for Your Highness to be afraid,” he said. “Whatever designs the Empress may entertain, I shall see to it that no harm comes to you.”

Ayla raised her face towards him. The stranger who had looked upon her with such cold displeasure had vanished, and in his place stood the familiar knight, composed and dutiful, offering the reassurance she had longed to hear. Her troubled heart quieted almost at once.

How easily this man could unsettle her, only to restore her peace with a few simple words. Before him, her pride, her dignity, and even the authority of her imperial birth seemed strangely powerless.

Ayla tightened her arms around him, clinging to an embrace that remained cool despite the many years they had known one another. She searched his pale blue eyes with a longing she could scarcely conceal. Those beautiful, hollow eyes always seemed fixed upon some distant place beyond her reach.

What is it that you see when you look at me?

Varkas gently brushed a few loose strands of hair away from her cheek.

“I gave my word to the former Empress that I would protect you and His Highness the Crown Prince to the utmost of my ability,” he said quietly. “And I intend to honour that promise, whatever may come. Your Highness need not trouble herself with needless fears.”

Ayla studied his face for a long moment before finally nodding. Yes, he had promised to protect them, and Varkas was not a man who broke his word. So long as he remained on their side, there was nothing to fear.

Nothing at all.

After trimming my broken fingernail, I felt an extraordinary sense of loss.

How many more weeks must I wait before it grows long enough to be of any use?

I examined the small pink nail which had only just begun to extend beyond the tip of my finger. The satisfaction of having wounded the Crown Prince had proved disappointingly brief. My noble brother would undoubtedly have summoned a priest at once, and by now his injured hand would have been restored without so much as a scar. I, on the other hand, had lost the little weapon I had spent weeks so carefully preparing.

And I had hoped to save it for Ayla…

How often had I imagined driving my nails into those green eyes of hers whenever she looked upon Varkas? The thought alone had afforded me a certain bitter pleasure, and now even that small consolation had been taken from me.

I scratched absently at the tender skin beside my broken nail, tearing away the thin scab which had begun to form. Fresh drops of blood appeared, and my irritation only deepened. For a while, I stared at the reddening fingertip before placing it between my lips and gently sucking away the blood.

At length, I rose from the windowsill. Varkas had been particularly difficult to find that day, and I had spent far too long watching the palace grounds, enduring the fierce sunlight in the foolish hope of catching even a passing glimpse of him. There was little reason to remain by the window any longer.

With a weary sigh, I crossed the room and poured some cold water into the basin upon the washstand. The coolness was a welcome relief against my face, which had grown warm and damp from the summer heat.

I had scarcely finished washing when a knock sounded at the door.

The nanny again, I suppose.

She had lately developed the tiresome habit of bringing refreshments at every opportunity. Taking a clean linen cloth, I dried my face and answered without much enthusiasm.

“Come in.”

The door opened, but the figure who entered was not my short, stout nanny, with her round limbs and familiar manner. Instead, a little boy stood upon the threshold, his dark reddish-brown curls framing a small face with remarkably bright green eyes.

I stared at him in surprise.
