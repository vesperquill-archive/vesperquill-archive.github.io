Chapter 63

The moment her unwelcome visitor had departed, Talia summoned the healer and instructed her to fill the bedchamber with the thick smoke of medicinal herbs.

She wished to forget the unpleasant encounter, along with every bitter thought it had awakened.

As the smoke rose from the brazier and spread through the room in wavering clouds, Talia drew it deep into her lungs. Its sharp, bitter scent stung her throat, yet she welcomed the familiar sensation.

Little by little, the turmoil within her began to quiet. A wonderful sense of relief washed over her weary body.

Just a little longer.

Soon, the haze will cloud my mind completely.

Then I shall no longer have to think of anything.

All she desired was to surrender herself to a sleep so deep that nothing in the world could disturb her.

She lay motionless upon the bed and closed her eyes. Yet however long she waited, sleep refused to claim her entirely. Some stubborn remnant of consciousness remained, holding her to the waking world.

Growing desperate, Talia leaned closer to the brazier and inhaled the bitter smoke more deeply. But instead of bringing the peace she sought, the smoke caught in her throat.

A violent fit of coughing seized her. Her entire body trembled beneath the force of it, and the faint drowsiness that had begun to settle upon her vanished at once.

Frustrated beyond measure, Talia turned her gaze towards the window. Without her noticing, the hours had slipped away. The sky beyond the glass had darkened into a deep crimson, as though the heavens themselves had been stained with blood.

She stared at the unsettling sunset for a long while. There was something strangely frightening about its colour, something that sent a chill through her despite the warmth of the evening.

Then, moved by an impulse she could neither explain nor resist, Talia slowly pushed aside the covers. She lowered her feet to the floor and rose from the bed, carefully placing her weight upon her injured legs.

To her surprise, she felt scarcely any pain. Perhaps the medicine had finally begun to work.

She ventured a few cautious steps. Her left leg moved with difficulty, and her foot dragged slightly across the floor. Nevertheless, the sharp pain that usually accompanied the bending of her injured joint remained strangely absent.

It seems I can walk after all.

She slipped her thin, bare feet into a pair of slippers before approaching the wardrobe. Taking out a hooded robe, she draped it loosely over her shoulders. Then, with slow and uncertain steps, she left her bedchamber.

At such an hour, Nanny would most likely be resting comfortably in her own quarters. The healer would have finished her duties and returned home, whilst the maids would have withdrawn to their rooms.

And indeed, as Talia made her way along the lengthy corridor and descended the staircase, she encountered not a single soul.

Passing through the great hall, she quietly slipped out of the Separate Palace through the side entrance ordinarily used by the servants.

A cool breeze brushed gently against her face. She drew a breath of the evening air, carrying the fresh scents of grass and flowers. Then she began walking, her steps slow and heavy.

She wandered without any particular destination. Only when she found herself standing near the training grounds did a faint question stir within her clouded mind.

Why have I come here?

She looked about the courtyard, now bathed in the crimson light of dusk. Her gaze remained vacant. Then, sensing movement nearby, she instinctively stepped behind a cluster of bushes.

On the far side of the grounds, several knights were practising with their swords, exchanging blows as they sparred with one another. Talia watched them in silence for a little while. Then, without any particular reason, she turned away and continued walking.

She wandered for what seemed an exceedingly long time. Gradually, the world before her eyes began to bend and shimmer. The medicinal herbs were finally exerting their full influence upon her senses.

Nevertheless, she continued forward at a painfully slow pace, dragging her weakened legs behind her.

It was only when she noticed her long shadow disappearing into a deeper darkness that she raised her head. Somehow, without realising it, she had entered a dimly lit building.

Where have I come to?

Talia frowned in confusion. As she looked down the unfamiliar corridor, a door at the far end caught her attention. She stumbled towards it and, after a moment’s hesitation, knocked softly.

For a short while, there was no response. Then a low, deep voice sounded from beyond the door.

“What is it?”

Talia blinked slowly. It was only upon hearing that familiar voice that she remembered why her feet had brought her here. With some difficulty, she managed to answer.

“I have come because… there is something I must tell you.”

Silence followed. Talia wondered whether her voice had been too faint to reach him. She cleared her throat, preparing to speak again.

But before she could utter another word, heavy footsteps approached from within. A moment later, the door opened.

Talia slowly lifted her head. Perhaps he had been resting after completing his duties for the day, for Varkas wore nothing more than dark cotton trousers and a loose linen shirt.

She examined him through her unfocused gaze. Then his cold voice sounded above her.

“Have you truly come all this way dressed in such a manner?”

Talia lowered her eyes towards herself. Beneath the loosely draped robe, she could see the summer nightgown Nanny had helped her put on earlier.

What is supposed to be wrong with it?

She frowned in bewilderment. Before she could speak, something heavy and warm settled over her shoulders.

Talia looked up in surprise. Varkas had removed his own coat and wrapped it securely around her. Having covered her properly, he glanced towards the corridor, where the evening shadows had already begun to deepen.

“Where is the person who accompanied you?”

“Accompanied me?”

His expression sharpened. Varkas gently caught her chin between his fingers and tilted her face upwards. Leaning closer, he examined her eyes with unsettling attention.

“How much sleep-grass have you been burning?”

Talia strained to focus upon his face. Yet his features seemed to drift in and out of view, refusing to remain still.

There was something unfamiliar about his expression. She could not recall ever having seen him look at her in quite that manner.

No… perhaps there is something wrong with my own senses.

The entire world looks so strange. How could I possibly expect him to appear otherwise?

She impatiently pulled his hand away from her chin.

“I have already told you. I came because there is something I wish to say.”

His eyes narrowed. Evidently, something about her condition displeased him.

After regarding her with a cold, searching look, Varkas straightened. He glanced towards the darkening sky beyond the window before turning his gaze towards the interior of his chambers. For a moment, he appeared to be considering something.

His silence only increased Talia’s impatience.

Have I somehow begun speaking in the language of the Elves or Dwarves?

Why does he refuse to answer me?

“Can you not hear me? I said there is something I must tell—”

Before she could finish, her strength suddenly failed her. Her knees buckled. Talia reached desperately for the doorframe, catching herself just before she fell.

The long walk from the Separate Palace had finally taken its toll upon her injured legs. The muscles of her left thigh began to twitch uncontrollably.

Pressing both hands against the wall, she shifted nearly all her weight onto her stronger leg, struggling to remain upright.

Then, without warning, her feet left the floor. Startled, Talia raised her head.

Varkas had lifted her into his arms. His face, now close above hers, bore an expression of unmistakable weariness.

Without a word, he carried her through the doorway and into his spacious bedchamber, where several candles cast a soft glow upon the furnishings.

Talia lowered her gaze and looked about. Everything seemed strangely familiar, yet entirely foreign.

She had come to this place on several occasions in the past, hoping to see him. And yet, until this very evening, she had never once been permitted to cross the threshold of his private chambers.

A hollow laugh escaped her lips.

How amusing.

It seems I had to lose the use of my legs before I was finally worthy of entering his room.

“We shall speak when the effects of the medicine have worn off.”

Varkas spoke with what sounded very much like a weary sigh. He carefully lowered her onto his bed.

But as he began to draw away, Talia reached out and seized the hem of his shirt. Through the thin linen, she felt the firm muscles beneath it grow momentarily tense.

Has he already guessed what I have come to tell him?

Struggling against the haze clouding her thoughts, she tightened her grip upon his shirt as though it were the only thing keeping her from drifting away.

“No. I must say it now. If I wait until my senses return, I shall never find the courage…”

She paused, trying to steady her uncooperative tongue. Then she looked up at him.

“You told me that if I consented, you would take me as your wife. Is that not what you said?”

Varkas did not answer. He merely gazed down at her, his eyes fixed upon her face with an intensity she could not understand.

Talia struggled to form her next words. Her tongue felt strangely soft and useless, as though it had lost all strength. Nevertheless, she forced herself to continue.

“Then let it be so. Cast Ayla Roem Guirta aside and marry me instead.”

A profound silence settled over the room. For several moments, neither of them spoke.

Varkas stood before her with his back towards the fading crimson sunset. Something unreadable passed across his face.

Have I taken him by surprise?

Perhaps he had offered marriage only because he believed she would never accept. Perhaps he had been so certain of her refusal that her sudden agreement had left him unable to respond.

Talia wondered whether she had finally succeeded in unsettling him. Yet when Varkas spoke, his voice remained perfectly calm.

“Very well. I shall marry you.”

Talia stared at him through her blurred vision. She searched his face, hoping to discover some trace of surprise, anger, or hesitation. But there was nothing.

His expression remained distant and composed, almost painfully indifferent. A hollow laugh escaped her throat.

How can he look so unaffected?

Because of this man, Ayla had been driven to swallow her precious pride and come begging before the half-sister she despised.

And Talia herself had become consumed by a bitter desire to destroy everything that stood before her.

Yet Varkas remained perfectly calm. As though the decision to abandon one princess and marry another were a matter of no particular importance. As though none of it concerned him at all.

Before that cold, impassive face, which seemed almost touched by boredom, Talia felt something deep within her begin to break.
