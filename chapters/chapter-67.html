Chapter 67

Varkas carried Talia through the cathedral's side passage, her feet scarcely touching the cold stone floor.

Her lips parted slightly, still sore from where she had bitten them.

Do you truly intend to marry me like this?

Are you certain this is what you wish?

She wanted desperately to ask him, but the words refused to leave her mouth.

Perhaps he was right.

They had already come too far to turn back.

Lowering her gaze, Talia looked towards the guests gathered inside the great hall.

Through her tear-filled eyes, the faces before her appeared blurred and distant.

There were high-ranking officials, influential nobles, and members of the Empire's most distinguished families. Among them were many who enjoyed the Empress's favour.

All were familiar figures of wealth and power, and all seemed to be watching her in oppressive silence.

At last, Talia understood why Varkas had not simply abandoned the wedding.

No matter how powerful the heir of a ducal family might be, even he could not publicly disgrace the Emperor by walking away from a marriage arranged under His Majesty's authority.

Not before the entire imperial court.

“The ceremony shall begin shortly. May I ask the bride and groom to proceed to the chamber beside the altar?”

A priest approached them and spoke with respectful caution.

Varkas gave no reply. He merely followed the priest towards the seats prepared near the altar.

Once he had set her down, Talia followed him on unsteady legs, her eyes moving anxiously from one side of the cathedral to the other.

The medicine still clouded her senses, yet she could clearly see the curious expressions of the guests.

Some watched with the interest one might show towards a theatrical performance.

Others made little effort to conceal their contempt.

To Talia, every pale and indifferent face seemed to be silently mocking her.

“Are your legs troubling you again?”

Noticing her sudden stiffness, Varkas reached towards her and gently lifted her chin.

Talia looked up at him in confusion.

His thumb brushed lightly beneath her damp eyes, wiping away the traces of her tears.

The tenderness of the gesture left her bewildered.

Is this truly happening?

Or has the medicine begun to make me imagine things?

Varkas studied her face for a moment before speaking quietly.

“Try to endure it a little longer. Once the ceremony is over, I shall make certain you have time to rest.”

He spoke with the gentle patience of someone comforting a frightened child.

Talia felt her throat tighten.

He is only being kind because he does not wish me to cause a scene.

He must be afraid I shall embarrass him before all these people.

She repeated those thoughts to herself, hoping they would calm the foolish beating of her heart.

But before she could regain her composure, a familiar voice interrupted them.

“How touching. Anyone who saw the two of you might believe this marriage was born of genuine affection.”

Talia froze.

Slowly, she turned towards the speaker.

Gareth was approaching through the rows of pews, dressed in a rich scarlet doublet. Half a dozen imperial guards followed closely behind him.

The moment she recognised him, the blood seemed to drain from her body.

“Why do you look so frightened, dear sister? Have I not come to offer my congratulations?”

Gareth stopped before her, a mocking smile playing upon his lips.

Talia stared at him, her eyes wide with terror.

Does he find this amusing?

Is he pleased to see me reduced to such a miserable state?

She had always been an unwelcome presence in his life.

Perhaps seeing her pale, trembling, and unable to defend herself brought him a satisfaction he could scarcely conceal.

A cruel amusement flickered across his face.

Leaning closer, he lowered his voice.

“You have exchanged the use of your leg for the title of Grand Duchess. Surely that is reason enough to smile.”

Talia could only stare at him.

Under ordinary circumstances, she would have gladly clawed that smug expression from his face.

But now, she could not even find the strength to speak.

Her thoughts, already muddled by the medicine, seemed to stop altogether at his unexpected appearance.

She stood trembling before him, her eyelids fluttering as though she were trapped in a dreadful dream.

Then, without warning, someone drew her head firmly aside.

“Your Highness.”

Varkas pressed Talia's face against his chest, shielding her from Gareth's gaze.

His voice was low, but there was a clear warning beneath its calmness.

“If you have come to attend my wedding, I would ask that you conduct yourself with proper courtesy. Surely you would not wish to disturb the ceremony of an old friend.”

A heavy silence settled between the two men.

Talia swallowed nervously, her forehead resting against the firm fabric of Varkas's tunic.

She could not understand why he was protecting her from Gareth.

Should it not be the other way around?

Has he not always stood beside Gareth and Ayla?

Why would he defend the bastard princess they despise?

Gareth's cold voice finally broke the silence.

“There is no need to be so defensive. As long as you honour our agreement, I shall honour mine.”

Talia immediately tried to turn her head.

Agreement?

What agreement?

But Varkas kept her face against his chest, preventing her from looking back.

His arm tightened slightly around her waist.

“If you intend to remain for the ceremony, then I suggest you take your seat.”

His reply was cold and final.

For a moment, Talia heard what sounded like Gareth grinding his teeth.

Then his footsteps began to fade.

Only when he had walked away did Varkas loosen his hold.

Talia turned her head and watched Gareth make his way towards the seats reserved for the most distinguished guests.

As he sat down, an elderly nobleman beside him leaned close to speak.

She recognised him at once.

Marquis Oristein.

Had they been discussing something together?

Was that why the Marquis had summoned Varkas earlier?

Talia looked around the cathedral with renewed suspicion.

It was only then that she noticed something peculiar.

Many of the nobles seated throughout the hall were conservative lords known for their loyalty to the Crown Prince.

Why are so many of Gareth's supporters here?

What is truly happening?

She was still struggling to understand when Varkas gently but firmly turned her face towards him.

“You need not concern yourself with any of them.”

His cold blue eyes held hers.

“Once this day is over, you shall never be obliged to face these people again. Do not trouble yourself over what they may think.”

There was such certainty in his voice that Talia found herself unable to answer.

Having spoken, Varkas turned towards the altar and guided her forward.

Talia bit her sore lower lip as she followed.

He does not seem surprised by any of this.

Perhaps he knew Gareth would come.

Gradually, her confused thoughts began to form an explanation.

Perhaps Gareth had attended the wedding to show the imperial court that his friendship with House Sierkhan remained unchanged.

Even though Varkas was marrying her, their political alliance would continue.

And Varkas, it seemed, had willingly played his part.

Of course.

That must be the reason.

From the very beginning, this marriage had been nothing more than an arrangement.

The Emperor wished to see his command obeyed.

Varkas was fulfilling his duty.

And she had agreed because of her own desire for revenge.

There was no affection between them.

No happiness awaiting them.

Only a ceremony in which each person played the role expected of them.

His loyalty still belongs to Gareth and Ayla.

Nothing has truly changed.

Suddenly, a herald's powerful voice echoed through the cathedral.

“His Imperial Majesty the Emperor and Her Imperial Majesty the Empress have arrived!”

Talia lifted her gaze towards the upper gallery.

The Emperor and Empress entered with solemn dignity, making their way towards the elevated thrones overlooking the ceremony.

They moved with the calm confidence of those who held absolute authority over everyone gathered below.

For a moment, Talia studied the Emperor.

His expression was stern, almost grave.

Then her eyes moved towards the woman beside him.

Senevier.

As always, the Empress was breathtakingly beautiful.

Her golden hair shone like molten sunlight, framing features so perfect they scarcely seemed real.

Every movement was graceful, and her elegant figure was adorned with the finest garments the Empire could provide.

Talia stared at her with a bitterness she could scarcely bear.

Once, I believed I might grow into such beauty.

I thought that one day I might stand as proudly as she does.

Now, the sight of Senevier's flawless appearance only reminded her of everything she had lost.

It was almost painful to look at her.

“The sacred marriage ceremony shall now begin.”

Once the imperial couple had taken their seats, the High Priest stepped before the altar.

His solemn voice carried throughout the cathedral.

Varkas guided Talia forward until they stood together before him.

Beyond the priest's aged and severe features, the sky had grown even darker.

Heavy clouds gathered outside the tall cathedral windows, threatening rain.

The High Priest began reciting prayers in the ancient sacred language.

His measured chanting echoed beneath the vaulted ceiling.

Yet to Talia, the entire ceremony seemed terribly unreal.

What a ridiculous performance.

There sat Senevier, wearing a faint smile that revealed nothing of her thoughts.

Beside her, the Emperor appeared unusually uncomfortable.

Before the altar stood the High Priest, solemnly reciting blessings over a marriage that held no love.

And behind them sat hundreds of guests, watching the spectacle with poorly concealed amusement.

How can they call this a sacred union?

It is nothing more than a cruel farce.

The priest's prayers continued for some time.

At last, he turned towards Varkas.

“Varkas Raedgo Sierkhan, do you willingly accept Talia Roem Guirta as your lawful wife? Do you promise to remain by her side, honour her, and fulfil your duties as her husband for as long as you both shall live?”

Talia lowered her eyes towards the stone floor.

Her lips were painfully dry.

A brief silence followed.

Then Varkas answered.

“I do.”

His voice was calm and without hesitation.

There was so little emotion in his reply that it sounded almost like an ordinary statement.

Talia felt a quiet ache in her chest.

The High Priest turned towards her.

“Talia Roem Guirta, do you willingly accept Varkas Raedgo Sierkhan as your lawful husband? Do you promise to remain by his side, honour him, and fulfil your duties as his wife for as long as you both shall live?”

Talia slowly raised her eyes towards the priest.

She wanted to answer.

She wanted to speak with the same composure Varkas had shown.

But when she opened her mouth, no words came.

Only a faint, strained breath escaped her throat.

It felt as though an invisible hand had closed around her neck.

The silence stretched on.

The guests waited.

The priest watched her expectantly.

Still, Talia could not speak.

Then she felt the arm around her waist tighten.

She turned towards Varkas.

His blue eyes were fixed upon her.

He said nothing, yet the firmness of his gaze made his meaning clear.

Answer him.

Do not prolong this any further.

Talia swallowed painfully.

Gathering what little strength remained, she finally forced the words from her throat.

“…I do.”

The High Priest inclined his head.

“By the sacred authority bestowed upon me by the heavens, and with the blessing of His Imperial Majesty the Emperor, I hereby declare you husband and wife.”

His voice rang clearly throughout the cathedral.

Then, after a brief pause, he spoke the final words of the ceremony.

“You may now seal your marriage with a kiss.”
