Chapter 321

As Talia blinked in bewilderment, Khan approached the small side gate and dropped the ring of keys he had been holding in his mouth onto the ground.

Talia bent down and picked it up. Perhaps because Khan had gripped it between his teeth, the linen cloth wrapped around the keys had been torn in several places.

She carefully removed the cloth and examined the keys to make sure none had been damaged. Just then, Khan raised one of his front paws and repeatedly scratched at the small gate.

Talia furrowed her brows deeply.

“Do you want to go outside?”

Khan let out a short growl before pawing at the gate again. Talia fiddled with the iron ring in her hand, her expression troubled.

Even if the guards had been withdrawn, leaving the castle without permission was an entirely different matter. If she disappeared without a word, it would undoubtedly cause an uproar. She gently scratched behind Khan's ears and tried to soothe him.

“Let's go out another time, once we've received proper permission.”

But Khan showed no intention of backing down. He seized the hem of her dress between his teeth and tugged her forcefully toward the gate.

“Khan!”

She shouted in a somewhat stern voice, but Khan paid her no attention. Planting all four paws firmly on the ground, he stubbornly growled with the fabric of her dress still clenched between his teeth.

His unusual impatience began to worry her. Normally, no matter how much of a tantrum he threw, a few words would be enough to calm him down.

Normally, even when Khan threw a tantrum, he would eventually listen to her. There had to be a reason why he was being so unusually stubborn.

“Do you really have to go out right now?”

As though he understood her words, the wolf nodded his head vigorously.

Talia looked down at him with a troubled expression. At last, she let out a resigned sigh and began searching through the keys.

After finally finding one that seemed to fit, she inserted it all the way into the keyhole and carefully turned it.

With a dull clicking sound, the lock released, and the small gate, which had been firmly shut, swung open.

Talia pushed the heavy wooden door, which appeared not to have been used for a long time, with all her strength.

As it creaked open, the forest of white birch trees, its leaves beginning to turn golden, spread out before her.

Perhaps it was because she had not ventured beyond the castle walls for several months. Talia gazed at the scenery that had once been so familiar to her with a sense of longing. Just then, Khan nudged her back with his head, as though urging her to hurry.

Unable to resist, Talia stepped outside the castle. Khan immediately darted ahead of her.

Only then did it occur to her that being confined within the castle must have been an even more painful ordeal for Khan.

Watching the wolf's retreating figure with guilt in her eyes, Talia finally began walking.

Perhaps remembering the paths where people used to pass, Khan headed straight toward the outskirts of Armond Forest.

As she followed closely behind him, Talia repeatedly glanced over her shoulder with an uneasy expression.

By now, the maids would surely have noticed her disappearance without a word. What if they misunderstood and assumed she had run away, prompting them to organize a search party?

Talia nervously bit her lip before lowering her head with a faint, bitter smile.

Surely, no one would go so far as to come looking for her.

Perhaps they would even celebrate her disappearance with a sense of relief. After all, in their eyes, was she not a traitor who had conspired with assassins in an attempt to take the Grand Duke's life?

Lost in these bitter thoughts, Talia suddenly felt a strong gust of wind sweep across her body, sending a chill through her. She turned her head.

Before she realized it, they had passed through the wilderness and reached the edge of the forest.

She tightened her coat around herself and slowly surveyed the surroundings.

The leaves that had been lush and green throughout the summer were gradually losing their color, while dried leaves lay scattered across the narrow woodland path.

The strangely desolate scenery made her shoulders droop without her even realizing it.

Just then, Khan, who had been walking a few steps ahead, abruptly changed direction and plunged his head into the thick bushes.

Talia examined the bushes with curious eyes.

“What's wrong? Is there something there?”

Khan burrowed deeper into the densely tangled branches, then suddenly began digging at the ground with his powerful front paws.

Talia watched him with a bewildered expression. Although Khan often raced through forests and fields as though possessed, he had never behaved like this before, digging through the soil or making holes in the ground.

Why on earth is he acting so strangely today?

Shielding her face with her sleeve from the clouds of dust flying through the air, Talia suddenly stiffened when she saw Khan pull something out of the hole.

Surely, he hasn't dug up the remains of some animal?

With a nervous expression, she carefully examined what appeared to be a leather pouch, now covered in dirt.

Just then, something shiny emerged from it with a faint metallic clinking sound...

Several objects spilled onto the ground. Talia's eyes widened in surprise.

A few pieces of exquisite jewelry, clearly crafted from pure gold, lay scattered across the dirt. After staring at them in astonishment for a moment, Talia finally picked them up and examined them carefully. They looked strangely familiar.

Frowning, Talia pulled the leather pouch toward her and emptied its contents.

Hairpins adorned with pearls, rings and necklaces set with precious gemstones, and pouches containing silver coins spilled out one after another.

She recognized several of the items immediately.

They were undoubtedly her own belongings. But why on earth had they been hidden here?

Talia turned toward Khan with a bewildered expression, only to see him digging up another pouch and dropping it at her feet.

Talia opened the pouch and examined its contents. One by one, she discovered flint and other fire-starting tools, a waterskin filled with wine, and small pouches containing dried meat and fruit.

Her entire body broke out in goosebumps.

The things Khan had dug up were clearly supplies prepared for a long journey.

After staring at them in disbelief, Talia turned toward Khan once more. The wolf was watching her with a calm, steady gaze.

The meaning behind those eyes was unmistakable.

This wolf wanted her to leave this place.

Talia tightly gripped the dirt-covered leather pouch and swallowed hard.

“Do you… want to leave this place?”

Khan gave a short growl, almost as though answering her.

Talia gazed silently into his blue eyes before slowly turning her head toward Raedgo Castle, which stood majestically upon the hill.

It was a place that had never once been her home. Perhaps, she thought, she had never truly known what it meant to have a place she could call home.

Talia gazed at the gray castle walls bathed in the morning sunlight for a long moment. Then, she picked up the leather pouch and shook off the dirt.

After carefully placing the scattered belongings back inside, she tied the opening securely with its leather cord.

She was about to sling the pouch over her shoulder when Khan pressed his head firmly against her back, as though urging her to place it on him instead.

After a moment's hesitation, Talia loosened the strap, fastened the leather pouch securely, and draped the remaining length around Khan's neck.

The wolf shook his body several times, seemingly checking that the load was properly secured. Then, apparently satisfied, he wagged his tail and began walking along the forest path.

Talia silently followed behind him.

She knew perfectly well how impulsive and reckless this decision was.

To follow a direwolf that was leading her was an act of recklessness she could not possibly undertake without hesitation.

She would inevitably have to spend most of her time camping in forests or open fields. Could she truly endure such a life, let alone survive it?

Nor could she ignore the dangers posed by wild animals, magical creatures, and bandits.

Having Khan by her side might keep certain threats at bay, but there was no guarantee that she would be safe from every danger.

She might even encounter the very band of bandits who had once kidnapped her.

Despite all the dangers running through her mind, Talia continued walking briskly between the densely growing trees, her steps surprisingly light.

Whenever she fell even slightly behind, Khan would slow his pace. As soon as she drew near, he would take the lead again.

The sight of him repeatedly glancing back, as though making sure she was following, brought a smile to her lips.

After all, Khan was a wolf who had once risked his life for her.

During the loneliest and most painful time of her life, this wolf had stood by her side.

To give this wolf, who had protected her, a life of freedom

If she could give him a life of freedom, she would gladly endure even a life of wandering.

Without knowing where she was going, Talia silently followed Khan.

Just then, cold raindrops began falling from the sky, which had gradually grown overcast.
