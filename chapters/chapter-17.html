Chapter 17

Talia regarded the little boy standing before her with undisguised suspicion. His bright, innocent eyes were fixed upon her face, and he appeared entirely unconcerned by the coldness of her welcome.

“What brings you here?” she asked.

“I slipped out of the palace to see you, Sister,” the boy replied cheerfully.

Talia frowned. Her younger brother, Asros, had only recently turned six, yet his very existence had long been a source of bitterness to her.

Though they shared the same parents, their positions could scarcely have been more different. Asros was the legitimate son of the Emperor and Empress, born within the sanctity of marriage, while Talia remained the shameful reminder of an affair that had taken place before their union.

She still remembered the day of his baptism, when the infant prince had been presented before the court amidst blessings and celebration. Even then, she had felt a jealousy so fierce that she could scarcely bear to look upon him. The helpless little creature, who had barely learned to open his eyes, had already possessed everything she herself had been denied.

Senevia had not failed to notice her daughter's resentment. Having little further use for Talia, the Empress had taken great care to keep her away from her precious son. Thus, the siblings had seldom met except during official ceremonies, and this was the first occasion since his baptism that Talia had stood so close to him.

She glanced uneasily along the corridor.

“Have you come all this way alone? If Mother should discover—”

“I did not come alone. Berens accompanied me.”

The boy pointed towards the far end of the passage. Only then did Talia notice a tall figure standing motionless in the shadows.

It was Berens, a man dressed entirely in black, whose pale and lifeless countenance gave him the appearance of a ghost. There had been a time when he had stood beside her in much the same manner. Now his duty belonged to Asros, and his dark eyes watched her with the vigilance of a man prepared to intervene at the slightest sign of danger.

A bitter feeling stirred within her. Even Berens seemed determined to remind her that she would never be precious to anyone.

Concealing her resentment, she turned once more to her brother.

“What business have you with me?”

“I heard that you would soon be leaving on a long journey, Sister, and I wished—”

“A long journey?”

Talia interrupted him so sharply that the boy drew back in surprise.

Asros hesitated before answering in a more cautious voice.

“Mother said that you were to accompany the pilgrimage…”

For a moment, Talia could only stare at him. Then, quite suddenly, she began to laugh.

The sound was so harsh and unexpected that Asros flinched and retreated a step. Talia pressed a hand against her stomach as her laughter continued, though there was little amusement in it.

At length, she bent towards him, assuming a gentleness that was entirely false.

“And what else did our dear mother tell you?”

Asros hesitated. Even at his tender age, he seemed to understand that his words had caused his sister considerable distress. Yet he was not a child easily persuaded to abandon what he had come to say.

“She said that you might soon be married. A gentleman named Count Serian has sent a proposal for your hand…”

His voice faded, and he fell silent.

Talia could only imagine what expression had appeared upon her face, for Berens immediately stepped forward and placed himself between her and the prince. Evidently, he feared that she might lose her temper and do the child some injury.

She paid him no attention. Her gaze remained fixed upon Asros, whose innocent face had grown pale with uncertainty.

“So that is why you have come,” she said with a cold smile. “To offer your congratulations? To bid a joyful farewell to the troublesome sister who is at last to be married and sent away from the palace?”

The boy's shoulders trembled at the bitterness concealed beneath her seemingly pleasant words.

“That is not what I meant!” he protested.

“Then pray tell me what you did mean.”

Asros looked at her with an expression of genuine distress.

“If you marry, Sister, it will become even harder for us to see one another. I wished to speak with you before you went away. After all, we have the same mother.”

There was a faint longing in his voice as he continued.

“I have always wished that we might be close, as our elder brother and sister are. But if you leave the palace, perhaps we shall never have the chance. That is why I came to see you.”

Talia looked down upon him without the slightest change in her expression.

It was because of this child that she had become so utterly worthless in the eyes of her family. The Emperor, the Empress, and their handsome, clever little prince formed a perfect picture of domestic happiness, while she remained an ugly stain upon its beauty.

The brighter Asros shone, the deeper her own misery seemed to grow.

She despised herself for envying a child so fiercely, yet the knowledge did nothing to lessen her hatred. Indeed, merely standing before him was almost unbearable.

A cruel smile curved her lips.

“Do you imagine that I shall devote myself to your service, as the First Princess devotes herself to securing the throne for her twin brother?”

“That is not what I meant!”

“Whether you desire it or not, our mother has doubtless already decided how I may best be used for your benefit. I should not be surprised if this marriage of mine has been arranged for that very purpose. So I advise you, little brother, to abandon such foolish hopes.”

Asros stared at her in bewilderment. He was plainly unaccustomed to being addressed with such hostility, and Talia could see how carefully he had been sheltered from the cruelties of the world.

This child had likely never passed a sleepless night trembling with fear. Perhaps this was the first occasion upon which anyone had deliberately wounded his heart.

The thought brought a sharp satisfaction to her troubled mind.

“I have no intention of becoming your affectionate and devoted sister,” she declared. “For I despise you every bit as much as I despise our elder brother and sister.”

Tears gathered in the boy's wide green eyes. He looked at her as though he could scarcely believe what he had heard, but Talia showed him no mercy.

“If you understand me, then kindly leave.”

Asros pressed his lips together, struggling to restrain his sobs. Without another word, he turned and hurried away down the deserted corridor. Berens followed close behind, disappearing as silently as he had appeared.

Talia shut the door and turned the lock before returning to the window.

The afternoon had already begun to fade. The sky, which had been a brilliant blue only a short while earlier, was now taking on the soft violet hues of approaching evening. The servants who had spent the day carrying luggage had gradually withdrawn to their quarters, and the courtyard lay almost deserted. Even the knights appeared to have retired from their duties.

Lost in thought, Talia raised her index finger towards her lips, only to stop when a sharp pain reminded her of the broken nail. Dark red blood had begun to seep once more from the torn skin.

She stared at the small wound, and the bitterness she had struggled to contain rose within her until she could scarcely breathe.

An anguished cry threatened to escape her throat, but she swallowed it with desperate effort. Unable to endure the confinement of her chamber any longer, she crossed the room and seized the cloak hanging near the wall.

She threw it over her thin surcoat and fastened it hurriedly about her shoulders.

Then, without summoning a maid or informing anyone of her intentions, Talia slipped quietly out of the Separate Palace.
