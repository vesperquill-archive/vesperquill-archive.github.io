Chapter 9

The following morning, Talia rose from her bed with an eagerness quite foreign to her usual disposition. For once, she submitted to the attentions of her maids without the slightest complaint, enduring the vigorous scrubbing of her skin and the merciless strokes of the comb that tugged painfully at her scalp. Ordinarily, such treatment would have provoked a succession of indignant protests, but on this particular morning, she bore every discomfort with remarkable patience. Her thoughts were occupied by an anticipation so great that even the unpleasant business of dressing seemed a small price to pay.

When her toilette was finally completed, she approached her wardrobe and selected a velvet gown which had remained untouched since her arrival at the Imperial Palace. It had been a parting gift from her maternal grandfather, a stern old gentleman whose regard for her had seldom extended beyond cold disapproval. Indeed, the gift had been so unexpected that Talia had never quite known what to make of it. Nevertheless, the dress was exceptionally beautiful, and she had preserved it with considerable care.

Standing before the mirror, she examined her reflection with an earnestness she had never before bestowed upon her appearance. The child reflected in the glass possessed a delicate, almost angelic beauty, with features so closely resembling those of Senevia that one might easily imagine the Empress herself restored to the age of nine. Talia leaned nearer, studying the deep blue eyes she had inherited from her mother. She wondered whether the boy would find her pretty, whether he would recognise her, and whether his expression might soften when he saw her approaching.

Satisfied at last, she straightened her shoulders and departed from her apartments with a determination that admitted no possibility of failure.

Yet the morning brought her an immediate disappointment.

The training ground, where she had grown accustomed to seeing the young noblemen practising beneath the summer sun, was deserted. Talia lingered beside the familiar window, searching the empty courtyard with increasing uneasiness. There was no sign of the fair-haired boy, nor of the other trainees whose exercises ordinarily filled the air with the sharp sound of wooden swords. She waited for some time, hoping that their absence was merely temporary, but the courtyard remained silent.

At length, unable to endure the uncertainty, she resolved to search for him herself.

Having successfully evaded the attendants assigned to her care, Talia ventured into the palace grounds and began wandering through the gardens and courtyards surrounding the Main Palace. She had no particular knowledge of where the young knights spent their hours when they were not training, and consequently her search possessed more determination than direction. Nevertheless, she continued, convinced that perseverance would eventually be rewarded.

The Imperial Palace was an extraordinary place, so vast and intricate that Talia sometimes imagined herself wandering through the body of some enormous sleeping creature. Its towers rose towards the heavens, its corridors stretched into seemingly endless distances, and its numerous gardens concealed secluded paths and buildings whose existence she had never suspected. Although she had resided within its walls for several months, there remained entire portions of the estate which were as unfamiliar to her as a foreign country.

She passed beneath archways adorned with elaborate carvings, crossed broad courtyards where the sunlight lay upon the pale stones, and wandered through gardens crowded with flowering shrubs and ancient trees. Still, the boy remained nowhere to be found.

As the morning advanced towards noon, the heat became increasingly oppressive. The velvet gown which had seemed so splendid before her mirror now felt intolerably heavy, and the delicate shoes she had chosen for their beauty proved quite unsuited to such prolonged wandering. Her feet began to ache, and with every step she felt the painful sting of blisters forming beneath the leather. Beads of perspiration gathered upon her forehead, disturbing the careful arrangement of her hair.

Yet she would not turn back.

She scarcely understood why the prospect of failing to find him caused her such profound distress. He was, after all, little more than a stranger. They had spoken only once, and their acquaintance had lasted no longer than a brief encounter beneath the spring rain. She did not know his name, his age, or anything of his family. Indeed, she could not even be certain that he remembered her.

Nevertheless, the memory of that afternoon had remained with her more faithfully than many events of far greater importance.

Why must I find him so desperately?

The question troubled her, though she already knew its answer.

On the day of their first meeting, he might easily have passed her by. There had been nothing to compel him to descend into that miserable hollow, nothing to oblige him to soil his fine clothing or ruin his shoes for the sake of a muddy little girl whom he had never seen before. Yet he had done so without hesitation. He had lifted her from the cold earth, sheltered her against the rain, and held her with a warmth she had not experienced since leaving the Taren estate.

And when she had entrusted the dying bird to his care, he had accepted the helpless creature with a gentleness which seemed wholly at odds with his severe expression.

It was such a small kindness, perhaps scarcely worthy of remembrance in the eyes of others. But to Talia, who had spent so many lonely nights within the splendid and indifferent walls of the Imperial Palace, it had become something immeasurably precious.

She wanted to see him again. She wanted to discover whether those remarkable silver-blue eyes would look upon her with the same quiet attention, and whether the warmth she remembered had been real.

With renewed determination, she continued along the outskirts of the towering palace buildings, eventually making her way towards the more secluded grounds behind the Main Palace. There, the cultivated gardens gradually gave way to groves of trees, their thick foliage casting welcome shadows upon the sunlit paths.

By then, however, her strength was nearly exhausted. Her legs throbbed from the morning's exertions, and her feet burned so painfully that she could scarcely suppress a wince with every step. She stopped beneath a great tree and raised her face towards the patches of blue sky visible through the leaves, drawing several weary breaths while she considered the unhappy necessity of abandoning her search.

It was at that very moment that she saw him.

A slender figure was moving through a grove of birch trees some distance ahead, his pale hair catching the sunlight as he passed between the white trunks. Although she could see little more than his back, Talia recognised him immediately. There was something unmistakable about his bearing, the graceful certainty of his movements and the composed manner in which he carried himself. No other boy she had ever observed walked quite as he did.

Her weariness vanished in an instant.

Without pausing to consider the condition of her feet, she gathered her skirts and hurried after him. Yet the boy was moving at a considerable pace, and despite her efforts, the distance between them scarcely diminished. She quickened her steps, then broke into an awkward run, her breath growing increasingly short as she struggled to follow him through the trees.

Where can he be going in such haste?

She wished to call out, but her breath had become so laboured that she could scarcely summon the strength to speak. Before she could decide what to do, the boy disappeared beyond the farther edge of the grove.

Talia followed as quickly as she could, but when she reached the place where she had last seen him, there was no trace of his figure.

For several moments, she stood motionless, staring at the deserted path with an expression of utter disbelief. Then, overcome by exhaustion and disappointment, she sank against the trunk of a nearby tree.

The sunlight pierced the foliage above her, falling in sharp golden fragments across her flushed face. Her beautiful gown was creased, her carefully arranged hair had begun to loosen, and her feet ached so dreadfully that she doubted she could walk another dozen steps. She closed her eyes, feeling as though all the hope with which she had awakened that morning had been foolishly squandered.

She remained there for some time, listening to the faint rustling of leaves in the summer breeze.

Then a sound reached her ears.

It was laughter, light and musical, carried from somewhere beyond the trees. The sound was so delicate that it reminded her of a little bird singing among the branches.

Talia opened her eyes and lifted her head.

The laughter came again.

Curiosity overcame her weariness, and she rose slowly to her feet. Following the sound, she made her way through the birch grove and pushed aside the branches of a thick shrub, beyond which an enchanting sight unexpectedly unfolded.

Before her lay a secluded garden of extraordinary beauty. Beds of lavender and marigolds surrounded a delicate arrangement of white blossoms, while a small fountain sent glittering streams of water into a marble basin. Beyond it stood a graceful pavilion of pure white stone, its slender columns and elegantly carved roof lending the entire place the appearance of a palace belonging to some fairy queen. Golden sunlight filtered through the surrounding trees, illuminating the flowers and falling water until the garden seemed almost unreal.

For a moment, Talia forgot the object of her search. She stood gazing at the lovely scene with wide eyes, scarcely able to believe that such a place existed within the grounds of the Imperial Palace.

Then she discovered the boy.

He was kneeling upon one knee before a marble bench near the fountain, his pale golden hair shining beneath the summer light. Talia's heart leapt with such sudden happiness that she nearly called out to him at once.

But he was not alone.

Upon the bench sat a little girl who appeared to be of much the same age as Talia herself. She possessed soft, dark-brown hair, rosy cheeks, and a charming countenance made still more lively by the animation with which she spoke. Her clear green eyes sparkled as she chattered, apparently recounting some amusing occurrence to the boy before her.

And Varkas was listening.

More than that, he was smiling.

It was only the faintest movement of his lips, scarcely more than a suggestion of amusement, yet Talia saw it with painful clarity. She had wondered so often what his smile might look like, had imagined the severe lines of his beautiful face softening beneath an expression of genuine happiness. Now that she witnessed it, however, the sight brought her no pleasure.

Instead, something sharp and unfamiliar seemed to pierce her chest.

For an instant, she felt as though a precious possession had been taken from her, though she knew perfectly well that she had no claim upon the boy. The two children before her plainly shared an intimacy born of long acquaintance. Their easy manner suggested that they had known one another for years, while she herself remained no more than a stranger who had once been rescued from the mud.

Talia lowered her eyes, ashamed of the resentment which had risen so suddenly within her.

How foolish I am. He has every right to smile at whomever he pleases.

Yet the thought did little to ease the discomfort in her heart.

She wanted to be included in the warmth that seemed to surround them. She wanted to stand beside the fountain and speak with them as naturally as the little girl spoke with Varkas. Above all, she wanted those beautiful silver-blue eyes to turn towards her, and she wanted to see that faint smile upon his lips because of something she had said.

After a moment's hesitation, she straightened her shoulders and resolved to introduce herself.

She was, after all, an imperial princess. Surely there could be no great difficulty in making the acquaintance of two children belonging to noble families. If she greeted them politely, perhaps they would invite her to join them. Perhaps Varkas would remember the rainy afternoon and speak to her with the same gentleness she had cherished for so long.

Encouraged by this hope, Talia stepped from behind the shrubbery and approached the fountain.

The sound of her footsteps interrupted the conversation.

Varkas raised his head, and his remarkable silver-blue eyes settled upon her. At the same moment, the little girl turned, revealing a pair of brilliant green eyes which regarded the unexpected visitor with evident surprise.

Talia suddenly found herself at a loss.

She had seldom enjoyed the companionship of children her own age, and although she knew perfectly well how to address servants and receive the formal greetings of nobles, she had little experience in the simple business of making friends. Her mouth grew dry, and for one uncomfortable instant she considered retreating before either of them could speak.

But pride came to her assistance.

Lifting her chin with all the dignity she could muster, she offered a small, confident smile.

“Good afternoon.”

The boy did not respond.

He remained perfectly still, regarding her with an expression she could not interpret.

Talia's confidence faltered.

Does he not remember me?

She supposed it was possible. When they had first met, she had been soaked to the skin, her hair disordered and her face stained with mud. Perhaps the splendidly dressed little princess now standing before him bore so little resemblance to that miserable creature that he could not recognise her.

She was just about to remind him of their encounter when the little girl suddenly uttered a piercing scream.

“No!”

Talia started violently.

The girl's expression had changed beyond recognition. The cheerful animation had vanished from her face, leaving her pale and trembling, while her green eyes widened with a terror so profound that Talia could scarcely comprehend it.

“No! She must not come here! Not here!”

Before Talia could speak, the girl sprang from the bench and threw herself towards Varkas, clinging desperately to his neck.

“Please, Varkas! Make her leave! I cannot bear to have her here. Do not allow her to come near me!”

Her voice broke into frightened sobs, and her slender shoulders shook with such violence that Talia stood rooted to the spot in bewilderment.

She had done nothing.

She had merely offered a greeting.

Yet the girl regarded her as though she were some dreadful apparition whose very presence threatened to bring calamity upon them.

Varkas placed his arms around the trembling child and drew her close, sheltering her against him with a protectiveness that appeared entirely instinctive. Then, over the girl's narrow shoulder, he raised his eyes towards Talia.

The expression upon his face was one she had never imagined seeing directed at herself.

His beautiful eyes, which she remembered as containing fragments of silver light, had become cold and unyielding. There was no trace of the quiet kindness with which he had once rescued her, nor of the faint smile she had witnessed only moments before.

“Leave this place,” he said.

Talia stared at him.

For a moment, she could not understand the words.

She had searched for him since morning. She had endured the painful walk beneath the summer heat, dressed herself in her finest gown, and gathered all her courage merely to speak with him.

And now he was ordering her away.

Varkas's gaze did not soften.

“Go. Now.”

Talia took an involuntary step backwards.

She wished to ask why. She wished to remind him of the rainy afternoon, of the little bird, of the promise he had made to care for it. Surely he would remember once she explained. Surely he could not look at her with such hostility if he understood who she was.

But the words would not come.

The little girl continued to sob against his shoulder, and Varkas held her as though protecting her from some terrible danger.

Talia realised, with a pain she could neither understand nor express, that the danger was supposed to be herself.

She turned away.

Then she ran.

The beautiful garden disappeared behind her in a confusion of colour and sunlight. She hurried through the birch trees without any thought of the path beneath her feet, scarcely noticing the branches that caught upon her gown or the pain of her blistered heels.

It seemed as though a vessel of icy water had been poured over her head, extinguishing every warm and hopeful thought she had entertained that morning. Her mind was strangely empty, and although her chest ached, she could not have said whether the sensation arose from exhaustion or grief.

Why did he look at me like that?

The question repeated itself without answer.

What have I done?

She had imagined their reunion a hundred different ways, yet never once had she considered that he might despise her. The boy who had descended into the mud to rescue a helpless stranger had looked upon her as though she were something contemptible.

Talia ran until the secluded gardens lay far behind her and the familiar walls of the Main Palace came into view. She had begun to slow her steps, struggling to recover her breath, when a sudden, violent force seized her from behind.

Someone had caught hold of her hair.

Before she could utter more than a startled cry, her head was jerked backwards with such force that she lost her balance. The world seemed to tilt beneath her feet, and an instant later she was thrown upon the grass.

Pain shot through her body.

She attempted to rise, but a heavy blow struck her abdomen before she could gather herself.

Talia collapsed, clutching her stomach as the breath was driven from her lungs. For several terrible moments, she could neither speak nor understand what had happened.

“How dare you!”

The voice above her was young, but it trembled with a fury so violent that she scarcely recognised it as belonging to a child.

“How dare you go to that place!”

Talia raised her head with difficulty.

A boy stood over her, his face contorted with rage. He possessed dark hair and a pair of brilliant green eyes which seemed almost to burn beneath his knitted brows.

She had never seen him before.

Yet there was something painfully familiar about his features. The shape of his eyes, the colour of his irises, and the youthful lines of his face bore an unmistakable resemblance to the little girl she had encountered beside the fountain.

Talia stared at him in astonishment.

She did not know his name, nor why he had followed her, nor what offence she could possibly have committed to provoke such extraordinary anger.

Before she could ask, he struck her again.

The pain was so severe that her vision blurred. She curled instinctively upon the grass, drawing her knees towards her chest in a desperate attempt to protect herself.

“Die!” the boy shouted.

Talia tried to breathe, but the effort sent another sharp pain through her abdomen.

The boy did not stop.

His fury seemed only to increase as she struggled helplessly upon the ground. He struck her repeatedly, shouting curses with a savagery that bore no resemblance to the ordinary quarrels of children.

“Die! Die! Just die!”

The words rang in her ears.

Talia had never experienced anything like it. She had been scolded, humiliated, and treated with cold indifference, but never had another person attacked her with such uncontrolled hatred.

She could not understand why he wished to hurt her.

She had entered a garden.

She had greeted two children.

That was all.

Yet the boy behaved as though she had committed some unforgivable crime.

Talia attempted to crawl away, dragging herself across the grass while shielding her head and stomach as best she could. Her beautiful velvet gown was stained with earth, and her carefully arranged hair had fallen into complete disorder.

She no longer cared.

All she wanted was to escape.

The boy's furious cries continued above her, each word striking her with almost as much force as his blows.

“Disappear! You should never have come here!”

At last, alarmed voices rose from the direction of the palace.

Several servants had witnessed the assault and were hurrying towards them. Two attendants seized the enraged boy and dragged him backwards, struggling to restrain him as he fought against their grasp.

“Your Highness! Please, you must stop!”

But the boy continued to struggle, his face flushed with fury and his green eyes fixed upon Talia with undiminished hatred.

She remained upon the ground, trembling so violently that she could scarcely raise herself upon her elbows.

Even as the attendants held him back, the boy continued to shout.

“Get rid of her! I never want to see her again!”

His voice rose into a terrible scream.

“Disappear from this world! You filthy bastard!”

Talia stared at him through her tears.

Only that morning, she had awakened with such happiness. She had chosen her prettiest gown and endured every discomfort in the hope of meeting the boy whose kindness had once given her comfort in the lonely Imperial Palace.

She had wanted nothing more than to speak with him again.

Instead, she had been driven from a beautiful garden as though she were something hateful, and now she lay bruised upon the grass while a stranger screamed that she ought never to have existed.

For the first time, Talia began to understand that there were people within the palace who despised her for reasons she had never been permitted to know.

And no matter how carefully she dressed, how politely she spoke, or how desperately she longed to be loved, she could not compel them to look upon her with kindness.
