Chapter 57

Asros wiped the perspiration from his forehead with his sleeve before bounding up the stone steps in a single breath. Reaching the entrance, he seized the handle of the firmly closed door and pulled it open.

Beyond the threshold lay the great hall, silent as a tomb.

He peered into the gloomy chamber, searching for some sign of life. Yet neither a guard nor a maid was anywhere to be seen. The emptiness unsettled him.

For reasons he could not quite explain, Asros found himself walking on tiptoe as he approached the staircase. He was even holding his breath.

Why am I being so quiet?

It was not as though he were trespassing. He was an imperial prince, after all, and had every right to visit his sister. Nevertheless, the oppressive stillness of the Separate Palace made him feel as though he had entered a place where the living were not welcome.

He had experienced the same strange sensation during his previous visit. The entire building resembled a mausoleum more than a royal residence. Its cold corridors and lifeless rooms seemed to swallow every sound, leaving behind a silence that made his chest tighten.

Asros glanced towards the windows, where heavy curtains concealed much of the daylight. Then, eager to reach his destination, he hurried up the staircase, taking two steps at a time.

Before long, he arrived upon the third floor. At the far end of the corridor stood his sister's bedchamber.

Asros approached the heavy mahogany door and paused to catch his breath. He adjusted the flowers in his arms, then raised one hand and knocked rather timidly.

No answer came. He waited. Still, there was no sound from within.

Perhaps she is sleeping.

He pressed his ear against the wooden door, listening carefully. Then he knocked again, a little more firmly this time.

A dull thud sounded somewhere inside.

Asros straightened. He hesitated for several moments before finally reaching for the handle. The door opened beneath his hand.

He cautiously peered inside. A faint haze of smoke hung in the air.

Asros immediately began to cough, the stale, unpleasant atmosphere catching in his throat. Then his eyes widened.

The room looked as though a violent storm had passed through it.

For a moment, he stood motionless in the doorway, scarcely able to believe what he was seeing. Then, almost as though some invisible force were drawing him forward, he stepped into the chamber.

Broken glass glittered across the floor. A shattered wine bottle lay upon the carpet, its dark contents having soaked into the fabric and left behind a heavy, sour odour.

Long strips of torn cloth were scattered everywhere, mingled with the white feathers that had spilled from a ruined pillow. Furniture stood out of place, and the entire chamber bore the unmistakable signs of violent disorder.

Asros looked around in bewilderment. Then his gaze fell upon the tall mirror standing against the wall.

He flinched.

The glass had been shattered into countless fragments, though much of it still remained within its frame. His reflection stared back at him, broken into dozens of distorted pieces by the web of cracks spreading across the surface.

A cold shiver travelled down his spine. He instinctively stepped backwards.

At that moment, a faint rustling sound came from behind him. Asros turned sharply.

And froze.

Talia was lying upon the carpet.

For an instant, he forgot how to breathe.

His elder sister wore nothing but a thin nightgown. Her body lay sprawled across the floor, while her vacant eyes remained fixed upon the ceiling.

There was something so terribly lifeless about her expression that Asros felt a sudden chill settle over his heart. She scarcely resembled the proud, sharp-tongued princess he remembered.

Her arms were painfully thin, resembling the fragile branches of a young birch tree. With slow, listless movements, she gathered a handful of feathers from the floor and tossed them into the air.

The white down drifted gently through the smoky haze, floating like tiny specks of dust. Talia watched them descend.

For several moments, her expression remained entirely empty. Then her gaze slowly shifted towards the doorway.

Her eyes settled upon Asros. He took an involuntary step backwards.

Her lips were dry and cracked, with dark scabs forming along their edges. When she finally spoke, her voice emerged as a faint, rasping whisper.

"...Have you come to stare at me?"

Asros felt his throat tighten.

"I-I only came..." he stammered. "I wished to see how you were feeling."

Talia did not answer. Her hollow gaze drifted towards the large bouquet resting in his arms.

Asros followed her eyes. Suddenly, his ears began to burn with embarrassment.

The flowers that had seemed such a thoughtful gift only moments earlier now appeared terribly out of place. Indeed, for some inexplicable reason, bringing them here felt almost cruel.

He lowered his gaze. Talia closed her eyes, as though even the effort of looking at him had exhausted her.

"...Leave them there," she murmured. "And go away."

Asros stood helplessly for a moment. He had imagined so many different ways their meeting might unfold.

Perhaps she would be surprised to see him. Perhaps she would smile, or even speak kindly to him for once. But he had never imagined finding her like this.

He did not know what to say. Nor could he think of anything that might ease the dreadful emptiness in her eyes.

At last, he approached the bed and carefully placed the flowers upon the low shelf beside it. He glanced towards his sister one final time.

Then he turned and hurried from the room.

Berens was waiting in the corridor. The moment he saw the young prince's pale face, his brows drew together.

His expression became suspicious, as though he feared Talia might have done something to frighten or harm the boy.

But Asros offered no explanation. He merely seized Berens's hand and pulled at it urgently.

"Let us return to the Main Palace," he whispered. "Quickly."

Without waiting for an answer, he began running down the corridor. The passage was as silent as a grave.

Asros could not have said what he was fleeing from. He only knew that he could not bear to remain in that cold, gloomy building for another moment.

His heart was pounding, and an unpleasant tightness had settled in his chest. He glanced once over his shoulder.

The door to Talia's bedchamber remained slightly ajar. The sight made him quicken his steps.

He rushed down the staircase and fled through the entrance of the Separate Palace like a frightened creature escaping from a trap.

It felt as though countless ants were crawling beneath her skin.

A sharp, prickling sensation tormented Talia's nerves, beginning in her shins before creeping upwards through her knees and thighs. From there, it spread into her hips and lower back, gnawing at her with relentless persistence.

It was not the blinding agony she had suffered before. In some ways, however, it was almost more maddening.

There was no escaping it.

Talia dug her fingernails into the uneven flesh covering her knees and began scratching. The swollen scars felt rough and unnatural beneath her fingers.

Yet the more she rubbed and clawed at them, the worse the dreadful sensation became. It was as though the invisible insects beneath her skin had been disturbed and were now burrowing deeper, straight into her bones.

Get out.

She scratched harder.

Get out of me!

Her nails tore into the thick, bark-like skin. She imagined that if she could only dig deeply enough, she might drag the wretched creatures from her body and finally find relief.

Blood began to seep from the torn flesh. Still, she continued.

The burning pain of her damaged skin scarcely mattered compared with the unbearable irritation beneath it.

She was digging her fingernails into the raw wounds when the door suddenly opened.

A sharp cry rang through the chamber.

"Your Highness! I have told you to stop doing that!"

The nanny hurried inside, carrying a tray. The moment she saw what Talia was doing, she set it down with a loud clatter and rushed towards the bed.

"Please! You must stop!"

She seized Talia's wrists and pulled her hands away from her injured legs.

Talia slowly raised her eyes. Her expression was dull and exhausted.

For a moment, she merely stared at the woman. Then she attempted to reach towards the scars with her other hand.

"Why must you insist upon doing this?" the nanny cried in exasperation.

She caught Talia's remaining wrist and held both hands firmly. A heavy sigh escaped her.

"Your legs are already unsightly enough. What will you do if you make them worse?"

Talia went still. She stared at the woman without speaking.

Unsightly...?

This was the same nanny who had once praised her beauty at every opportunity.

How often had the woman declared that Talia possessed the loveliest complexion, the most delicate features, and the same exquisite appearance that Empress Senevier had enjoyed in her youth?

She had repeated those compliments so frequently that Talia had sometimes grown tired of hearing them.

Yet now...

Even she thinks I am hideous.

Something cold and bitter settled in her chest. With a sudden burst of strength, Talia wrenched her wrists free and shoved the woman by the shoulder.

"Get out!" she cried. "I cannot bear the sight of you!"

The nanny staggered slightly. Then she straightened, her expression hardening.

Without another word, she marched towards the side table and picked up a bowl of porridge and a small bottle of medicine from the tray.

She returned to the bed and thrust them towards Talia.

"Eat this, and I shall leave you in peace," she replied sharply. "You need not concern yourself about that."

Talia immediately swung her arm towards the bowl, intending to knock it from the woman's hands.

But the nanny had evidently learned from experience. After enduring the same behaviour time and again, even the most patient servant would eventually become prepared for it.

She stepped neatly beyond Talia's reach. Then she fixed the princess with a stern gaze.

"If Your Highness refuses to eat, I shall not light the sleep-grass."

Talia glared at her. Resentment burned within her, but she had little strength remaining with which to resist.

And more than anything, she wanted the relief that the smoke would bring.

At last, she reached for the bowl. She took the spoon and began forcing the porridge into her mouth.

The thick, tasteless mixture seemed little better than mud. Each mouthful made her stomach turn.

Nevertheless, she continued eating, swallowing mechanically until the bowl was empty. Then she took the medicine bottle and drank its bitter contents.

Only after she had finished did the nanny approach the burner. She placed a fresh bundle of dried herbs inside and set it alight.

Soon, the acrid fragrance of sleep-grass began spreading through the chamber.

Talia sank back against the pillows. She drew the smoke into her lungs, welcoming the familiar heaviness that slowly settled over her body.

Little by little, the dreadful sensations beneath her skin began to fade. Her thoughts grew hazy.

A pleasant numbness crept through her limbs, dulling both the pain and the restless agitation that had tormented her.

Talia released a faint breath of relief. Then she turned her head towards the window.

Beyond the clear glass, the evening sky had begun to glow a deep crimson. The sight made her heart sink.

Soon, the sun would disappear beneath the horizon. And once darkness fell, the familiar terror would return.

Even through the growing fog in her mind, she could feel dread creeping into her chest.

She had come to fear the night. There was something unbearable about lying awake in the darkness, waiting endlessly for time to pass.

The memory of those long, silent hours seemed to tighten around her throat.

Yet there was something she feared even more.

Morning.

For when morning came, she would be forced to open her eyes and face another day within this miserable body.

Another day of pain. Another day of humiliation. Another day of enduring an existence she could scarcely bear.

Talia squeezed her eyes shut. A desperate prayer formed within her heart.

Please...

Let the time I have left pass while I am asleep.

Let me sleep through all of it.

She wished that the remaining days of her life might simply slip away without her noticing, until there was nothing left to endure.

Then, perhaps, she would finally be free from the suffering that seemed to have no end.

But as always, no answer came.

Someone was shaking her shoulder.

Talia stirred reluctantly. Her eyelids felt heavy, and for several moments, she could not understand why she was being disturbed.

Then she turned her head towards the window. Morning sunlight was streaming into the room.

A hollow sigh escaped her lips.

Another day...

Another hateful day had begun.

She pressed one hand against her throbbing temple, wishing she could close her eyes and return to sleep.

Just then, an unfamiliar voice broke through the quiet.

"Your Highness, you must awaken now."

Talia stiffened. That was not her nanny's voice.

Startled, she turned her head towards the bedside. A dark figure stood beside her.

Talia immediately pushed herself upright.

Before her stood a woman of remarkable height, with broad shoulders and an imposing bearing.

Her eyes were the colour of cold steel, and her expression was so stern that she seemed more suited to commanding soldiers than attending upon a princess.

Every movement she made was measured and precise. The woman inclined her head in a perfectly executed bow.

"Forgive me for entering your bedchamber without prior permission, Your Highness."

Her voice was composed and formal.

"My name is Trania Meldren. I serve as Head Maid of the Empress Palace."

Talia stared at her in bewilderment.

Trania continued.

"By Her Majesty the Empress's command, I have come to oversee Your Highness's preparations for the day."

Talia's brows drew together.

Preparations?

She looked around the chamber. Only then did she notice that the room had been thoroughly tidied while she slept.

The disorder that had filled it the previous evening had disappeared. And standing neatly along one side of the chamber were twelve maids.

Each wore a uniform bearing the crest of the Empress Palace.

Several held carefully arranged garments across their arms, while others carried the various articles required for dressing a member of the imperial family.

Talia stared at them. A dreadful suspicion began to form.

Her throat tightened.

"Preparations..." she repeated faintly.

She looked back towards Trania.

"For what?"

The Head Maid regarded her with unwavering composure.

"Today, Your Highness," she announced, "you have been granted an audience with His Imperial Majesty, the Emperor."
