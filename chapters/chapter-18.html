Chapter 18

As Talia passed the Main Palace, the servants who recognised her hastily lowered themselves to the ground, pressing their foreheads against the marble floor. She wondered whether they feared that the notoriously ill-tempered Second Princess might cause another disturbance.

Ignoring the startled maids who stared at her unexpected arrival, she continued towards the Empress's Palace, the most splendidly furnished residence within the Imperial grounds.

At this hour, Senevia was usually either dining with the Emperor in the Main Palace or spending a quiet evening in her own chambers. Talia suspected that her mother had chosen the latter, and she was soon proved correct.

Senevia had withdrawn to the secret workshop concealed behind her private library.

Talia made her way between the towering bookshelves and descended the narrow staircase leading beneath the palace. As she entered the underground chamber, the mingled scents of medicinal herbs, fragrant oils, and faint smoke filled her nostrils.

The spacious room was crowded with curious instruments and glass vessels containing ingredients of every imaginable kind. A violet haze lingered in the air, while beside the blazing hearth stood great piles of books written in the languages of various races. It was a place of remarkable disorder, scarcely resembling the private retreat of a woman who surrounded herself with nothing but the finest and most luxurious possessions.

Yet Talia was familiar with its every corner. She crossed the chamber without hesitation and approached the desk at which her mother sat.

Senevia reclined comfortably in a chair adorned with velvet cushions, her attention fixed upon a parchment scroll. She did not so much as raise her eyes when her daughter approached.

The indifference made Talia's anger burn all the more fiercely. Leaning over the desk, she spoke through clenched teeth.

“Asros came to see me a little while ago.”

Only then did Senevia lift her deep blue eyes.

“I am told that I am soon to be married,” Talia continued bitterly.

“I had not realised Asros had taken such an interest in you,” Senevia remarked, setting aside the parchment.

Even now, her mother's first concern was Asros. Talia felt a sudden desire to seize the objects upon the desk and hurl them across the room. Instead, she tightened her hands around the folds of her skirt and struggled to contain her temper.

“Why must I hear of my own marriage from him? And what is this nonsense about my accompanying the pilgrimage? What scheme are you devising now?”

“A scheme? What an unpleasant accusation.”

Senevia sighed softly and rose from her chair. For an instant, Talia found herself watching the graceful elegance of her movements, but she quickly recovered and fixed her mother with a suspicious glare.

The Empress smiled with the innocent sweetness of a young maiden.

“You have reached an age at which marriage is perfectly appropriate. A proposal happened to arrive from a suitable gentleman, and I merely thought it prudent to consider the matter.”

“By a suitable gentleman, do you mean that serpent of a man, Therian, or whatever he calls himself?”

Talia's lips twisted with disgust as she recalled the oily, unpleasant gentleman who had escorted her during the banquet.

“I suppose you have examined his qualifications most thoroughly, Mother?”

She had intended the remark as a cruel reference to her mother's former lovers, but Senevia answered with such composure that Talia immediately regretted speaking.

“If you are inquiring whether Therian is capable of fulfilling his duties as a husband, then you need have no concern. He is remarkably accomplished in that regard. I daresay he would attend to you most willingly.”

A shudder of revulsion passed through Talia. The words seemed to crawl across her skin like a multitude of spiders, and the composure she had so desperately maintained finally deserted her.

“I want nothing to do with that disgusting man!” she cried. “I would sooner bite off my own tongue and perish than allow such a creature to lay a hand upon me!”

“Oh, my poor child.”

Senevia rested one hand against her cheek and regarded her daughter with an expression of gentle pity.

“Then we must find you another husband. As it happens, I have already been considering several other gentlemen.”

She indicated the parchment upon her desk.

Talia glanced at the names written upon it and felt her blood run cold. She recognised several of them, and every one belonged to a man whose devotion to Senevia bordered upon fanaticism.

Was her mother truly intending to give her in marriage to one of the very men who had once pursued the Empress herself?

Fear and fury mingled within her until she could scarcely distinguish one from the other.

“I shall not marry!” Talia declared. “You have never troubled yourself about me before, so why begin now? Leave me alone, as you have always done!”

Senevia regarded her thoughtfully.

“Does that mean you would refuse every gentleman who is not the heir of House Sierkhan?”

The question struck Talia with such force that the colour drained from her face. She instinctively drew back, but the faint smile upon her mother's lips only deepened.

“If you desired him so desperately, my dear, you ought to have done whatever was necessary to secure him. I kept that man at your side for seven long years. Pray tell me, what were you doing throughout all that time?”

Senevia shook her head with an air of disappointment.

“And now, in a matter of months, the gentleman you covet shall belong to another woman. Do you truly intend to stand aside and watch it happen?”

Talia's shoulders began to tremble. It was dreadful to discover that her mother had understood her feelings all along, perhaps more clearly than Talia herself had ever dared to acknowledge.

Senevia observed her daughter's stricken expression before assuming once more the gentle countenance of a loving mother.

“My dear Talia, everything I have arranged has been for your benefit. I wished only to offer my precious daughter an opportunity to escape her present unhappy circumstances.”

Her long, pale fingers brushed against Talia's cheek. The touch was light and delicate, yet to Talia it felt as though a white serpent were gliding across her skin. She stood rigid, unable to move.

Senevia leaned closer and spoke in a whisper scarcely louder than a breath.

“There are two paths before you, my child. You may employ whatever means are necessary to obtain the man you desire, or you may resign yourself to becoming a somewhat less miserable failure.”

Her voice was soft and sweet, yet every word seemed to cling unpleasantly to Talia's ears.

“I chose the former course, and by doing so, I obtained everything I wished for. But if you lack the courage or ability to follow my example, you may still preserve some measure of dignity by choosing a husband with whom you can be reasonably content.”

Senevia smiled faintly.

“It is a rather pitiful compromise, I grant you. Nevertheless, if one must accept defeat, is it not wiser to preserve what little pride remains?”

Talia recoiled as though she had narrowly escaped the jaws of a trap.

Her mother merely regarded her with an indulgent smile.

“This pilgrimage is the final opportunity I intend to offer you. Consider carefully which path you wish to take.”

Talia cast her a look of fierce defiance before turning and fleeing the workshop.

Behind her came Senevia's light, cheerful laughter, as sweet as the song of a bird. Yet to Talia, the sound was more dreadful than any threat. It followed her through the passageways and remained lodged in her thoughts long after she had escaped her mother's presence.

In the end, Talia found herself preparing to depart upon the pilgrimage in the company of the very half-siblings who despised her.

The Empress's household had undertaken responsibility for the servants, provisions, and equipment required for her journey, leaving Talia with little to arrange herself. Senevia had even attempted to assign several of her private soldiers and high-ranking mages to accompany her daughter as personal guards.

That proposal, however, had been abandoned following the Crown Prince's vehement objections.

Gares was already furious at being compelled to travel alongside the half-sister whom he regarded as a perpetual nuisance. The prospect of having the Empress's devoted followers join their company had evidently proved more than he was prepared to endure.

According to the servants' whispers, the Crown Prince had sought a private audience with the Emperor and argued so fiercely that Senevia's arrangements were eventually withdrawn.

For once, Talia found herself grateful for her brother's temper. She had no desire to spend the journey surrounded by men whose loyalty belonged entirely to her mother.

For my sake? What a ridiculous pretence…

Standing near the waiting carriages, Talia watched the servants carry trunks and travelling chests towards the vehicles. She picked absently at the loose skin beside her fingernail, her expression dark with suspicion.

She knew her mother far too well to believe that such elaborate preparations had been made out of affection. Senevia would never have gone to so much trouble without expecting something in return.

There was another purpose behind this pilgrimage. Of that, Talia felt certain.

Her eyes narrowed as she examined the servants dispatched from the Empress's Palace, searching their faces for some indication of the intentions they might be concealing.
