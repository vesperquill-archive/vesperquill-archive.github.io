Chapter 62

It felt as though countless tiny insects had crept beneath Talia’s eyelids whilst she slept.

A sharp, crawling pain lingered behind her eyes, yet she endured it as she fixed her gaze upon her half-sister.

As always, Ayla stood before her with perfect composure, looking down upon her with the graceful dignity expected of an Imperial Princess.

The sight of that proud, untroubled face only made the burning sensation behind Talia’s eyes grow worse.

Clutching the blanket tightly against her chest as though it were a shield, she returned her sister’s gaze with undisguised hostility.

“Have you come merely to amuse yourself at my expense? If you have something to say, then say it quickly and leave.”

“…How have you been feeling?”

“How do you suppose I feel?”

At Talia’s bitter reply, Ayla’s pale green eyes slowly examined her from head to toe.

“You appear to be in rather poor health.”

Talia pressed her lips together.

Her fingertips trembled with anger.

If only my legs were still whole, I would seize her by the hair and throw her out of this room myself.

Struggling to contain the fury rising within her, she forced herself to speak in a measured voice.

“If you have finished looking at me, perhaps you might leave. Your presence alone is enough to make me feel worse.”

Ayla pressed her lips into a thin line.

Silence settled between them.

As the moments passed, Talia felt her patience wearing dangerously thin. The pain she had barely managed to suppress began spreading through her bones once more.

Unable to endure it any longer, she raised her voice.

“Did you not hear me? I told you to leave!”

“I have already told you that I came because I wished to speak with you,” Ayla replied, a trace of impatience entering her voice.

Talia narrowed her eyes.

“Then speak and be gone! The very sight of your face makes me ill. Did you truly expect me to sit here patiently until it pleased you to speak? How absurd! Tell me what you want this instant, or get out of my sight!”

Ayla’s expression stiffened beneath the force of those words.

Her gaze grew cold.

“Very well. Since you insist upon it, I shall tell you why I have come. I wish to know what you intend to do from this moment forward.”

“What I intend to do? In regard to what?” Talia asked coldly.

Her headache was growing steadily worse.

The unpleasant sensation behind her eyes now seemed to burrow deep into her head, as though those invisible insects were eating their way into her very brain.

A piercing ache spread towards the back of her skull.

Every nerve seemed to cry out in protest, yet Ayla continued speaking.

“Do not pretend ignorance. You know perfectly well what I mean.”

“Am I supposed to possess the power to read your thoughts? How should I know what you have yet to tell me?”

“You…!”

Ayla’s voice rose sharply.

Talia tilted her head and watched with a mocking smile as humiliation disturbed her half-sister’s usually flawless expression.

Ayla drew a deep breath, evidently struggling to regain her composure.

When she spoke again, her voice was considerably calmer.

“What I wish to know is whether you truly intend to marry that man.”

Talia offered no reply.

She merely stared at her sister with an expression of complete indifference.

It was Ayla who eventually found the silence unbearable.

Unable to conceal the anxiety in her voice, she continued.

“You despise Varkas. Ever since we were children, you have done nothing but torment him. Surely you cannot seriously intend to become his wife now?”

The desperate hope in Ayla’s eyes was unmistakable.

At the sight of it, a hollow laugh escaped Talia’s lips.

Ayla’s mouth tightened.

But Talia could no longer restrain her laughter.

The more she looked at her sister’s miserable expression, the more bitterly amused she became.

For a brief moment, she forgot the terrible ache in her head and the sharp pains shooting through her injured legs.

Clutching her stomach, she bent forward and laughed until she could scarcely breathe.

“So that is why you have come all this way? Because you are frightened?”

Ayla’s face turned deathly pale.

Talia lowered her voice, taking wicked pleasure in every change that crossed her sister’s face.

“How dreadful it must be for you, imagining that I might take your precious fiancé away.”

She paused, allowing the words to settle.

“Now, what ought I to do? Seeing you in such a state makes me wish to take him from you all the more.”

Ayla’s delicate features twisted with anger.

Her eyes, ordinarily as clear and green as the leaves of an evergreen tree, darkened with resentment.

A strange satisfaction stirred within Talia.

This was the sister who had always regarded her from above, never allowing the slightest imperfection to disturb her graceful appearance.

Yet now, that very sister stood before her with her wounded pride laid bare.

“Varkas agreed to marry you only because he believes it is his duty!” Ayla cried, finally losing her temper.

“He feels responsible because your body was left in such a terrible condition! But what happened to you was not his fault. Why should he be forced to sacrifice his own happiness for your sake?”

The smile vanished from Talia’s face.

All traces of amusement disappeared, replaced by a fury so cold that it seemed to freeze her blood.

Her fingers twitched.

She wanted nothing more than to seize Ayla by the throat and tear out the tongue that dared to speak as though Varkas belonged to her.

Yet Talia swallowed the curses rising within her.

When she finally spoke, her voice was almost unnaturally sweet.

“I had always supposed you possessed a little intelligence. It seems I have greatly overestimated you.”

Ayla went still.

Talia continued, carefully choosing each word.

“Those are hardly the words you ought to be saying to me at present. If you truly wish to keep him, you should be begging for my mercy.”

Her lips curved into a cruel smile.

“Properly, of course. On your knees, if necessary.”

Then she added, with deliberate slowness,

“You should be pleading with me not to marry Varkas Raedgo Sierkhan.”

Ayla’s eyelid twitched.

Talia watched her humiliation with undisguised delight.

For a moment, Ayla merely stood there, her lips trembling.

At last, she moistened them and forced out the words with visible difficulty.

“If I were to beg you… would you agree to refuse the marriage?”

“Well, now…”

Talia answered lightly, as though considering a matter of little importance.

“Surely that would depend upon how earnestly you begged.”

Ayla bit her lower lip.

Her gaze fell to the floor, and for some time she remained silent, struggling even to steady her breathing.

At length, she lifted her head.

A look of grim determination had settled upon her face.

Then, from those delicate pink lips, came a quiet and miserable plea.

“I beg you. Please refuse the marriage to Varkas. If you reject the arrangement, His Majesty will surely not insist upon it. So please…”

Her voice faltered.

Unable to finish, she fell silent.

Talia regarded her sister’s pitiful display and swallowed a bitter sneer.

How remarkable.

She truly believes she has sacrificed something precious by lowering herself before me.

To Ayla, the dignity of an Imperial Princess was a treasure beyond measure.

She seemed to believe that by humbling herself before a sister she regarded as a lowborn illegitimate child, she had made a sacrifice worthy of reward.

Talia lowered her eyes towards the blanket covering her legs.

Only after her body had been ruined beyond repair had anyone considered her worthy of becoming Varkas’s bride.

And yet Ayla believed she could reclaim him merely by surrendering a small portion of her pride.

So that is all my suffering is worth to her.

My ruined legs mean less to her than her precious dignity.

Talia spoke without emotion.

“Very well.”

Colour immediately returned to Ayla’s pale cheeks.

Talia watched her quietly before adding,

“However, there is one condition.”

“A condition?”

Ayla’s expression grew wary.

Talia turned towards the small table beside her bed.

Upon a tray lay some fruit, bread, butter, and several pieces of silver cutlery.

She reached out and took the small knife used for spreading butter.

Without another word, she tossed it towards her sister’s feet.

The silver knife slid across the floor and came to rest against the hem of Ayla’s gown.

Ayla stared down at it in bewilderment.

Talia spoke quietly.

“Take that knife and stab yourself in the leg.”

“…What?”

Ayla raised her head, looking as though she could scarcely believe what she had heard.

Talia met her astonished gaze and repeated herself, carefully pronouncing every word.

“I am telling you to wound your leg until it is ruined. Just as mine has been.”

Slowly, she drew back the blanket.

Ayla’s eyes widened as the terrible scars upon Talia’s legs were revealed.

The flesh was swollen and uneven, marked by long, disfiguring wounds.

Talia placed a finger against one of the deep scars upon her shin.

Then, with deliberate slowness, she traced its entire length, ensuring that Ayla saw every inch of the damage.

“From here…”

Her finger travelled down the scar.

“…all the way to here.”

She raised her eyes to meet Ayla’s.

“Make a wound of the same length with that knife. If you can do so, I shall grant you what you ask.”

Ayla looked helplessly from the knife upon the floor to her own legs, concealed beneath the rich velvet of her gown.

Her eyelids fluttered uncontrollably.

For several moments, she seemed unable to speak.

Then a cold, mocking laugh escaped her colourless lips.

“So you never intended to listen to my plea in the first place.”

Talia said nothing.

Before long, the familiar mask of the First Princess returned to Ayla’s face.

Her expression became composed once more, as though she had never lowered herself to beg for anything.

Lifting her chin with renewed pride, she turned towards the door.

Her footsteps were brisk and measured.

Yet just as she reached the threshold, she came to an abrupt halt.

With one hand resting upon the door handle, Ayla glanced back over her shoulder.

A chilling light shone in her darkened green eyes.

“You shall come to regret what you have done today, Talia Roem Guirta.”

The words fell from her lips like a curse.

Then, as she stepped into the corridor, she delivered one final threat, her voice as cold as winter.

“I shall see to it that you do.”
