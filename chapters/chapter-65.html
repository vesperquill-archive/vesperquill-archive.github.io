Chapter 65

“The Imperial Palace has always been such a place. It is filled with monsters who would sacrifice anything for the sake of power. If you intend to survive within these walls, you must learn to harden your heart.”

Theoric gave Edric's shoulder a firm, solemn pat.

Still struggling to comprehend what he had just heard, Edric slowly raised his head and looked at his superior.

“Does Lord Sierkhan know of this as well?”

“Certainly. It was the Commander himself who ordered the investigation.”

“Then why would he…?”

Edric's voice trailed away.

No matter how he considered the matter, he could not understand it.

The older knight studied his troubled expression for a moment before giving a slight shrug.

“Who could possibly claim to understand what passes through that man's mind?”

He paused, then continued more thoughtfully.

“Nevertheless, whether we understand his intentions or not, there must be some reason he has agreed to take the Second Princess as his wife.”

Edric frowned.

What reason could possibly justify such a decision?

He tried to imagine what might lie behind the Commander's perpetually unreadable expression, but no answer came to him.

While he remained lost in thought, a heavy hand suddenly struck his back.

Startled, Edric turned around.

Sir Hart, who had delivered the hearty slap, spoke in a casual manner, as though determined to put an end to their gloomy conversation.

“In any event, you would do well to put aside your worries concerning the Second Princess.”

His expression then grew serious.

“She will soon be leaving the Imperial Palace. Once she has departed, there will be little reason for your paths to cross again. Cast aside these needless concerns and give some thought to your own future.”

The final words carried a distinct note of reprimand.

Theoric shook his head.

“You have already earned the Crown Prince's displeasure during the pilgrimage. Have you not enough troubles of your own without taking upon yourself the misfortunes of another?”

Edric's face grew pale.

His shoulders drooped as he was reminded of how uncertain his own position had become.

Seeing the younger knight's sudden change in manner, Theoric gave a dry laugh of disbelief.

“Well, would you look at that? After seeing how recklessly you behaved, I had taken you for a man who cared nothing for rank or advancement. It seems I was mistaken.”

“That was not my intention, sir…”

Edric hesitated before continuing earnestly.

“I merely believed that I ought to fulfil my duty as an Imperial Knight. Whatever Her Highness's circumstances might have been, I had been appointed to protect her.”

At his stubborn reply, a complicated expression passed across Sir Hart's face.

He regarded his subordinate in silence for a moment before releasing a long breath.

“I shall tell you once more. Your duty to the Second Princess has come to an end. It is time you returned to your rightful place.”

Edric looked up at his superior's resolute face.

Then his gaze wandered towards the towering palace walls rising beyond the thick trees.

Between the dark green branches, he could just make out the roof of the Separate Palace, where the Second Princess resided.

He had served as her escort for scarcely two months.

Had he not witnessed her being so cruelly injured before his very eyes, she would likely have remained someone to whom he gave little thought.

Sir Hart is right.

His service to her was finished.

However pitiful her circumstances might be, he possessed neither the authority nor the means to change them.

Perhaps this truly is for the best.

As Sir Hart had said, the Imperial Palace was filled with people whose hearts had been corrupted by ambition.

And was the Second Princess not thoroughly despised within those walls?

If she married Lord Sierkhan and departed for the eastern territories, she would at least be removed from those who openly wished her harm.

Perhaps that alone would bring her some measure of peace.

Edric released a quiet sigh.

Resolving to set aside the faint guilt lingering in his heart, he nodded.

“I understand, sir.”

Apparently satisfied, Sir Hart offered him a small smile.

He draped an arm across the younger knight's shoulders and urged him forward.

“Good. Come along, then. We have duties awaiting us.”

Almost dragged along by his superior, Edric cast one final glance towards the distant roof of the Separate Palace.

Then he turned his head, straightened his posture, and continued walking.

Talia lay upon her bed, watching tiny specks of dust drift through the air.

The shimmering particles floated lazily upon the faint currents passing through her chamber before settling gently upon her body.

She raised one hand and disturbed them, sending them dancing into the air once more.

Then she watched as they slowly descended again.

Why she occupied herself with such a meaningless pastime, she could not have said.

She felt rather like a piece of seaweed drifting beneath the water.

Her movements came without purpose, as though she were a reed being carried along by the gentle current of a river.

Perhaps it was not merely my leg that was broken.

Could something within my mind have broken as well?

While she remained absorbed in these empty thoughts, the door to her bedchamber suddenly opened and closed.

Talia slowly turned her eyes towards the sound.

Her nanny stood before her, carrying several armfuls of fabric.

The woman's face was twisted into an expression of fierce displeasure.

“How can you possibly still be lying there in such a state?!”

Her shrill voice grated painfully upon Talia's ears.

Talia immediately pulled the blanket over her head.

But the nanny seized it and dragged it away before launching into another furious scolding.

“Get up and wash yourself this instant! You made such a dreadful commotion, screaming that no one was to lay a hand upon you, that every maid was driven from the room! And now you are simply lying here as though nothing has happened!”

Talia narrowed her eyes.

The woman's words made very little sense.

Have I not been asleep all this while?

Did someone truly come into my room?

Her unfocused gaze wandered across the chamber.

Broken pieces of porcelain and scattered belongings lay strewn across the floor.

Apparently, she really had caused some disturbance.

“I told you to get up!”

The nanny seized her and forcibly pulled her limp body upright.

Still heavy with drowsiness, Talia merely blinked at the woman's flushed face.

Overwhelmed with frustration, the nanny struck her own chest with a clenched fist.

“Just how much of that dreadful herb did you burn while I was away? You can scarcely walk as it is! How do you expect to make your way into the wedding hall in such a condition?!”

“The wedding hall?”

“The wedding ceremony, My Lady!”

“Who is getting married?”

The nanny's round face turned such a deep shade of purple that she resembled an overripe plum.

Her expression was so thoroughly outraged that Talia found it almost amusing.

Her tongue felt loose and numb, and the words slipped from her mouth without the slightest consideration.

“Then allow me to congratulate you upon your marriage, Nanny.”

“It is you who are getting married today, My Lady!”

The nanny's voice rose into a piercing shriek.

“You were the one who insisted upon this marriage! How can you behave as though you know nothing of it now?!”

Talia knitted her brows.

Ah… now that I think of it, I did agree to marry Varkas.

But when had she made that decision?

She could no longer remember.

Her sense of time seemed to have dissolved into the medicinal smoke and disappeared altogether.

After blinking several times, Talia finally swung her legs over the side of the bed.

So the wedding is today.

If that is so, I suppose I cannot remain in bed forever.

With considerable effort, she rose and dragged herself behind the dressing screen.

“I shall assist you with your bath and undergarments, but the remaining preparations must be entrusted to the maids from the Empress Palace.”

“…Very well.”

“And you are not to scratch or strike any of them, no matter what happens.”

“Do you take me for a cat? Why should I go about scratching people?”

The nanny's face twisted as though she had swallowed something exceedingly unpleasant.

Seeing her expression, Talia let out a weak giggle.

The nanny answered with a long, weary sigh before pulling the nightgown over her head.

“Enough of your nonsense. Hurry and wash yourself.”

Talia carefully stepped into the bathing tub.

The very next moment, a great quantity of cold water came pouring over her head.

She rubbed her stinging eyes and pushed away the wet strands of hair clinging to her face.

It was plain that her nanny was in a terrible hurry.

The woman emptied an entire bottle of fragrant oil over her before taking up a coarse brush and scrubbing her vigorously.

The treatment bore a greater resemblance to washing a muddy hunting dog than preparing an Imperial Princess for her wedding.

Nevertheless, Talia endured it without a word of complaint.

Her senses were so dulled that she scarcely noticed the roughness of the brush.

“Come now, out you get.”

After pouring water over her head four times in rapid succession, the nanny hurried her from the tub.

Talia stepped out, water streaming from her body.

The nanny immediately wrapped her in a large towel and began drying her with remarkable speed.

In what seemed scarcely more than a moment, she had dressed Talia in her undergarments and petticoat.

It was the first time Talia had ever witnessed the woman, who ordinarily moved more slowly than a garden snail, working with such extraordinary haste.

She watched in mild fascination.

The nanny wiped the sweat from her forehead with the back of her hand before rushing from the chamber to summon the attendants.

Before long, Talia found herself surrounded by what appeared to be several dozen women.

No… surely there cannot be so many.

One figure seemed to divide into two, two became three, and three became four.

In truth, there were probably far fewer attendants than she imagined.

But through her clouded vision, they appeared to fill the entire room.

Overcome with dizziness, Talia blinked once before allowing her eyelids to close.

Some time later, the maids completed their preparations and began leading her away.

She felt as though she were being carried out to sea by an unstoppable tide, with no power to resist its current.

By the time she became properly aware of her surroundings, she was already seated inside a large carriage.

The vehicle swayed and jolted as it travelled along the road.

Talia turned her unfocused gaze towards the window.

Beneath a sky dark as charcoal, the towering silhouette of the cathedral gradually came into view.

The moment she recognised its blurred outline, an inexplicable dread tightened around her throat.

Cold sweat broke across her skin.

The cathedral entrance was drawing nearer with every passing moment.

Suddenly, she reached for the carriage door.

She was on the verge of forcing it open and throwing herself outside when the carriage came to an abrupt halt.

The sudden jolt sent Talia tumbling from her seat onto the wooden floor.

By cruel misfortune, her injured knee struck the boards.

A searing pain shot through her leg and travelled all the way to her hip.

She bit down hard upon her lower lip, desperately suppressing the cry that threatened to escape.

At that very moment, the carriage door swung open.

A large shadow fell across her.

Talia jerked her head upwards.

Her breath caught in her throat.

Standing before her, his figure appearing almost as though it were concealed behind a thin grey veil, was Varkas.

He wore a formal uniform of immaculate white, with a mantle embroidered with the crest of House Sierkhan draped loosely across his shoulders.

Varkas leaned down towards her.

Through her blurred vision, Talia could feel his cold blue eyes slowly travelling over every feature of her face.
