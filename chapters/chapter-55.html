Chapter 55

Talia drifted through fragments of the past, as though she were floating upon a cloud, until the distant world of her memories slowly began to fade.

Little by little, consciousness returned.

She struggled to lift her heavy eyelids, and the first thing that greeted her was the gentle flicker of a candle flame.

For several moments, she merely stared at the trembling light, her thoughts too clouded to comprehend her surroundings. Gradually, however, her dulled senses began to awaken.

A strange, hollow ache weighed upon her body.

With considerable effort, she pushed herself into a sitting position and looked around.

Where am I?

Nothing about the room seemed familiar.

The bed beneath her was not her own, nor did she recognise the richly furnished chamber surrounding it. Fine curtains hung beside the windows, and expensive ornaments gleamed faintly in the candlelight.

Talia gazed at the unfamiliar luxury with vacant eyes.

Then an unpleasant sensation drew her attention downwards.

Her legs lay uncovered beneath the short hem of her undergarment.

For a moment, she could only stare.

No.

Those could not possibly be her legs.

They cannot be mine.

The limbs resting upon the bed were so grotesquely altered that she could scarcely believe they belonged to her.

With trembling hands, she reached towards her knees.

The moment her fingertips touched the skin, a dreadful chill passed through her.

The flesh was uneven and strangely swollen, its surface resembling hardened candle wax that had been poured carelessly over the skin and allowed to cool.

Even the shape of her legs had changed.

Her kneecaps and shins appeared slightly twisted, while thick, coarse scars spread across her once-smooth skin like the rough bark of an ancient tree.

Talia's breathing grew shallow.

She traced the jagged marks running from her calves, across her knees, and towards her thighs. The irregular lines reminded her of cracks spreading through delicate porcelain.

What has happened to me?

A desperate thought seized her.

Perhaps the disfigured flesh was merely something covering her real skin. Perhaps, if she could remove those dreadful patches, the smooth, pearl-pale beauty she remembered would appear beneath them.

She began scratching at the raised scars.

At first, her movements were hesitant. Then, driven by mounting panic, she clawed at the swollen flesh with increasing desperation.

Her nails tore across the dark red marks.

Pain stung through her legs, but she scarcely noticed.

Come away... Please, just come away.

She scratched harder, as though she might peel away the evidence of everything that had happened.

Soon, small droplets of blood appeared upon the damaged skin.

The crimson beads gathered and began trickling down her legs.

Talia stared at them in horror.

Her hands fell still.

Just then, a floorboard creaked somewhere nearby.

She snapped her head towards the sound.

Seated in a velvet armchair a short distance from the bed was Empress Senevier.

Talia's eyes widened.

The Empress had evidently been watching her for some time. Even in the dim candlelight, her blue eyes gleamed with an unsettling brilliance.

Her crimson lips parted, and she spoke in a voice as sweet and melodious as ever.

"Must you insist upon reopening wounds that required such considerable effort to heal? Summoning those physicians again would be no small inconvenience."

She placed the little booklet she had been reading upon the table beside her and drew her delicate brows together.

Talia could only stare.

Her throat felt painfully dry, and when she finally managed to speak, her voice emerged as little more than a whisper.

"What... what have you done to my body?"

At the accusation, Senevier's eyes widened ever so slightly.

Then they curved into slender crescents.

A soft laugh escaped her lips, as though her daughter had said something delightfully amusing.

"Good heavens, Talia. Is that truly how you address your mother after she has gone to the trouble of summoning members of the Eternal Clan to restore your health?"

Talia remained silent.

The Empress shook her head with an air of gentle disappointment.

"Come now, do not look at me in such a manner. I am perfectly aware that you have little faith in me."

Her voice remained pleasant, almost affectionate.

"Nevertheless, for once, I assure you that I spared no effort on your behalf. I did everything within my power to secure the finest treatment available."

She paused, allowing her gaze to settle upon Talia's trembling figure.

"And I must confess, I too am disappointed that this was all they could accomplish."

Her cold blue eyes travelled slowly downwards.

They came to rest upon the bleeding scars covering Talia's legs.

Talia instinctively seized the blanket and pulled it over herself.

She concealed her disfigured limbs as quickly as she could, her fingers trembling beneath the Empress's gaze.

There was something in the way her mother looked at her that made her feel unbearably ashamed, as though she had become some unpleasant creature unworthy of being seen.

Senevier released a quiet sigh.

"I had half a mind to demand an explanation from those mages," she continued. "Yet after hearing their account, I am inclined to believe they truly did everything that could be done."

She leaned back in her chair.

"According to them, your injuries were far more severe than anyone had anticipated. It was not merely the bone that had been damaged. Portions of the surrounding muscles and nerves had been ruined as well."

Her tone remained perfectly composed.

"Indeed, they insisted that restoring your legs to their present condition was nothing short of a miracle."

Talia sat motionless, her hands clutching the blanket.

The Empress continued as though discussing an unfortunate but rather ordinary household matter.

"As for those dreadful scars, I am afraid even the finest healing magic proved insufficient."

She gave a faint shake of her head.

"They explained that they had cut into the damaged areas several times and repeated the treatment, hoping to restore the skin. Yet each time, those hideous marks returned in precisely the same form."

A delicate sigh escaped her.

"It would seem that leaving the wounds untreated for so long caused permanent changes to the tissue beneath."

Talia felt the room begin to sway.

She could scarcely understand what she was hearing.

Permanent...?

Senevier continued without pausing.

"Still, I suppose we cannot place the blame entirely upon the imperial physicians. Had they closed the wounds immediately, your skin might have retained a more pleasing appearance."

Her expression grew thoughtful.

"Unfortunately, doing so would have left you unable to walk for the remainder of your life."

She offered a small, reasonable smile.

"At least you have regained the use of your legs. We ought to be grateful for that much, should we not?"

The words pierced Talia more cruelly than any blade.

Each calm observation seemed to drive a heated skewer deeper into her chest.

Her mother spoke of her ruined appearance with such dreadful indifference, as though she were discussing a damaged piece of furniture rather than her own daughter.

Talia could not answer.

She sat frozen upon the bed, scarcely able to breathe.

Then Senevier delivered her final observation.

"It truly is a terrible pity."

Talia slowly lowered her head.

The blanket trembled beneath her clenched fingers.

For a moment, the chamber fell silent.

Then Senevier rose from her armchair and approached the bed.

Her movements were graceful and unhurried. A faint fragrance of flowers accompanied her as she leaned towards her daughter.

Cool, delicate fingers came to rest against Talia's cheek.

"Talia, my dear."

The tenderness of her voice was almost unbearable.

"Do you remember what I once told you about those who possess beauty but lack the strength to defend it?"

Talia struggled to raise her eyes.

Through the tears gathering in her vision, her mother's face appeared distorted and strangely distant.

That exquisite countenance, seemingly fashioned from pearls, gold, and sapphires, wavered before her like a reflection upon troubled water.

Senevier smiled.

"Beauty, when accompanied by weakness, merely invites others to seize what they desire. Such people become prizes for those strong enough to claim them."

Her fingers brushed lightly against Talia's cheek.

"Now, tell me, what do you suppose becomes of those who are both weak and hideous?"

Talia's lips trembled.

No answer came.

The Empress continued in the gentle manner of a mother recounting an old fairy tale to her child.

"Why, they become objects of ridicule and contempt, of course."

Her voice remained sweet.

"They are not even considered worthy prizes. No one troubles themselves to claim them, for there is nothing desirable to possess."

Senevier tilted her head slightly.

"Instead, they are trampled upon, mocked, and cast aside. Often, there need be no reason for such cruelty at all."

Talia stared at her through gathering tears.

"People are curious creatures, my dear. They are forever searching for someone beneath themselves, someone they may despise in order to feel superior."

The Empress's smile did not falter.

"And when a person bears an obvious flaw, they become the easiest prey imaginable for such miserable creatures."

Talia fought desperately to hold back her tears.

She pressed her lips together, determined not to break down before her mother.

But the effort proved useless.

A ragged sob escaped her throat.

The pain of those words was sharper than anything she had suffered in her wounded legs.

Senevier looked down upon her daughter's tear-streaked face.

Then she clicked her tongue in an imitation of sympathy.

"Oh, my poor child. There is no need to surrender yourself to despair."

She gently brushed the damp strands of hair away from Talia's cheek.

"After all, I have no intention of allowing my daughter to remain in such a pitiful condition."

Talia raised her eyes.

The Empress's cold fingers lingered against her skin.

And within those unfathomable blue eyes, something dark seemed to stir.

Her gaze narrowed into a slender, smiling crescent.

It was a smile that offered no comfort.

Rather, it seemed to promise that a far greater misfortune was yet to come.

✦ ✦ ✦

Within the grand temple situated upon the grounds of the Imperial Palace, thirty-four coffins had been arranged in a solemn row.

The vast chamber was filled with the low murmur of prayers and the heavy fragrance of incense.

Priests moved slowly between the coffins, sprinkling holy water upon the polished wood while chanting sacred verses for the souls of the departed.

One by one, the mourners approached to place white flowers upon the coffins.

The ceremony continued with all the solemn dignity demanded by imperial custom.

Seated amongst the mourners, Asros watched the proceedings with growing weariness.

His gaze wandered towards his half-siblings.

His elder brother occupied the seat of honour, carrying himself with the same arrogant self-importance that seemed to accompany him everywhere.

Not far away sat Ayla Roem Guirta.

The Perfect Princess appeared to be mourning the fallen with exemplary grace, her expression composed and suitably sorrowful.

To any casual observer, there was nothing unusual about the imperial family.

Everything seemed precisely as it ought to be.

And yet, Asros sensed that something was amiss.

He studied his half-sister more closely.

After a few moments, he realised what had been troubling him.

She is furious.

Ayla's face bore an entirely convincing expression of grief, but her eyes were cold and rigid.

Even the corners of her mouth seemed unusually tense.

It was a subtle difference, one that most people would never have noticed.

But Asros knew her well enough to recognise it.

Whatever could have angered her so greatly?

Unlike their elder brother, who displayed every passing whim for the entire court to witness, Ayla was remarkably skilled at concealing her true feelings.

She could endure the most unpleasant circumstances without allowing so much as a flicker of irritation to disturb her gentle smile.

For that reason, the stiffness in her expression was particularly striking.

Could she be so displeased because her wedding has been postponed?

The possibility seemed reasonable enough.

Asros allowed his gaze to drift towards the man to whom she was betrothed.

Varkas Raedgo Sierkhan stood near the altar, observing the funeral rites in complete silence.

His posture was so perfectly upright and unmoving that he resembled one of the marble statues adorning the cathedral rather than a living man.

Asros found himself studying him with considerable interest.

The heir to House Sierkhan wore a sharply tailored doublet that followed the broad line of his shoulders before narrowing neatly at the waist.

His fitted breeches were almost as precise in their shape as a suit of armour, while a deep navy cloak hung from his left shoulder.

The attire was restrained, particularly when compared with the elaborate finery worn by many of the nobles attending the ceremony.

Yet there was something about Varkas that made such ornaments unnecessary.

Even dressed so simply, he possessed a commanding presence that surpassed those who had spent fortunes upon their appearance.

Asros considered him for a moment.

Well, perhaps I can understand why she is so displeased about the delay.

His thoughts turned towards the circumstances surrounding the postponed marriage.

After a calamity of this magnitude, there is little chance they will be permitted to undertake the pilgrimage before next year.

And if the pilgrimage could not proceed, would that mean the marriage between Ayla Roem Guirta and the future Duke Sierkhan must also be delayed for an entire year?

The thought made Asros's expression darken.

An unpleasant heaviness settled in his chest.

He had been looking forward to the day his half-sister would finally depart the Imperial Palace.

Ayla had always regarded him with a contempt so profound that he sometimes felt she considered him no better than vermin.

The prospect of her leaving for the eastern duchy had been one of the few consolations available to him.

Now, it seemed that even this small hope might be taken away.

Surely they could make an exception.

Asros glanced towards Varkas once more.

Perhaps they might disregard the customary arrangements and proceed with the wedding as originally planned.

He silently directed his most fervent wishes towards the future duke.

Please, Lord Sierkhan.

Take Ayla Roem Guirta away to the East.

At that very moment, as though he had somehow heard the unspoken plea, Varkas turned his head.

Their eyes met.

Asros flinched.

He immediately lowered his gaze, his heart giving a dreadful lurch.

For one terrifying instant, it had seemed as though those piercing eyes had looked straight through him and discovered every thought he had been foolish enough to entertain.
