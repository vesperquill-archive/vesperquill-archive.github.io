Chapter 26

Gares regarded his half-sister's profile with growing suspicion, until he suddenly became irritated with himself for paying such close attention to so insignificant a creature. What did it matter what schemes occupied that troublesome head of hers? Once he ascended the throne, he intended to rid the world of her existence forever. Until that day arrived, he need only endure her presence.

As though to banish the unpleasant thought, he raised his goblet and swallowed a generous mouthful of strong wine.

Meanwhile, Talia brought her own glass to her lips, affecting an expression of complete indifference. Yet when she noticed the faint trembling of her fingers, she immediately set it down. Striving to appear perfectly composed, she concealed her hands beneath the table and moistened her dry lips.

The place where Varkas had touched her still seemed to burn. His bare skin had not even brushed against hers, yet the sensation of his firm fingers through the cold leather of his gloves lingered upon her hand, as though it had penetrated her very flesh.

She straightened her shoulders, which threatened to draw inward, and discreetly wiped her damp palms against her skirts. The delicate fabric clung unpleasantly to her skin, and a sudden wave of frustration swept over her. The gown she had chosen expressly to provoke Gares now felt almost suffocating.

Her exposed shoulders prickled, and an uncomfortable shiver travelled down her spine. She knew perfectly well that Varkas had no reason to look at her, yet the mere possibility made every nerve in her body painfully alert. Talia fought against the desire to turn and discover where his gaze rested.

Years of practice had taught her to maintain an expression of perfect composure, but no amount of acting could prevent the cold perspiration gathering upon her skin. The thin fabric of her gown seemed to cling more tightly with every passing moment, and she bit her lower lip in frustration.

What a foolish choice of dress.

“Is the meal not to Your Highness's liking?”

Talia flinched at the unexpected question. A young monk seated across from her was observing her with considerable interest. Was this not the Abbot who had welcomed Gares earlier? She gave a careless shrug.

“It is rather meagre compared with the meals served at the Imperial Palace.”

The monk's expression faltered slightly. Talia turned away, making it clear that she had no desire to continue the conversation, and picked up a small piece of cake. If she appeared occupied with eating, perhaps he would finally leave her in peace.

The dry morsel crumbled unpleasantly in her mouth. She chewed without enthusiasm, feeling as though she were swallowing a piece of sponge, and hastily washed it down with wine to suppress the urge to gag.

It was then that she noticed several monks stealing persistent glances in her direction. Their lingering stares filled her with greater disgust than the rich, oily dishes spread across the table. Unable to endure their attention any longer, she abruptly rose from her chair.

“This banquet has proved rather disappointing. I believe I shall retire to my chamber and seek some rest.”

Gares cast her an irritated glance. Ordinarily, Talia would have seized the opportunity to deliver another cutting remark, but her stomach was churning so violently that she could scarcely bear to remain in the hall another moment. Without further ceremony, she departed.

The moment she escaped the oppressive mixture of roasted meat, wine, and burning candles, her nausea began to ease. She drew a deep breath, brushed the cold perspiration from her forehead, and proceeded along the quiet corridor with light, measured steps.

A cool night breeze swept across her bare shoulders. Shivering, she folded her arms around herself and quickened her pace.

At times, she scarcely understood her own behaviour. Why did she insist upon making a spectacle of herself? What satisfaction could there be in provoking others merely to invite their contempt?

The Crown Prince simply cannot bear your existence. At times, I believe he despises you even more than he despises me.

Those words, spoken by Senevia many years ago in a voice filled with quiet satisfaction, suddenly returned to her memory.

Talia remembered the occasion all too well. It had been the memorial service for the late Empress Bernadette, and she had entered the ceremonial hall holding her mother's hand. The moment Gares saw her, he had flown into a terrible rage.

He had shouted at her before suddenly seizing her by the throat. The assembled nobles were horrified, and several attempted to intervene, but the Crown Prince refused to release his grip. It had taken two knights rushing forward to pull him away from the young princess.

Once freed, Talia had crawled towards her mother and curled herself into a trembling little ball at Senevia's feet. Her mother had immediately gathered her into her arms, appearing to shield her from further harm.

For one brief moment, Talia had been so overwhelmed with relief that she nearly burst into tears. Then she looked up and caught a fleeting expression of satisfaction upon Senevia's beautiful face.

Even now, she could remember it clearly.

Perhaps it had been from that very day that she began deliberately provoking Gares whenever the opportunity arose. Her reputation, already scarcely worth defending, sank to even greater depths, yet she cared little for the consequences.

Every scandal that damaged her own name also cast a shadow upon the Crown Prince's honour. Whenever Gares disgraced himself through his uncontrollable temper, Senevia appeared pleased, and that alone had seemed reason enough to continue.

A hollow laugh escaped Talia's lips. How absurd it was that, even after everything she had endured, she was still desperately struggling to earn the smallest measure of affection from that woman.

She might drag the Crown Prince's reputation through the mud a hundred times over, yet Senevia would never truly love her.

Her mother loved no one. Not the Emperor, nor even Asros, upon whom she appeared to lavish so much attention. To Senevia, every person was merely an instrument to be employed for her own purposes.

Perhaps it was precisely because Talia understood this that she continued to behave as she did. For if she could no longer prove herself useful, what would remain of her in her mother's eyes?

Nothing.

“Princess.”

The unexpected voice startled her from her thoughts. Talia turned sharply and discovered a tall figure standing in the shadows at the far end of the dimly illuminated corridor.

As he stepped into the light, she recognised one of the monks who had been watching her so persistently during the banquet. Her entire body stiffened.

Has he followed me?

She glanced cautiously about her. The long passage leading towards the garden was entirely deserted, with not a servant, guard, or fellow traveller in sight. The thought that this man might have followed her silently until she reached such a secluded place sent an unpleasant chill through her.

Nevertheless, she raised her chin and spoke with deliberate authority, taking great care to conceal her uneasiness.

“What business have you with me?”

Fortunately, her commanding manner appeared to unsettle him, for he hesitated beneath her sharp gaze. Talia regarded him coldly, hoping that her displeasure would be enough to send him away.

“I asked what business you have with me.”

“I-It concerns something Your Highness said earlier…”

The monk stumbled over his words, and Talia narrowed her eyes.

“What did I say?”

“Well… earlier, during the banquet…”

He shifted awkwardly upon his feet. His freckled face turned a deep shade of red, and his eyes wandered over her in a manner she found deeply disagreeable.

Talia resisted the instinct to retreat. She knew that displaying fear might only encourage him, and so she planted her feet firmly upon the floor and lifted her chin with an air of imperial arrogance.

“I have not the faintest idea what you mean. If you have no particular business, I shall take my leave.”

She turned towards the garden, but before she could depart, the monk hurriedly spoke again.

“Y-Your Highness said… that you wished to fulfil the Crown Prince's expectations…”

Talia stopped abruptly and slowly turned her head towards him. For a moment, she could scarcely comprehend what he was suggesting.

Then the memory returned. During her quarrel with Gares, her brother had accused her of behaving improperly towards the priests and threatened her with disgrace. In her anger, she had declared that she was almost tempted to fulfil his expectations.

Surely this foolish man had not taken those words seriously? Surely he had not followed her into an empty corridor because of that reckless remark?

A terrible coldness spread through her body, as though someone had poured a bucket of icy water down her spine.
