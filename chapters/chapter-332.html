Chapter 332

A shiver ran down his spine at the surreal sight. The moment he met those hollow eyes, as empty as vacant holes, an eerie chill swept over him, as though he were facing something wearing human skin.

In overwhelming despair, he watched as the man slowly drew his sword. A biting wind carrying the first snow swept relentlessly around his unnervingly refined features.

This man probably wasn’t even aware of what he was doing right now. He was simply doing what he believed he ought to do…

He probably believed he was simply doing what he ought to do, dismissing the loss and pain she would suffer as something she deserved to endure.

Regret turned into tears that streamed down his face without end. He wanted to plead with the man standing before him.

Please, go back to her.

Stay by her side.

Don’t hurt her anymore.

But all that escaped his parted lips was a faint breath that seemed about to give out.

At last, the man raised his arm.

The reflection of scarlet flames flickered along the long blade. He stared up at it without even blinking, but the sword, raised as though it were about to slit his throat at any moment, briefly stopped in midair.

He knew the man was struggling with his decision. He also knew that his hesitation wouldn’t last long. The man’s arm came down.

Is this how it all ends, so meaninglessly?

In that fleeting moment, something inside his head shattered into pieces with a deafening crack.

He felt something that had been dwelling deep within him violently stirring his blood. It was as though a massive explosion had occurred inside his body. The heat that had been compressed to its limit burst through his body and spread in every direction.

In the blink of an eye, that powerful energy took the shape of a net, enveloping every living creature in the surrounding area. One of them came rushing from somewhere like the wind and stood directly in front of him.

He heard a fierce roar that seemed to tear through the night air.

At the same time, a sharp sound cut through the air, and hot blood sprayed in every direction like a fountain.

Unable to make sense of what was happening, he opened his unfocused eyes slightly. When he barely managed to lift his head, he saw the man standing against two direwolves.

Another wolf already lay sprawled at the man’s feet, its throat slit. For a moment, he stared blankly at the beasts, unable to tell where they had come from. Then, unsteadily rising to his feet, he began walking deeper into the forest.

However, he barely made it any distance before collapsing again.

By then, the man had killed the other two direwolves and followed the trail of blood he had left behind. He tried once more to use the heat within his body to stop the man, but it did not work as he had intended.

Overcome by severe dizziness, he gasped for breath.

The man had already drawn close and once again pointed the tip of his sword, dripping with blood, at the back of his neck. There was a strong sense of wariness in the man’s eyes. He clearly thought there was something unusual about the direwolves suddenly appearing and attacking him.

Pressing his lips firmly together as though he had made up his mind, the man raised his arm once more.

He watched listlessly as the blade came flying toward him. A flash of light came down toward the back of his neck.

But the pain never came.

He stared blankly at the blade that had struck deep into the ground with a dull thud before turning his gaze back to the man. His face was twisted in anguish, as though he couldn’t accept his own actions.

He vividly remembered the frustration he had felt back then. Even after things had reached this point, the man was probably disgusted with himself for being unable to turn a blind eye to her pleas. He must have found it utterly unbearable that a few unreasonable words from her could sway him so easily.

A few snowflakes drifted down with the wind and clung to his eyelashes. Before long, they melted into clear droplets and ran down the man’s cheeks.

After standing motionless for some time, the man pulled his sword from the ground. His gaze, as cold as ice, slowly swept over his blood-soaked body. Even if he were left as he was, his chances of survival were slim.

The man seemed to have reached the same conclusion. He slowly turned around and approached the direwolf lying a few steps away. With a single stroke of his sword, he severed the wolf’s head, put it inside a leather sack, and tied the sack to the end of his saddle.

Shortly afterward, the man mounted his horse and rode away into the darkness.

With unfocused eyes, he watched the figure grow smaller in the distance. The sound of hoofbeats gradually faded until only the eerie sound of the wind remained in the forest.

As even the pale outlines of the birch trees looming in the darkness began to blur like ink spreading in water, he could no longer keep his increasingly heavy eyelids open and weakly closed his eyes.

The pain weighing down his entire body and the biting cold gradually faded away.

Now, all he could feel was an overwhelming sadness.

* * *

He was walking through the darkness.

At the center of a pitch-black world where not a single ray of light could penetrate, a red river flowed slowly. He approached it and quenched his thirst, his throat burning as though he had swallowed fire.

He could vividly feel the essence of life that had dwelled within their bloodline for countless years flowing into every part of his parched body.

As he frantically drank the blood, he shuddered, feeling his heart beat so rapidly that it seemed about to shatter his ribs.

The violent pounding soon spread through his bones and throughout his entire body. It felt as though something enormous had taken him whole into its mouth and was chewing him to pieces.

He twisted his body uncontrollably in pain, as though his bones were being crushed and his flesh mangled.

How long had he been struggling like that?

Suddenly, the pressure constricting his limbs vanished as though it had never been there, and an indescribable vitality surged from deep within his body.

He slowly opened his eyes. Birch trees with pale bark surrounded him on all sides.

For a while, he blinked blankly, unable to remember why he was lying in such a place. Then he lowered his gaze and stared at his paws, stained black with dark red blood and mud.

Only then did the memories of what had happened before he lost consciousness begin returning to him one by one.

He turned his head and carefully examined his body. The arrows that had been lodged in his legs and torso were nowhere to be seen. Most of the wounds that had covered his skin had also healed.

He cautiously put strength into his front legs and tried to stand up. Having not used them for a long time…

His muscles, seemingly weakened from not being used for a long time, twitched slightly, but he felt no pain. He stretched his back and slowly looked around.

The dense birch forest resembled a maze, and the scenery looked much the same in every direction, making it difficult to determine exactly where he was. One thing, however, was certain: this was not the place where he had lost consciousness. He searched the surrounding area thoroughly but found no trace of the fight, let alone the bodies of the direwolves.

Apparently, he had moved to a safe place without realizing it. While barely conscious, he must have followed his body's instincts and retreated deeper into the forest, removed the arrows, and rested until his body had recovered.

He stared at the new flesh that had grown where the arrows had pierced his body, then slowly began walking along the forest path.

Just how long had he been unconscious?

Signs of winter were clearly visible throughout Armund Forest. Unmelted snow lay piled up in shaded areas, and a thin layer of ice covered the water that had collected between the rocks.

It seemed that more than just a day or two had passed since he lost consciousness.

After wandering anxiously around the area, he decided to get out of the forest first and began running in the direction of the sun.
