Chapter 61

Rumours spread swiftly throughout the Imperial Palace, and the courtiers were not slow to offer their opinions.

Although the misfortune that had befallen the Second Princess was indeed regrettable, many believed it most unjust to place the whole burden of blame upon Sir Varkas. Under ordinary circumstances, would it not have been sufficient to punish the knight who had been entrusted with the Second Princess’s protection?

More troubling still was the disregard shown towards the dignity of the First Princess.

Though her engagement to Sir Varkas had been arranged for political advantage, the two had known one another since childhood. Their formal betrothal had lasted three years, yet the understanding between their families had existed for more than a decade.

Thus, the Emperor’s sudden decree, which threatened to sever their long-standing union, drew considerable criticism.

Many suspected that the Empress had influenced His Majesty’s unreasonable decision. After all, it was hardly a secret that she sought to secure the throne for her own young son.

“What do you suppose will become of the alliance between the Crown Prince and House Sierkhan?”

A maid, who had been stirring a pot with a wooden ladle, turned towards the healing sorceress sent from the Empress’s Palace.

The woman was Marisen, a sorceress of middle age who frequently visited the Main Palace and was well acquainted with the rumours circulating through the Imperial Court.

Without looking up from the fresh herbs she was cutting, Marisen replied in an indifferent tone.

“Who can truly say? Yet if Sir Varkas breaks his engagement to the First Princess and takes the Second Princess as his wife, I daresay that once-unbreakable alliance shall come to an end.”

“Then do you think House Sierkhan might lend its support to the Second Prince instead?”

Marisen looked doubtful.

“Considering Sir Varkas’s disposition, I cannot imagine him changing his allegiance so readily.”

“But why ever not? Surely the Second Prince would make a far better ruler than that dreadful scoundrel of a Crown Prince.”

At such careless words, Marisen immediately cast the maid a stern glance.

“Pray, take greater care with your tongue. However secluded this residence may be, you must remember that even the walls of the Imperial Palace have ears.”

The maid pursed her lips in displeasure.

Nevertheless, Marisen’s warning had plainly unsettled her, for she began glancing nervously about the room.

Seeing this, the sorceress gave a brief, humourless laugh before returning to her work.

Whatever might become of the alliance between the Crown Prince and House Sierkhan was of little concern to her.

She listened to palace gossip only because such knowledge might prove useful in protecting herself. Beyond what was necessary for her duties and safety, she had no desire to become entangled in the affairs of the Imperial Court.

Marisen gathered the rare herbs she had gone to considerable trouble to obtain and placed them into a small pot.

She then worked the bellows to strengthen the fire beneath it.

Before long, the Mandrake Sap began to boil, releasing a thick, unpleasant vapour that filled the kitchen with its powerful scent.

Once the mixture had brewed sufficiently, she transferred it into a jar and placed it upon the windowsill to cool.

Just then, an impatient voice rang out from beyond the residence.

“Is there no one here to receive us?”

The maid, who had been sweating over the stove whilst feeding firewood into it, raised her head in alarm.

“Were we expecting visitors?”

“None that I have been informed of…”

Marisen approached the window and peered towards the entrance of the Separate Palace.

Beyond the thick shrubbery, she could make out several figures dressed in long, flowing cloaks.

At first glance, they did not appear to be anyone sent by the Empress.

An uneasy feeling stirred within her.

Turning to the maid, she quietly urged her towards the rear entrance.

“I shall attend to them. You must hurry to the Empress’s Palace and bring back the guards.”

At present, the Separate Palace was occupied by no more than three or four maids, the Second Princess’s nanny, and Marisen herself.

Because the Second Princess was known to be exceedingly difficult in her dealings with others, proper guards had not even been stationed at the entrances.

Should these unexpected visitors harbour malicious intentions, there would be little anyone could do to prevent them from entering.

Understanding the danger, the maid quickly slipped through the kitchen door.

Marisen waited until she was certain the girl had left the grounds before making her way through the corridor to the main entrance.

Upon opening the door, she found two uniformed men and three women dressed in the manner of noble ladies standing upon the steps.

She studied them cautiously before speaking.

“May I inquire what business brings you here?”

“I have come to see Talia Roem Guirta.”

The woman who had been standing behind the others stepped forward as she spoke.

The moment Marisen recognised her face, her breath caught in her throat.

Before her stood a woman whose rank was surpassed only by that of the Empress herself.

Marisen immediately lowered her head.

“Your Highness, the First Princess. I humbly offer my greetings.”

“You may dispense with the formalities. Take me to her.”

Ayla Roem Guirta interrupted her, weariness evident in her voice.

Marisen raised her head hesitantly.

After a moment, she ventured to refuse as politely as she could.

“I beg Your Highness’s forgiveness, but the Second Princess has yet to recover her strength. Might I humbly ask that you return another day?”

“Do you imagine that I have come here to harm my own sister?”

The First Princess’s voice turned cold.

“Your devotion to your duties is commendable. However, you appear to have forgotten to whom you are speaking.”

Her gaze hardened.

“I did not come here to seek your permission. I have given you an order.”

“Now, if you have understood me, you shall lead the way without further delay.”

Under that commanding gaze, Marisen felt much like a helpless mouse cornered by a serpent.

She could do nothing but turn and guide the Princess inside.

Throughout their passage along the corridor, the First Princess remained entirely silent.

Marisen occasionally glanced over her shoulder, hoping to judge the Princess’s mood.

Yet the closer they came to the bedchamber, the more uneasy she became.

Several hours had passed since the medicinal incense had been lit, so the Second Princess ought to have awakened by now.

Even so, Marisen could not help wondering whether her frail and suffering patient was in any condition to receive such an intimidating visitor.

“Let the others remain here. I shall enter alone.”

Upon reaching the end of the corridor, the First Princess issued this brief instruction to her attendants.

She then turned an imperious gaze upon Marisen.

Understanding that she was expected to announce their arrival, the sorceress timidly knocked upon the bedchamber door.

“Your Highness, you have a visitor. May we come in?”

There was no reply.

Could Her Highness still be asleep?

Marisen hesitated for a moment before carefully turning the handle.

As the door opened, a sickeningly sweet odour drifted towards her, resembling the smell of fruit that had begun to decay.

It mingled with the sharp, bitter scent of medicinal herbs.

Marisen frowned at the unpleasant mixture of smells.

Then her eyes fell upon the figure lying motionless upon the bed, and she started in alarm.

Talia lay sprawled across the mattress, so still and lifeless that she might have been mistaken for a corpse.

Marisen hurried to her bedside and placed two fingers beneath the Princess’s nose.

Only when she felt the faint but steady warmth of her breathing did she allow herself a small sigh of relief.

Yet that relief vanished almost immediately.

Beneath the hem of Talia’s gown, which had ridden up during her restless movements, Marisen caught sight of her injured legs.

The bandages had been torn away once more.

Fresh, angry scratches covered the scarred flesh, unmistakable evidence that the Princess had been clawing at her wounds again.

Marisen sighed heavily.

She gently placed a hand upon Talia’s leg and began murmuring a simple healing incantation.

Without warning, a thin, bony hand shot out and seized her wrist.

Marisen flinched and turned sharply.

A pair of clouded blue eyes stared back at her.

The Princess’s pupils wandered without focus, as though searching for something that was not there.

Whenever her gaze shifted, the deep blue of her irises seemed to ripple like dark water.

There was something strangely unsettling about those eyes.

They seemed capable of drawing a person’s thoughts into confusion, much like the intoxicating smoke the Princess inhaled each day.

“…Who permitted you to enter my chambers?”

Talia’s lips parted slowly.

They were cracked and stained with dried blood, yet the voice that escaped them, though thin and almost metallic, was remarkably clear.

Recovering from her surprise, Marisen hastily drew the coverlet over the Princess’s exposed legs.

“Forgive me, Your Highness. A visitor has arrived and insists upon seeing you…”

As she straightened and gestured towards the doorway, Talia’s eyes moved past her shoulder.

At once, Marisen felt the Princess’s fragile body grow rigid beneath her hand.

With considerable difficulty, Talia struggled to raise herself into a sitting position.

Then she fixed her half-sister with a look of unmistakable hostility.

“What brings you to a miserable place such as this?”

“I have come because there is something I wish to discuss with you.”

The First Princess stepped across the threshold.

For a long moment, she silently regarded her younger sister’s pale and sickly face.

Then she turned towards Marisen.

“Leave us.”

Overwhelmed by the First Princess’s imposing presence, Marisen bowed and slowly retreated towards the doorway.

As she began closing the door, the sight of the two sisters, born of different mothers, remained fixed in her mind.

Talia Roem Guirta appeared so frail that the slightest touch might cause her to break.

By contrast, Ayla Roem Guirta stood before her in the full radiance of health and beauty, seemingly untouched by suffering.

The difference between them was so striking that it left a bitter feeling within Marisen.

For another moment, she lingered by the narrowing opening, her troubled gaze resting upon Talia.

Then, with a long and weary sigh, she quietly closed the door.
