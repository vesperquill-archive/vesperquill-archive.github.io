Chapter 27

The monk looked at Talia with an unsettling expression. His eyes slowly moved over her figure before returning to her face, making her feel increasingly uncomfortable.

Talia froze. She knew she needed to leave immediately, but her legs refused to move. Fear had taken hold of her, leaving her unable to react.

The man moved closer, a strange eagerness showing on his face.

"If Your Highness would allow me, I…"

Before he could finish speaking, he reached for the golden hair resting on her shoulder. Talia watched in horror as he wrapped a few strands around his rough fingers.

She wanted to shout at him and demand how he dared touch her, but no words came out. Seeing that she remained silent, the monk grew bolder and slowly reached toward her face.

His rough palm brushed against her cheek, making her pull away in fear. Just then, a large hand covered in a metal gauntlet appeared from the darkness and grabbed the monk's wrist.

Talia flinched and looked up. The moment she saw those familiar silver-blue eyes, her heart sank.

It was Varkas.

She instinctively pressed her back against the wall. Varkas looked at her without showing any emotion before slowly turning his attention to the monk.

The monk immediately went pale. Whatever he saw in Varkas's eyes frightened him so badly that he began trembling.

"I-I was only… Her Highness…"

"Quiet."

Varkas's deep voice cut him off. The monk's face twisted in pain as Varkas tightened his grip on his wrist. Although he appeared to be holding him casually, the pressure was clearly painful.

"Neither of us wants this to become a bigger problem," Varkas said calmly. "Return to your duties without causing any more trouble."

The monk nodded repeatedly.

Varkas released his wrist but added another warning.

"And I suggest you stay out of my sight until we leave this place."

"I-I understand, my lord."

Holding his bruised wrist, the monk bowed nervously. When Varkas motioned for him to leave, he hurried away without looking back.

Varkas watched him disappear before turning toward Talia.

She immediately lowered her eyes. Although she had done nothing wrong, she suddenly felt embarrassed. Her mouth went dry, and she could feel her face growing warm as she stood awkwardly before him.

"Follow me. I'll escort you back to your quarters."

His voice was calm and distant.

Talia felt exhausted. The fear she had experienced moments earlier was slowly fading, but in its place came a strange mixture of sadness and resentment.

Something terrible almost happened to me. I was so frightened I couldn't even move. So why are you acting as though nothing happened?

She bit her lip and walked past him without answering. No matter how upset she was, she refused to let him see her weakness.

Keeping her head high, she crossed the garden. Varkas followed quietly behind her until they reached the building where the female pilgrims were staying.

Several knights stood guard near the entrance. Seeing them, Talia finally felt a little safer.

"We're here, so stop following me!"

She hurried toward the entrance, wanting to get away from him as quickly as possible. However, before she could take more than a few steps, Varkas moved in front of her, blocking her path near the wall.

Talia looked up at him in surprise.

"Wh-what are you doing all of a sudden?"

"If you've already been hurt once because you were playing with fire…"

His voice sounded unusually rough. He paused briefly before continuing in a calmer tone.

"Shouldn't you have learned to be more careful by now?"

Talia felt the colour drain from her face.

Varkas studied her closely, his expression cold and difficult to read.

"Or do you really intend to keep putting yourself in danger so carelessly?"

Anger immediately rose within her. Without thinking, Talia raised her hand to slap him, but Varkas caught her wrist before she could reach his face.

"If that's what you intend to do, then please control yourself for now," he continued. "I'd rather avoid any more trouble until this journey is over."

Talia pressed her trembling lips together. She was so upset that she could barely find the words to respond.

Varkas released her wrist and turned away.

"I trust you understand."

For a moment, Talia was filled with such intense anger that she wanted to hurt him just as deeply as his words had hurt her.

She stood there, glaring at his retreating figure. But as she watched him walk away, her vision slowly blurred.

Talia quickly turned her face aside. Even though she knew he wouldn't look back, she was terrified that he might see her crying.

She roughly wiped her eyes with her sleeve and hurried inside the building.

The following morning, the religious ceremonies continued as though nothing had happened.

Talia sat on the windowsill of her room, watching the procession below. Gares and Ayla were heading toward the altar, while Varkas followed behind them like a guardian.

Normally, Talia should have been with them. The ceremony was mandatory for members of the imperial family, but she had excused herself by claiming that she wasn't feeling well.

She knew it was a weak excuse.

Under normal circumstances, the ceremony should have been postponed because of her absence. However, Gares had insisted that everything continue as scheduled.

Perhaps the monks were relieved as well. After all, they probably weren't pleased with the idea of giving a sacred blessing to an illegitimate princess.

Maybe I should have attended after all.

Talia let out a small laugh, but her smile quickly disappeared when she saw Ayla and Varkas standing beside each other in front of the altar.

Perhaps it was better that she hadn't gone. If she had been there, she might have lost control of her emotions and done something terrible.

I don't even know which of them I hate more anymore.

Out of habit, she raised a finger toward her mouth, intending to bite her nail. However, she stopped herself, not wanting to ruin the nails that had finally grown out.

Unable to continue watching, Talia closed the curtains and threw herself onto the bed.

As she curled up beneath the blanket, memories of the previous night returned.

The monk's uncomfortable stare, his hand reaching toward her, the fear she had felt—and Varkas's cold eyes.

If you've already been hurt once because you were playing with fire, shouldn't you have learned to be more careful by now?

Talia pulled the blanket over her head.

Varkas believed she had encouraged the monk's behaviour.

The thought hurt, but perhaps she couldn't entirely blame him for thinking that way. In the past, she had repeatedly caused trouble through her reckless actions, and Varkas had always been the one forced to rescue her.

He had only recently been freed from that responsibility, yet another incident had already occurred.

It was only natural that he would be tired of dealing with her.

He must really be sick of me now.

Talia buried her face in the pillow and let out a quiet, bitter laugh.

She suddenly remembered the day Varkas had walked out of her life. Perhaps the events of the previous night had brought those painful memories back.

Turning her head, she stared at the thin line of sunlight slipping through the curtains.

Then she slowly closed her eyes.

Memories she had tried so hard to forget began returning, one after another.
