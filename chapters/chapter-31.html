Chapter 31

Margus slowly moved his hand across Talia's waist, touching her through the soft fabric of her dress as though he had every right to do so. His unwanted familiarity made her increasingly uncomfortable, especially when she realised that he had no intention of moving away.

"You're wearing the same perfume as your mother," he murmured. "Lady Senevia's hair used to have this exact fragrance. It's a scent that's impossible to forget. Once you've grown attached to it, you find yourself wanting to experience it again and again, almost like an addiction."

As his hand began moving higher, Talia instinctively twisted her body to escape his embrace. However, Margus remained firmly in place, refusing to release her. He leaned closer and pressed an unwanted kiss near her ear, causing her entire body to stiffen with disgust.

For a moment, Talia was too shocked to react. She could only tremble as she tried to understand what was happening. Then, overwhelmed by fear, she suddenly began struggling with all her strength. Margus responded by forcing her onto the carpet and holding her down.

Talia kicked and pushed desperately, trying to free herself. During the struggle, the nearby table overturned, sending the castle puzzle they had spent hours building crashing to the floor. The carefully assembled towers broke apart, scattering pieces everywhere.

She scrambled across the carpet, hoping to reach the door. But before she could get away, Margus caught her ankle and pulled her back. Talia stared up at him with terrified eyes. Until that moment, she had never truly understood how powerless she could be against someone much stronger than herself. No matter how desperately she struggled, she could not escape his grasp.

"Stop struggling," Margus said impatiently. "Just stay quiet for a moment."

He seized both her wrists with one hand and held her face with the other. Talia tried to turn away, but he forced an unwanted kiss upon her, ignoring her resistance. A terrible feeling of nausea rose in her throat. She wanted to scream, yet her voice seemed trapped inside her chest. Tears filled her eyes as she struggled beneath him, unable to believe that someone she had trusted could treat her so cruelly.

Margus continued holding her down, seemingly lost in his own thoughts. "Ah… Lady Senevia. I've missed you so much."

Hearing her mother's name made Talia feel even more horrified. She finally understood that Margus had never truly seen her as herself. To him, she was merely someone who resembled the woman he had admired for years.

When his behaviour became even more threatening, Talia was overcome with fear and pain. She tried desperately to protect herself, but he ignored every attempt she made to push him away. "Please… stop…" she whispered.

Margus paid no attention to her plea. "There's no need to be so frightened," he said quietly. "It will only be uncomfortable for a little while. Soon, you'll understand that—"

He never finished speaking. Without warning, the weight holding Talia down suddenly disappeared. She immediately scrambled away, crawling towards the nearest corner. Curling into herself, she trembled uncontrollably, unable to understand what had just happened.

Several seconds passed before she finally raised her head. Varkas was standing near the wall, dressed in the formal uniform of the Roem Knights. One of his hands was gripping Margus by the head, forcing him against the hard surface.

Talia stared at the scene in disbelief. Margus's face was already covered in blood, and painful groans escaped his mouth. The man who had seemed so powerful only moments earlier was now struggling helplessly in Varkas's grasp.

"P-please! Let me go! We can talk about this!"

Varkas did not answer. He grabbed Margus by the hair and struck his head against the wall again. A heavy sound echoed through the study, followed by another cry of pain. Talia covered her mouth, barely managing to suppress a frightened scream.

Then Varkas spoke. "Talia." His voice was surprisingly quiet, and his attention remained fixed on the injured man. "Go back to your bedroom."

The calmness of his voice was almost frightening. Talia could hardly believe that the person speaking so evenly was the same man who had just attacked Margus with such force. She remained motionless, unable to understand that he was speaking to her.

Finally, Varkas turned his head. His blue eyes looked unusually dark, as though a heavy grey mist had settled over them. When their gazes met, Talia felt another wave of fear.

"Talia Roem Guirta," he said slowly, addressing her by her full name. "Didn't you hear what I said? I told you to return to your room."

Still, Talia could not move. She remained curled up in the corner, staring at him as though she had forgotten how to respond. Then Varkas suddenly raised his voice. "Go! Right now!"

The sharp command startled her so badly that she immediately scrambled to her feet. Without looking back, Talia fled the study and hurried down the corridor. When she finally reached her bedroom, she climbed onto the bed and buried herself beneath the thick blankets.

Only then did she begin noticing the dull pain throughout her body. She rubbed her skin desperately, trying to remove every reminder of Margus's unwanted touch. Even though he was no longer near her, the memory of what had happened remained painfully clear.

No matter how much she rubbed her arms, neck, and legs, she could not shake the horrible feeling. Her movements became increasingly frantic. Eventually, overwhelmed by frustration and distress, she began striking her own legs, as though she could somehow make the memories disappear.

Everything felt unbearable. Margus had betrayed her trust. He had frightened and hurt her. And worst of all, Varkas had witnessed her in such a helpless situation. The thought filled her with shame, even though she had done nothing to deserve what happened.

Talia buried her face in a pillow and cried uncontrollably. Her sobs grew louder until her throat began to ache, but she could not make herself stop. She had no idea how much time passed.

Eventually, she sensed someone standing near the doorway. Talia immediately lifted her head. Varkas stood at the entrance to her bedroom.

As always, he appeared calm and composed. His uniform was neatly arranged, and not a single strand of his pale hair seemed out of place. Looking at him now, it was difficult to imagine the violent confrontation that had taken place in the study.

"You won't have to see that man again," he said flatly.

Talia stared at him for a moment before finally finding her voice. "Did… did you kill him?"

Varkas frowned slightly at the question. After a brief silence, he answered. "No. He will be permanently exiled. For the rest of his life, he will be forbidden from entering the capital or any of the empire's major cities."

For several seconds, Talia could only stare at him. Then something inside her seemed to break. She grabbed the nearest pillow and threw it towards him.

"Why?! Why would you let him live?!" Her voice rose into a desperate scream. "You should have killed him! After everything he did to me, why would you spare him?! You should have made sure he could never hurt anyone again! Those disgusting hands that touched me… and the horrible things he did…!"

Her words broke apart as she began trembling violently. Suddenly, she clutched her throat, struggling to breathe. Her chest felt painfully tight, as though something were preventing her from drawing enough air into her lungs.

"If he's still alive, he'll come back!" she cried. "He'll find me again! He'll hurt me again! He said he couldn't stop himself. He said he would keep searching for that fragrance because he was addicted to it!"

A broken sob escaped her throat. Talia covered her face with both hands, gasping for breath as tears streamed down her cheeks. Her fear had become so overwhelming that she could no longer think clearly.

Then, overcome by anger and panic, she began throwing everything within her reach towards Varkas. "You're completely useless! I don't need someone like you protecting me!" A book flew across the room, followed by a doll. "Get out! I don't want to see you anymore! Didn't you hear me? Leave!"

Varkas remained standing near the doorway, silently enduring her outburst. His lack of response only made her more upset. "I said get out! Get out of my sight!"

Talia continued throwing objects without paying attention to what she was grabbing. Then, in the middle of her frenzy, she picked up a heavy metal candlestick and hurled it towards him. The moment it left her hand, she froze.

The candlestick struck Varkas near his forehead and temple before falling to the floor with a loud metallic sound. Talia stared at him in horror. A thin stream of dark red blood appeared near his temple and slowly ran down his pale cheek towards his neck.

Her mouth fell open, but she could not speak. A faint, broken sound escaped her throat as she struggled to understand what she had done. Varkas looked at her with cold, unreadable eyes. Then, without saying a word, he turned around.

An icy feeling spread through Talia's entire body. "Wait…" She immediately scrambled off the bed, desperate to stop him. But by the time she reached the doorway, Varkas had already disappeared into the corridor.

Talia stood staring into the darkness for several moments before her legs finally gave way. She collapsed onto the floor, feeling as though she wanted the ground to open beneath her.

Varkas had saved her. He had arrived when she was in danger and rescued her from Margus. Yet instead of thanking him, she had screamed at him, insulted him, and even injured him. She had directed all her fear and anger towards the very person who had protected her.

He must be disgusted with me now.

The thought made her chest ache. After everything she had done, how could he possibly want to remain by her side? She had given him every reason to become tired of her. Talia lowered her face against the carpet and began crying again, unable to bear the shame and regret overwhelming her.

After that day, Varkas stopped appearing before her. He no longer visited the Separate Palace, and Talia had no opportunity to speak with him. Days turned into weeks, and eventually several months passed without her seeing him.

The next time she laid eyes on him was during the formal ceremony appointing him Commander of the Imperial Knight Order. Talia watched from a distance as Varkas knelt on one knee before the Emperor and calmly recited his oath of loyalty.

Her father drew the imperial sword and placed it upon Varkas's shoulder. Then, in a solemn voice, he officially announced that Varkas would assume the position of Head of the Imperial Guard.

It was an enormous responsibility for someone who had not yet reached his twentieth birthday. Nevertheless, no one questioned whether he deserved the appointment. His abilities and achievements had already earned him the respect of those around him.

When the ceremony ended, Varkas slowly rose to his feet and turned away from the throne. Sunlight poured through the tall windows, falling across his pale hair and his composed face. His posture was straight, and his movements were calm and confident.

Talia watched him closely, trying to remember every detail of his appearance. She wanted to preserve this moment in her memory.

Varkas began walking through the gathered nobles, making his way towards the exit. As he passed near her, Talia caught a brief glimpse of his familiar profile. But he did not look at her. He continued walking with the same distant expression, as though she were merely another person in the crowd.

Talia stood motionless, watching his retreating figure. He was leaving her behind. The boy who had spent seven years by her side was now walking towards a future she could no longer follow.

Looking at his back, she whispered so softly that no one else could hear. Goodbye. But almost immediately, another thought rose from the deepest part of her heart. Please don't go.

Talia pressed her lips together, desperately trying to stop the tears gathering in her eyes. By the time she looked up again, Varkas had disappeared completely from view.

And just like that, the boy her mother had given her as a gift seven years earlier was gone from her side.
