Chapter 56

Lord Sierkhan had not even been scowling, so why had that brief glance felt so terribly unsettling?

Asros could not understand it. For some time, he remained seated in the pew, pretending to be perfectly at ease while his fingers idly toyed with one of the buttons upon his coat. Only after a considerable interval did he dare raise his head again.

By then, Lord Sierkhan had already moved towards the northern transept, where he stood speaking with the High Priest. Asros narrowed his eyes with curiosity. There was something distinctly unpleasant about the atmosphere between the two men.

The future duke stood with his back towards the congregation, concealing his expression from view. The High Priest, however, appeared considerably less composed. The veins upon the elderly man's withered neck stood out sharply, while his shoulders rose and fell with the force of his agitated speech.

Whatever grievance he was expressing, he seemed determined to make himself understood. And although Asros could not see Lord Sierkhan's face clearly, he caught a fleeting glimpse of cold displeasure in the man's expression when he turned slightly.

Well, this is interesting.

The two men were clearly not enjoying a friendly conversation. Asros's eyes brightened.

Whatever could they be discussing?

The priesthood was known to support the Crown Prince, and the future Grand Duke Sierkhan was likewise counted among his most influential allies. Why, then, should two men belonging to the same political faction be engaged in such an unpleasant exchange?

The mystery was far too tempting to ignore. Driven by curiosity, Asros quietly slipped from his seat.

He was just about to make his way behind one of the great stone pillars near the crossing, where he hoped to overhear their conversation, when a firm hand suddenly caught the collar of his coat.

Asros stopped short. He twisted his head upwards and found himself looking into Berens's stern face. His shoulders immediately drooped.

Berens regarded him gravely before speaking in a low, reproving voice.

"Your Highness, those gentlemen are your political adversaries. You must not approach them so carelessly."

Asros frowned.

"Political adversaries? What nonsense. I have no such people."

He pursed his lips in protest, but Berens showed no intention of releasing him. With a thoroughly displeased expression, Asros glanced once more towards the northern transept.

Unfortunately, the conversation had already come to an end. Lord Sierkhan turned away from the High Priest and began striding towards the crossing.

Asros immediately retreated behind Berens, concealing himself as best he could behind the man's legs. As the future duke passed, his cold gaze flickered briefly towards the young prince.

Then he continued through the nave with his usual measured, elegant stride. Asros watched his retreating figure from the safety of Berens's shadow.

After a moment, he leaned forward and whispered, "What do you suppose the High Priest was saying to Lord Sierkhan?"

Berens followed the direction of his gaze.

"I imagine he was demanding that Lord Sierkhan answer for the recent calamity."

Asros blinked.

"But why? Lord Sierkhan and the Cathedral are allies, are they not? Even if he made some dreadful mistake, ought they not protect him?"

A faint, rather bitter smile appeared in Berens's eyes.

"Alas, Your Highness, the affairs of this world are seldom so straightforward."

His voice remained gentle, though there was a quiet note of admonishment beneath it. Turning his gaze towards the altar, where the funeral rites were still proceeding, Berens continued.

"There are many within the priesthood who harbour a profound resentment towards the Khan. Among the more devout and uncompromising priests, in particular, hostility towards House Sierkhan has endured for generations."

Asros opened his mouth to ask why. Then he paused. Something from his history lessons suddenly came back to him.

Long ago, during the great war of unification led by Dorian Roem Guirta, the Khan had resisted the imperial armies until the very end. Even when other tribes had surrendered, they had continued to fight.

During the final battle in the north, their warriors had inflicted a mortal wound upon Wigru, the knight revered throughout the Empire as one chosen by God.

Although the eastern territories had eventually been brought beneath the rule of the Roem Empire, the Khan had never fully embraced the customs and beliefs of western society. Nor had the resentment of the imperial people towards them ever entirely disappeared.

Recalling these lessons, Asros gave a quiet snort.

"How foolish."

Berens looked down at him.

"The other tribes fought fiercely as well, did they not?" Asros continued. "Why should the Khan be despised merely because they were the last to surrender? That seems dreadfully petty."

A flicker of surprise crossed Berens's face. Then the corner of his mouth lifted slightly.

"It is not quite so simple as that, Your Highness."

He considered his words before continuing.

"The resentment is not born solely of old grievances. I believe it is more accurately described as fear."

"Fear?"

"Indeed. Fear of the extraordinary power once possessed by the Sierkhan bloodline."

Asros tilted his head.

"Extraordinary power? What sort of power?"

Berens was silent for a moment. Then he began to explain.

"According to the ancient records, there were once certain members of House Sierkhan who were born with remarkable gifts."

He lowered his voice slightly.

"Some were said to possess the ability to glimpse events that had yet to occur. Others could perceive the hidden thoughts within a person's heart. There were even those who could command wild beasts as easily as ordinary men command their servants."

Asros's eyes widened. Such abilities sounded as though they belonged to the pages of an old legend.

Berens continued.

"Those who possessed such gifts inspired considerable terror. To ordinary people, their powers seemed unnatural, almost beyond human comprehension."

Asros listened with growing fascination.

"Then does Lord Sierkhan possess one of these extraordinary abilities as well?"

"I should think not."

Berens answered without hesitation.

"The priests examined him most thoroughly and discovered nothing unusual."

He raised a hand to his chin, stroking it thoughtfully.

"It may be that the ancient blood has weakened over successive generations, until those extraordinary gifts have all but vanished."

Asros leaned closer.

"Have none of them appeared in recent years?"

"Not for a very long time. The last person known to possess such an ability was a powerful reader of minds, born some eighty years ago."

Berens paused.

"Since that time, no member of House Sierkhan has been recognised as a Primal Sorcerer."

Having delivered this explanation, he seemed satisfied with the matter. Yet a moment later, another recollection caused him to rub his chin again.

"Although, now that I think of it, there was once a rumour concerning the late Empress."

Asros looked up sharply.

"What rumour?"

"Some claimed that Her Majesty possessed the gift of foresight."

Asros stared at him.

"You mean my brother's mother?"

Berens hesitated. For a brief moment, he appeared to consider whether he ought to have mentioned the matter at all. Then he shook his head.

"I should not place much faith in such tales, Your Highness. It was most likely an invention of those who wished to elevate the late Empress into something resembling a divine being."

He offered a small, reassuring smile.

"After all, neither the Crown Prince nor the First Princess has displayed any extraordinary ability. They are perfectly ordinary in that respect."

He gave a thoughtful nod.

"Perhaps the ancient powers of the Khan have disappeared entirely."

Asros frowned. There was something about the relief in Berens's voice that displeased him.

Why should that be a comforting thought?

Why ought anyone to rejoice at the possibility that House Sierkhan had lost the remarkable gifts of its ancestors?

Asros had never wished to oppose his elder brother. He had no desire to struggle against the Crown Prince for power, nor did he regard House Sierkhan as an enemy merely because its future duke supported Gareth.

If I have no wish to fight my brother, why must his allies be considered my foes?

The thought troubled him. Yet he knew perfectly well what would happen if he voiced it. Berens would merely regard him with that patient expression and dismiss his objections as the foolish complaints of a child.

And so Asros kept his thoughts to himself.

"The ceremony appears to be drawing to a close," Berens observed.

He glanced towards the seats of honour, where several distinguished guests had begun rising to depart.

"We ought to return, Your Highness."

Placing a gentle hand upon the young prince's back, Berens guided him towards the exit. Asros offered no resistance. He was quite willing to leave.

After all, he had little desire to encounter his half-siblings, who seemed to regard his very existence as an inconvenience.

Together, they avoided the main entrance, where a great crowd of mourners had gathered, and made their way towards the garden behind the temple.

Unfortunately, the rear grounds were not entirely deserted. Several groups of nobles stood beneath the trees, conversing quietly amongst themselves.

Asros recognised a number of them at once. They were devoted supporters of Gareth. His expression darkened.

How tiresome.

Naturally, none of those gentlemen would dare lay a hand upon an imperial prince. Nevertheless, Asros had no desire to exchange pleasantries with men who looked upon him with such poorly concealed contempt.

He caught Berens by the hand and tugged him towards a narrow pathway shaded by overhanging branches.

They had scarcely taken a few steps when a familiar name reached his ears.

"What do you suppose are the chances that Talia Roem Guirta will recover?"

Asros stopped.

Another voice answered.

"Who can possibly say? From what I have heard, she was scarcely better than a corpse when they found her."

A brief silence followed.

"Even the elves would have considerable difficulty restoring someone in such a dreadful condition."

Asros's eyes widened. He turned sharply towards Berens.

"Is that true?"

Berens did not answer at once. A troubled expression crossed his face, as though he were weighing the wisdom of telling the young prince what he knew.

At last, he gave a slow nod.

Asros felt his heart sink. He had heard that his elder sister was unwell. But he had never imagined that her injuries were so grave that people were openly questioning whether she might ever recover.

His face grew pale with dismay.

"Why did no one tell me that my sister had been so terribly hurt?"

Berens looked down at him.

"Because it is not a matter with which Your Highness need concern yourself."

Asros stared at him in disbelief.

"Not concern myself?"

His voice rose sharply.

"She is my sister! Of course I ought to have been told!"

The words rang through the quiet garden. At once, the murmured conversations nearby fell silent.

It was as though someone had thrown a basin of cold water over the gathering. Asros turned his head.

Several nobles were staring at him in surprise. The moment they realised that the young prince had noticed them, they hurriedly lowered their heads in respectful bows.

Asros frowned. He had no wish to remain amongst such people for another moment.

Turning away, he strode out of the garden with quick, determined steps. Berens followed him, releasing a weary sigh.

"Your Highness."

Asros continued walking.

"That lady bears no affection for you," Berens said quietly. "Even if you were to express concern for her condition, I fear she would not welcome it."

Asros stopped so abruptly that Berens nearly walked into him. He turned and looked up at the older man with an angry expression.

Of course he knew. Talia Roem Guirta despised him. Had she not told him so herself?

He remembered her words all too clearly. And yet something within him refused to accept that her feelings could never change.

"Perhaps..." he began hesitantly.

Berens waited.

"Perhaps she regrets saying those things to me."

His voice grew softer.

"People sometimes say cruel things when they are having a dreadful day, do they not? They become angry and speak without thinking."

He searched Berens's face, hoping for agreement.

"Perhaps that was all it was."

Berens remained silent. Asros pressed on, encouraged by the possibility he had just imagined.

"And if I were to visit her, perhaps she would apologise."

He paused.

"Do you not think so?"

It was an impulsive hope, born almost entirely from his own longing. Yet the more he considered it, the more reasonable it began to seem.

Yes. Perhaps she never truly meant those words.

Perhaps she was merely angry that day.

And if he went to see her now, when she was suffering so terribly, surely she would understand that he had come because he cared for her.

Perhaps she would even be pleased. The thought filled him with sudden determination.

Without waiting for Berens to reply, Asros turned on his heel and set off towards the Separate Palace.

Along the way, he stopped beside the flowerbeds and began gathering the loveliest blossoms he could find. He chose them carefully, selecting the freshest and most beautiful flowers until he had collected a generous armful.

It seemed only proper to bring a gift when visiting someone who was ill. And surely his sister would appreciate such a thoughtful gesture.

She will be quite surprised to see me, I imagine.

A small smile began to form upon his lips.

Perhaps she will even think it rather pleasant to have such a considerate younger brother.

The possibility warmed his heart.

Holding the flowers carefully against his chest, Asros continued across the vast palace grounds with renewed enthusiasm. He did not pause to rest, nor did he allow himself to dwell upon Berens's warnings.

For the first time that day, he felt genuinely hopeful.

Before long, the splendid gardens gave way to a quieter part of the imperial grounds. Beyond the flourishing trees and brightly coloured flowerbeds, a grey building came into view.

Its austere walls and sombre appearance stood in striking contrast to the beauty of the surrounding gardens.

It was the Separate Palace.

And somewhere within that gloomy residence lay the sister whom Asros still desperately hoped might one day accept him.
