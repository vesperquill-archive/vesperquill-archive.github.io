Chapter 15

Ayla frowned at the unwelcome interruption of her private moment with Varkas.

Turning towards the approaching footsteps, she saw a knight of the Imperial Guard hurrying through the garden. It was Sir Rowen, who had been assigned to attend upon the Emperor while Varkas, as commander of the forthcoming journey, oversaw the final preparations.

The knight stopped before them and bowed respectfully. After offering Ayla a brief greeting, he turned towards his superior with an expression of urgency.

“His Majesty requests your immediate presence, Commander.”

Ayla felt her heart sink.

She had little doubt that Senevia was responsible for this sudden summons. The grave expression upon Sir Rowen's face only strengthened her suspicion that something unpleasant was about to occur.

She pressed her lips together.

The pilgrimage had already received the formal approval of the Imperial Council. Moreover, her marriage to Varkas was an arrangement long supported by the conservative nobles who stood firmly behind Crown Prince Gares.

Surely, even Senevia could not overturn matters which had already been settled with such care.

Yet Ayla could not rid herself of an ominous feeling.

For years, that woman had employed every means at her disposal to weaken the bond between Gares and Varkas. She had even succeeded in overcoming the fierce objections of the Grand Duke of Sierkhan, compelling Varkas to remain at Talia's side for seven long years, despite his having only recently received his knighthood.

What scheme has she devised this time?

Ayla feared that Senevia was once again attempting to influence the Emperor for her own purposes.

Though she knew it was improper to interfere in an official summons, she stepped forward before either man could depart.

“I shall accompany you.”

Sir Rowen looked startled.

“Your Highness, His Majesty requested only—”

“I am a Princess of the Imperial Family,” Ayla interrupted firmly. “Surely I require no special permission to seek an audience with my own father. I have decided to visit His Majesty, and I shall do so in the company of my fiancé.”

The knight hesitated, plainly uncertain how to respond.

At last, he turned towards Varkas, silently seeking his instructions.

Ayla felt a flicker of irritation. It was rather insulting that her wishes should appear to require another person's approval.

Yet she concealed her displeasure. The knights held Varkas in such extraordinary esteem that she had long since grown accustomed to their unquestioning obedience.

Varkas considered the matter only briefly before nodding.

“Her Highness may accompany us.”

Sir Rowen immediately bowed.

“As you command.”

He took his place beside Ayla, and the three departed from the garden.

Holding herself with all the dignity expected of an Imperial Princess, Ayla made her way towards the Main Palace.

The summer sunlight streamed through the towering windows of the Great Hall, illuminating the polished white marble floors. She crossed the magnificent entrance chamber and ascended the broad staircase, its steps covered with a rich carpet of deep crimson.

At the upper landing stood the great doors of the Emperor's audience chamber.

Their elaborate carvings depicted Darian, the first Emperor of Roem, surrounded by the loyal knights who had helped him establish the Empire.

Ayla passed the familiar figures without a second glance. She had seen those celebrated heroes so many times that their grandeur no longer stirred any particular admiration.

Beyond the doors lay an immense chamber adorned with richly patterned carpets and magnificent decorations. At its far end stood the golden Imperial Throne.

Ayla straightened her back as she approached.

Her father sat upon the throne with his customary solemn expression, while beside him stood the woman whom Ayla despised above all others.

Senevia.

The moment Ayla's eyes fell upon the Empress, her thoughts came to an abrupt halt.

Bathed in the golden light pouring through the windows, Senevia appeared almost impossibly beautiful.

Indeed, she seemed to grow lovelier with every passing year.

The sight filled Ayla with a helpless resentment bordering upon despair.

That golden devil…

Whenever she looked upon Senevia, Ayla was reminded of an old tale she had read during her childhood.

Once, in a distant village, there lived two brothers who loved one another dearly. Their affection was so sincere that nothing in the world seemed capable of dividing them.

But a wicked devil, who despised all things good and beautiful, resolved to destroy their happiness.

One day, the creature placed a great lump of gold upon the road the brothers travelled.

Dazzled by its brilliance, the brothers began to quarrel over who should possess it. Their disagreement grew into hatred, and in the end, they killed one another.

When Ayla had first beheld Senevia, she had thought that the devil's golden temptation had taken the form of a woman.

Her beauty had blinded the eyes and heart of a once-honourable Emperor. It had brought endless grief upon an innocent wife and condemned her children to years of sorrow and humiliation.

And as though such suffering were not enough, Senevia had eventually borne the Emperor a son, placing the succession itself in danger.

Ayla's gaze shifted towards the young boy standing beside the Empress.

Prince Asros Roem Guirta regarded the newcomers with bright green eyes, his expression filled with innocent curiosity.

Though still a child, there was a sharp intelligence in his gaze which never failed to trouble Ayla.

Each time she saw him, she was reminded of the danger he might one day pose to her brother.

She could scarcely understand why Gares directed so much of his anger towards Talia, whose very existence was little more than a source of scandal, while paying comparatively little attention to Asros, who stood next in the line of succession.

Certainly, Ayla had not forgotten the humiliation her mother had endured because of Talia's birth. The memory alone was enough to make her blood boil.

Yet Talia possessed neither influence nor any meaningful position within the Imperial Court.

Since entering the palace, the girl had done little beyond tormenting servants and causing one disgraceful disturbance after another.

Her behaviour was frequently condemned, and her reputation had become a subject of ridicule among the nobility.

How could such a foolish child possibly threaten the Crown Prince?

To Ayla, Talia was merely an unpleasant reminder of the past, a girl who inspired a mixture of dislike and reluctant pity.

That was all.

It was precisely because she regarded Talia as so insignificant that she had been able to overlook the girl's various acts of spite.

But Asros was another matter entirely.

Ayla knew that the young prince was well liked by many within the Imperial Palace. His gentle appearance and innocent manners had won him considerable affection.

More troubling still, she had heard whispers that certain nobles, dissatisfied with Gares's fierce and unpredictable temper, were beginning to place their hopes upon the Emperor's younger son.

Such thoughts filled her with unease.

“What are you standing there for? Have you forgotten your manners?”

The Emperor's deep voice interrupted her reflections.

Ayla looked up to find her father regarding her with a questioning expression.

Realising that she had neglected the customary greeting, she moved to stand beside Varkas and offered a graceful bow.

“I wished to see you, Father, and therefore took the liberty of accompanying Lord Sierkhan. I beg Your Majesty's forgiveness for presenting myself without an invitation.”

“Raise your head, my child. You need never seek permission to visit me.”

The voice of Emperor Virius Roem Guirta was unusually gentle.

Ayla knew that her father often treated her with particular kindness, perhaps because the sight of her reminded him of the wife whose trust he had betrayed.

His indulgence did little to soften her heart.

Suppressing a bitter smile, she bowed once more.

“Your Majesty is most gracious.”

“In truth, it is rather fortunate that Her Highness has come,” Senevia remarked. “She would have learned of the matter soon enough. We may as well explain everything while we are all assembled.”

The Empress spoke softly as she stroked the reddish-brown curls of the little prince beside her.

Asros looked from one adult to another with evident curiosity, appearing thoroughly fascinated by a conversation whose importance he could scarcely understand.

Ayla withdrew her gaze from the boy and turned towards Senevia.

“What matter requires such an explanation?”

“His Majesty shall inform you himself.”

Senevia answered with a gentle smile before resting her hand lightly upon the Emperor's.

Ayla forced herself to ignore the familiar gesture of affection.

An uncomfortable silence followed.

The Emperor appeared to be considering his words, and for several moments he said nothing.

At last, he turned towards Varkas.

“The reason I have summoned you concerns a change to the arrangements for the pilgrimage.”

Varkas bowed his head slightly.

“I am listening, Your Majesty.”

He displayed no surprise at the announcement.

The Emperor seemed faintly unsettled by his commander's complete composure, but after a brief hesitation, he continued.

“Talia shall accompany the party.”

For a moment, the words seemed to hang in the stillness of the audience chamber.

“See that additional guards are assigned to her protection,” the Emperor continued, “and make whatever arrangements are necessary for her journey.”

Ayla stood motionless.

Of all the commands she had feared her father might deliver, this was one she had never imagined.

What…?

Talia is coming with us?

She could scarcely believe what she had heard.
