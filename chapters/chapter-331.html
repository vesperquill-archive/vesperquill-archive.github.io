Chapter 331

The knight glared at her fiercely. His bearded cheeks twitched as though he were about to threaten her, but he soon turned toward the soldiers who had followed him.

“It couldn’t have gone far yet. Spread out and look for its tracks!”

Thalia instinctively grabbed the knight’s arm.

“No!”

Furious glares immediately shot toward her. Though Thalia shrank back under their hostility, she refused to let go of the knight’s arm.

“Leave Khan alone! He was only…!”

“Escort Her Highness the Grand Duchess back to the castle.”

Cutting her off, the knight issued an order to the soldiers standing beside him.

“His Excellency the Grand Duke will decide how this matter is to be dealt with.”

Then, in a voice that seemed barely able to contain his anger, he added,

“Keep her under strict guard and make sure she does not leave her quarters until further orders.”

Thalia hurriedly opened her mouth to protest. But before she could utter a single word, the soldiers approached from either side and seized her arms. Thalia instinctively struggled to shake off their hands…

But she soon gave up resisting and let her arms fall limp. She realized that the more she tried to defend Khan, the angrier they would become.

Thalia staggered along as they led her away, turning to look behind her. Through her tear-blurred vision, she could see the birch forest slowly disappearing into darkness.

For a long while, she couldn’t tear her eyes away from the eerie sight, as though the forest had swallowed Khan whole.

****

A cold, gloomy wind swept through the bare branches. Having wandered aimlessly through the forest with his head hanging low, he suddenly raised his head and looked around.

The pale tree trunks surrounding him on all sides resembled the iron bars of a prison. He gazed at the faintly visible spire of Raedgo Castle rising beyond the trees before turning away and walking once more.

Except for the occasional rustling of dry leaves swept along by the wind, everything around him was eerily silent. It felt as though he were wandering inside the grave of some enormous creature. As he trudged between the bare trees that resembled ancient bones, he suddenly realized he had been walking in circles and came to a stop.

He had resolved countless times to go far away, just as she wished. Yet whenever he came to his senses, he would find himself standing once again at the edge of the forest path, looking up at the castle.

He couldn’t even remember when he had returned to this place. At some point, he had begun experiencing gaps in his consciousness. While his mind remained clouded, as though under the influence of some powerful drug, his body moved entirely on animal instinct.

By the time he finally came to his senses, it was already too late. Everything had happened.

He trembled slightly as he recalled the taste of his younger brother’s blood, a memory he couldn’t seem to erase. He had told himself countless times that he would never allow the same tragedy to happen again, but it had all been in vain.

Perhaps even if he turned back time once more, the outcome would be no different. The moment he saw Lucas violating her, an uncontrollable urge to kill had clouded his vision in red. Even at the sight of his younger brother bleeding profusely from his torn neck, he hadn’t felt the slightest trace of guilt. All he feared were the consequences she would have to face because of his actions.

Recalling Thalia’s face as she screamed at him, he closed his eyes in anguish.

In the end, he had failed to protect her.

No. Far from protecting her, he had pushed her into an even deeper abyss.

He shuddered at the crushing despair that seemed to eat away at his insides, letting out a faint groan.

If I can’t change anything, then why did I come back? Did I turn back time only to stand by helplessly and watch her endure the same hell she had already suffered?

He looked toward Raedgo Castle once more, his eyes filled with helplessness. By now, Thalia was probably confined to her bedroom, enduring all manner of humiliating treatment. She might even be trembling in fear, worried about him.

Ice-cold tears streamed down his face. He had never known he was capable of shedding so many tears. Suppressing the feeling that a piece of his heart was being torn away, he forced himself to keep walking.

Before long, he discovered a spring nestled between the rocks, its surface beginning to freeze over. He approached it to quench his thirst when he heard the faint sound of hoofbeats in the distance.

His ears immediately perked up. Though they were still far away, dozens of mounted soldiers were moving swiftly.

He could clearly tell that dozens of mounted soldiers were moving swiftly, despite the distance. Feeling the blood in his veins turn cold, he hurried across the forest until he reached the top of a slope overlooking the open fields.

Even from such a great distance, he could clearly recognize Varkas Raedgo Sierkhan leading the cavalry toward the castle gates. As he recalled what would happen next, his stomach twisted painfully, and a cold sweat broke out all over his body.

Though he knew it was a futile struggle, he turned around and began running through the forest. He didn’t even know where he was going. All he could think about was getting away from Kalmor. He ran and ran until he was completely out of breath.

Suddenly, his surroundings grew dark, and a thick darkness settled over everything.

He looked around in confusion.

When on earth had the sun gone down?

Perhaps he had lost consciousness again, because he couldn’t remember a thing.

Looking around anxiously, he raised his head toward the sky, hoping to determine exactly where he was. Just then, he heard the sound of people talking somewhere nearby.

He instinctively turned his head and froze when he caught sight of flickering lights beyond the pitch-black darkness.

That man had brought the pursuers with him.

He immediately turned around and began making his way down the steep slope. However, the search party had already spread out across a large portion of the surrounding area.

Watching the dozens of torches flickering between the densely packed trees, he moved cautiously in a direction where he couldn’t sense anyone’s presence, keeping his footsteps as quiet as possible.

It was then that he sensed the presence of that man.

Every muscle in his body tensed as he stared into the darkness. A familiar scent drifted toward him through the chilly night air.

Realizing that the other man had sensed his presence as well, he immediately broke into a full sprint.

At that moment, an arrow whistled through the air and lodged deep in his hind leg. A sharp cry escaped him. The excruciating pain, as though the arrow had pierced straight through his bone, caused him to lose his balance, and his vision tilted sharply.

He barely managed to steady himself before continuing to run. Every time he put weight on his injured leg, the pain nearly made him lose consciousness, but he couldn’t afford to slow down.

Desperate to escape the man relentlessly pursuing him, he veered toward a densely wooded area. Yet the man stayed close behind, following him as though performing some kind of acrobatic feat.

Several more arrows came flying at him. Sharp, stabbing pain tore through his back and torso, but he only ran faster.

As he did, the arrows embedded in his flesh…

He could feel the metal arrowheads shifting inside his flesh. His breathing gradually became uneven, and bloody foam began to gather around his mouth. Just as the thought crossed his mind that one of his lungs might have been injured, the strength drained from his tense muscles, and his vision went black.

He finally collapsed to the ground.

For a while, he couldn’t hear anything. With his cheek pressed against the cold earth, he struggled to steady his breathing. Little by little, sounds began to filter back into his muffled ears.

Desperately moving his front paws, he dragged himself across the uneven ground. He could no longer even remember what he was so desperately trying to escape from.

All he knew was that he had to get away.

But cruel fate had inevitably caught up with him.

He stared blankly at the leather boots that had come to a stop right in front of his nose, then slowly lifted his gaze.

The man stood silently looking down at him, a torch in one hand. Snowflakes swirled wildly around the man’s silver hair and pale face, there he was Varkas Raedgo Sierkhan.
