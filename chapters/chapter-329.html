Chapter 329

In the end, rather than face Lucas head-on, Thalia changed her approach. She would simply be gone before he came looking for her.

Each morning, before the sun had fully risen, she took Khan out to the back garden and wandered the forest trails for hours. In the afternoons, she retreated to Varkas’s study or the library, and some days she even hid away in the maids’ workroom. If he came looking for her and found nothing a few times, she thought, Lucas would grow tired of it and give up on his own.

But her plan backfired completely. As if her blatant attempts to avoid him had only spurred him on, he began following her around even more persistently. He didn’t seem to care in the least what the servants might think when they saw him like that.

He asked the maids openly where she was, and he even went around the castle searching for her himself. After this had gone on for several days, Thalia finally lost her patience. She called him aside and gave him a firm warning.

“From now on, don’t come looking for me unless you have a specific reason.”

Lucas, whose eyes had sparkled with delight that she had come to him first, now wore a clouded expression. Thalia ignored it and made herself perfectly clear.

“And stop bringing me gifts. You don’t need to come along when I take Khan out, either.”

With that, she turned to leave, but Lucas hurried to block her way.

“Why do you hate me so much?”

He asked with a hurt look on his face.

“I know very well that I was rude to you when you first came to Kalmor. But ever since then...”

“I don’t particularly hate you.”

Lucas faltered and closed his mouth. His face brightened a little, as if her unexpected words had given him hope. As if to crush that hope, Thalia added bluntly,

“To be more precise, I don’t feel anything toward you at all. You’re just a nuisance and a pain.”

The color drained from his face in an instant. For a moment, a faint pity stirred in a corner of her heart, but she quickly shook it off.

Half-hearted sympathy would only give him false hope.

She looked him straight in the eye and spelled it out, word by word.

“So I’d like you to stop hanging around me. Unless you mean to have people pointing fingers at me for carrying on with my brother-in-law, on top of being suspected of heresy.”

His cheeks, which had gone so pale they looked almost ashen, now flushed a deep crimson. Thalia swallowed a hollow laugh at how obviously flustered he was. Had he really not realized how obvious he had been all this time?

His face red all the way to his ears, Lucas stammered for a moment before hurriedly blurting out an excuse.

“I just couldn’t bear seeing you so isolated and lonely here in the castle Sister-in-law... So I hoped I could be of some help to you, even a little...”

“...I only wanted to be of some help to you. If I treated you with special regard, others wouldn’t dare treat you carelessly.”

Thalia’s mouth twisted as if she found that absurd.

Lucas seemed to realize she didn’t believe a word he said, and he raised his voice.

“You were falsely accused, weren’t you? Even if I can’t clear away every suspicion against you right now, I at least wanted to stop people from openly disrespecting you. So...”

“I wasn’t falsely accused.”

Thalia cut him short coldly.

Even if everything Lucas said was true, it changed nothing. The reason this man cared about her situation wasn’t just pity or sympathy...

She wasn’t so dense that she couldn’t tell this man cared about her situation for more than just pity or sympathy.

Looking up at him with mocking eyes, Thalia went on in a scathing tone.

“Whatever the reason, it’s a fact that I drugged Varkas’s wine. So there’s no need to treat me like some innocent victim and try to protect me.”

Lucas’s face twisted in anguish. Thalia paid it no mind and lashed out even more harshly.

“I don’t want your sympathy or your protection. So stop sticking your nose where it doesn’t belong.”

“It’s not because of sympathy. I just...!”

Lucas, who had been about to shout something, suddenly shut his mouth. She could see the veins standing out on his fist, clenched so tightly that his knuckles had gone white. The words that had risen all the way to his throat.

He looked as though he were forcing down the words that had risen all the way to his throat. Thalia made herself look away and said stiffly,

“I’ve said all I had to say, so I’ll be going now.”

With that, she turned sharply and crossed the quiet back garden. She could feel his gaze clinging stubbornly to her back, but Thalia never once looked back.

After that day, Lucas stopped coming to see her. When they happened to run into each other in the corridor, he only greeted her politely. He no longer cheerfully tossed out jokes or bothered her with idle chatter the way he used to.

But in the eyes that watched her, a longing even deeper than before was seething.

Thalia knew all too well what that look meant. When she had been going half-mad, unable to let go of her love for her sister’s fiancé, she must have worn the very same look. Maybe that was why. Lucas’s feelings felt like a burden and made her uncomfortable, yet at the same time, she felt a little sorry for him.

Sitting at her desk, Thalia had been staring blankly down at her diary. Slowly, she turned her head and gazed out the window. The fading sunlight stretched long across the room from the window frame. She quietly watched the sky turning red, then closed her diary and rose from her seat.

There was still a little time left before sunset. Rather than sit in her room lost in useless thoughts, she figured she’d be better off taking Khan for a walk.

“Khan, let’s go outside for a bit.”

Khan, who had been lying at the foot of the bed, sprang up as if he’d been waiting for this. He had been cooped up inside the castle for weeks and must have felt quite restless, because his tail, which had been drooping low, gave a few small wags.

The sight made her glad. He had seemed so listless lately that she had been quietly worried. She slowly stroked Khan’s back, which had grown visibly thinner in just a few days, then picked up her coat and left the room.

The cold air of late autumn hung over the rear garden. Most of the trees had already shed their leaves, and even the flower beds the gardener tended had faded to a pale brown.

Thalia let Khan roam freely as he pleased and strolled leisurely along the walking path behind the castle.

Just then, a figure sitting on a marble bench caught her eye. Thalia’s gaze idly swept over a man sprawled lazily with his long legs stretched out, and she suddenly stopped in her tracks. It was Lucas, his head tilted back and his eyes gently closed.

Thalia quietly studied his shadowed face, which looked somehow weary, then silently turned away. But before she had taken even a few steps, a low voice behind her stopped her in her tracks.

"Are you not even going to acknowledge me anymore?"

Thalia glanced back. Lucas had already gotten to his feet and was striding toward her. She took a step back and answered in a stiff voice.

“I just didn’t want to disturb your rest.”

“Liar.”

Lucas leaned his face in close to hers and blurted out,

“You’re avoiding me because you don’t want to see me.”

Thalia’s face stiffened at the strong smell of wine coming off him. Only then did she notice that his voice was looser and lazier than usual. She pushed him away coldly.

"Go throw your drunken tantrum somewhere else."

She tried to walk past him, but of all things, her foot caught on a tree root. Thalia instinctively flailed her arms. Just as she was about to fall flat on the ground, a strong, muscular arm wrapped around her waist. Thalia went rigid. A broad, firm chest and strong, calloused hands. His masculine scent, and the heavy smell of alcohol soaked into his clothes... All of it dredged up an old nightmare. Thalia pushed him away with all her strength.

“Let go of me!”

But his arm only tightened around her. It seemed he only meant to keep her from sprawling onto the ground, but Thalia was in no state to think about that. On instinct, she raised her arm and struck him hard across the cheek. With a sharp crack like the snap of a whip, his face turned to one side. Thalia froze in fear. For an instant, something like sparks flashed in the man’s eyes. He roughly wiped away the blood seeping from the split corner of his mouth with the back of his hand, then he pinned her against a tree trunk and growled,

“What on earth did I do that was so wrong that you can’t stand me this much?”
