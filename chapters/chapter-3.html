THE FORGOTTEN FIELD · CHAPTER 03

Chapter 3

The imperial ballroom was a theatre in which every noble face played its appointed part. Beneath the lustre of chandeliers and the measured courtesies of the court, Talia found herself studying the empty places where her half-siblings ought to have stood. Gareth was ordinarily the centre about which the gathering revolved; Aila, faultless in bearing, was seldom far from his side.

Tonight, however, their absence offered Talia an interval of uneasy liberty. The memory of Aila’s recent humiliation returned with a bitterness that was almost pleasurable. It ought to have been enough to see the favoured princess brought low before the very people who adored her. Yet Talia knew, with a chill of self-disgust, that her resentment had demanded something darker.

Such thoughts were interrupted by a gentleman in green, a familiar ornament of her mother’s entertainments. Count Serian greeted her with the extravagant attentions of a man well practised in the language of admiration. His courtesy was impeccable; his nearness was not. Talia endured the kiss bestowed upon her hand while privately wishing herself a hundred paces away.

The Count admitted, with a candour disguised as gallantry, that the Empress had charged him with ensuring her daughter’s enjoyment of the evening. He also observed that the other gentlemen lacked the courage to approach her, fearing the Crown Prince’s displeasure. He, having forfeited Gareth’s favour long ago, had rather less to lose.

It was a peculiar species of kindness, Talia reflected, to be offered companionship as though one were a difficult duty assigned by the sovereign. Nevertheless, she accepted his arm. An escort, however unwelcome, might serve as a defence against the eyes that followed her everywhere.

When Count Serian invited her to dance, she hesitated. She had no wish to place herself in the keeping of a near stranger, still less to reward the court with another spectacle. But to withdraw would be to concede that its whispers possessed authority over her.

They would have her vanish, if they could; and if not, they would have her remain in some forgotten corner, silent enough to be mistaken for a shadow.

No. She would not grant them the satisfaction.

She took his hand. The Count proved a dancer of rare accomplishment, guiding her through the figures with such assurance that even her guarded spirit could not wholly resist the pleasure of movement. For a brief and dangerous moment, Talia felt the room alter around her. The same guests who had regarded her with contempt now watched in fascination.

She understood, then, a fragment of her mother’s power: beauty could compel attention, and attention could masquerade as triumph. Yet it was a brittle victory. The music ceased, the company stirred, and Gareth and Aila entered at last. Their effortless splendour restored the old order of things in a single instant.

Talia saw in their welcome everything that had been withheld from her. They possessed the ease of those whose birth had never been questioned, whose dignity seemed to require neither proof nor defence. Against them stood the history of Senevia and the scandal that the court had fastened to her daughter’s very name. The contrast wounded Talia more keenly because she could not pretend it was imaginary.

Do not set me beside them. Do not weigh my worth against theirs.

Count Serian, perceiving her agitation, suggested that she offer her greetings first. His amusement was scarcely concealed. He knew, as she did, that her appearance at the banquet had not been an innocent attempt at family affection. She had come to disturb the serenity of those who could so easily pretend she did not exist.

With the wineglass held tightly in her hand, Talia gathered what remained of her courage and turned towards her half-siblings. One deliberate step would begin the encounter. She had scarcely taken it when a new arrival drew her attention to the entrance.

Barkas Raedgo Siyeokan had appeared.

At once the purpose that had sustained her seemed to desert her. The ballroom, its glittering company, even the quarrel she had rehearsed in her mind fell strangely distant. Her heart struck against her ribs with the startled violence of a creature discovered in hiding.
