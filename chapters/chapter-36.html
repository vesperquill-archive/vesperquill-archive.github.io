Chapter 36

Talia stood in the carriage doorway, her blue eyes blazing with anger. Her slender shoulders trembled as she glared at Edric, who instinctively stepped back under the force of her furious expression.

"How many times must I repeat myself before you finally understand?" she demanded. "I've already told you to stay away from me. Do you find my orders so amusing that you think you can simply ignore them?"

Edric tightened his hold on the basket, looking thoroughly offended. "Your Highness, I only came because I was concerned about your health. You've hardly eaten anything today, and I was afraid you might fall ill."

Talia gave a short, bitter laugh. "And since when did someone of your standing have the right to concern himself with my affairs?"

"I'm your appointed escort knight," Edric replied, struggling to maintain his composure. "Ensuring your safety and well-being is part of my duty."

His answer only made her laugh harder. Edric felt heat rush to his face. He had never been ridiculed so openly before, especially when he had done nothing to deserve it.

"Do you truly believe I'm foolish enough to accept that excuse?" Talia asked, her smile turning cold. "The Roem Knights have always been devoted to the Crown Prince. You're nothing more than his obedient servants, ready to carry out whatever he commands."

Edric's expression hardened, but before he could defend himself, her gaze fell upon the basket he was carrying.

"And what makes you think I would eat something you brought me? For all I know, there's something harmful hidden in that food. Perhaps this is simply another way for your precious master to get rid of me."

"That's enough, Your Highness!" Edric exclaimed, unable to contain his indignation. "I am a knight who has sworn an oath before God. My duty is to protect the imperial family, not to bring harm upon them. By accusing me of such a thing, you're questioning the honour of every knight who serves the Empire."

Talia regarded him without the slightest change in expression. "And you expect an oath to convince me? I've heard enough empty promises to know better than that."

Edric stared at her, momentarily unable to find an answer. There was something deeply unsettling about the certainty in her voice. She did not sound like someone making an accusation merely to provoke him. She sounded as though she genuinely believed every word.

"If you wish to prove how loyal and useful you are, then go and attend to my brother or sister," she continued coldly. "I'm sure they'll be delighted by your devotion. I have no interest in receiving it."

Before Edric could respond, Talia slammed the door in his face. The sudden noise left him standing motionless, his fingers gripping the basket so tightly that the handle nearly bent.

For a moment, he considered opening the door again and demanding an explanation for her outrageous behaviour. However, he quickly realised that arguing with her would accomplish nothing. He had offered his assistance, only to be treated like a criminal.

Enough. I've done more than anyone could reasonably expect.

With that thought, Edric turned away and marched towards the communal barracks. He placed the untouched basket rather roughly on a shelf near the entrance before heading to the area where the other knights were having dinner.

Several men were already gathered around the campfires, enjoying their meals after a long day of travelling. Edric joined them, filled his plate generously, and began eating with unusual determination.

Let her starve if that's what she wants. It's none of my concern anymore.

He repeated the thought several times, although it did little to improve his mood.

✦ ✦ ✦

Long after the campsite had fallen quiet, Talia remained awake inside her carriage. She had spent hours turning restlessly beneath her blanket, but sleep refused to come. Eventually, she pushed herself upright and carefully drew back the curtain.

Outside, a handful of lanterns glimmered in the distance where the night guards stood watch. Beyond their faint light, the plains were swallowed by darkness. There was not even a moon to illuminate the sky.

Talia waited until she was certain no one was nearby before opening the carriage door and stepping onto the ground. Her legs felt unsteady beneath her, weakened by several days of eating little more than bread and fruit preserved in honey.

As she steadied herself against the carriage, she remembered the basket Edric had brought earlier.

Perhaps I should have accepted the food after all.

The knight had looked genuinely furious when she accused him of plotting against her. His expressions were so easy to read that she doubted he possessed the cunning required to carry out a secret scheme.

For a moment, she wondered whether she had misjudged him. Then she immediately dismissed the possibility.

No. I've made that mistake before.

She knew better than anyone how dangerous a seemingly harmless person could be. Those who approached with friendly smiles and gentle words were often the ones who caused the greatest suffering. She would not allow herself to be deceived again simply because a young knight appeared sincere.

After checking the positions of the guards, Talia slipped between the tents. Her eyes slowly adjusted to the darkness, allowing her to distinguish the outlines of supply wagons, resting horses, and rows of temporary shelters.

The night air was cool, and a breeze from the hills moved through the folds of her gown. Around her lingered the smell of damp grass and dying campfires. She walked carefully over the uneven ground, listening for footsteps while making her way towards the attendants' quarters.

When she finally reached the correct tent, she looked around to make sure she had not been followed. A baggage wagon stood nearby, its bed filled with wooden crates. Talia climbed onto it and settled into a narrow space between the boxes, where she could watch the tent entrance without being seen.

She had come to observe the woman her mother had secretly placed among her attendants. The memory of the suspicious meeting at the monastery remained fresh in her mind, especially the small container the priest had handed over. If the woman intended to carry out another secret task, Talia wanted to discover where she would go.

Drawing her knees against her chest, she fixed her attention on the entrance and waited.

The campsite was far from silent. Soldiers snored inside their tents, horses occasionally snorted or shifted their weight, and insects chirped endlessly in the surrounding grass. Every unfamiliar sound made Talia tense, forcing her to listen carefully before convincing herself that nothing was wrong.

Time passed with painful slowness. She tried counting the seconds to distract herself, but the effort only made her more conscious of the cold and the growing stiffness in her limbs.

Still, she refused to leave her hiding place.

Eventually, the black sky began to soften into a deep shade of blue. Dawn was approaching, and Talia was beginning to believe that her long wait had been pointless.

Perhaps she has no intention of doing anything tonight.

She cautiously stretched her cramped legs, wincing when her joints cracked. Her muscles ached from remaining in the same position for so many hours, and she was massaging her numb calves when a movement near the tent caught her attention.

A slender figure had emerged from the entrance.

Talia immediately became still. Although the woman's features were hidden by the darkness, her build was familiar enough for Talia to recognise her.

It's her.

Ignoring the discomfort in her legs, Talia climbed down from the wagon and began following the attendant. She kept several paces between them, using the parked carriages and tents to conceal her movements.

The woman walked quietly through the camp, occasionally glancing around as though checking whether anyone was watching her. Talia followed with growing anticipation until they approached the carriage belonging to Ayla.

At the sight of it, her heart began to race.

Please, let that be where you're going.

She watched the attendant with desperate hope. If her mother's agent intended to harm Ayla, then perhaps Talia would no longer have to act herself. Someone else might accomplish what she had been unable to do.

But the woman continued walking.

She passed Ayla's carriage without slowing down and headed towards the far side of the campsite. Talia's hopeful expression faded as she tried to understand where the attendant was going.

Then she noticed a familiar banner fluttering above one of the larger tents. Embroidered upon it was the black horse emblem of the Sierkhan family.

Talia stopped abruptly.

Why would she be going there?

A terrible suspicion began to form in her mind. She hurried after the attendant, but in the brief moment she had looked towards the banner, the woman disappeared among the tents.

Talia searched the surrounding area, her gaze moving frantically from one entrance to another. There was no sign of the attendant anywhere.

Slowly, her eyes returned to the Sierkhan tent.

Could she have gone inside?

A cold feeling spread through her chest as she considered what that might mean.

Varkas's marriage to Ayla would greatly strengthen the Crown Prince's political position. The Sierkhan family held enormous influence throughout the eastern and northern territories, and an alliance with them would place the powerful Union of Nobles firmly behind Gares.

Once Ayla became the Grand Duchess of Sierkhan, opposing the Crown Prince would become even more difficult for the Empress.

What if Mother intends to prevent that marriage by getting rid of Varkas?

The possibility filled Talia with sudden terror. Senevia had never been someone who hesitated to remove obstacles standing in the way of her ambitions. If she had decided that Varkas was a threat, there was no telling what she might do.

Talia remembered the small container the attendant had received at the monastery. If it contained poison, then the danger might be far greater than she had imagined.

Her heart pounded as she rushed towards the tent. She no longer cared whether anyone saw her or questioned why she was entering the quarters of a man.

Pushing aside the entrance flap, she hurried inside.

The interior was dim, with only enough light to reveal the outlines of the furniture. Talia quickly searched the room before noticing a curtain dividing the sleeping area from the rest of the tent.

She pulled it aside.

The bed was empty.

For a moment, she stood motionless, struggling to control her breathing. Varkas was known for beginning his duties before sunrise, so it was entirely possible that he had already gone to inspect the campsite or check on Torque.

Yet the explanation did little to ease her fear.

What if something has already happened to him?

She approached the bed and began examining the sheets, searching for any sign of blood or a struggle. Her fingers moved hurriedly over the bedding, and she was so absorbed in her search that she failed to notice someone approaching.

A heavy footstep suddenly sounded behind her.

Talia spun around.

A tall man stood at the entrance, his broad shoulders outlined against the faint morning light. For one brief instant, relief rushed through her.

Varkas!

She hurried towards him, only to stop when she saw his face clearly.

It was not Varkas.

Gares stood before her, dressed in light clothing, his expression filled with surprise. His gaze travelled over her dishevelled appearance before returning to her face, and his eyebrows drew together in suspicion.

"What exactly are you doing in here, Talia?"
