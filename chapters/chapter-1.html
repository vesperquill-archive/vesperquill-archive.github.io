THE FORGOTTEN FIELD · CHAPTER 01

A Serpent in the Darkness

A reading companion by ChaChiLu

⚠ Chapter 1 spoilers. This is an original, fan-written retelling of the chapter's events in summary form, with commentary—not the complete novel text or an official translation.

An Uneasy Meeting

Night has settled over the imperial gardens. Beside a still pond, Crown Prince Gareth waits for news of the investigation into Princess Ayla's sudden illness at a banquet. The man who comes to meet him is Varkas Raedgo Shierkhan, a knight destined to inherit the powerful eastern Grand Duchy.

Varkas's arrival is as unsettling as his reputation. His pale appearance and immaculate uniform suggest the perfection of an aristocratic portrait, yet Gareth finds little comfort in his presence. There is a coldness about the future Grand Duke that even the Crown Prince cannot entirely dismiss.

Gareth is impatient for answers. He expects the inquiry to confirm his suspicions against the Empress. Instead, Varkas reports that the substance found in Ayla's wine came from an ordinary medicinal preparation, not from the Empress's household.

✧ ♡ ✧

The Accusation

According to the investigation, the medicine was obtained through a maid serving in the main palace. In excessive quantities, it could cause painful stomach symptoms, but it was not treated as a dangerous poison. More troubling to Gareth is Varkas's conclusion about who arranged for it to be placed in the wine: the Second Princess, Thalia.

Gareth's response is immediate and vicious. He has no affection for Thalia, whom he regards as an embarrassment to the imperial family and a rival to his beloved sister. In his eyes, jealousy offers a simple explanation for the incident—particularly now that Varkas is expected to marry Ayla.

Yet Varkas refuses to indulge the Crown Prince's desire for a dramatic punishment. The available evidence is circumstantial, he explains, and a maid's testimony alone would hardly be enough to secure a conviction against a princess. Even if responsibility were proved, the comparatively limited harm caused by the medicine would make a severe sentence difficult to justify.

✧ ♡ ✧

A Dangerous Question

Gareth grows furious at what he perceives as indifference to Ayla's suffering. He even seizes Varkas by the collar. But the knight does not answer anger with anger. His quiet rebuke is enough to make the prince retreat, and he reminds Gareth that the future emperor ought not to surrender his dignity over every petty act of malice.

Varkas has already arranged care for Ayla and precautions against any further tampering with her food or drink. As far as he is concerned, his role in the matter is finished.

That refusal troubles Gareth. He begins to wonder whether Varkas's reluctance to pursue Thalia conceals some private loyalty. When he asks whether Varkas is protecting her, the atmosphere changes.

For a fleeting moment, the mask of indifference slips. The suggestion draws from Varkas a sharp, bitter reaction unlike anything Gareth is accustomed to seeing. He makes it plain that he wants no further connection with Thalia.

✧ ♡ ✧

What Gareth Believes

Gareth watches Varkas depart through the darkened arcade and draws his own reassuring conclusion. If the future Grand Duke truly despises Thalia, then his loyalty to Gareth and Ayla should be all the more secure.

But the chapter leaves something unresolved. Gareth sees an ally's hatred and believes he understands it. Readers, however, have only witnessed the surface of Varkas's reaction. Whether Gareth has interpreted that emotion correctly remains an open question.

ChaChiLu's Reading Notes 🌸

What strikes me most about this opening is the contrast between Gareth's explosive temper and Varkas's carefully controlled responses. Gareth assumes he can read Varkas's heart simply because they appear to share an enemy. Yet the chapter never gives us Varkas's private thoughts.

And that is what makes his final reaction so intriguing. Why does the very suggestion of protecting Thalia provoke such intensity in a man who otherwise seems incapable of showing emotion? Chapter 1 plants that question without answering it—and it is a powerful way to begin the story.
