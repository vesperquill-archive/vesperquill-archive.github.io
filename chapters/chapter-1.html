THE FORGOTTEN FIELD · CHAPTER 01

A Serpent in the Darkness

Fan-made literary adaptation · The Vesperquill Archive

The measured cadence of approaching footsteps disturbed the stillness of the night.

Seated upon a stone bench overlooking the garden pond, Crown Prince Gareth turned his gaze towards the shadowed corridor from whence the sound had arisen.

Beneath the dim illumination of the arcade, a solitary figure emerged.

Varkas Raedgo Shierkhan.

Clad in the immaculate uniform of the Imperial Knights, he advanced through the long colonnade adjoining the gardens with an unhurried, almost spectral grace. There was something profoundly disquieting in the manner of his approach, something that brought to mind a pale serpent gliding soundlessly beneath the surface of dark, unfathomable waters.

Gareth had often found himself contemplating the peculiar nature of this man.

His hair was of the palest flaxen gold, as though the colour had been washed away by countless winters. His eyes, a cold blue touched with silver, possessed the distant brilliance of moonlight upon frost. Even his complexion was extraordinarily fair, resembling finely sculpted alabaster.

Every feature seemed fashioned from the most subdued and colourless shades nature could bestow.

And yet, how was it that a man composed of such delicate hues could inspire so ominous an impression?

Gareth could never quite explain it.

Whenever he found himself in Varkas's presence, an inexplicable chill would steal along his spine.

"Your Imperial Highness, the Crown Prince."

Upon reaching him, Varkas placed a hand against his breast and inclined his head in a formal bow.

Gareth dismissed the courtesy with an impatient gesture.

"Spare me these tiresome formalities."

Though Varkas presently served among the Imperial Guard, the day was fast approaching when he would inherit the title of Grand Duke Shierkhan and assume dominion over the eastern territories.

He was, by no means, a man whom even the Crown Prince might treat with careless disregard.

Nevertheless, Gareth merely lifted his chin towards the vacant place beside him, addressing the future Grand Duke with the imperious familiarity one might reserve for an attendant.

"How much longer do you intend to oblige me to crane my neck? Sit down."

If the discourtesy offended Varkas, not the slightest indication of it appeared upon his countenance.

Neither wounded in his pride nor eager to display obedience, he simply lowered himself upon the marble bench.

His expression remained as impassive as ever.

Gareth studied the exquisitely chiselled features of the man beside him. There was an almost unnatural perfection to that face, as though some master sculptor had fashioned it from marble and neglected to bestow upon it the warmth of human feeling.

To an ordinary observer, nothing in Varkas's manner would have appeared amiss.

Yet Gareth had known him far too long to be deceived by appearances.

Something had displeased him.

Had the investigation failed to proceed as intended?

The Crown Prince narrowed his eyes.

"Well? What became of the interrogation? Surely you do not mean to tell me that you failed to discover who was responsible?"

"We have ascertained all that could reasonably be discovered."

Varkas adjusted the tightly fastened front of his uniform with one hand before continuing in a voice utterly devoid of inflection.

"Nevertheless, I fear the findings will not be to Your Highness's satisfaction."

One of Gareth's brows rose.

"Explain yourself."

"The substance discovered in Her Highness the Princess's wine did not originate from the Empress's Palace."

"Then whence did it come?"

"It was a medicinal preparation prescribed by an imperial apothecary to a maid employed within the main palace. Strictly speaking, it is not classified as a poison. When consumed in excessive quantities, it may induce vomiting and severe abdominal discomfort. For that reason, its distribution is not subject to particularly stringent restrictions."

For a moment, Gareth could only stare at him.

Then a short, incredulous laugh escaped his lips.

"Am I to understand, then, that some witless maid in the main palace suddenly took leave of her senses and decided to pour medicine into my sister's wine?"

Though his laughter suggested amusement, the rigid cords standing out upon his thick neck betrayed the violence of his temper.

Varkas regarded him with the same dry, untroubled gaze.

"The maid did not administer the substance herself."

"Then who did?"

Gareth's patience finally deserted him.

"Enough of these tedious explanations! If the Empress was not responsible, and neither was it some servant's blunder, then tell me plainly—who possessed the insolence to tamper with my sister's wine?"

"The Second Princess."

The answer was delivered with such perfect composure that, for an instant, Gareth failed to comprehend it.

Then he went still.

"That bitch?"

His features contorted with unconcealed disgust.

Whenever the Empress became the subject of conversation, Gareth's anger resembled molten lava threatening to burst through the earth.

But when the discussion turned to the illegitimate daughter she had brought into the imperial household, his expression assumed an altogether different character.

It was as though the mere mention of her name had left some unspeakable filth upon his tongue.

His teeth ground together audibly.

"If it was that mad little bitch, I can well believe it. Whenever my sister is concerned, jealousy so thoroughly consumes her that she becomes incapable of the smallest measure of reason."

The vulgarity of his language would scarcely have disgraced a common ruffian.

Yet Varkas offered neither objection nor response.

Gareth watched him for a moment before returning, with stubborn persistence, to the matter that truly occupied his thoughts.

"Still, would it not be premature to absolve Senevia—that woman—of all involvement? Who is to say she did not encourage her daughter to—"

"Had the Empress been responsible, she would have employed an actual poison."

The interruption was as cold as a winter blade.

A faint glimmer stirred within Varkas's pale eyes.

"Nor would she have managed the affair with such incompetence as to leave herself exposed to discovery."

Gareth's lips tightened.

He disliked being interrupted, particularly by one who showed so little inclination to acknowledge his royal authority.

Yet the contempt underlying Varkas's words was sufficiently gratifying to temper his irritation.

For there was one matter upon which Gareth had long taken considerable satisfaction.

This young man, so indifferent to nearly everything beneath heaven, seemed invariably to sharpen his claws whenever the disgrace of the imperial household—Thalia Roem Guirta—became the subject of conversation.

It was reassuring.

Indeed, it served as yet another confirmation that Varkas stood upon his side.

Their side.

"Quite so," Gareth conceded at last. "Senevia is far too cunning to jeopardise her own position through such a foolish contrivance."

His humour improved considerably.

"So Thalia has contrived to dig her own grave. I always knew that troublesome creature would bring calamity upon herself sooner or later."

A mischievous smile spread across his face, recalling the reckless expression he had so frequently worn during his years at the Academy.

"She must have quite lost her wits when my sister took you from her. That bastard-born little brat never did understand her station. For how many years did she presume to command the heir of a Grand Ducal house as though he were some obedient lapdog?"

He gave a contemptuous laugh.

"And now, the Imperial Knight whom she once treated as her personal plaything is soon to become the husband of a true princess. With a disposition such as hers, I daresay the humiliation proved more than she could endure."

Varkas remained silent.

Ordinarily, this would have occasioned no surprise.

Varkas Raedgo Shierkhan had never been a man inclined towards unnecessary speech.

Indeed, his silence was so habitual that it had become almost inseparable from his person.

Yet there were occasions when that very silence inspired in Gareth an irritation bordering upon fury.

This was one of them.

He fixed the man beside him with a searching stare, as though determined to compel some visible reaction.

"In any event, she shall not escape punishment so easily this time. With a little good fortune, we might even succeed in having the matter treated as an attempted poisoning—"

"That outcome is unlikely to satisfy Your Highness."

For the second time that evening, Varkas interrupted him.

Gareth's gaze sharpened dangerously.

Yet the man seated beside the future sovereign of the Empire appeared wholly undisturbed.

"To begin with, the evidence is merely circumstantial. There exists no conclusive proof of her involvement. Should the Second Princess persist in denying the accusation, establishing her guilt will prove exceedingly difficult."

"We need only summon the maid who supplied the medicine to testify!"

"Does Your Highness truly imagine that the testimony of a single maidservant would suffice to bring the Empress's eldest daughter before a tribunal?"

Gareth opened his mouth, only to discover that no satisfactory answer presented itself.

Varkas continued without haste.

"Furthermore, even were her responsibility established beyond all reasonable doubt, the prospect of imposing any severe punishment would remain slight. The substance occasioned nothing more than gastric distress. Should she maintain that the affair was merely an ill-considered jest, the most that could reasonably be expected would be a period of confinement."

"A jest?"

For several heartbeats, Gareth stared at him in astonishment.

Then his fury erupted.

He sprang to his feet and seized the future Grand Duke by the collar.

"Do you comprehend the humiliation suffered by the woman who is to become your wife? Ayla, who has never once disgraced herself before an assembly, was made to collapse in the midst of a banquet after being violently sick!"

His voice rose with every word.

"Have you the faintest conception of the agony she endured? And yet you stand there and speak of it as though it were some harmless jest?"

Even beneath the darkness, the Crown Prince's bronze complexion had become flushed with rage.

The memory of that disastrous evening seemed to unfold anew before his eyes.

His teeth clenched.

"Indeed, I had quite forgotten it in the confusion of the moment—but when Ayla fell, I saw Thalia smiling from the opposite side of the hall! I saw it with my own eyes!"

His grip tightened.

"That filthy, bastard-born wretch ought to be torn limb from limb for daring to subject my sister to such indignity—!"

"Your Highness."

Only two words.

They were neither loud nor openly threatening.

Yet something in the manner of their utterance brought Gareth's tirade to an abrupt end.

His fingers loosened involuntarily.

A chill of perspiration gathered along his spine.

He released Varkas's collar and retreated a step, watching him with sudden wariness.

The future Grand Duke merely lowered his gaze and smoothed the creases from his disordered uniform.

His movements were deliberate, almost languid.

"That woman is scarcely deserving of so much of Your Highness's attention."

He spoke with the simple certainty of one observing that darkness invariably follows the setting sun.

"Her accomplishments extend no further than the invention of petty cruelties and malicious little stratagems. Is it truly fitting that the man destined to govern an Empire should permit himself to be so profoundly disturbed by such trifles?"

There was something almost paternal in his tone.

As though he were correcting the excesses of an unruly child.

Under ordinary circumstances, Gareth would have found such presumption intolerable.

But conscious that he himself had first transgressed the bounds of propriety, he restrained his indignation.

Varkas rose.

Without waiting to be dismissed, he prepared to depart.

It was a flagrant disregard for courtly etiquette.

And yet, upon him, even such discourtesy seemed strangely natural.

"I have already arranged for medicinal herbs to be delivered to Her Highness to alleviate her discomfort. Henceforth, every dish and beverage presented to her shall be subjected to the strictest supervision."

Gareth stared at his retreating figure.

"Then you would have me abandon the matter altogether?"

"Should Your Highness remain determined to uncover the entire truth and exact punishment from the Second Princess, I shall not presume to interfere."

Varkas paused.

"Nevertheless, I would prefer to have no further part in the affair."

A flush of resentment crept once more across Gareth's face.

It was evident that Varkas intended to dismiss the entire incident as little more than the spiteful mischief of an immature and jealous girl.

And, considered dispassionately, there was a certain wisdom in his position.

For the Crown Prince and the future ruler of the Eastern Grand Duchy to become excessively entangled in so trifling a dispute would scarcely enhance their dignity.

Nevertheless, Gareth found himself troubled by the man's extraordinary indifference.

And it was not merely his apparent lack of concern for Ayla that disturbed him.

He studied Varkas with renewed suspicion.

"Tell me something."

His voice lowered.

"You are not, by any chance, attempting to protect that woman?"

Varkas, who had already turned towards the arcade, came to an abrupt halt.

Slowly, he looked back over his shoulder.

His gaze fell upon Gareth with the merciless chill of winter.

A breeze drifted across the garden from the lakeside, stirring the pale strands of his ash-blond hair.

Yet the gentle movement did nothing to soften the severity of his countenance.

Then, quite unexpectedly, he smiled.

It was not the courteous smile of a nobleman, nor the faint expression of amusement one might bestow upon a foolish remark.

It was a smile as thin and keen as the edge of a newly sharpened blade.

For the first time that evening, something resembling genuine emotion disturbed the marble perfection of his face.

"Protecting her?"

A low laugh escaped him.

"Do you truly imagine that I would protect her?"

There was amusement in his voice.

But within the cold blue depths of his eyes, a fury of almost frightening intensity flickered for the briefest instant.

Gareth found himself bereft of speech.

He had always understood that Varkas disliked Thalia.

Indeed, such an aversion had seemed entirely natural, given the circumstances of their acquaintance.

Yet never had he imagined that the sentiment extended to so profound a hatred.

Was this not the very man whose passions had supposedly been extinguished beneath the stern ministrations of the priests?

Throughout the more than ten years of their acquaintance, Gareth could scarcely recall a single occasion upon which Varkas had openly betrayed the workings of his heart.

And yet, at the mere suggestion that he might be protecting Thalia, that impenetrable composure had fractured.

What, in heaven's name, had the girl done to him?

What offence could have driven so cold and unyielding a creature to bare his fangs?

Gareth regarded him with mounting curiosity.

He had heard, of course, the rumours concerning the years Varkas had spent in service as Thalia Roem Guirta's Imperial Guard Knight.

There had been no shortage of accounts describing the indignities she had supposedly inflicted upon him.

Her unreasonable demands.

Her imperious whims.

The countless humiliations to which she had subjected the proud heir of the Shierkhan Grand Duchy.

Yet Gareth had never attached much significance to such tales.

Varkas had always seemed far too dignified, too proud, and too utterly removed from ordinary human frailty to be genuinely wounded by the hysterical caprices of a spoiled young princess.

Surely Thalia had been nothing more to him than an inconvenience.

A tiresome creature whose presence he had been obliged to endure.

But perhaps Gareth had been mistaken.

For the cold brilliance of Varkas's eyes had taken on an almost sinister quality.

And when he spoke again, each word seemed to emerge from between tightly clenched teeth.

"I simply wish never to have anything to do with that woman again."

There was a dreadful finality to the declaration.

Varkas offered no further explanation.

Nor did he linger to observe the Crown Prince's reaction.

He merely turned away and proceeded down the shadowed arcade, retracing the path by which he had come.

Gareth remained where he stood, watching the pale figure disappear into the darkness.

There was something unnervingly fluid about the man's retreating silhouette.

Like a water serpent gliding across the blackened surface of a moonless lake, he seemed to vanish without so much as a whisper.

For a long moment, the Crown Prince stood in contemplation.

Then, slowly, one corner of his mouth curved upwards.

Thalia…

Whatever it was that you did to him, I cannot pretend to understand it.

But this suits my purposes exceedingly well.

For Varkas Raedgo Shierkhan was destined to become one of the most formidable pillars of Gareth's future reign.

And if the man who ought to be his strongest ally harboured so bitter an enmity towards the very woman Gareth himself despised, then surely their alliance could only be strengthened.

The thought pleased him immensely.

With satisfaction settling upon his features, the Crown Prince at last rose from the garden bench.

The night had grown quiet once more.
