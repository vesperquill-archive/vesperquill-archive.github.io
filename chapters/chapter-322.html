Chapter 322

Thalia looked up at the sky. Through the bare branches, the sky appeared blurred and hazy.

The sun's outline still seemed to shine faintly through the thin clouds, giving her hope that the weather might soon clear. However, with her clothes already soaked, she was beginning to shiver from the cold wind before their journey had even truly begun.

Glancing around the forest with a troubled expression, Talia eventually quickened her pace, searching for a place where they could take shelter from the rain.

After walking for some time, Khan, who had been quietly keeping pace beside her, suddenly turned toward a cluster of densely growing trees. Talia obediently followed him, quickening her steps. She had decided to follow wherever Khan intended to lead her.

She carefully pushed aside the branches that spread out like a bird's wings and stepped onto the damp, muddy path. At the end of the gently sloping trail, a small hut came into view, its roof nearly collapsed.

“…Could this be a building once used by a forest keeper?”

Talia approached cautiously and surveyed her surroundings with a wary gaze. Fortunately, she sensed no sign of anyone nearby. The building appeared to have been abandoned long ago.

After examining the overgrown weeds around the entrance and the crumbling outer walls covered in patches of mold, Talia finally approached the doorway and reached for the doorknob.

Just then, the rusted hinge.

With a creaking sound, the thin wooden door slowly swung open.

Talia tilted her head and peered inside. The empty building was filled with the musty smell of aged wood and damp moss.

‘There aren't any rats or insects in there, are there?’

She hesitated to step inside, carefully examining the filthy floor, thickly covered with cobwebs hanging from the ceiling and layers of dust.

Just then, Khan pushed the door wide open with his head and squeezed his large body through the narrow doorway with surprising ease. Once inside, he settled down and stared at her, wagging his tail as though urging her to come in.

Unable to resist, Talia stepped over the threshold. Although the place was rather unpleasant, it would still be better to shelter from the rain somewhere with a roof over her head.

She removed the luggage from Khan's neck, then took out an oil-coated cloth bundle from inside the pouch.

She took it out and spread it across the floor. Then, settling herself upon it, she leaned against Khan's side.

Although his fur was slightly damp, his body was naturally so warm that simply pressing herself against him was enough to drive away the chill.

Talia lifted one of Khan's front legs and draped it across her body before curling up against his belly, where his fur was relatively sparse.

Khan shifted his body sideways, seemingly trying to make her more comfortable, then rested his chin upon the crown of her head and let out a low growl.

A faint smile touched her lips at the sound, which seemed to carry both reassurance and contentment.

For a moment, she lifted her pale, weary face and gazed out through the window.

The thickening mist had turned the leaves of the birch trees a ghostly white.

Contrary to what she had expected, it seemed unlikely that the weather would clear anytime soon.

“…It would be better to set off as soon as the rain stop, even a little.”

The possibility that someone from the Grand Duke's residence would come looking for her was slim. Nevertheless, she could not ignore the danger of encountering patrolling guards or bandits.

Even if they were not suspicious of her, there was no telling what accusations might be leveled against her if it became known that she had secretly slipped out of the castle and wandered through the forest.

Talia nervously bit her lip. As soon as the sound of the rain began to subside, she immediately gathered her belongings and stepped outside.

Fine, threadlike raindrops were still falling from the sky, but sunlight appeared to be filtering through the clouds. It seemed the weather would clear before long.

After examining the sky with her eyes slightly narrowed, Talia resumed walking along the forest path.

The deeper they ventured into the forest, the fainter the trail became. However, Khan stayed close beside her, guiding her along the path.

Thanks to Khan staying close beside her and guiding the way, she was able to walk more easily than she had expected.

Talia descended a gentle slope, holding on to the bundle hanging around Khan's neck. Soon, a field of reeds, stained a pale brown, spread out before her.

For a moment, she was overcome by a strange sense of liberation, as though she had stepped into an entirely different world.

She quietly gazed across the meadow, shimmering beneath the golden sunlight. The rain clouds that had hung low over the sky were gradually lifting, and the warm sunlight spread across the vast grassland.

After gazing absentmindedly at the dry reeds, which swayed gently in the breeze and reached as high as her knees, Talia finally followed Khan through the thick grass.

She could feel the cold raindrops and warm sunlight alternately dampening her cheeks and forehead.

She lifted her head and quietly savored the sensation when Khan gently nudged her side.

Turning her head, Talia saw Khan crouching low to the ground and narrowed her eyes slightly.

“…Are you telling me to climb onto your back?”

Khan answered with a short growl.

After hesitating for a moment, Talia finally grasped the fur around Khan's neck and carefully climbed onto his back.

Khan waited until she had settled herself comfortably before beginning to make his way through the reeds at a leisurely pace.

With every step the gray wolf took, the dense stalks parted on either side. Slender ears of grain brushed against the hem of her dress, while raindrops clinging to the tips of the stems scattered in every direction.

The breeze that occasionally swept past them carried the scent of rain-soaked earth mingled with the fresh fragrance of grass.

Talia gazed into the distance at the scenery flowing past her like a gentle current.

It was as though she had fallen into a sea of shimmering gold.

Sunlight poured through the gaps between the clouds.

The sunlight was gradually bathing the vast meadow in an even brighter glow.

Whenever the wind swept across the reeds, the stalks leaned together in the same direction, forming golden waves. The rustling of dried leaves surged toward her like the sound of rolling waves before gradually fading into the distance behind her.

How long had she been lost in that peaceful scenery?

Talia caught the scent of wildflowers carried on the gentle breeze and closed her eyes.

The familiar fragrance of dry grass, the earthy scent of the soil, and the fresh aroma of the forest, all of which she had known so well since childhood, mingled together and filled her lungs. A pleasant sensation spread through her entire body.

Without thinking, she tightened her embrace around Khan's fur.

Khan, who had been moving slowly through the reeds, came to a halt and turned his head toward her.

Talia met his blue eyes with her clouded gaze for a moment before shifting her weight and beginning to lower her feet toward the ground.

Khan pressed his head against her again, as though trying to stop her.

Talia took a step backward and shook her head slightly.

“I… can't go.”

Khan's eyes widened slightly before narrowing sharply. As though questioning her reasons, he fixed her with an intense gaze.

Talia looked back over her shoulder toward the birch forest.

Even if she returned, no one would welcome her. To Varkas, she was nothing more than a burden who remained in a home he had long since abandoned. Perhaps disappearing altogether would be the kindest thing she could do for him.

Then why couldn't she bring herself to go any farther? Why, when no one was holding her back…?

“I can't leave.”

Staring blankly down at her own two feet, Talia muttered in a suppressed voice. Suddenly, a suffocating sense of despair washed over her. Twisting her face in agony, she clutched at the fabric of her clothes near her chest

“I… want to go back.”

The heavy weight of despair turned into tears that streamed down her cheeks.

Could such a feeling truly be called love, when it would not allow her to take even a single step away from her endless suffering?

No. This was nothing short of a curse.

A curse from which she could never escape.

Yet the lingering attachment and obsession that had accumulated over so many years had already become a part of her. She could not even begin to imagine what would remain of her if she tore them away.

“I'm sorry. I'm so sorry, Khan…”

Her tears flowed endlessly, mingling with the rain.

Talia trembled and turned away.

Seeing this, Khan caught the hem of her dress between his teeth and tugged at it fiercely.

Talia shook her head firmly.

“Let's go back, all right?”
