Chapter 45

Edric hurried through the campsite, weaving between soldiers who were busily carrying out their duties. Barely an hour had passed since the battle ended, yet the place that had so recently been a scene of terrible slaughter was gradually being restored to order.

The soldiers were retrieving weapons from the fallen wyverns and dismantling what remained of the ruined tents. Labourers carried crates and bundles towards the supply wagons, while the maids attended to the sorrowful task of gathering the dead and wrapping their bodies in silk. Everywhere Edric looked, there were signs of the dreadful battle that had taken place.

As he walked, he silently counted the bodies that had been recovered. Seventeen lay before him, but that number did not include those unfortunate souls who had been swallowed whole by the monsters. Taking them into account, he estimated that somewhere between twenty-five and thirty people had lost their lives. When the wounded were added to the count, the extent of their losses became even more distressing.

Edric clenched his fists, troubled by more than the number of casualties.

A flock of wyverns appearing during the Season of Fire...

Something about the attack was terribly wrong. Wyverns ordinarily awakened from their winter slumber towards the end of the Season of Rest. They did not begin gathering in large groups until the Season of Water, when their mating period approached. Once that season had passed, they generally separated into pairs and scattered across their territories.

For that reason, encountering more than a dozen wyverns together during the Season of Fire was exceedingly unusual. Furthermore, the surrounding hills and forests were hardly suitable for sustaining creatures of such enormous size.

Could this truly have been nothing more than dreadful misfortune?

Edric narrowed his eyes as he looked towards the enormous carcasses scattered across the low hills.

No... Something about this does not seem right.

Nevertheless, he forced himself to set those suspicions aside. There would be time to investigate the strange circumstances of the attack later. For now, there were far more urgent matters demanding his attention.

Without further hesitation, he made his way towards the knights gathered near the centre of the camp.

"Commander!"

Varkas, who had been issuing instructions to several soldiers, turned at the sound of his voice.

Edric stopped almost involuntarily. Under the flickering torchlight, his commander's appearance was unusually frightening. Dark blood stained much of his face, making his already severe features appear even colder. His sharp eyes reflected the dancing flames, giving him an almost unsettling presence.

Edric knew that he himself was probably in no better condition after the battle. Yet there was something particularly intimidating about Varkas standing there, his composed expression contrasting sharply with the blood covering his face.

"Have you found her?" Varkas asked.

His voice was as calm and even as ever.

Edric quickly gathered his thoughts and delivered his report.

"We discovered signs of a fallen wyvern to the north, Commander. From the marks left upon the ground, it appears the creature plunged directly into the canyon."

Varkas turned his gaze towards the northern forest. Beyond the reach of the camp's torches, the trees stood beneath a thick blanket of darkness. The small flames carried by the knights illuminated only a short distance into the woods, leaving the deeper wilderness entirely concealed.

There was no telling what other dangerous creatures might be lurking beyond the light. Edric knew the risks, but he also knew that every passing moment could lessen their chances of finding the missing princess alive.

"We ought to organise a search party immediately," he urged. "If we delay any longer, Her Highness may—"

"What search party?!"

The furious interruption made Edric turn sharply.

Crown Prince Gareth was approaching them, accompanied by several attendants. His thick black hair had fallen into disorder, giving him the appearance of an enraged lion as he strode across the campsite.

Edric immediately lowered his head in greeting.

"Your Highness."

But Gareth paid him no attention. He walked straight past the young knight and stopped before Varkas, his expression twisted with anger.

"That girl is surely dead by now!" Gareth snapped. "Why must we waste precious time searching for someone who cannot possibly have survived? Order the men to prepare for departure at once! What shall we do if another flock of those monsters descends upon us while we remain here?"

Edric's jaw tightened.

The Crown Prince's complete disregard for Talia's fate filled him with resentment. Gareth's face was noticeably paler than usual, and his agitation made it clear that the battle had frightened him considerably.

Having never encountered anything more dangerous than an ordinary ogre, it was perhaps understandable that he had been terrified by the sudden appearance of wyverns. Even so, Edric found himself unable to summon the slightest sympathy for him.

How can he speak so cruelly when his own sister is still missing?

Suppressing his anger, Edric glanced towards Varkas, hoping to discover how his commander intended to respond.

But Varkas remained strangely silent.

Edric felt a growing uneasiness.

Surely he cannot mean to obey such an order?

Whatever her circumstances of birth, Talia Roem Guirta was still an imperial princess. The Emperor's own blood flowed through her veins, and she was also the eldest daughter of the reigning Empress.

To abandon an imperial daughter in the wilderness, leaving her body to be devoured by wild beasts merely to satisfy the Crown Prince's wishes, would be a disgrace to the honour of every knight present.

Unable to remain silent any longer, Edric stepped forward.

"Commander, if you will grant me permission, I shall lead the search myself. I ask only that you allow us to look for Her Highness. Please—"

"You insolent little wretch!"

Gareth's furious voice rang across the gathering.

"Who gave you permission to speak while I am addressing your commander?"

Before Edric could react, the Crown Prince seized him by the collar and pulled him forward.

Edric remained remarkably composed. He had witnessed Gareth's violent temper often enough to know that arguing would only make matters worse. He therefore kept his voice respectful, despite the anger rising within him.

"Forgive my interruption, Your Highness. However, we cannot simply abandon the Princess without making an effort to find her."

"The Princess?"

Gareth gave a harsh, contemptuous laugh. He leaned closer, speaking each word with deliberate cruelty.

"Listen carefully, you fool. There is only one princess worthy of that title in this Empire, and that is Ayla Roem Guirta. As for that wretched Talia, she ought never to have been born. If those monsters have torn her apart, then she has merely received the miserable end she deserves—"

"Enough."

The single word was spoken quietly.

Yet it brought the Crown Prince's furious speech to an immediate halt.

Edric turned towards Varkas in astonishment. His commander had always been a man of strict discipline, particularly when dealing with members of the imperial family. No matter how unreasonable Gareth became, Varkas had never openly disregarded the formal courtesies owed to the Crown Prince.

But this time, he had interrupted him without hesitation. There had been no respectful address, no elaborate expression of apology.

Only a cold command.

Gareth appeared equally stunned. His eyes widened as he stared at Varkas, seemingly unable to believe that anyone had dared silence him.

But Varkas showed no sign of regret.

"Your Highness would do well to return to your tent and rest," he said evenly.

Then, without waiting for an answer, he turned towards the knights standing nearby.

"Send for a mage immediately. Choose ten of our swiftest men and prepare a search party."

The knights obeyed at once, dispersing to carry out his orders.

Gareth stared after them in disbelief. Then he shoved Edric aside and stepped directly into Varkas's path.

"Are you truly defying me?" he demanded. "And all because of that girl?"

Varkas regarded him without the slightest change in expression.

"His Imperial Majesty entrusted me with the safe escort of all three members of the imperial family," he replied calmly. "I am therefore obliged to carry out that command to the best of my ability."

His gaze remained steady.

"Surely Your Highness would not expect me to place your personal wishes above an order issued by the Emperor himself."

"Do not attempt to deceive me with such excuses!"

Gareth moved to block his path once more, his anger growing more difficult to contain.

"Do you imagine I have failed to notice the way you have been behaving towards her? You have been far too concerned with that girl lately!"

Varkas said nothing.

Gareth's expression darkened further.

"Tell me the truth. What exactly is she to you? Why are you so determined to protect—"

"Gareth!"

A clear, commanding voice interrupted him.

Edric, who had been watching the confrontation with growing apprehension, turned towards the speaker.

Princess Ayla was approaching them, accompanied by two attendants. She passed between the gathered knights with her usual graceful composure, though the exhaustion of the evening was plainly visible upon her face.

Ever since the wyverns had been defeated, Ayla had been occupied with directing the maids in caring for the wounded. She had scarcely been given an opportunity to rest, yet she carried herself with remarkable dignity as she approached her brother.

"Lord Sierkhan is correct," she said firmly. "We cannot simply leave Talia behind. Whatever our personal feelings may be, she is still our sister. We share the same blood."

Gareth turned towards her in disbelief.

"Have you completely lost your senses?"

His face twisted with indignation.

"After everything our mother suffered because of that girl, you would still defend her? Have you forgotten the humiliation she brought upon our family?"

"Please, Gareth. Be reasonable."

Ayla interrupted him before he could continue.

Her voice remained calm, but there was an unmistakable firmness beneath it. Although she had been born only a few minutes before her brother, she possessed a degree of maturity that Gareth had never seemed capable of attaining.

She met his furious gaze without flinching and continued in a measured tone.

"Consider what will happen if we return to the Imperial Palace without Talia. Do you truly believe people will remain silent about such a matter?"

Gareth frowned, but Ayla did not allow him to interrupt.

"If it becomes known that we abandoned an imperial princess in the wilderness, there will be endless rumours and accusations. Lord Sierkhan will undoubtedly be questioned, but you will not escape criticism either."

She paused briefly, allowing her words to settle.

"Why should we expose ourselves to such unnecessary trouble when a search can still be conducted?"

Edric stood motionless, deeply unsettled by what he had just heard.

Princess Ayla was celebrated throughout the Empire for her kindness, grace, and boundless compassion. Yet not once had she expressed any hope that Talia might still be alive. She had not urged her brother to save their missing sister, nor had she shown the slightest concern for the suffering Talia might be enduring somewhere in the wilderness.

Instead, she had spoken only of reputation, scandal, and the inconvenience that abandoning an imperial princess might bring upon them.

And now, as though the matter were nothing more than a minor disagreement, Ayla softened her expression.

She addressed Gareth in a gentle, soothing voice, much like a mother patiently reasoning with a difficult child. There was not a trace of urgency in her manner, nor any sign that she believed Talia might still be waiting to be rescued.

"At the very least, Gareth, we ought to recover her body," she said calmly.

Her voice remained sweet and composed.

"Surely we can allow her the dignity of a proper funeral."

Edric felt a chill settle over him.

A funeral.

Not a rescue.

Not a search for someone who might yet be alive.

A funeral.

Ayla had spoken as though her sister's death were already an established fact.

And perhaps what disturbed him most was how effortlessly those words had fallen from the lips of the Empire's beloved, compassionate princess.
