Chapter 32

By noon, heavy rain began pouring from the sky.

The soldiers, who had been preparing for their departure, hurried to protect their belongings from the downpour. They covered the wagons with waterproof sheets and quickly led the horses back into the stables.

From inside her carriage, Ayla watched the commotion before raising her eyes towards the dark sky. Lightning flashed between the thick clouds, followed by the distant rumbling of thunder. The rain was falling so heavily that it seemed unlikely to stop anytime soon.

"It seems we'll have no choice but to remain at the monastery for another day."

A knight from the royal guard approached her carriage after spending some time in the knights' quarters. It appeared that the decision to delay their departure had already been made. He opened the carriage door slightly and handed her a waterproof cloak.

Ayla accepted it with a quiet sigh. She was growing increasingly frustrated by the repeated changes to their schedule. The journey had already been filled with enough uncertainty, and another delay was the last thing she wanted.

As she put on the cloak, she glanced outside again.

"Where has the Crown Prince gone?"

"His Highness is currently visiting the Abbot at his private residence," the knight answered.

Ayla paused while pulling the hood over her head. She frowned slightly before turning towards him.

"Does that mean he intends to spend the rest of the day there as well?"

"That appears to be the case, Your Highness."

The knight lowered his gaze, offering no further explanation. Ayla looked towards the monks gathered beneath the covered walkway, her expression becoming thoughtful.

The Abbot was standing beneath the roof, giving instructions to several priests. Once he finished speaking, the monks bowed and went their separate ways. Ayla quietly observed the exchange.

His manner was noticeably different from the way he behaved around Gares. When speaking to the Crown Prince, the Abbot had been courteous and welcoming. But now, his expression was stern, and his instructions were delivered with unmistakable authority.

So this is how he normally behaves.

Ayla narrowed her eyes.

The imperial pilgrimage was not simply a religious journey. It was also an opportunity for the imperial family to strengthen its relationships with influential figures and gain the support of the people living in different regions. For that reason, there was nothing particularly unusual about Gares developing a closer relationship with the Abbot.

However, something about the priest continued to trouble her.

For someone of mixed heritage to become the Abbot of such an important monastery, he must be exceptionally skilled at dealing with powerful people. Otherwise, someone influential must be supporting him.

Although their conversations had been brief, Ayla had already realised that the Abbot was far from an ordinary priest. His polite behaviour concealed a sharp and calculating mind. She had noticed the way he carefully observed Gares, as though he were constantly evaluating him.

The possibility that he might be connected to the Empress crossed her mind.

For generations, the Taren family had maintained close relationships with demi-humans, dating back to the Era of Warring States. Considering those connections, it seemed rather convenient that a quarter-elf had risen to such an important position within the Church.

Mordawin was no small monastery. It was a large and influential religious institution, and the man who governed it would naturally possess considerable authority.

Could the Empress already be gathering supporters within the Church? Perhaps she's preparing another faction to strengthen the Second Prince's position.

"Your Highness?"

The sudden voice interrupted her thoughts.

Ayla raised her head and found her personal guard standing beside the carriage, looking at her with concern. Rain continued pouring over his cloak as he waited for her.

Realising that she had left him standing outside for quite some time, Ayla gave him an apologetic smile.

"I'm sorry. I've kept you waiting in the rain while I was distracted. Come, let's return to our quarters."

The knight offered his hand and helped her step down from the carriage.

Ayla carefully placed her feet on the muddy ground. The rain struck her hood and shoulders with surprising force, and the courtyard had become even more difficult to see through the downpour.

She pulled her cloak more tightly around herself and hurried towards the covered walkway surrounding the monastery garden. Once she reached the shelter of the cloister, she noticed the Abbot speaking with the Vice-Abbot nearby.

Ayla approached them as though she had simply happened to pass by.

"Your Highness."

The Abbot noticed her immediately and bowed respectfully.

Ayla greeted him with her usual gentle smile.

"It seems we'll have to trouble you with our presence for another day."

"Please don't think of it as a burden, Your Highness. It is a great honour for our monastery to welcome members of the imperial family."

The priest remained respectfully bowed as he spoke.

"If there is anything you require during your stay, please inform us. We will do everything within our means to accommodate your wishes."

"That's very kind of you. I truly appreciate your hospitality."

Ayla hesitated briefly before continuing in a more thoughtful tone.

"Actually, there is something I would like to ask of you."

"Of course, Your Highness. Please tell me what you need."

"Since our departure has been delayed, I was wondering whether we might arrange a separate blessing ceremony for Talia tomorrow. Would you be willing to perform the ritual for her before we leave?"

The Abbot's eyes widened slightly.

Ayla watched him carefully, searching for any unusual reaction. His surprise lasted only a moment, but she noticed a brief look of caution in his pale violet eyes before he quickly regained his composure.

"Are you referring to Her Highness, the Second Princess?" he asked carefully.

"Yes, of course. Who else would I mean?"

Ayla maintained her pleasant smile as she continued.

"Since we'll be staying another day, wouldn't it be a good opportunity for her to receive the blessing as well? It would be unfortunate for her to leave without taking part in the ceremony."

"I wasn't aware that Your Highness was so concerned about the Second Princess."

The unexpected voice came from behind her.

Ayla immediately turned.

Varkas had approached through the rain without making a sound. He pushed back his wet hood, revealing his pale hair, and looked at her with an expression that was difficult to read.

Ayla had been about to greet him warmly, but her smile faded when she saw his face.

His features seemed even colder than usual, perhaps because of the rain running down his cheeks. The unmistakable displeasure in his eyes made her feel strangely uneasy.

Ayla believed she understood why Varkas reacted so strongly whenever Talia was mentioned. After all, he had spent seven years serving as her escort knight, enduring her difficult behaviour and countless unreasonable demands.

Considering everything he had experienced, it was only natural that he would have unpleasant memories of her half-sister.

At least, that was how Ayla explained his behaviour to herself.

Yet there was something about his reaction that continued to bother her.

Varkas was usually so indifferent that it was almost impossible to tell what he was thinking. Even when faced with matters that would upset most people, he rarely showed any strong emotion.

But whenever Talia was involved, that composure seemed to change.

Ayla found it increasingly difficult to ignore.

For a moment, she forgot that she had approached the Abbot to test his reaction. Instead, she answered Varkas more sharply than she had intended.

"I'm not doing this because I'm particularly worried about Talia. I'm thinking about your position. His Majesty personally placed her under your care. If he discovers that the blessing ceremony was held without her, you may be blamed for it."

Varkas regarded her calmly before answering.

"Even so, I cannot force someone to participate in a religious ceremony when she has already refused."

His response carried a faintly sarcastic edge.

Ayla's expression stiffened.

She was accustomed to Varkas's distant manner, even though his coldness sometimes hurt her feelings. However, she found it difficult to accept being spoken to so dismissively, especially when the disagreement concerned Talia.

She lifted her chin and replied in a firmer voice.

"Then we should ask Talia what she wants before making a final decision. You know how easily she changes her mind. It's entirely possible that she'll agree to the ceremony tomorrow."

"We're scheduled to depart at dawn," Varkas answered immediately. "I have no intention of delaying the journey simply because the Second Princess decides she wants something different."

His words left little room for discussion.

Ayla felt her cheeks grow warm with anger.

She was not accustomed to having her suggestions rejected so openly. Under different circumstances, she might have reprimanded him for speaking to her in such a manner.

However, the Abbot and several other priests were standing nearby. She had no desire to embarrass Varkas or weaken his authority in front of them.

Forcing herself to remain composed, she gave a restrained smile.

"Very well, Sir Varkas. If you've already made your decision, I suppose there's nothing more to discuss."

Varkas did not respond to her displeasure.

Instead, he turned towards the Abbot.

The priest had been quietly observing their disagreement with considerable interest. When Varkas's gaze settled on him, however, he immediately lowered his eyes.

Varkas spoke in a calm but unmistakably firm voice.

"I would appreciate it if the monastery remained peaceful this evening. Since we're leaving early tomorrow, His Highness the Crown Prince will need sufficient rest."

His meaning was clear.

He did not want another banquet or gathering arranged for Gares that evening.

The Abbot seemed to understand the warning immediately. His expression became serious as he lowered his head.

"Of course, Sir Varkas. We will make the necessary arrangements."

Satisfied with the response, Varkas turned back towards Ayla.

He extended his hand to her.

"Come, Your Highness. I'll accompany you back to your quarters."

Ayla looked at him for a moment before quietly releasing a sigh.

Then she placed her hand in his and allowed him to escort her away.
