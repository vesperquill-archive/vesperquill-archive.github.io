Chapter 66

“Have you been using those medicinal incense sticks again?”

Varkas regarded her sternly, his eyes lingering on her unusually dilated pupils.

Talia felt her cheeks grow warm.

She had only burned the incense to relieve her pain. Surely there was nothing wrong with that. Yet beneath his disapproving gaze, she could not help feeling like a child who had been caught misbehaving.

Unable to meet his eyes any longer, she lowered her head.

A quiet sigh escaped his lips.

“…Perhaps it is better this way.”

There was a bitter note in his voice.

Before Talia could understand what he meant, Varkas knelt before her and slipped one arm behind her back. His other arm moved beneath her knees, and he carefully lifted her into his embrace.

Talia stared up at him in surprise. His sharply defined jaw was so close that she instinctively held her breath.

Then, remembering her gown, she hurriedly gathered the fabric around her legs. The thought of her skirts slipping upwards and exposing her injuries filled her with dread.

“I—I can manage on my own. You need not carry me.”

“There is no need to force yourself to remain clearheaded.”

Varkas stepped out of the carriage with her in his arms.

“Let the medicine ease your mind. It may help you endure the ceremony.”

Talia flinched at his indifferent words.

So that is how he regards our wedding.

Nothing more than an unpleasant duty to be endured.

Her heart sank, and she lowered her head in silence.

Just then, a man approached them.

“Commander, it seems His Majesty has yet to arrive.”

Startled by the unfamiliar voice, Talia instinctively pressed herself closer to Varkas.

He adjusted his cloak, drawing the dark fabric around her to shield her from view.

“Then inform the High Priest that we shall wait.”

Having given his instructions, Varkas continued towards the cathedral.

Talia caught hold of his shirt without thinking. The familiar scent of peppermint lingered upon the pale fabric, offering a strange comfort amid her growing unease.

Beyond his shoulder, the sky was covered with heavy grey clouds.

The cathedral stood beneath that gloomy sky, its marble walls and solemn statues appearing almost lifeless in the dim light. The carved faces of the saints seemed to watch her as she approached.

A dreadful feeling settled in her stomach.

Before she could stop herself, Talia murmured, “What a miserable day for a wedding.”

Varkas lowered his gaze towards her.

Avoiding his eyes, she continued in a hesitant voice.

“Surely people ought to marry beneath clear skies. I cannot imagine anyone choosing a day as gloomy as this.”

Perhaps we should postpone it.

The suggestion hovered upon her tongue.

But before she could speak, Varkas replied calmly.

“Then ours shall be the first wedding held beneath such skies.”

Talia fell silent.

His answer had been so simple, so utterly composed, that she could think of nothing to say.

He must be tired of hearing me complain.

He is merely saying whatever will keep me quiet.

She tried to convince herself of this, yet her heart betrayed her by beating faster.

Ashamed of her foolish reaction, she lowered her face to hide her flushed cheeks.

Soon, they passed through the cathedral's grand entrance.

The cold air inside immediately seeped through her thin wedding gown.

Talia drew her shoulders inward and cautiously peered out from beneath Varkas's cloak.

The sight before her made her breath catch.

Hundreds of guests filled the great hall.

She had not expected so many people to attend.

Surely none of them have come to offer us their blessings.

They were here because they feared offending Senevier.

Or perhaps they had simply come to witness the spectacle of the Emperor's disgraced daughter marrying the Commander of the Imperial Knights.

They must be curious to see what has become of the crippled bastard princess.

Talia immediately lowered her gaze towards her legs.

Her wedding gown was made of generous layers of silk, its skirts falling well beyond her feet. There was no reason anyone should be able to see the injuries concealed beneath it.

Nevertheless, she anxiously gathered the fabric around her ankles.

When she finally dared to look up, she found countless pairs of eyes directed towards her.

Her entire body stiffened.

Is there something dreadful about my appearance?

Why must they all stare?

Just then, Varkas shifted his cloak, shielding her from the crowded hall.

“We shall have to wait for His Majesty before proceeding.”

His voice was unexpectedly gentle.

He carried her towards a quieter section of the cathedral, away from the assembled guests.

“Until then, you ought to rest. There is no need to exhaust yourself.”

Talia looked up at him in bewilderment.

Rest?

Has anyone ever heard of a bride resting during her own wedding?

She was about to question him when a familiar voice sounded behind them.

“My lord.”

Talia glanced over Varkas's shoulder.

One of his knights was approaching.

“Marquis Oristein has requested a private word with you. He wishes to speak before the ceremony begins.”

Talia's expression changed.

Marquis Oristein was the maternal grandfather of Gareth and Ayla.

Why would he wish to speak with Varkas today?

She looked up at the man carrying her.

His jaw had tightened.

After a moment of silence, Varkas lowered her carefully onto a seat in the choir stalls. He then removed his cloak and placed it around her shoulders.

“Remain here for a little while. I shall return as soon as I can.”

Talia instinctively reached towards his sleeve.

But before her fingers could touch him, she drew her hand back.

He has only agreed to this marriage because the Emperor commanded it.

I have no right to cling to him whenever he turns away.

Varkas looked towards his subordinate.

“See that Her Highness is properly attended to until I return.”

“Yes, my lord.”

With that, Varkas departed.

Talia watched his retreating figure, her teeth pressing anxiously against her lower lip.

Marquis Oristein must have come to oppose the marriage.

What could he possibly say to change Varkas's mind?

Would Varkas listen to him?

She remained seated, troubled by these thoughts, until she became aware of someone watching her.

Turning her head, she found the knight standing nearby, his dark brown eyes fixed upon her.

The moment their gazes met, he appeared startled and quickly looked away.

Talia felt a chill run through her.

Why was he looking at me so strangely?

Is there something wrong with my appearance?

She glanced down at her gown once more.

The delicate silk lay in soft folds over her legs.

Could their shape still be visible beneath the fabric?

The knight cleared his throat.

“Your Highness, would you care for a little water while you wait?”

Talia straightened her back, determined not to reveal her unease.

“No. I have no need of anything.”

Unfortunately, her tongue was still heavy from the medicine, and the words emerged less clearly than she intended.

She moistened her dry lips and looked around.

Several priests and attendants stood along the walls, waiting for the ceremony to begin. Beyond them, guests lingered near the central aisle.

Everywhere she looked, someone seemed to be glancing in her direction.

Their attention grew increasingly unbearable.

Talia bit her lower lip until she tasted blood.

She wanted to demand why they were all staring at her.

Then Senevier's cruel words returned to her mind.

Those who are ugly and broken exist only to be ridiculed and trampled upon.

Talia wanted to flee.

She wanted to leave the cathedral before another person could look upon her with pity or disgust.

But how could she possibly escape?

Her legs could scarcely support her weight.

If she attempted to run, she would surely collapse upon the stone floor before everyone.

And then the laughter would begin.

Bastard.

Cripple.

The wretched bride.

“Your Highness, you seem rather unwell. Shall I ask one of the priests to attend to you?”

The knight approached, concern evident in his expression.

Talia shook her head.

“No. Leave me alone.”

“Perhaps a simple healing spell would help ease your discomfort—”

“I have already refused. Must I repeat myself?”

The knight immediately fell silent.

Yet he continued to watch her with that same troubling expression.

Talia could no longer bear it.

Every glance felt like another reminder of how far she had fallen.

She turned her eyes towards the passage through which Varkas had disappeared.

Why has he not returned?

What could they possibly have to discuss for so long?

Suddenly, she remembered Ayla's final words.

You will regret this day, Talia Roem Guirta.

I shall make certain of it.

Her heart began to race.

What if Ayla sent her grandfather to stop the wedding?

The thought seemed horribly reasonable.

After all, a marriage between herself and Varkas had never made sense.

What if they arranged all of this merely to humiliate me?

Perhaps Varkas had already changed his mind.

Perhaps he had left the cathedral altogether.

And here she sat, foolishly waiting for a bridegroom who had no intention of returning.

She would become the object of even greater ridicule.

A bastard princess.

A crippled woman.

And now, a bride abandoned before the altar.

The thought was unbearable.

“I have decided to leave.”

The knight blinked in surprise.

“Your Highness?”

Talia pulled Varkas's cloak from her shoulders and let it fall to the floor.

Then she rose unsteadily.

She could no longer endure sitting there beneath so many curious eyes.

Gathering her skirts, she began making her way towards a small side door.

The knight hurried after her and stepped into her path.

“Your Highness, where are you going? The ceremony will begin shortly. Please, you must remain here.”

“Stand aside. I wish to leave.”

“I am afraid I cannot allow you to go alone—”

“Then get out of my way!”

Talia reached forward and tried to push him aside.

But her weakened arms could scarcely move a man dressed in armour.

Her frustration turned to desperation.

“Did you not hear me? I told you to move!”

She pushed against him once more.

At that moment, her injured knees gave way.

Losing her balance, Talia instinctively seized the knight's arm to keep herself from falling.

She felt his body stiffen beneath her touch.

The sensation made her stomach turn.

She could scarcely bear being touched by a man.

His strength, the heavy armour, and the knowledge that she was powerless to resist him filled her with dread.

There was only one man whose touch she could endure.

Varkas.

But even he has abandoned me.

Tears gathered in her eyes.

“Do not touch me! Let me go!”

She struggled against the knight, striking away the hand that reached to steady her shoulder.

Then, without warning, someone lifted her from the ground.

Talia gasped and turned her head.

Cold blue eyes met hers.

Varkas had returned.

For a moment, he simply looked at her.

His gaze rested upon her tear-stained face and trembling body, his expression unreadable.

Then he turned towards the knight.

The young man immediately lowered his head, his face flushing with embarrassment.

Varkas regarded him in silence for several moments before turning away.

Holding Talia securely in his arms, he began walking back towards the ceremony.

“All the guests have arrived. The wedding is ready to begin.”

His deep voice sounded cold and firm above her head.

“There is no turning back now.”
