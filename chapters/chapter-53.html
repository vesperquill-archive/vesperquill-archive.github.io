Chapter 53

Varkas had been speaking with a priest, handing over a bundle of parchment containing the names and particulars of the deceased, when he turned at the approaching footsteps.

The moment his eyes fell upon her, Talia hastily lowered her gaze.

Beneath the merciless summer sun, she felt dreadfully exposed. Her dishevelled appearance, her wounded leg, and the humiliating manner in which she was being carried made it impossible for her to hold her head high.

"The Second Princess is in urgent need of medical attention," the Taren mage announced with respectful composure. "I must therefore request that the customary formalities be set aside so that Her Highness may be taken directly for treatment."

When Varkas offered no immediate answer, Talia cautiously lifted her eyes.

He was studying her face with unusual intensity, a faint crease appearing between his brows.

Does he find it strange that I am allowing myself to be carried without putting up a fight?

After all, she had once flown into a rage whenever anyone attempted to touch her without permission. Now she hung helplessly over a stranger's shoulder, unable to offer even the slightest resistance.

Varkas's narrowed gaze travelled over her pale, exhausted features before slowly moving downwards. His eyes paused upon her back and the hands supporting her knees, then descended towards her motionless calves, which dangled beneath the hem of her gown.

When he noticed the dark crimson stains spreading through the bandages around her injured leg, his expression hardened. The crease between his brows deepened, and he immediately directed a sharp, questioning look towards the Taren mages.

The mage appeared not to notice his displeasure.

"Her Majesty will surely understand the necessity of allowing the Second Princess to return directly to her own quarters," he continued smoothly.

"Indeed. There is no reason to delay the poor child any longer."

The answer came not from Varkas, but from a clear, melodious voice nearby.

Talia turned her head and stiffened.

Ayla was approaching through the crowded courtyard, accompanied by a small gathering of maids. Her movements were as graceful as ever, and even amidst the confusion of the returning procession, she appeared perfectly composed.

Coming to a halt beside Varkas, Ayla lowered her eyes towards her younger sister with an expression of gentle pity.

"She has endured such a dreadful journey whilst suffering from grievous injuries," Ayla remarked softly. "Surely it would be unkind to keep her waiting when she is so desperately in need of rest and proper treatment."

Talia clenched her teeth.

The sympathetic gaze directed towards her was almost more than she could endure. Humiliation burned through her chest, and a fierce desire to lash out seized her.

How dare she look at me like that?

Had Talia possessed the strength to move even a single finger, she might have reached up and scratched that infuriating expression from her half-sister's face.

But her body remained utterly powerless.

Whether Ayla failed to notice her fury or simply chose to disregard it, she turned towards Varkas with a pleasant smile and rested one delicate hand upon his forearm.

"I shall explain the circumstances to His Majesty myself," she assured him.

Then, with a gentle movement, she urged him towards the palace entrance.

"Come, Varkas. We must not keep His Majesty waiting any longer."

Only then did Varkas, who had remained as motionless as a stone pillar, finally begin to walk.

Talia watched his retreating figure for several moments. Her gaze lingered upon his broad back until he disappeared amongst the gathering courtiers.

At last, she closed her eyes.

The mages carried her away from the Main Palace, following a path that led through an immense garden overflowing with summer blossoms. Beyond the flowering hedges stood a magnificent building, its elaborate architecture rising above the surrounding grounds.

As they passed through its grand entrance, Talia felt an inexplicable chill.

It seemed almost as though she were being swallowed by some enormous creature, carried helplessly deeper into its belly.

The mages crossed the polished marble floor with brisk, purposeful strides before ascending a broad staircase. They proceeded directly to Lady Senevier's study, a familiar chamber lined with towering bookshelves containing hundreds upon hundreds of volumes.

Without hesitation, one of the mages approached a section of the wall between two pillars.

A concealed passage opened before them.

Talia watched in growing uneasiness as they entered the hidden corridor with the confidence of men who had travelled that way countless times.

Before long, they emerged into Senevier's laboratory.

A powerful mixture of aromatic oils, dried herbs, and medicinal substances filled the air. The heavy fragrance immediately made Talia's head swim, and she instinctively grimaced.

"Put her in here."

One of the mages crossed the laboratory and opened a door beside a tall display cabinet.

Although Talia had visited her mother's laboratory on numerous occasions, she had never been permitted to enter this particular room.

The elf carrying her stepped inside and lowered her onto a bed positioned in the centre of the chamber.

Talia looked around with mounting dread.

The room was immaculate, every object arranged with unsettling precision. Yet the strange instruments displayed along the walls and upon the surrounding tables were unlike anything she had ever encountered.

Some were fashioned from polished metal, while others possessed shapes and mechanisms whose purposes she could not begin to imagine.

Her frightened gaze returned to the two mages.

They had begun arranging various implements upon a nearby table, exchanging brief remarks as they worked.

What are they planning to do to me?

A dreadful comparison entered her mind.

Is this how a calf feels when it is brought into the slaughterhouse?

Cold perspiration gathered at the back of her neck. Had the spell not robbed her of movement, she would have screamed and struggled to escape.

Instead, she could only lie helplessly upon the bed and watch.

"I shall begin by examining the injury," one of the mages announced as he approached her legs.

Without waiting for permission, he lifted the hem of her gown.

Talia's entire body stiffened with terror.

The mage began removing the thick bandages around her injured leg. His movements were swift and precise, displaying none of the hesitation that had troubled the physicians who attended her during the journey.

Once the wound lay exposed, he examined it closely and gave a faint click of his tongue.

"This is considerably worse than I was led to expect."

He pressed his icy fingers around her knee, carefully feeling the damaged area beneath the skin.

Talia's breath caught as pain shot through her leg.

"The bone has been set with astonishing incompetence," he observed. "If it is allowed to heal in this position, the surrounding nerves will lose their function. She will never be able to walk properly upon this leg again."

His companion gave a low murmur of contempt.

"Such is the miserable standard of human magic."

The first mage continued his examination before reaching towards the table and selecting a small, sharp blade.

"There is no alternative. We shall have to break the bone once more and set it correctly."

A broken sob escaped Talia's constricted throat.

Break it again?

The words struck her with such horror that she could scarcely comprehend them.

She had already endured more pain than she believed possible. The thought of having her leg deliberately broken once more filled her with unbearable terror.

Only then did the mage appear to notice her deathly pale expression.

He lowered the cloth covering his mouth and offered what seemed intended to be a reassuring smile.

"There is no cause for such alarm, Your Highness."

But the sight brought Talia no comfort.

There was something profoundly unnatural about his expression. His lips curved in the manner of a smile, yet the gesture seemed entirely devoid of warmth, resembling the grotesque imitation of a human expression upon the face of a fish.

Her blood ran cold.

The mage continued speaking in the same indifferent tone.

"I cannot promise that the limb will be restored precisely to its former condition. Nevertheless, I shall see to it that you are able to walk without difficulty."

Talia struggled desperately to part her lips.

No... Stop!

If her leg could never be restored completely, what was the purpose of putting her through such dreadful suffering?

Then leave it alone! I cannot endure any more!

She wanted to scream those words at him, to beg him to abandon the procedure.

But no matter how fiercely she struggled, only a faint, choking sound escaped her throat.

The mage paid her little attention.

He drew the cloth back over his mouth and turned towards his companion.

"Prepare the slumberweed."

The second mage brought a small brazier to the bedside and placed a bundle of dried herbs inside it.

A moment later, he set them alight.

The pungent smoke began curling into the air.

Talia recognised the danger at once and held her breath.

She refused to inhale it. If she could only remain conscious, perhaps she might somehow prevent them from carrying out their dreadful intentions.

But her lungs soon began to ache.

Her chest tightened, and the desperate need for air became impossible to resist.

At last, she drew an involuntary breath.

The bitter smoke entered her lungs, and almost immediately, her vision began to blur.

The room seemed to sway around her. The figures of the mages grew indistinct, their voices fading into a distant murmur.

Talia fought to keep her eyes open, but her eyelids had become unbearably heavy.

Darkness gathered at the edges of her vision.

And then, as though she were sinking into a bottomless lake, consciousness slipped away.

✦ ✦ ✦

Rain was falling upon the lake.

The grey surface trembled beneath countless droplets, while the surrounding trees swayed in the cold wind.

Talia knew at once that she was dreaming.

A memory from many years ago unfolded before her, so vivid that she could almost feel the damp earth beneath her hands.

She was fourteen again.

Beneath the sprawling branches of an old tree, a younger Talia sat huddled against the trunk, glaring furiously at the rain-soaked lake as though the water itself had committed some unforgivable offence.

Her clothes were damp, her hair clung uncomfortably to her cheeks, and her temper had grown fouler with every passing minute.

Then footsteps approached through the wet grass.

Varkas appeared beside the tree, thoroughly drenched by the rain.

"Have you finally decided that our little game of hide-and-seek has come to an end?"

Talia raised her head and glared at him with undisguised hatred.

He had evidently searched every corner of the palace grounds before finding her. His usually immaculate uniform was soaked through, and the pale hair he took such care to keep neatly arranged had fallen into complete disarray.

Even so, the sight afforded her no satisfaction.

If anything, his composed expression made her angrier.

She reached down, scooped a handful of wet mud from the ground, and hurled it straight at him.

"Go away! I have no wish to see your face!"

The mud struck his embroidered velvet uniform, leaving several ugly dark stains across the expensive fabric.

Varkas did not so much as flinch.

His indifference only inflamed her temper further.

Talia gathered another fistful of mud and threw it at him.

"Did you not hear me? Leave me alone! Go and find Ayla instead!"

"Believe me, Your Highness, nothing would please me more."

He released a quiet breath before lowering himself onto one knee beside her.

"Unfortunately, my duty requires me to remain in your service until your sixteenth birthday."

Talia stared at him in outrage.

The reminder that she would be forced to endure his presence for another two years made her feel so miserable that tears threatened to spill from her eyes.

But she would rather have bitten her tongue than allow him to see her cry.

Instead, she widened her stinging eyes and twisted her mouth into the most contemptuous sneer she could manage.

"Two more years of having to look at that dreadful face of yours? How revolting! The very thought is enough to turn my stomach."

Varkas regarded her without answering.

His silence was unbearable.

Talia clenched her fists and continued, determined to wound him with every cruel word she could summon.

"I despise you more than anyone alive! You are hateful, disgusting, and utterly unbearable. Everything about you makes me sick!"

"Have you quite finished?"

"Certainly not!" she snapped. "And you smell like a horse!"

For the first time, something in his expression changed.

One of his brows rose ever so slightly.

Talia immediately faltered.

She knew perfectly well how absurd the accusation was.

Having spent much of his boyhood in a monastery, Varkas possessed an almost excessive devotion to cleanliness. His clothes were always immaculate, his appearance unfailingly neat, and he invariably carried the faint, pleasant fragrance of freshly washed linen and soap.

Even Talia, who delighted in finding fault with him, could not truthfully accuse him of neglecting his person.

He knows I am lying.

Embarrassed by her own childish insult, she hastily lowered her eyes.

Yet Varkas made no attempt to expose her foolishness.

Instead, he unfolded the cloak he had been carrying and draped it over her shoulders.

The heavy fabric settled around her with a soft rustle, shielding her trembling body from the cold rain.

Then he rose to his feet.

"You may continue telling me how thoroughly you despise me once we have returned to the palace," he said evenly. "For now, get to your feet. Your lips have already begun to turn blue."
