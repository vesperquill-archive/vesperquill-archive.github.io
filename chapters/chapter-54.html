Chapter 54

"And what concern is it of yours whether my lips turn purple, green, or any other dreadful colour?"

Talia snatched the cloak from her shoulders and flung it angrily onto the muddy ground. She raised her voice in defiance, only to hear Varkas release yet another weary sigh.

The sound made her eyes sting with tears.

He could smile so easily whenever he was in Ayla's company, yet whenever he stood before Talia, all he seemed capable of doing was sighing in exasperation. The unfairness of it made her heart ache.

She could scarcely bear to look at him.

"Even if I were to freeze to death beneath this tree, I would take great care not to inconvenience you!" she cried. "So go on! Run along to your precious twins' birthday celebration! You may flatter them and fuss over them to your heart's content!"

Varkas regarded her with his usual infuriating calm.

"Even if Your Highness remained here until morning, I very much doubt you would freeze to death," he replied. "At worst, you would catch a rather unpleasant cold."

"Then I shall die of the cold instead!"

Her shrill declaration rang through the rainy air.

Varkas swept a hand roughly through his damp hair, pushing the strands away from his eyes.

Talia flinched.

It was a small gesture, but she recognised the irritation behind it. He had always been so composed that even the slightest sign of displeasure seemed terribly significant.

Yet when he spoke again, his voice remained perfectly level.

"Tell me, Your Highness, what precisely must I do to persuade you to abandon this performance as a tragic heroine?"

His cutting remark sent a fresh wave of anger rushing through her.

There he goes again!

He never spoke to Ayla in such a manner. With her, he was unfailingly courteous and considerate, yet he seemed to take particular pleasure in answering Talia's complaints with the sharpest sarcasm.

It was enough to drive her mad.

Glaring at him through eyes bright with unshed tears, Talia suddenly reached towards her hair.

Her elaborate braids had been secured with a beautiful pearl pin. She seized it, pulled it free, and without the slightest hesitation, hurled it into the lake.

The ornament vanished beneath the rain-dappled surface.

"Go and fetch that for me," she demanded, lifting her chin. "If you bring it back, perhaps I shall stop."

Varkas narrowed his eyes.

Talia waited for the inevitable outburst.

Surely he would finally lose his temper. Perhaps his face would turn cold with anger, or he would simply abandon her beneath the tree and return to the palace.

Let him leave, then.

If he dared walk away, she would remain exactly where she was until she collapsed from the cold.

And when they find me lying here, lifeless and alone, let him suffer for what he has done!

She imagined the guilt that would consume him, and the thought offered a bitter sort of satisfaction.

Yet, as always, Varkas refused to behave as she expected.

Without a word, he began undoing the buttons of his uniform.

One by one, the fastenings came loose.

Talia stared at him in astonishment.

Then she gave a contemptuous little snort.

Oh, what a ridiculous performance.

He was surely pretending. He must have believed that the sight of him preparing to enter the lake would frighten her into withdrawing her demand.

There was no possibility that he would actually leap into the water merely to retrieve a pearl pin.

Determined not to give him the satisfaction of seeing her hesitate, Talia remained where she was and watched.

Varkas removed his knight's tunic and laid it neatly beneath the tree. He then bent to pull off his tall boots, placing them beside the discarded garment.

Talia's expression grew increasingly uncertain.

Does he truly intend to carry this foolishness through?

He unfastened the sword belt around his waist and set it aside as well.

Soon, he stood at the edge of the lake wearing nothing but his light trousers and a thin linen shirt, the fabric almost transparent from the rain.

For several moments, he remained motionless, gazing across the grey water.

Talia folded her arms stubbornly.

Just as I thought. He is waiting for me to surrender first.

She had scarcely finished the thought when Varkas suddenly plunged into the lake.

A sharp splash broke through the steady patter of rain.

Talia sprang to her feet.

The dark water closed over his head, swallowing him completely.

For an instant, she could only stare.

Then she stumbled towards the water's edge, searching frantically for any sign of movement beneath the murky surface.

"Varkas?"

No answer came.

The lake remained quiet, its surface disturbed only by the falling rain.

There was no trace of the eighteen-year-old young man who had grown so remarkably tall and imposing over the past two years.

Talia's heart began to pound.

"Varkas!"

Her voice carried across the water, only to fade into the rain.

Still, he did not appear.

A terrible fear seized her.

"S-stop this nonsense!" she shouted, her voice beginning to tremble. "Come out at once!"

A gust of wind swept across the lake, sending ripples over its surface.

But there was still no sign of him.

Talia felt as though something had tightened around her throat.

Why is he not coming back?

The thought was unbearable.

Without pausing to consider what she was doing, she rushed straight into the lake.

The cold water surged around her ankles, then her knees. Within a few hurried steps, it had reached her waist.

She splashed desperately through the water, beating her arms against its surface.

"Varkas! Varkas! Where are you?"

Her feet slipped over the treacherous mud beneath the lake. Submerged branches brushed against her legs, but she scarcely noticed.

She continued stumbling towards the deeper water.

Before she realised it, the freezing water had risen above her chest.

Her breathing became ragged.

She stretched her arms forward, clawing helplessly at the water as though she might somehow pull him back from its depths.

"Varkas!"

Tears mingled with the rain upon her cheeks.

"I was wrong!" she sobbed. "I-I am sorry! Please, just come back!"

Her voice broke into desperate cries.

"I shall never do it again! Please, Varkas!"

Just then, the water beside her surged violently.

A tall figure burst through the surface.

Talia froze.

Varkas emerged from the lake, throwing his head back to clear the water from his face. Streams of rain and lake water ran down his pale hair and across his cheeks.

He turned towards her, his blue eyes glimmering faintly beneath his drenched lashes.

"Here."

Reaching out, he rested one hand lightly upon her shoulder.

With the other, he held something before her.

Talia stared at his open palm.

"Shall we consider the matter settled now?" he asked.

There, resting upon his hand, was the pearl pin she had thrown into the lake.

For several moments, Talia could only look at it.

Then a strange, hollow laugh escaped her lips.

She raised a trembling hand to her forehead and began to giggle softly, almost helplessly.

The sound continued for a few moments before her expression abruptly changed.

Her face twisted with renewed fury.

Without warning, she snatched the pearl pin from Varkas's palm and hurled it as far into the lake as she could.

The ornament disappeared beneath the water once more.

Varkas watched it vanish.

Yet he offered no protest.

He did not even appear surprised.

That unchanging composure finally shattered what remained of Talia's restraint.

"You did that deliberately, did you not?" she cried, striking him sharply across the cheek. "You wanted to frighten me!"

The sound of the slap rang through the rainy air.

Varkas remained silent.

He neither raised his voice nor attempted to defend himself.

And somehow, his refusal to react made her angrier still.

Talia began striking his chest and shoulders with both fists.

"You were waiting for me to apologise! That was your plan all along!" she sobbed. "You hateful wretch! I despise you! I despise you more than anything!"

"That is quite enough."

Varkas caught both her wrists in a single swift movement.

His voice had grown low and firm.

Talia looked up at him, her eyes overflowing with tears. Her face was twisted with such bitter resentment that he released a long, exasperated breath.

"You have indulged yourself quite sufficiently for one day," he said. "Now put an end to this tantrum and come out of the water."

"No!"

She struggled violently against his hold.

"I shall never do as you say! I mean to stay right here until I drown, so leave me alone!"

Varkas paid no attention to her furious declaration.

Keeping a firm hold upon her, he dragged her towards the shore.

Talia resisted every step of the way.

Even after they reached the bank, she continued beating her fists against his back, shouting insults and demanding that he release her.

Varkas merely shook his head in disbelief.

He retrieved the cloak she had thrown into the mud and wrapped it tightly around her shivering body.

Then, before she could begin striking him again, he brought the sleeves together and tied them firmly, trapping her arms beneath the heavy fabric.

Talia stared at him in outrage.

She struggled desperately to free herself, but the knot held fast.

Unable to use her hands, she lifted her foot and kicked him sharply in the shin.

"Have you taken leave of your senses?" she shrieked. "How dare you bind an imperial princess like some common criminal? You brute!"

Varkas looked down at her.

"That is a rather remarkable accusation, coming from you."

He released another long sigh.

Then, before Talia could devise some fresh means of attacking him, he bent down and hoisted her over his shoulder.

She gave a furious screech.

"Put me down this instant!"

Varkas ignored her.

"I am a princess, you imbecile! A princess!" she cried, writhing like an enraged bird. "What manner of knight dares treat a member of the imperial family in such a disgraceful fashion?"

Without answering, Varkas gathered his sword and discarded uniform.

Then he began the long walk back towards the palace.

Rain continued to fall around them.

Talia struggled fiercely upon his shoulder, twisting and kicking whenever she could. She exhausted every insult she knew, hurling them at him with all the indignation of a thoroughly offended princess.

Yet Varkas continued walking without a word.

Eventually, her struggles grew weaker.

Her voice became hoarse, her limbs heavy, and at last, she hung limply over his shoulder, too exhausted to continue fighting.

He did not put her down until they reached the Separate Palace.

The moment her feet touched the ground, Talia glared at him with what little strength remained.

She was breathing so heavily that she could scarcely speak.

Then, overcome by exhaustion, she collapsed.

And that evening, precisely as Varkas had predicted, she developed a dreadful fever.

Her body burned beneath the bedcovers, while chills swept through her so fiercely that her teeth occasionally chattered.

Varkas brought a chair to her bedside and settled himself beside her.

There he remained, quietly turning the pages of a book.

Talia found the sight almost intolerable.

How could he sit there so peacefully when she was suffering so terribly?

His calm expression, his relaxed posture, and the soft rustle of turning pages seemed deliberately designed to provoke her.

Unable to endure it any longer, she sought another quarrel.

"I suppose you would be delighted if I fell ill every single day," she muttered resentfully.

Varkas did not even raise his eyes from the book.

"Occasionally," he replied, turning another page, "it is not altogether unpleasant."

Talia stared at him through fever-bright eyes.

Then, without another word, she pulled the covers over her face.

Occasionally...

She repeated the word silently.

Well, occasionally is certainly better than never.

It was a foolish thought, and some part of her knew it.

Yet she could not help feeling strangely pleased.

After all, Varkas had remained beside her rather than attending Ayla's birthday banquet.

He had chosen to sit in this quiet room, keeping watch over her fever, while the celebrations continued elsewhere.

For that alone, Talia felt she could generously forgive his cold remarks and infuriating manner.

Beneath the covers, a small smile tugged at her lips.

She pressed her eyes shut, determined to fall asleep before he noticed.

But the smile refused to disappear.

Despite the fever burning through her body, an inexplicable desire to laugh kept rising within her chest.

How utterly ridiculous.

That was always the trouble with Varkas.

Being near him could be unbearably painful. He made her angry, miserable, and so frustrated that she sometimes felt she could scarcely breathe.

And yet, at other moments, the mere knowledge that he was beside her filled her heart with such happiness that it seemed almost too much to contain.

There were times when she felt that no one in the world was closer to her than he was.

Perhaps... perhaps we are something rather like friends?

It was a shamelessly hopeful thought.

Nevertheless, she clung to it.

And so the years passed.

Day after day, the two of them remained together, their time filled with quarrels, misunderstandings, and the occasional moments of tenderness that Talia treasured far more than she dared admit.

Perhaps those years had been nothing but an exhausting obligation to Varkas.

Perhaps he had counted each passing day until he would finally be released from his duty.

But to Talia, those memories were more precious than jewels.

For all the misery she had endured, for all the loneliness that had weighed upon her young heart, his presence had given her something to hold on to.

It was because Varkas had been there that she had managed to endure those long, desolate days.

Yet now, when she looked back upon them, those cherished memories offered her no comfort.

What had once sustained her had become the very thing that bound her.

The past she had treasured so dearly had turned into an unbreakable chain, fastening itself around her heart and refusing to let her go.
