Chapter 24

As the young Crown Prince led his procession through the gates of Mordawin Monastery, hundreds of citizens scattered flower petals along the road to welcome him.

Gares raised his hand in acknowledgment, and the cheers grew louder. He had performed the same gesture countless times throughout the journey, yet the admiration of the people never failed to please him. Lifting his chin proudly, he urged his horse forward.

Beyond the crowded streets lay a spacious courtyard and a magnificent temple. Gares brought his knights to a halt before a building that appeared to serve as a prayer hall.

“Your Imperial Highness, you must be weary after such a long journey.”

A man dressed in immaculate white monastic robes stepped forward to greet him.

Gares examined the stranger from horseback. He possessed sharp, refined features and pale silver hair touched with a faint bluish hue. His complexion was unusually fair, and the slight points of his ears revealed that he was not entirely human.

He was likely of half-elven or quarter-elven descent.

Such appearances were not uncommon in the northeastern territories of the former Kingdom of Osiria, where the blood of elves and dwarves had long mingled with that of mankind.

Suppressing his natural distaste for those of mixed ancestry, Gares addressed him with imperial dignity.

“Are you the Abbot of this monastery?”

“I am, Your Imperial Highness. My name is Basilis, and I have been entrusted with the administration of Mordawin Monastery.”

The young Abbot offered a courteous smile.

“It is my great honour to welcome Your Highness to Mordawin.”

Gares dismounted with the proud bearing customary to the imperial family.

“This is the very land upon which my ancestor, the Great Emperor Darian, achieved his first victory against the North. It is also the sacred place where he received the divine revelation that led him to unite the nations. I am therefore greatly pleased to stand upon this ground.”

He paused before continuing.

“In accordance with imperial tradition, I wish to receive the blessings of the saints. Offer your prayers that divine grace may guide both my sister and myself in the years to come.”

“I shall do so with the greatest pleasure, Your Imperial Highness.”

Basilis bowed respectfully before adding, with some hesitation, “Might I suggest that Your Highness first retire to my private residence and recover from the fatigue of your travels? We have spent several days preparing a banquet in honour of your arrival.”

Gares considered the invitation.

According to the original arrangements, the imperial party was to lodge in the quarters reserved for pilgrims. Accepting accommodation within the Abbot's private residence might be interpreted as granting him a particular political favour.

He glanced over his shoulder towards Varkas, who stood behind him like a silent shadow.

The knight had drawn his long hood low, concealing nearly half his face, perhaps to avoid attracting unnecessary attention.

Varkas surveyed the monastery thoughtfully before giving a slight nod.

“Your Highness may do as you please.”

“Very well,” Gares declared. “We shall accept the Abbot's hospitality for this evening.”

At once, the waiting servants descended the steps in orderly fashion to attend to the imperial guests.

Gares surrendered his reins and turned towards Varkas.

“See that Ayla is properly cared for. She has never travelled such a distance before, and these unfamiliar surroundings must be most uncomfortable for her.”

Varkas, who had been gently patting his horse, acknowledged the instruction with a slight inclination of his head.

Gares frowned.

He sometimes wished the man would bestow even half the affection upon his sister that he appeared to reserve for his horse.

Muttering under his breath, the Crown Prince turned to follow the monks. Before he had gone far, however, an ornate carriage standing at the edge of the courtyard caught his attention.

His gaze settled upon its heavily curtained windows.

Talia Roem Guirta had remained inside her carriage for nearly the entire journey, scarcely showing her face.

Whether she had finally learned her place or was merely concealing some new mischief, Gares could not say.

He had been fully prepared to punish her the moment she caused any disturbance. Her unexpected silence had almost rendered that determination unnecessary.

It would be best if she continued to behave herself.

Yet he knew better than to trust such good fortune.

Talia had insisted upon joining the pilgrimage with questionable intentions. There was no telling what trouble she might cause, nor when she might choose to cause it.

Still regarding the carriage with displeasure, Gares called sharply to Varkas.

“And make certain that girl understands she is to remain as quiet as a mouse and keep herself out of my sight, just as she has done thus far.”

Varkas's eyes narrowed almost imperceptibly.

Gares noticed the reaction at once. The knight evidently disapproved of his openly hostile treatment of his half-sister.

Indeed, Varkas had recently taken to reminding him, rather unusually, that he ought to exercise greater caution in his words and conduct for the sake of his reputation.

Gares gave a contemptuous snort.

What purpose was there in concealing his feelings? Everyone in the Empire knew perfectly well how much the Crown Prince despised his father's illegitimate daughter.

With that thought, he turned away and followed the monks towards the grand residence behind the temple.

The Abbot's mansion was scarcely less luxurious than an annex of the Imperial Palace. Its splendid appearance brought a satisfied smile to Gares's lips.

At least he would enjoy a comfortable night's rest.

Basilis personally conducted him to the finest chamber in the residence.

Gares surveyed the spacious bedroom with a critical eye. It was evidently the Abbot's own chamber, for paintings depicting holy wars adorned the walls, while a prayer book and several volumes of theology rested upon the writing desk.

The decorations were not entirely to his taste, but the accommodations were otherwise satisfactory.

Removing his cloak, which had acquired the unpleasant scent of horses during the journey, he addressed the servants waiting by the door.

“I wish to bathe before dinner. Have a large tub prepared with clean water, and see that it is spacious enough for me to stretch my legs.”

The servants immediately departed to carry out his instructions.

Gares settled into a chair beside the window and gestured towards the two squires who had accompanied him.

At his silent command, the boys approached and began removing his armour.

Meanwhile, he reached for a wineglass resting upon a nearby shelf. An attentive servant promptly filled it.

Leaning back comfortably, Gares took a sip.

The wine was cool and rich, its deep flavour spreading pleasantly across his tongue. He savoured it for a moment before releasing a contented sigh.

Perhaps the evening banquet would prove more enjoyable than he had expected.

He had tasted some of the rarest wines in the Empire, yet the monastery's selection was remarkably agreeable.

The business of maintaining a holy sanctuary must be exceedingly profitable.

A faint smirk appeared upon his lips as he gazed through the window at the extensive monastery grounds.

High-ranking members of the clergy frequently possessed fortunes comparable to those of the nobility. Judging by the splendour of this residence, the Abbot of Mordawin enjoyed a life scarcely less extravagant than that of a distinguished aristocrat.

Once his armour had been removed, Gares discarded his sweat-soaked garments and entered the bath prepared by the servants.

The warm water offered immediate relief to his weary limbs.

Attendants began washing him with soft brushes while he rested his head against the edge of the tub and leisurely finished his wine.

He remained there for some time, allowing the warmth to ease the exhaustion brought on by half a day's riding.

When he finally emerged, much of his strength had returned.

The servants dressed him in suitable evening attire for the summer season, and he completed his appearance with a velvet outer robe bearing only modest ornamentation.

He then left the chamber under the guidance of the monks.

“The evening meal has been prepared in the hall directly below, Your Imperial Highness,” explained the monk carrying a lantern as they descended the carpeted marble staircase.

Gares acknowledged the information with a slight nod.

He saw little reason to engage in unnecessary conversation. A future monarch ought to understand the value of speaking sparingly.

Indeed, he had learned the power of silence from the man who had accompanied him since childhood.

As he approached the dining hall, he found Varkas standing near the entrance, apparently awaiting his arrival.

Gares's brows drew together.

For reasons he could never fully explain, the sight of Varkas often stirred an unpleasant sense of hostility within him.

It was strange, considering that the knight had never openly defied him.

Perhaps it was the man's imposing presence, or the difficulty of discerning what lay behind his perpetually composed expression.

Gares had known him since they were boys, yet Varkas still seemed as distant and unreadable as a stranger.

That uncertainty troubled him more than he cared to admit.

Can I truly entrust my other half to this man?

“Where is Ayla?” he asked.

“Her Highness is resting in the quarters reserved for the priestesses. She was fatigued by the journey and has chosen not to attend the banquet.”

“That is hardly surprising. She has endured several nights of sleeping outdoors.”

“I have provided her with medicine to assist her recovery. There is no cause for serious concern.”

Gares frowned at the indifferent manner in which the words were delivered.

He knew that Varkas could speak more gently to Ayla when he chose to do so. Yet even towards his own fiancée, the man displayed remarkably little warmth.

For a gentleman in the prime of his life, Varkas Raedgo Sierkhan possessed an extraordinary indifference towards women.

He treated most of them as though they were no more interesting than stones upon the roadside. Those who attempted to approach him were met with such coldness that even Gares sometimes found it uncomfortable to witness.

Perhaps he ought to be grateful that Varkas at least permitted Ayla to remain close to him.

Nevertheless, his restrained affection was far from satisfactory.

Ayla was the most precious treasure of the imperial family. To have been granted her hand in marriage and yet display so little gratitude seemed an intolerable arrogance.

Gares addressed him in a sharper tone.

“Ayla is your fiancée. Should you not show a little more devotion when attending to her?”
