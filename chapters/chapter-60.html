Chapter 60

Talia stood with both feet firmly upon the marble floor, straightening her back as much as her weakened body would allow.

A sharp, stinging pain travelled from her knees all the way up her spine. She must have injured herself further when she fell earlier, for even the slightest movement now sent an unpleasant ache through her body. Though she longed to sink to the floor and surrender to her exhaustion, she forced herself to remain upright, arranging her features into an expression of composure as she faced her father.

Nearly half a year had passed since she had last seen him. Even then, she had caught no more than a fleeting glimpse of his figure from afar. Now, standing before him, she found that looking upon her own father felt no different from facing a complete stranger.

The Emperor appeared to share the sentiment.

A faint expression of distaste crossed his features before he turned his eyes away.

“I have been informed that you have suffered a most unfortunate ordeal.”

His voice was flat and detached, as though he were discussing the misfortunes of some distant household rather than those of his own daughter.

“I am also told that your injuries have left your body permanently disfigured.”

Talia's expression hardened.

To her, the man seated upon the throne was no more familiar than a stranger encountered upon the street. Yet here he was, speaking so carelessly of the wounds she had struggled desperately to conceal, as though her suffering were merely a subject for idle conversation.

Anger flared within her chest.

“Is that the reason Your Majesty has summoned me?” she asked coldly. “To satisfy your curiosity concerning my misfortune?”

A sharp, bitter laugh escaped her lips as she deliberately smoothed the folds of her skirt.

“Would Your Majesty care to examine me personally? Shall I display my injuries so that you may judge for yourself just how terribly I have been ruined?”

Virus Roem Guirta drew his thick brows together in a deep frown.

Talia braced herself for an angry reprimand, certain that her insolence would provoke his wrath. Yet, to her surprise, the Emperor said nothing.

Instead, his gaze lingered upon her face.

For a brief moment, he regarded her with an unfamiliar expression, almost as though he were seeing his daughter properly for the very first time.

“There is no necessity for such an examination,” he replied at last.

His words were blunt, and he soon turned his attention towards Varkas.

Talia watched the Emperor's expression change as his eyes settled upon the young man standing beside her. There was something complicated in his gaze, as though several unpleasant considerations were weighing upon his mind.

A faint uneasiness crept along the back of her neck.

Why has he summoned us both?

She looked between the two men, her suspicion deepening with every passing moment.

At length, the Emperor broke the silence.

“Varkas Raedgo Sierkhan, I entrusted you with the safety of my children, placing my faith in your ability to protect them. Yet this is the unfortunate result of that trust.”

Talia felt the blood drain from her face.

The Emperor rested one hand upon the arm of his throne, tapping his fingers against it as he continued in a grave voice.

“Through your failure to fulfil that duty, the Second Princess has suffered grievous injuries, leaving her with damage that may never be remedied. Do you acknowledge that responsibility for this misfortune rests upon your shoulders?”

Varkas turned his eyes towards Talia for the briefest moment.

Then he looked away.

“I acknowledge my failure, Your Majesty.”

His voice was utterly devoid of emotion.

There was neither indignation nor remorse in his reply, nor the slightest indication that he intended to offer an excuse. He spoke with the barren calm of a man acknowledging a fact that could not be disputed.

“I shall willingly submit to whatever punishment Your Majesty sees fit to impose.”

Before she could stop herself, Talia took a step forward.

He did nothing wrong.

The words rose instinctively to her throat.

Yet her lips remained tightly sealed.

Did he truly do nothing wrong?

Her gaze dropped towards her legs, which had begun to tremble faintly beneath the heavy folds of her gown. Then she slowly raised her eyes towards Varkas's composed profile.

An image flashed across her mind.

She saw him running away from her.

She saw his back disappearing into the distance while she remained trapped beneath the darkness and rubble.

That sight had been branded so deeply into her memory that she knew she would carry it for the rest of her life.

And the recollection robbed her of every word she had intended to speak.

“Since you acknowledge your responsibility so readily, there is little need to prolong this discussion.”

The Emperor released a low sigh before continuing.

“The marriage arrangement between the Second Princess and the count's household has been rendered impossible by this unfortunate incident. Furthermore, given the injuries she has sustained, securing another suitable marriage prospect befitting her imperial station will prove exceedingly difficult.”

Talia stood motionless.

The Emperor spoke of her future as though she were not standing directly before him. Her ruined body, her broken engagement, and the difficulty of finding another man willing to marry her were discussed with the same indifference one might show towards an inconvenient political arrangement.

He cast a brief glance at her pale face before continuing.

“It is therefore only fitting that you assume responsibility for the consequences of your failure.”

His gaze settled firmly upon Varkas.

“You shall dissolve your engagement to Ayla Roem Guirta and take the Second Princess, Talia Roem Guirta, as your lawful wife.”

Talia's breath caught.

For several moments, she could not comprehend what she had just heard.

She stared blankly at the Emperor, her mind struggling to make sense of his words. Then, almost instinctively, she turned towards Senevier.

The Empress was smiling.

It was a thin, calculated smile, resembling that of a venomous serpent watching its helpless prey.

A dreadful chill travelled down Talia's spine.

Cold perspiration broke out across her skin.

She immediately lowered her head, lacking the courage to look upon Varkas's face.

No...

Surely he will never agree to such madness.

Varkas was the heir to one of the Empire's most powerful noble houses. His position was not one that could be treated lightly, even by the Emperor himself.

He was the future Grand Duke of the East.

Surely no sovereign, however powerful, possessed the right to force such a humiliating marriage upon him.

He will refuse.

Of course he will.

He has every right to reject this command.

Talia clenched the fabric of her skirts until her knuckles turned white.

The silence stretched on.

Each passing moment seemed longer than the last, until the seconds felt like hours.

Then Varkas finally spoke.

“If such an arrangement is also agreeable to Her Highness the Second Princess, I shall abide by Your Majesty's command.”

Talia's head snapped upwards.

His dark, piercing eyes were fixed upon her.

He spoke slowly and deliberately, as though weighing each word before allowing it to leave his lips.

“I shall take Her Highness as my wife.”

For an instant, Talia's heart seemed to cease beating.

She stared at him in utter disbelief, her gaze fixed upon the lips from which those impossible words had emerged.

What...?

What did he just say?

Had she truly heard him correctly?

Surely her ears had deceived her.

Perhaps the pain and exhaustion had finally overwhelmed her senses, causing her to imagine things that could never happen.

Yes. That must be it.

I must be imagining this.

There could be no world in which Varkas would willingly speak of taking her as his wife.

“You have heard Lord Sierkhan's answer, Princess.”

The Emperor's voice cut through the haze of her bewilderment, dragging her thoughts back to the present.

“Should you give your consent, the position of Grand Duchess of Sierkhan shall be yours. What answer do you offer?”

There was something faintly cynical beneath his words.

And only then did Talia begin to understand.

He expects me to refuse.

The Emperor had made his decree, perhaps because Senevier had pressed him into doing so. Yet Talia could scarcely believe that he genuinely wished to see his illegitimate daughter take the place intended for Ayla.

Ayla was the daughter favoured by the Empress, the princess whose marriage to the heir of Sierkhan had already been arranged.

Surely the Emperor had no desire to see that union destroyed.

He is counting upon my refusal.

Perhaps Varkas had reached the same conclusion.

Perhaps he had agreed only because he was certain she would recoil from the proposal, throw one of her customary fits of temper, and reject the marriage herself.

Yes... That must be the reason.

A strange, hollow calm gradually settled over her.

If she accepted, the man she had desired for so many years would become hers.

Varkas would be her husband.

The very man she had longed for throughout her life would stand beside her, bound to her by marriage.

Yet as that possibility took shape within her mind, another thought followed.

Is this truly what I want?

Talia lowered her gaze towards the legs concealed beneath her silk gown.

Her body had been ruined.

The mere thought of exposing her scarred and twisted flesh before Varkas made her stomach churn.

What if he looked upon her with disgust?

What if she saw pity in those cold eyes?

She could endure his indifference. She had endured it for years.

But to witness him recoil from her ruined appearance, or to see him regard her with the compassion one might offer a pitiful, broken creature...

No.

I could never bear it.

If such an expression ever crossed his face, whatever remained of her sanity would surely crumble.

She would be broken beyond all hope of repair.

Slowly, Talia raised her eyes towards him once more.

This was the man who had always turned his face away from her.

The man who had repeatedly shown her his back.

The man who had chosen to save Ayla, leaving Talia behind in the darkness.

A man who would never love her.

Not in this lifetime.

Perhaps not even in death.

Could I truly endure spending the rest of my life beside him?

Could I willingly bind myself to such misery?

Her throat tightened.

“I... I...”

Talia parted her dry lips, struggling to force the words out.

She could not do it.

She could not bear to be hurt any further.

Whatever foolish affection she had once cherished, whatever desperate hopes she had clung to, she knew that accepting this marriage would only condemn her to a lifetime of torment.

I must refuse.

I cannot allow myself to be broken again.

The words trembled upon her tongue.

But just as she was about to speak, her gaze fell upon Senevier.

The Empress was no longer smiling.

Her beautiful face had become cold and expressionless, and the look she directed towards Talia carried a silent warning.

It was a gaze filled with venom, one that promised there would be consequences if the princess dared disappoint her.

Talia's stomach twisted painfully.

“I...”

Her voice faltered.

She looked towards Varkas, then the Emperor, and finally back at Senevier.

Their faces seemed to blur together.

The chamber began to spin around her, the magnificent walls and gleaming throne dissolving into a sickening whirl of colour.

A violent wave of dizziness struck her.

Talia raised both hands towards her temples, desperately trying to steady herself.

Cold sweat covered her forehead. She dragged her sleeve across her brow, but the moisture continued to gather, trickling into the corners of her eyes and making them sting.

Irritated, she attempted to wipe it away.

Then the world tilted.

The floor seemed to shift beneath her feet, and her weakened knees suddenly gave way.

Before she could catch herself, her body struck the cold marble.

The impact drove the breath from her lungs.

A shrill cry echoed somewhere in the distance.

She thought she could hear the Empress's maids screaming in alarm, but their voices sounded strangely faint, as though they were coming from the far end of a long corridor.

Talia lay sprawled upon the floor, struggling to keep her eyes open.

Her vision had grown dim and cloudy, as though she were looking at the world through a sheet of frosted glass.

Through the haze, she could just make out a familiar face.

Varkas.

He was looking at her.

His expression appeared strangely frozen, and his face seemed to have turned deathly pale.

For a fleeting moment, Talia wondered whether what she saw was real.

Has he truly gone pale?

Because of me?

The thought was almost laughable.

Surely it was nothing more than a foolish illusion born from the lingering affection she had never managed to extinguish.

Yes... It must be my imagination.

This was a man who had shown scarcely any emotion even after witnessing the dreadful state of her injuries.

Why would he suddenly turn pale merely because she had collapsed?

There could be no reason for it.

A crushing exhaustion spread through her limbs.

Talia struggled desperately to keep her eyes open, clinging to the fading image of his face with what little strength remained.

But at last, even that effort became too much.

Her eyelids slowly fell.

And she surrendered to the darkness.

News of the broken engagement between the heir of Sierkhan and the First Princess, and of the Second Princess being chosen to take her place, spread throughout the Imperial Palace like wildfire.
