Chapter 21

Edric glanced between his superior's retreating figure and the disapproving faces of the citizens before urging his horse forward with a weary sigh.

The Second Imperial Princess had remained concealed within her carriage throughout the procession, as though her refusal to appear before the people were a silent declaration of displeasure. It was not until they had left the capital behind and halted beside the river for their evening rest that she finally emerged.

The servants, who were occupied with pitching tents and preparing the encampment, regarded her with apprehensive eyes.

Talia's earlier declaration that she intended to change her attire several times a day had evidently been no idle threat. She now wore a magnificent silk gown embroidered with golden thread, entirely different from the one in which she had departed the Imperial Palace.

Around her slender, pale neck rested a necklace of such extraordinary value that its price might have purchased a respectable castle. Her delicate wrists, scarcely thicker than young birch branches, were adorned with elaborate bracelets set with glittering diamonds.

Watching her from a distance, Edric could scarcely believe his eyes.

They had merely stopped to dine and spend the night beneath the stars. What possible reason could there be for dressing with such extravagance? The Princess's behaviour was so utterly beyond his understanding that he already felt exhausted by the prospect of attending her throughout the journey.

Suppressing another sigh, he handed his horse's reins to a waiting squire and approached her.

Talia, who had been surveying the encampment with a displeased expression, immediately turned a fierce gaze upon him.

For one extraordinary moment, Edric forgot what he had intended to say.

Beneath the fading splendour of the sunset, the Princess appeared almost as though she had been fashioned from fire and gold. The strong breeze sweeping across the river lifted her wheat-coloured hair into a magnificent golden cloud around her face, while her flawless, chalcedony-pale complexion possessed a cool radiance even amid the crimson evening light.

Edric unconsciously took a step backwards.

There was something almost unsettling about her beauty. Every feature seemed so exquisitely delicate that he feared she might shatter at the slightest touch, scattering fragments sharp enough to wound anyone who ventured too near.

A strange uneasiness passed through him, though he could not have explained its cause.

Then her crimson lips parted, and the illusion was abruptly broken.

“I dislike this place. Have the camp moved elsewhere.”

For an instant, Edric could only stare at her.

The sheer absurdity of the demand restored his senses more effectively than a bucket of cold water.

He straightened his shoulders and reminded himself that this beautiful creature was rather like a poisonous mushroom. However pleasing its appearance, anyone foolish enough to approach without caution would undoubtedly suffer for it.

Adopting the firm, respectful manner expected of an imperial knight, he replied, “This location was selected by the Imperial Guard for reasons of safety, Your Highness. We cannot abandon it and search for another at this hour.”

“Did I ask for your opinion?”

Talia fixed him with a gaze as cold as winter frost.

“I commanded you to move the camp. Your duty is merely to obey.”

Edric paused, silently gathering what remained of his patience.

“Lord Sierkhan has been entrusted with authority over the arrangements of this expedition. I must repeat that we cannot alter the formation or move the encampment without his permission.”

“So it is Varkas's permission that I require?”

Before Edric could answer, Talia turned sharply and began walking away.

He stared after her in disbelief. It seemed that she had been seeking an excuse to quarrel with Lord Sierkhan from the very beginning.

With a muttered sigh, he hurried after her.

They had scarcely completed the first day of their journey, yet the Princess appeared determined to create as much disorder as possible. Edric could not comprehend how anyone might possess such an inexhaustible appetite for conflict.

His longer strides soon brought him alongside her.

“May I ask precisely what Your Highness finds so objectionable about this place?”

“Everything,” she snapped without slowing.

“I detest being so near the water, and I detest being so near the forest. The whole place will be crawling with insects.”

“But how are we to establish a camp without access to water? Moreover, griffins and harpies have been known to appear in this region. The forest provides a natural barrier against attacks by larger monsters—”

“Safety? What nonsense are you speaking of?”

Talia rounded upon him indignantly.

“I have already been bitten twice by those wretched flying creatures! If I remain here throughout the night, the vermin will devour my skin. And what of that dreadful noise coming from the woods? I cannot endure the constant rustling of the leaves, and with the birds making such a racket, how am I expected to sleep?”

Edric looked down at her in astonishment.

Even a spoiled child of five, he thought, would scarcely have made such an unreasonable complaint.

He struggled to suppress the irritation rising within him.

“Your Highness, we shall be required to camp outdoors for several nights. Unless you begin to accustom yourself to such inconveniences—”

“Then move the camp!” she interrupted shrilly. “Why should I be obliged to endure something I find intolerable?”

With that, she swept past him and continued through the encampment at a furious pace, her determination resembling that of a racehorse charging towards the finish.

Edric clenched his hands at his sides. It required considerable restraint not to seize her by the arm and put an end to this foolishness himself.

“The men are exhausted after an entire day's march,” he said sternly, stepping into her path. “We cannot strike the tents and wander about the countryside merely because Your Highness dislikes a few insects. I suggest you cease behaving like a spoiled child and return to your carriage to rest.”

The beautiful face before him twisted with anger.

An instant later, a sharp crack rang through the evening air.

Edric felt a burning pain spread across his right cheek.

For several moments, he stood motionless, scarcely believing that she had struck him.

His eyes hardened as he looked at her.

When he had first been appointed to the Princess's service, he had expected to endure a certain amount of physical mistreatment. Yet the humiliation of receiving such a blow proved far more offensive than he had imagined.

“How dare you presume to command me?”

Before he could recover from the insult, Talia seized the front of his uniform and drew him towards her.

Her voice fell to a furious hiss.

“I am the Emperor's daughter, and you exist to serve my convenience. If I command you to walk throughout the night, then you shall walk until dawn. That is the duty for which you were appointed. Now hold your tongue and remove yourself from my sight.”

She thrust him away and resumed her hurried progress along the riverbank.

Edric remained where he stood, staring after the small, retreating figure with an expression of grim resentment.

Had he been free to act according to his own wishes, he would have lifted the ill-mannered Princess over his shoulder and deposited her inside her carriage, precisely as his superior had done earlier that day.

Unfortunately, Edric possessed neither the rank nor the influence to lay hands upon a member of the imperial family without suffering severe consequences.

He could only watch as she disappeared farther along the river.

At last, he released a long, bitter sigh.

Was he truly expected to endure the tyranny of this wicked woman until the pilgrimage came to an end?

The very thought was enough to make his head ache.

As Talia walked beside the river, she became increasingly conscious of the glances directed towards her.

An unpleasant prickling sensation crept across her back, and she turned abruptly to look over her shoulder.

Several servants who had been watching her immediately averted their eyes, hastily pretending to occupy themselves with their duties.

Such behaviour was hardly unfamiliar. Had people not always regarded her as though she were a barrel of gunpowder, liable to explode at the slightest provocation?

Yet there was something about their glances this evening that troubled her. They seemed cautious, certainly, but there was also an unsettling persistence in the way their eyes followed her whenever they believed she was not looking.

Surely Senevia must have planted a spy among them.

Talia examined the faces of the attendants one by one, her suspicion growing with every passing moment.

Perhaps there was not merely one spy. Perhaps every servant her mother had sent was secretly acting under her instructions.

It would hardly have been beyond Senevia's capabilities.

For years, the Empress had devoted herself to weakening the Crown Prince's position and undermining those who supported him. This pilgrimage might provide the perfect opportunity to remove every obstacle standing between her son and the imperial throne.

The thought sent a sudden chill through Talia.

Her heart began to beat unevenly.

If Senevia intended to harm Gares or Ayla, Talia would have felt little concern. Indeed, depending upon the nature of the scheme, she might even have been willing to lend her assistance.

But if her mother's intended victim were Varkas…

The possibility struck her with such force that she stopped walking.

Her anxious gaze swept across the rows of military tents.

She could not allow such a thing to happen.

Whatever Senevia might be planning, Talia had to ensure that Varkas remained beyond the reach of her mother's servants.

The camps must be separated.

If she could persuade him to establish their quarters at some distance from the others, it would be considerably more difficult for Senevia's agents to carry out any secret design during the night.

With this thought firmly established in her mind, Talia quickened her steps.

Before long, she recognised a familiar grey stallion tethered before one of the larger tents.

Torque.

The warhorse was among Varkas's most cherished possessions, and Talia knew him well. As she passed, the great animal lifted his head and gave a loud snort, apparently recognising her.

She paid him little attention and went directly into the tent.

The interior was dimly illuminated by a candelabrum, its wavering flames casting long shadows across the neatly arranged furnishings. Everything was kept in such immaculate order that the place might almost have been a small sanctuary rather than the temporary quarters of a military commander.

A finely made storage chest stood against one wall, while a rack nearby held pieces of armour arranged with meticulous care.

Talia surveyed the familiar surroundings before noticing a dark silhouette beyond a partition at the rear of the tent.

Without hesitation, she marched towards it.

“I wish to have my camp moved elsewhere,” she announced indignantly. “Yet my exceedingly gallant knight informs me that I cannot go anywhere without Lord Sierkhan's permission.”

She continued speaking as she approached the partition, her irritation mounting with every word.

“It would seem your subordinates have come to believe that the Commander of the Roem Knights possesses greater authority than the imperial family itself. I cannot imagine how the Imperial Guard has fallen into such a disgraceful state that its own members no longer understand the proper order of rank—”

Talia seized the edge of the partition and drew it aside.

Then she stopped.

The remainder of her complaint died upon her lips.

Varkas stood on the other side, bare from the waist upwards, a towel in his hand as he wiped the water from his face.

For a moment, Talia could do nothing but stare.
