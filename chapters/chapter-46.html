Chapter 46

The moment Princess Ayla finished speaking, a strange, rasping laugh escaped Gareth's lips. It was a thin, unpleasant sound, almost like air escaping from a punctured vessel.

"I see..." he murmured, his mouth twisting into a cruel smile. "I suppose presenting the Empress with that wretched girl's corpse would not be such a terrible idea after all."

The thought appeared to amuse him greatly, for his quiet laughter soon deepened into a satisfied chuckle. He reached out and placed a heavy hand upon Varkas's shoulder, leaning closer as though sharing some private amusement.

"Very well, then. Since you insist upon being so stubborn, I shall not trouble myself to stop you. Go ahead and search for her to your heart's content. Spend the entire night turning over every rock in those mountains if that is what pleases you."

With a forceful pat upon the commander's shoulder, Gareth turned away and strode across the ruined campsite, his attendants hurrying after him.

Ayla prepared to follow her brother. Before departing, however, she turned towards Varkas and regarded her betrothed with her usual calm expression.

"Then I shall leave the child in your care, Lord Sierkhan."

Having spoken those words, she quietly took her leave.

As soon as the royal siblings had departed, the search party set out towards the northern forest.

Edric led the way with a torch held firmly in one hand, its flickering flame casting a narrow circle of light upon the path ahead. The forest was shrouded in such heavy darkness that he could scarcely distinguish the trees beyond a few paces.

His heart grew increasingly uneasy as they hurried forward. Too much time had already been wasted arguing with the Crown Prince, and he feared that the traces left by the fallen wyvern might have become difficult to follow.

"Mind your footing, Edric. If you continue rushing about like that, you may find yourself at the bottom of the ravine before we even begin searching."

The warning came from Sir Theoric Hart, the senior knight walking beside him. His voice was low but carried a note of genuine concern.

Edric gave a quick nod without answering. He raised his torch higher and continued examining the uneven ground, determined not to overlook a single sign.

Before long, the trees began to thin, revealing a stretch of jagged rocks and a steep descent beyond the dark trunks of the conifers. The ground dropped sharply into a ravine, its depths concealed beneath the shadows.

Edric stopped and pointed towards the edge.

"This is the place, Commander."

Varkas, who had followed the party in silence, stepped forward.

Edric immediately moved aside, allowing his superior to inspect the terrain. Varkas studied the steep slope for a moment, then drew a hooked chain from the equipment secured at his waist.

Without a word, he fastened the hook firmly into a narrow opening between the rocks. Once he was satisfied that it would hold his weight, he lowered himself over the edge and began descending the nearly vertical cliff.

Edric remained above, keeping his torch directed towards the commander's descent. Only after he saw Varkas reach the bottom safely did he begin climbing down himself.

Fortunately, the ravine was not as deep as it had first appeared. By carefully finding footholds among the protruding rocks, Edric managed to reach the valley floor without difficulty.

He immediately raised his torch to signal the knights waiting above.

Seeing the flame, the remaining men began their descent.

Edric waited until they had secured their footing before turning his attention to the surrounding area.

The impact of the wyvern's fall had clearly caused considerable damage. Large stones had broken away from the canyon walls, and loose earth and shattered rocks were scattered across the ground. In several places, entire sections of the slope appeared to have collapsed.

Searching through such a disorderly landscape would not be easy.

Edric looked around anxiously, lifting his torch as high as he could. The darkness made it difficult to distinguish one mound of rubble from another, and every passing moment seemed unbearably precious.

He climbed over a fallen slab of stone and hurried across the uneven ground. Then, through the faint light, he noticed a large, dark shape lying against a pile of broken rocks.

He approached cautiously and brought his torch closer.

It seems we have found the right place.

The enormous carcass of a wyvern lay sprawled across the rubble. One of its wings had been burned almost entirely black, as though it had been caught in a great fire.

Edric's heart tightened.

If Her Highness fell with this creature...

He feared that the princess might have been crushed beneath its enormous body.

Holding the torch before him, he began circling the carcass, carefully examining the surrounding ground. But before he had gone very far, he noticed a familiar figure standing a short distance away.

Varkas was beside a large boulder, his tall figure almost blending into the darkness.

Edric immediately hurried towards him.

"Commander! Have you found Her Highness—"

The rest of his words died in his throat.

There, upon the cold earth, lay the motionless figure of a young woman.

Edric stopped abruptly.

The torch trembled in his hand, its unsteady light falling upon Talia's pale face and dishevelled hair. Her complexion was almost entirely drained of colour, and her body lay so still that, for one dreadful moment, he feared she had already passed away.

Then his gaze travelled lower.

A broken sound escaped his lips.

Several heavy stones had fallen across the lower half of her body, trapping her beneath their weight. Her legs were terribly injured, and the sight of them filled him with horror.

Edric had known from the beginning that her chances of surviving such a fall were exceedingly small. He had tried to prepare himself for the worst, knowing that they might discover nothing more than her remains.

Yet seeing her in such a dreadful condition was far more painful than he had imagined.

His blood seemed to turn cold.

Forcing himself to move, he approached her on unsteady legs.

Her upper body appeared to have escaped the worst of the impact, but her lower limbs had suffered terribly. One of her legs was pinned beneath an enormous slab of stone, twisted at an angle no living limb should ever have taken.

Edric immediately bent down and began moving the smaller rocks away.

As he pushed aside a heavy stone, several loose fragments slipped from above and tumbled across the rubble. He stopped briefly, realising that removing the debris carelessly might cause further harm.

He needed assistance.

"Commander, please—"

Edric raised his head, intending to ask Varkas for help.

But the words caught in his throat.

Varkas stood perfectly still beside the injured princess.

He was looking down at her, yet his expression was so strangely empty that Edric could scarcely recognise him.

There was no visible anger, no alarm, and not even the slightest movement upon his face. His eyes remained fixed upon Talia, but the cold, distant look within them seemed almost devoid of life.

A shiver ran down Edric's spine.

He knew that Varkas and the Second Princess had never been on good terms. Their relationship had been marked by years of resentment and bitter quarrels.

But surely that could not explain such a reaction.

Had they not known one another since childhood?

Whatever feelings Varkas might harbour towards her, how could he stand so motionless before someone who had been so terribly injured?

Edric's voice rose with desperate urgency.

"Commander! What are you doing? Please, help me move these stones!"

But Varkas did not answer.

He did not even stir.

A sudden wave of frustration swept through Edric. His face grew hot with indignation, but he had no time to question his superior's strange behaviour.

He turned back towards Talia and placed both hands against the heavy slab pinning her legs.

Summoning his strength, he pushed against it.

Then, from somewhere beneath the rubble, came a faint sound.

It was so weak that he nearly mistook it for the wind. A soft, broken gasp, like the desperate breathing of a wounded animal.

Edric froze.

He immediately turned towards Talia.

Under the trembling torchlight, he saw the faintest movement upon her pale face.

For a moment, his knees nearly gave way.

Then relief rushed through him so suddenly that he could scarcely contain himself.

"She's alive!" he cried. "Commander, Her Highness is alive! She's still breathing!"

As though his voice had reached her through the darkness, Talia's eyelids began to flutter.

Slowly, she opened her eyes.

Her gaze was clouded and unfocused, and it was clear that she was barely conscious. Nevertheless, she was alive.

Edric felt an overwhelming sense of relief.

They had found her.

Against all expectations, the princess had survived.

But there was no time to celebrate. Her injuries were severe, and she needed a healer immediately.

Edric hurriedly straightened, intending to raise his torch and summon the other knights. Yet just as he was about to call out, he noticed something that made him stop.

A faint smile had appeared upon Talia's bloodless lips.

It was scarcely more than the slightest movement, but it was unmistakable.

Edric stared at her in bewilderment.

Then, from her cracked and bloodstained lips, came a whisper so soft that it was almost lost in the night air.

"Varkas..."

Edric's eyes widened.

At that very moment, the man who had been standing motionless beside her finally moved.

Varkas slowly lowered himself onto one knee.

Talia's unfocused gaze drifted towards him. The moment her eyes settled upon his face, something in her expression changed.

Though she was terribly injured and scarcely able to remain conscious, she looked at him as though she had finally found the one person she had been waiting for.

The faint smile upon her lips deepened.

"I... I knew it..." she whispered.

Her voice was weak and broken, and each word seemed to require a painful effort.

"I knew... you would come."

Tears gathered beneath her lashes.

As she looked up at him, her eyes curved with fragile relief, and the tears slowly slipped down her colourless cheeks.

She had waited for him.

Even while lying alone in the darkness, trapped beneath the fallen rocks and suffering unbearable pain, she had continued believing that he would come to find her.

And now, seeing him before her, she smiled as though that desperate hope had finally been rewarded.

Varkas remained silent.

The torchlight flickered across his face, casting restless shadows over his severe features. Its golden glow reflected faintly in his eyes, but his gaze seemed strangely hollow.

He looked down at Talia without speaking, as though he had become lost in some distant thought from which he could not return.

Edric watched him with growing uneasiness.

Something was terribly wrong.

He had served beneath Varkas long enough to recognise his usual cold composure. The commander was not a man who easily revealed his emotions, even in the most dangerous circumstances.

Yet this silence was different.

There was something almost broken about the way he looked at the injured princess.

Edric could not explain it, but the sight filled him with a strange apprehension.

After a moment's hesitation, he reached out and lightly caught Varkas by the arm.

"Commander..." he began uncertainly. "Are you... are you quite all right?"

Varkas slowly raised his head.

He looked at Edric, a faint crease appearing between his brows, as though he could not understand why such a question had been asked.

Edric himself could scarcely explain it.

Why had he suddenly become concerned for the commander when it was the princess who lay so terribly injured before them?

And why had Varkas looked so utterly devastated?

Before Edric could make sense of what he had witnessed, the sound of hurried footsteps echoed through the ravine.

Several knights were approaching from behind the rocks.

Remembering the urgency of their situation, Edric quickly raised his torch high above his head.

The approaching men caught sight of the signal and immediately broke into a run towards them.
