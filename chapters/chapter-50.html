Chapter 50

Talia's face twisted with fury as she raised her hand and struck Varkas across the cheek with what little strength remained in her body.

"You insufferable wretch! Must you always stand in my way? Can you not bear to see me do anything without interfering?"

For the briefest instant, something flickered within his pale blue eyes. Yet his expression remained as cold and composed as ever. That dreadful calm, which seemed incapable of being disturbed by anything she said or did, only deepened her anger.

With a furious cry, Talia dug her nails into his cheek and dragged them down his skin. Varkas did not so much as flinch. Instead, he calmly caught both her wrists in one hand before turning his attention towards the disorderly scene around them.

His icy gaze passed over the frightened maids, whose faces had gone deathly pale, then moved towards the bewildered knights standing nearby. Finally, his eyes settled upon the servant lying upon the ground, sobbing as she pressed her hands against her badly burned face.

A weary sigh escaped his lips.

"Take that woman to the healer."

With a brief inclination of his head towards the injured maid, Varkas turned away, evidently considering the matter settled.

Talia immediately began struggling in his arms, her voice rising into a furious shriek.

"By whose authority do you dare give such an order? That woman has committed a crime! Have her executed this instant!"

From the corner of her eye, she noticed soldiers and servants hurrying towards the commotion. They gathered at a cautious distance, whispering amongst themselves as they watched the princess struggling against the commander.

Ordinarily, Talia would have been painfully conscious of their curious stares. But she had long since lost the strength to concern herself with appearances. Her fury had become too great to contain, and her voice rang across the sleeping camp.

"You contemptible scoundrel! What sort of knight are you supposed to be?"

Varkas offered no reply.

Without so much as blinking, he carried her through the rows of tents, ignoring her furious protests. His footsteps remained steady and unhurried until he reached his own quarters, where he entered without a word and deposited her upon the wide cot.

Talia scarcely noticed where he had brought her. Her thoughts remained consumed by the humiliation she had suffered, and the sight of his indifferent face only served to make her anger burn more fiercely.

"You have never protected me! Not even once!" she cried. "Every single time, you stand aside and leave me to suffer! You had no intention of saving me this time either, did you? You wanted me to perish! That is why you abandoned me! That is why you took so long to come looking for me! Do you think I do not know?"

Varkas appeared entirely unmoved by her accusations.

He caught her wrists and pressed them firmly against the mattress before carefully prising open her tightly curled fingers.

The skin of her palms had been badly burned by the flaming branch. Angry blisters covered the tender flesh, and blood and clear fluid seeped from the damaged skin.

A faint crease appeared between Varkas's brows.

Without speaking, he reached towards a small shelf beside the bed and retrieved a glass vial.

The moment Talia saw him tilt the unfamiliar liquid towards her injured hands, she recoiled in alarm.

"No! Stop it! I told you to leave me alone!"

But Varkas paid no heed to her protests. He carefully applied the medicinal salve to the burns, then took several strips of clean linen from his medical supplies and wrapped them securely around her wounded palms.

Throughout the treatment, Talia continued striking his shoulder with her free hand, struggling against him with all the strength she could summon. Yet her blows gradually weakened, until at last even the effort of raising her arm became too much to bear.

Exhausted beyond measure, she finally fell limp against the mattress.

Varkas looked down at her for a moment, his expression strangely vacant. Then he slowly straightened his back.

"I shall fetch you a sedative."

Talia lay with half her face buried in the pillow, drawing in ragged, uneven breaths. At his words, she turned her head just enough to follow him with her eyes.

He crossed the room with his usual measured steps and began examining the labels upon the bottles arranged along the shelf.

As Talia stared at his broad, unyielding back, another image suddenly rose before her eyes.

She saw him running towards Ayla.

She remembered the terrible moment when danger had descended upon them, and Varkas had rushed to the First Princess without so much as looking back.

The memory sent a pain through her chest far sharper than the burning in her wounded hands.

Her lips curled into a bitter smile.

"You must be terribly disappointed that I am still alive."

Varkas's hand stopped.

For a long moment, he remained utterly motionless, his fingers suspended above the small collection of bottles.

Then, with almost painful slowness, he turned his head towards her.

Talia looked into his handsome, immaculate face, searching it with a bitterness she could scarcely contain. There was something unbearable about that expression, so composed and seemingly untouched by human feeling.

As she stared at him, something deep within her seemed to fracture.

A crooked smile trembled upon her lips.

"What a dreadful misfortune for you," she murmured. "You had such a rare opportunity to be rid of the troublesome woman who has been a thorn in your side for so long."

Her voice remained sharp, but tears had already begun gathering in her eyes.

Before she could restrain them, they spilled over and traced warm paths down her cheeks. Varkas's expression blurred before her, his face wavering through the tears until it seemed as though she were looking at him from beneath the surface of deep water.

He approached the bed without speaking.

Then he lowered himself onto one knee beside her and gently brought the rim of a small vial towards her lips.

"Drink this," he said quietly. "It will ease your pain."

Talia turned her face away.

"I do not want it."

A heavy silence followed.

Then, with what little strength remained in her voice, she added, "I do not want anything from you ever again."

Varkas slowly lowered the bottle.

At that very moment, the flame of the oil lamp flickered and dimmed, casting a deep shadow across his face.

But Talia had no need to see his expression.

She knew it well enough already.

That same cold indifference. That same empty composure. Or perhaps he is merely irritated and weary of dealing with me.

The thought filled her with fresh bitterness.

Without another word, she turned her back towards him and buried her face against the pillow.

Varkas remained beside the bed for several moments. The room was so quiet that she could hear the faint rustling of the tent fabric in the night breeze.

At last, he rose.

His footsteps moved towards the entrance, and a moment later, he disappeared into the darkness.

Talia lay motionless, listening until the sound of his retreating steps had faded completely.

Only then did she slowly extend a trembling hand towards her legs.

Her fingertips brushed against the stiff, unresponsive limb beneath the blankets. The strange, wooden heaviness of it sent a dreadful chill through her entire body.

Cripple.

The word appeared in her mind with merciless clarity.

Talia immediately forced it away.

No. That is impossible.

It was nothing more than cruel gossip, malicious nonsense spread by servants who despised her. She would not allow herself to believe it.

The Imperial Palace possessed the finest healers on the continent. Surely, among so many gifted physicians and mages, someone would be capable of restoring her injured leg.

And if ordinary healing magic proved insufficient, there was still her mother.

The Empress commanded sorcerers who practised arts forbidden to most of the world. Their powers reached far beyond those of common healers, and Talia knew her mother would not hesitate to employ them if necessary.

Mother will make me whole again.

Whatever the cost, whatever sacrifices might be required, her mother would find a way.

And when her body had been restored to its former perfection, Talia would walk proudly before every servant who had dared laugh at her misfortune.

She would make certain they saw her.

She would let them look upon her flawless appearance and remember every cruel word they had spoken.

Clutching her throbbing knees, Talia squeezed her eyes shut and held desperately to that promise.

✦ ✦ ✦

The magnificent pilgrimage procession that had departed from the imperial capital in such splendour had become a sombre funeral procession.

The court attendants now wore black mourning robes over their customary crimson uniforms, while the knights had draped their armour with dark, faded banners in honour of the fallen.

The freight wagons, which had once carried costly wines, luxurious silks, and precious gold, were now burdened with the embalmed remains of thirty-four men.

At appointed hours, the royal musicians played slow, mournful melodies, their brass instruments filling the air with a dreary lament that accompanied the procession across the countryside.

From within her carriage, Talia listened to the monotonous music with growing irritation.

The dull ache in her injured leg had begun to intensify again, and she reached blindly towards the small incense burner beside her.

Her fingertips encountered only cold metal.

Looking down, she discovered that the brass vessel contained nothing but a heap of grey ashes. The last of the medicinal incense had burned away.

A quiet curse escaped her lips.

With considerable effort, Talia pushed herself upright amongst the cushions and reached towards the wooden compartment beneath the carriage bench. After fumbling with the latch, she opened the box and retrieved a fresh stick of incense.

It was dark and brittle, fashioned from a potent mixture of dried Frost Grass, Evening Primrose, Mandrake root, and crushed Red Shard Flowers.

She placed the slender stick amongst the ashes and struck a firestone, carefully bringing the flame to its tip.

Before long, thick bluish smoke began curling upwards from the little burner.

The heavy fragrance slowly spread throughout the carriage, filling the enclosed space with its familiar, intoxicating scent.

As the powerful fumes began to cloud her thoughts, Talia sank back amongst the blankets and allowed her body to relax.

Ever since the procession had begun its return journey, she had spent nearly every waking moment beneath the influence of the pain-relieving incense.

It was the only means by which she could escape the relentless suffering of her injured leg.

Wrapped in the heavy, bitter-sweet smoke, she gradually lost all sense of time. One day slipped into the next without distinction, until yesterday, today, and tomorrow seemed to dissolve into the same endless, dreamlike haze.

Occasionally, she became dimly aware of an imperial mage entering the carriage to examine her wounds. At other times, an attendant would appear beside her, urging her to swallow a few spoonfuls of broth.

But these visits scarcely registered in her mind.

Their voices drifted towards her from some distant place, and their faces faded almost as soon as they disappeared.

There was, however, one person who possessed the unfortunate ability to drag her back into the painful reality she so desperately wished to escape.

Varkas.

Talia narrowed her glassy, unfocused eyes as a tall silhouette appeared in the open doorway of her carriage, momentarily blocking the daylight.

For reasons she did not understand, her carriage had been moved from the very rear of the procession to a position directly behind the vanguard.

Worse still, it now travelled beneath the constant supervision of the Captain of the Imperial Knights himself.

Perhaps he believes I require his personal watchfulness, lest I cause another disturbance.

The thought stirred a faint trace of irritation within her otherwise clouded mind.

Varkas stepped into the carriage and looked down at her.

Talia lay sprawled amongst the cushions and blankets, her limbs loose and heavy, resembling a piece of seaweed washed helplessly upon the shore.

He regarded her for a moment before bending slightly and reaching towards her face.

His fingers felt cool against her feverish skin as he gently brushed aside the damp strands of hair clinging to her perspiring forehead.

"You must not burn that incense so frequently," he told her in a quiet, measured voice. "If you continue using it at this rate, your body will soon grow accustomed to its effects, and it will cease to provide you with the relief you require."

Talia merely stared at him.

Through the thick haze clouding her thoughts, his face seemed strangely distant. She regarded him with the same weary indifference one might reserve for some tiresome obligation that had been postponed for far too long.

She offered no reply.

Varkas waited for a moment, apparently expecting her to acknowledge his warning.

When she remained silent, he released a faint sigh.

"We shall make camp here for the night."

Talia continued looking at him with dull, uncomprehending eyes.

What a remarkably unnecessary thing to say.

The sun had already set, and the carriage wheels had stopped turning. Naturally, they were preparing to spend the night there.

She could not understand why he had felt the need to announce something so painfully obvious.

How peculiar. This man can remain silent even when he has every duty to speak, yet now he chooses to waste his breath telling me what I already know.
