Chapter 70

Varkas had been standing by the window, breathing in the cool evening air, which carried the damp scent of approaching rain. At last, he turned towards her.

A sombre expression rested upon his face, and his eyes were as still and distant as the pale light before dawn.

Talia found herself wondering what thoughts lay hidden behind that cold gaze.

Was he troubled by the future that had become so hopelessly entangled?

Or had he already begun to regret his decision?

She had sworn that she would no longer concern herself with his affairs. Yet, as though mocking that very promise, these questions continued to trouble her.

Just as I suspected. It was never because you loved Ayla that you intended to marry her.

I knew it all along.

You are simply a man who cannot love anyone with true passion.

Perhaps it had never mattered to him which woman became his wife.

That would explain how he had managed to play the part of a devoted fiancé to Ayla, only to become her own attentive husband so easily.

To him, such duties were merely responsibilities to be fulfilled.

Talia raised her hand and rubbed the corner of her mouth where his fingers had touched earlier, as though she wished to erase the memory of his kindness.

I am nothing more than another burden he has chosen to carry.

I will not allow myself to be deceived by a little kindness offered out of pity.

With that resolution, she turned her back towards him and pulled the duvet over her head.

At some point, she must have fallen asleep.

The strain of the long day, together with the considerable amount of wine she had consumed, had finally overcome her.

She lay with her face buried in the pillow, her limbs resting heavily beneath the soft covers.

For a time, she slept without disturbance.

Then a dull, familiar ache began to creep into her knees.

Talia shifted uneasily beneath the bedding, hoping the discomfort would pass. Instead, the pain steadily grew worse until she could no longer remain asleep.

With great difficulty, she opened her heavy eyelids.

Nothing greeted her but darkness.

There was not even the faintest trace of light.

The last remnants of sleep vanished at once.

Talia stared into the blackness, her heart suddenly seized by terror.

The darkness seemed to press against her from every direction. She could scarcely breathe, and her throat tightened as though an invisible hand had closed around it.

Then, without warning, a terrible pain shot through her knees.

It was so sharp that she nearly cried out.

She immediately reached down and seized her calves with both hands.

The weakened muscles beneath her fingers were contracting violently, twisting into painful knots.

Cold sweat broke across her back.

It felt as though countless blades were being driven into her bones.

Biting down upon her lower lip, Talia dug her fingernails into the spasming muscles, desperately attempting to ease the pain.

Just then, the sharp sound of flint striking broke through the darkness.

A small flame appeared.

Talia lifted her head abruptly.

Beside the flickering candle stood Varkas, his pale figure emerging from the shadows.

The moment he saw her bloodless face, his expression hardened.

He immediately approached the bed and leaned towards her.

Talia screamed.

“Stay away! Do not touch me!”

She struck wildly at the hands reaching towards her legs and desperately tried to retreat to the opposite side of the bed.

But she had scarcely moved before Varkas caught her.

With one firm hand, he held her shoulders in place to prevent her from injuring herself further. With the other, he reached towards her calves, where her own fingernails had left deep marks.

Talia cried out as though she were being burned alive.

“No! I forbid you to touch me!”

“Be still. I am merely trying to examine your injuries.”

“No! I told you not to!”

She struggled with all her remaining strength, twisting beneath his hold until she could scarcely draw breath.

Varkas muttered something under his breath.

Then, with a swift movement, he pulled her upper body against his chest and held her securely enough to restrain her frantic movements.

Keeping one arm around her, he reached towards the bellpull above the bed and gave it a sharp tug.

A short while later, the bedroom door flew open.

Several maids hurried inside, their faces pale with alarm.

Varkas immediately issued an order.

“Fetch a healer at once!”

The entire chamber was thrown into confusion.

An elderly mage soon arrived to administer restorative magic, while the maids hurried about lighting candles and placing soothing incense beside the bed.

Throughout the commotion, Varkas continued to hold Talia in his arms.

He did not release her for even a moment.

Pressed against his chest, Talia could hear the steady, powerful beating of his heart.

Her frightened eyes darted about the room.

She was terrified that someone would attempt to lift her skirts and examine her damaged legs.

Drawing her knees as close together as she could, she gathered the fabric of her gown tightly around them.

She held it as though it were the only thing protecting her.

After a short examination, the elderly mage approached with a small phial made of bluish glass.

“Your Highness, please drink this medicine. It should relieve your pain almost immediately.”

Talia regarded the phial with suspicion.

If I drink that, I shall fall unconscious.

And while I am asleep, someone will lift my skirts and see everything.

Her lips pressed together.

She turned her head away in refusal.

But a moment later, long fingers gently yet firmly caught her chin and turned her face back.

The rim of the phial was brought against her mouth.

Talia clenched her teeth and stubbornly refused to drink.

Varkas looked down at her with eyes as cold as winter ice.

Then, without another word, he raised the phial to his own lips.

Before Talia could understand what he intended, he took a mouthful of the bitter medicine and leaned towards her.

His lips suddenly met hers.

Talia's eyes widened in astonishment.

Holding her jaw firmly, Varkas pressed against her cheek until her lips parted.

Then he transferred the medicine into her mouth, using his tongue to make certain she swallowed it.

The bitter liquid spread across her tongue.

Talia seized the front of his thin shirt, gripping the fabric so tightly that it strained beneath her fingers.

She could scarcely comprehend what was happening.

As she struggled to swallow the medicine, her throat tightened, and the unexpected closeness of his mouth left her utterly bewildered.

His tongue brushed against hers as he continued to administer the draught.

A faint, broken sound escaped her lips.

What is he doing?

Has he given me poison?

The medicine burned unpleasantly as it passed down her throat.

“Hah...”

When Varkas finally drew away, Talia was left staring at him in disbelief.

For a moment, she had forgotten even the terrible pain in her legs.

But Varkas was not finished.

He raised the phial once more and swallowed the remaining medicine.

Then he leaned towards her again.

Talia immediately dug her fingernails into his forearm.

Yet he continued, pressing his lips against hers and carefully transferring the remaining draught into her mouth.

The medicine carried a strong, earthy scent, like wild roots gathered from a dark forest.

Talia struggled against its bitterness, but her throat swallowed instinctively.

She curled her toes beneath the covers.

Shame, confusion, and a strange unfamiliar sensation overwhelmed her all at once.

She had never experienced anything quite like it.

Whether it was the effect of the medicine or the bewilderment of the moment, the room began to turn around her.

The bitter taste gradually faded, but Varkas remained close.

His continued touch made her wonder whether he was truly doing this merely to make her swallow the medicine.

Unable to bear the confusion any longer, Talia struck his chest with her hand.

Only then did he slowly withdraw.

She gasped for breath and stared up at him.

Her entire body was trembling.

Yet Varkas appeared perfectly composed.

His face revealed none of the confusion that had overtaken her.

That calmness only made her more uncertain.

Was I merely imagining it?

Did I misunderstand what just happened?

Before she could find an answer, Varkas raised his hand and brushed his thumb across her lips.

Only then did Talia become aware of the moisture upon her mouth.

Mortified, she immediately ducked beneath the duvet.

A quiet sigh escaped him, stirring the hair at the crown of her head.

“...You should sleep now.”

He gently cupped the back of her head and drew her against his chest once more.

Surrounded by his warmth and the steady rhythm of his heartbeat, Talia gradually found herself breathing more calmly.

Before long, the medicine began to take effect.

A pleasant heaviness spread through her limbs, and her vision slowly grew blurred.

Still, she tightened her fingers around his shirt.

Do not dare touch my legs while I am asleep.

She murmured the warning so faintly that she could scarcely hear herself.

Whether Varkas understood her words or not, he offered no reply.

Instead, his hand moved gently down her back in a quiet, soothing gesture.

Soon, the last of her strength deserted her.

Her fingers loosened.

The room faded into darkness, and Talia fell into a deep, dreamless sleep.

When she opened her eyes again, bright morning sunlight was pouring into the bedroom.

For several moments, she lay staring at the ceiling, her thoughts still clouded by sleep.

Then she became aware that she was not alone.

Startled, she turned her head.

Varkas was standing beside the window, changing his clothes.

The pale morning light fell across his bare upper body, illuminating the strong lines of his shoulders and chest.

His figure seemed almost carved from white marble.

For a moment, Talia found herself staring without realizing it.

Then his eyes turned towards her.

She immediately drew her shoulders inward.

Varkas regarded her with the same calm, unreadable expression he always wore.

There was not the slightest indication that anything unusual had taken place between them during the night.

His face was entirely indifferent.

As though the events of the previous evening had never happened at all.
