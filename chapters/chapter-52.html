Chapter 52

As the towering gates of the Imperial Palace came into view, Talia found herself overcome by a feeling she could scarcely put into words.

Relief, perhaps. Or something dangerously close to hope.

At long last, her suffering would soon come to an end. She would no longer be at the mercy of some timid physician who dared not make a decision without first considering Gareth's displeasure.

Once she was returned to the Empress's Palace, the most accomplished mages in the empire would attend to her. They would mend her broken body and restore everything she had lost.

I shall be myself again.

Clinging to that promise, Talia moistened her dry lips and cautiously lowered her gaze to examine her appearance.

What she saw filled her with dismay.

The delicate cream-coloured gown she wore did little to conceal the dreadful state of her body. Beneath its thin fabric, the heavy bandages wrapped from her thigh to her ankle formed an ugly, uneven shape.

Although healing magic had closed the burns upon her palms, her fingernails remained discoloured, stained a deep purplish black by bruising and dried blood.

Her face was hardly in a better condition. Having scarcely eaten throughout the long journey, she had grown painfully thin. Her cheeks were hollow, her features drawn, and whatever beauty she had once taken such pride in seemed to have vanished beneath exhaustion and suffering.

A sudden dread tightened around her heart.

What if they laugh when they see me?

The thought came unbidden, followed by the dreadful memory of those servants whispering over her injuries.

She could almost hear their cruel laughter again.

Look at her now. At last, she has received the punishment she deserves.

A chill ran down her spine. Talia seized the carriage curtains and pulled them shut with trembling hands.

She could not endure another round of mockery. If even one person dared suggest that her injuries were the result of divine punishment, she feared she might lose what little remained of her composure.

Her eyes fell upon a crumpled linen blanket lying on the carriage floor. She dragged it towards herself and pulled it over her head, concealing her face and body beneath its folds.

A short while later, the carriage began to shake as it passed beneath the great stone archway of the palace gates.

Talia cautiously lifted one corner of the blanket.

Outside, the courtyard was alive with noise. Horses stamped and snorted upon the gravel, while the commanding shouts of soldiers rang above the clatter of wheels.

Somewhere in the distance, the royal musicians continued their solemn funeral march, the deep notes of their instruments lending a dreadful gravity to the procession's arrival.

Beneath these sounds rose the ceaseless murmur of the gathered crowd. To Talia's exhausted ears, the whispering resembled the unpleasant buzzing of a thousand insects.

She could bear no more. With a shudder, she drew the blanket over her head once again.

The carriage continued slowly across the courtyard before finally coming to a halt.

Almost immediately, the commotion outside grew louder. Judging by the footsteps, voices, and hurried commands, it seemed that half the Imperial Palace had gathered to witness the procession's return.

Talia curled herself into the farthest corner of the carriage.

Let them finish their ceremonies without me.

She intended to remain hidden until Gareth and Ayla had completed their formal audience before the Emperor. By then, perhaps the curious attendants and flattering courtiers would have dispersed.

The last thing she wanted was to present herself before their devoted followers in such a miserable condition.

She pressed her hand against her aching knee and waited, scarcely daring to breathe.

Then, without the slightest warning, the carriage door was flung open.

A tall figure appeared against the brilliant sunlight.

Talia raised her head in alarm, only for her eyes to widen with recognition.

"Nanny...?"

The familiar woman hurried into the carriage with surprising speed for someone of her generous build. Before Talia could say another word, her nanny gathered her into a tight embrace.

"Oh, my precious little lady! Whatever have those dreadful people allowed to happen to you?"

Her voice trembled with distress.

Drawing back just enough to examine her, the woman cupped Talia's face between her broad, warm hands. Tears spilled freely down her round cheeks as she studied the princess's hollow features.

"Merciful heavens, look at you! You have grown so terribly thin! And to think you have suffered such grievous injuries... Oh, my poor child, there is scarcely anything left of you!"

For a moment, Talia could only stare.

Then her expression crumpled.

"Nanny..."

She threw her arms around the woman's neck and clung to her desperately.

All the fear, humiliation, and misery she had struggled to contain throughout the journey seemed to break free at once.

She buried her face against her nanny's ample bosom like a frightened young creature seeking shelter from the world.

The older woman held her close, rubbing her back in slow, comforting strokes. It was the same gentle touch Talia remembered from childhood, when a simple embrace had seemed capable of making every sorrow disappear.

The familiar warmth undid the last of her restraint.

The scent of cinnamon rising from her nanny's curled hair filled her senses, and Talia began to sob.

"Nanny, I cannot bear it any longer," she cried brokenly. "Everything hurts so terribly. I feel as though I shall die from it."

"Hush now, my darling. You must not speak so."

Her nanny gently wiped the tears from her cheeks.

"Lady Senevier has already made the necessary arrangements. You shall be properly cared for now, and before long, you will be well again."

As she spoke, the woman glanced towards the open carriage door.

Talia followed her gaze. Only then did she notice the stranger waiting upon the carriage steps.

He was a lean, imposing man whose lower face was concealed beneath a white cloth. Above the covering, a pair of dark grey eyes, strangely rimmed with gold, regarded her with an unsettling lack of warmth.

His gaze travelled slowly over her body.

There was something deeply unpleasant about the manner in which he examined her, as though she were no more than an object being inspected for its defects.

Talia instinctively drew closer to her nanny.

The older woman patted her shoulder reassuringly.

"The moment Lady Senevier received word of your misfortune, she summoned the mages of the Taren family. They have come especially to restore your health, my lady. You need only place yourself in their care."

Having offered this reassurance, she gently loosened Talia's hold upon her and moved aside to admit the waiting mages.

The moment the dark-robed figures approached, Talia's uneasiness deepened. She retreated as far as the carriage seat would allow.

"There is no need for all this," she said hastily. "I am perfectly capable of making my own way. Kindly keep your distance."

The lean mage bent towards her, tilting his head with evident disbelief.

"On those legs of yours?"

Humiliation sent a rush of heat into Talia's cheeks.

"I said I can manage! I require no assistance to reach the Empress's Palace!"

The man's eyes narrowed.

He regarded her in silence for a moment, rubbing his chin as though considering how best to deal with a particularly troublesome object.

Then, without warning, he reached forward and seized her injured leg.

An agonising cry tore from Talia's throat.

The pressure of his fingers against her wounded flesh sent a blinding pain shooting through her body. He gripped her so roughly that fresh blood began seeping through the thick bandages.

"Ah! Stop! You are hurting me!"

The mage showed no sign of concern.

"If you insist upon dragging yourself about on limbs in such a condition, you will only tear the wounds open again," he replied coldly. "And when that happens, it is we who shall be held accountable. Kindly put an end to this foolish display of pride."

Talia stared at him in horror.

The man released her leg as abruptly as he had seized it. Then he turned towards his companion.

"Take her."

The second mage stepped forward and reached beneath Talia's back.

The instant his hands touched her, an unpleasant shiver travelled across her skin. His touch was unnaturally cold, reminding her of the smooth, clammy scales of a reptile.

Talia recoiled violently.

"Keep your hands off me!"

She twisted away and struck out blindly, her palm connecting sharply with the man's jaw.

A low, harsh sound escaped him. He muttered something in a strange, metallic voice, and Talia froze.

She recognised the language.

Elvish...

Her eyes darted towards the shadow beneath his hood. There, partly concealed by his ash-grey hair, she caught sight of an unnaturally long, pointed ear.

His skin was an almost lifeless white, and faint strands of blue ran through his pale hair.

Talia's heart seemed to stop.

An Origin Elf.

He was no ordinary man with a trace of elven ancestry. Neither half-blood nor quarter-blood, he belonged to the pure-blooded race itself.

And Talia knew enough about such beings to fear them.

Origin Elves possessed powers beyond those of ordinary humans, yet they were notorious for their complete indifference towards human suffering.

The realisation filled her with terror.

The two mages exchanged several sharp remarks in their own language. Before Talia could retreat any farther, they seized her arms and forced them against the carriage seat.

She opened her mouth to scream.

But no cry emerged.

A cold, damp hand suddenly descended over her eyes.

In the very next instant, every trace of strength vanished from her body.

It was as though her bones had turned to dust and her muscles had dissolved beneath her skin. Her arms fell uselessly at her sides, and even the effort of moving a finger became impossible.

The mage withdrew his hand with an irritated click of his tongue.

"What an absurd waste of mana."

Talia stared up at him, her eyes wide with terror.

She wanted to scream at him, to demand how he dared use magic upon an imperial princess without her consent.

Yet when she tried to speak, nothing emerged but a faint, dry rasp. Her body would no longer obey her.

Satisfied that she could offer no further resistance, the mage straightened and addressed his companion.

"She is quite incapable of struggling now. Take her directly to the healing chambers."

The elf whom Talia had struck obeyed without hesitation.

He lifted her with effortless strength and slung her over his shoulder as though she were nothing more than a sack of grain.

Talia could neither protest nor struggle. Her helpless gaze turned desperately towards her nanny.

Please... help me.

But the older woman seemed entirely unaware of her distress.

"Only a little longer, my sweet lady," she said, wiping her tear-stained cheeks with the edge of her sleeve. "You must be brave. Everything will soon be put right."

With those words, she stepped down from the carriage.

The elf followed, carrying Talia over his shoulder.

The brilliant midday sunlight struck her eyes so suddenly that she winced. She blinked rapidly, struggling to make sense of the scene around her.

The carriage was surrounded by a detachment of armed guards, each bearing the familiar insignia of the Empress's Palace.

Their presence should have reassured her. Instead, it only made her feel more thoroughly trapped.

Talia searched the crowded courtyard with frantic eyes.

Beyond the line of soldiers, she spotted a familiar figure pacing anxiously.

It was her personal knight.

He appeared deeply troubled, and the moment he saw the mages carrying her away, he attempted to force his way through the guards.

One of the Taren mages immediately stepped into his path.

"Your services are no longer required," the mage declared. "Her Highness has been entrusted to our care. You may withdraw."

The knight drew himself upright.

"I am the princess's appointed personal guard. Until I am formally relieved of that duty, I must remain beside Her Highness."

The mage gave a short, contemptuous laugh.

"Her Highness has returned in a condition that speaks rather poorly of your protection, does it not? I should think your claim to that position has already become meaningless."

The knight's face tightened.

For a moment, he seemed ready to argue, but the words caught in his throat.

The mage dismissed him with an impatient gesture and pushed past him, continuing across the courtyard between the crowded baggage wagons.

Talia watched helplessly as her knight disappeared behind the line of guards.

No...

A terrible loneliness settled over her.

She turned her eyes desperately towards the palace steps, searching the gathered soldiers and courtiers for anyone who might intervene.

Then, amidst the imperial guards assembled before the Main Palace, she caught sight of a familiar head of pale flaxen hair.

Her breath caught.

Varkas.

For one foolish instant, she nearly called out to him.

His name rose instinctively to her lips, as though some deeply buried part of her still believed he would come if she asked.

But before she could make a sound, reason returned.

What am I thinking?

She swallowed the name.

Varkas would not lift a finger for her.

Had she not learned that lesson often enough?

She could not even understand why her eyes had sought him in the first place.

These mages had been summoned by Lady Senevier herself. They were servants of the Empress's Palace, entrusted with restoring Talia's health.

Surely they had no reason to do her harm.

They have come to heal me.

She repeated the thought silently.

Mother sent them. They would not dare hurt me.

Yet the reassurance did little to quiet the fear tightening around her heart.

And then she realised something that made her entire body go rigid.

The elf carrying her was not heading towards the Empress's Palace.

He was walking directly towards Varkas.

Talia's eyes widened as the familiar figure drew nearer.

Her heart began to pound.

And as Varkas's face came into view, she could do nothing but stare.
