Chapter 33

After sending her attendants away, Ayla followed Varkas out of the covered walkway. Beyond the cloister stretched a garden filled with marigolds, daisies, and rosemary, all bending beneath the rain. The soaked plants filled the air with the rich fragrance of wet leaves and herbs. Ayla breathed it in before looking up at the man beside her.

"Varkas, did something happen last night?" she asked hesitantly.

He had been walking in silence, but her question made him turn. Ayla studied his face, hoping to discover what troubled him. His pale blue eyes remained as unreadable as ever, seeming to reflect everything around him while revealing nothing of his own thoughts. The familiar distance between them made her chest ache. Will there ever be a day when those eyes look at me with affection? Will I ever have a place in his heart?

At last, Varkas answered. "Nothing happened that Your Highness needs to worry about."

"So something did happen," Ayla replied quietly. "You simply don't want to tell me."

He gave no further explanation. Instead, he stepped out into the rain, which immediately covered his broad shoulders and back. Ayla watched him with growing frustration until he turned and extended his hand towards her.

"The puddles are deep," he said. "You'll have difficulty crossing the garden."

Ayla understood what he was offering, and warmth rose to her cheeks. After the cold way he had treated her, she did not want to accept his kindness so readily. Yet she could hardly leave her fiancé standing in the downpour. Pretending to hesitate a little longer, she finally stepped towards him.

Varkas bent down and placed one arm beneath her knees and the other around her back. He lifted her easily, as though she weighed nothing. Ayla rested her head against his shoulder, just as she had done since she was five years old.

"Do you know how mean you can be sometimes?" she murmured.

His eyebrows rose slightly at the unexpected accusation. Ayla did not try to explain the many feelings tangled inside her. She simply held him a little more tightly. Varkas adjusted his cloak around her to keep off the rain and carried her across the wide garden.

Ayla pressed her cheek against his collar. His familiar scent surrounded her: sharp herbs, the faint metallic smell of his armour, and something dry and earthy that reminded her of fallen leaves. As she breathed it in, her earlier irritation began to fade. She almost laughed at herself. How ridiculous that such a familiar gesture could still make her feel like a girl experiencing her first love.

Varkas had carried her this way countless times. To him, it was probably no more than a habit. She knew that his kindness came from the promise he had made to her mother, a duty he continued to honour. It did not necessarily mean he loved her. Yet knowing the truth did nothing to stop her heart from hoping.

Cruel man, she thought sadly. If you had always been cold to me, perhaps I could have accepted our engagement as nothing more than a political arrangement. Why must you keep giving me reasons to hope?

"I'll ask the servants to prepare a warm bath for you," Varkas said as they reached her lodging. "You should change out of your damp clothes and rest."

Ayla nodded. Varkas climbed the stone steps and leaned forward to set her down, but a sudden flash of lightning split the sky. A tremendous crash of thunder followed, and Ayla instinctively tightened her arms around his neck.

The storm raged overhead. Golden lightning tore through the dark clouds, illuminating the courtyard so brightly that the scene looked almost unreal. Still clinging to Varkas, Ayla glanced over his shoulder and noticed a pale figure sitting on a second-floor windowsill.

For a moment she wondered whether the storm was playing tricks on her eyes. Another flash of lightning revealed the figure's face, and Ayla went still. It was Talia.

Her half-sister sat beside the window, staring down at them. Her snow-white face and delicate features stood out against the darkness, and her slender neck seemed almost fragile. Ayla had always known that Talia was extraordinarily beautiful, but she had never seen her look so frightening. Hatred burned in her eyes, making her seem like some terrible spirit from an old story.

Ayla could only stare. Then Talia reached for a vase on the windowsill. Without looking away from them, she hurled it towards the entrance below.

The vase struck a pillar and shattered. Ayla screamed as fragments scattered across the stone steps. Varkas immediately turned his body to shield her. She was unharmed, but a sharp fragment grazed his cheek and left a thin cut.

"Varkas! You're hurt!" Ayla cried. She quickly pulled a handkerchief from her pocket and pressed it towards his face. He accepted it calmly and covered the wound himself, showing no sign of pain. Then he looked up at the window.

Ayla followed his gaze. Talia was still there, glaring down at them without the slightest hint of regret. Her lips twisted into a bitter expression, their deep red colour standing out against her pale face. Instead of anger, Ayla felt a sudden, chilling fear.

She had always regarded Talia as a troublesome girl who could be dismissed or ignored. Now, however, the hatred in her half-sister's eyes seemed frighteningly real. Ayla remembered how much her own mother had suffered because of the late Empress Senevia, and an ominous thought took hold of her. What if Talia brings the same misery into my life?

The thought made Ayla shiver. She turned away from the window, unable to shake the dreadful feeling.

The rain continued through the night and finally weakened near dawn. Talia had barely slept. When morning arrived, she sat on her bed and stared out at the ruined garden with hollow eyes.

Muddy water covered the ground, and the once-bright leaves hung heavily with moisture. Flowers that had decorated the beds the day before now lay scattered across the earth, their stems broken by the storm. The air smelled strongly of damp soil. Talia watched the scene for a long while before rising from her bed.

She crossed the room to the small table near the fireplace. A silver tray held the meal she had refused to eat the previous evening. The food had gone cold and dry. She looked over it without interest until her eyes settled on the little knife beside the plate.

Talia picked it up and studied the blade. It was meant for cutting food, but its edge was sharp enough to cause a serious wound. She carefully touched the point with her fingertip, then slipped the knife into the pocket of her gown and left the room without calling for her attendants.

The corridor was silent and damp. Moisture from the storm lingered in the heavy air as she moved towards the stairs, keeping one hand inside her pocket. Her fingers closed tightly around the cold handle of the knife. Sweat covered her palms, though she could not tell whether she was frightened, excited, or both.

She swallowed and moistened her dry lips before climbing the steps as quietly as she could. Ayla occupied a room on the top floor. When Talia reached the landing, she pressed herself against the wall and peered along the dim corridor. Fortunately, no guards stood outside her sister's door.

Breathing a little easier, Talia approached the wooden door at the end of the passage. A faint herbal fragrance drifted towards her, the scent of candles often burned to calm troubled nerves. Her lips curved into a bitter smile. So you had trouble sleeping too.

She remembered how pale Ayla had looked when she saw her at the window the night before. The memory drew a short, humourless laugh from Talia. For one fleeting moment, she was satisfied to know that her sister had been frightened. Then another image returned, and the feeling vanished.

Varkas had been carrying Ayla through the rain.

Talia's face tightened. She gripped the knife so hard that her fingers ached, and her whole body began to tremble. She had believed there was nothing left inside her that could still be broken. Yet seeing him hold Ayla so naturally had destroyed something she had treasured for years.

She wiped her wet eyes roughly with her sleeve. That was the only memory I had left.

Ever since childhood, she had kept that small moment hidden deep in her heart. Whenever loneliness became unbearable, she had returned to it in secret. Even after everything between her and Varkas had changed, she had believed that memory belonged to them alone. It was the last precious thing she thought no one could take away.

Did you really have to take that from me too? Her throat tightened. Couldn't you have left me just one memory that was special between us?

She knew how unreasonable those thoughts were. Varkas had every right to care for Ayla. They were engaged, and he had known her since childhood. There was nothing strange about him carrying his fiancée through the rain. Talia understood that perfectly well, but understanding did nothing to ease the hurt.

In her heart, she could forgive neither of them. Ayla already had everything Talia had once longed for: affection, respect, and the promise of happiness. Now even the one memory Talia had protected seemed to have lost its meaning. She wanted to punish her sister for taking away her last comfort. More than anything, she wanted Varkas to feel even a part of the pain she had endured.

If I have to suffer this much, then I want you to understand what it feels like.

Talia raised her eyes to the closed door. Once she crossed that threshold, there might be no way to undo what followed. Perhaps history would remember her as a wicked princess who harmed her innocent sister. Her name might become a warning about cruelty and jealousy. But she could no longer bring herself to care.

People already believed the worst of her, whatever she did. They've always treated me like a villain. What more can they take from me?

The thought hardened her resolve. She stepped closer, her hand trembling so badly that she struggled to keep it steady. Nevertheless, she reached out and wrapped her fingers around the doorknob.
