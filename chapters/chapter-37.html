Chapter 37

For once, Talia could think of nothing to say. Her lips trembled as she struggled to find a suitable excuse, but her exhausted mind refused to cooperate. Ordinarily, she would have answered Gareth's suspicions with her usual arrogance. Now, however, her strength was nearly spent, and she could scarcely gather her thoughts.

She pulled her hood lower to conceal her troubled expression and attempted to slip past her half-brother. Before she could reach the entrance, Gareth seized her wrist, his fingers closing around it with painful force.

"Have you suddenly lost your tongue?" he demanded. "I asked what business you had searching through Varkas's private quarters."

"Take your hands off me!"

Talia struggled to free herself, but Gareth only tightened his grip. With an angry growl, he dragged her closer, his expression darkening as he studied her face.

"Answer me, you wretched girl. What mischief are you plotting this time? Surely you didn't creep into his tent without some wicked purpose."

Pain shot through Talia's wrist. She bit her lower lip to keep from crying out, furious that he dared treat her so roughly.

That brainless brute. Has he nothing in his head but muscle?

Without another moment's hesitation, she raised her foot and kicked him hard in the shin. Gareth had not yet put on his leg armour, and the blow struck him directly.

"Damn you!"

He stumbled backwards with a furious curse, loosening his grip just enough for Talia to wrench her arm free. She immediately rushed past him and fled through the entrance.

Behind her came a tremendous crash, followed by the sound of Gareth's angry footsteps. Something inside the tent had been overturned, but Talia did not dare look back. She ran as quickly as her weakened legs would carry her, weaving through the narrow passages between the tents.

At last, her carriage came into view. She scrambled inside, slammed the door shut, and secured the lock before collapsing into the nearest corner.

Her heart pounded violently against her ribs. For several terrifying moments, she expected Gareth to appear and tear the carriage door from its hinges. Yet the minutes passed, and no one came.

Perhaps he had thought better of pursuing her in front of so many soldiers.

Talia finally released the breath she had been holding and buried her face against her knees. Relief, however, soon gave way to bitter humiliation. The more she considered what had happened, the more disgusted she became with herself.

What possessed me to behave so foolishly?

She had rushed into Varkas's quarters without considering the consequences, driven by nothing more than the fear that something dreadful might have happened to him. Worse still, she had allowed Gareth to witness her panic.

Did he notice anything?

The possibility made her stomach tighten. She nervously bit her lip before lifting the curtain just enough to look outside.

The campsite was already stirring beneath the pale light of dawn. Knights were taking down the tents, servants were gathering supplies, and soldiers were preparing their horses for another day of travel. No one appeared particularly concerned, and there was no sign that her encounter with Gareth had attracted attention.

Talia searched the camp a little longer before allowing the curtain to fall back into place.

As morning approached, the knights mounted their horses and assembled in their assigned positions. Among them was Varkas, who was inspecting the soldiers at the front of the procession with his usual stern expression.

Talia watched him through a narrow opening in the curtain. The sight of him unharmed should have eased her mind, yet she could not entirely forget the fear that had driven her into his tent.

When Varkas turned his horse towards her carriage, she immediately withdrew from the window.

Has Gareth told him? Is he coming to question me?

Her thoughts raced as she tried to prepare an explanation. But Varkas merely stopped beside the escort, exchanged a few brief words with the knights, and returned to the head of the procession.

Talia's tense shoulders relaxed.

A moment later, one of the guards called out, "Everyone to your positions! The procession is departing!"

She remained silent. The knight appeared to expect no reply, for he immediately rode forward to join the others. Soon afterwards, the carriage began to move, its wheels jolting over the uneven ground.

Talia leaned against the cushions and looked towards the pale morning sky through the narrow gap in the curtains.

Another long day awaited her, filled with heat, exhaustion, and questions she could not answer.

The procession had departed at dawn, yet it came to an unexpected halt long before evening.

Gareth, whose patience had been wearing thin throughout the journey, had finally declared that he would travel no farther that day. His decision was made without consulting anyone, as though the entire imperial procession existed solely for his convenience.

According to the Emperor's orders, authority over the pilgrimage's route and schedule belonged entirely to Varkas, who had been appointed commander of the expedition. Nevertheless, few men were willing to challenge the wishes of the future Emperor.

In the end, Varkas allowed the procession to stop. The soldiers began establishing a temporary camp upon a barren stretch of land, not far from Ulgram Hill, the second sacred destination of their pilgrimage.

Many of the knights were displeased by the decision. They would have preferred to continue until they reached a more suitable place to rest, rather than spend the night exposed upon an empty plain.

For Talia, however, the unexpected halt was a blessing.

She had endured the entire day inside the swaying carriage, without proper food or sufficient rest. Her body had grown so weak that even sitting upright required considerable effort.

Curled upon the cushioned bench, she closed her eyes and tried to ignore the dizziness that came over her in waves.

Then a familiar voice sounded from outside.

"Your Highness, I've brought you some fresh fruit. Might I trouble you to have a little?"

Talia's eyebrows drew together.

That tiresome fool again.

After the insults she had thrown at him the previous evening, she had expected Edric to keep his distance. Yet here he was, once again attempting to attend to her needs.

His persistence only made her more suspicious. Surely no one would continue showing such concern after being treated so harshly unless he hoped to gain something from it.

"Go away," she answered coldly.

Edric did not leave.

"Yesterday, Your Highness said you couldn't trust food when you didn't know what had been put into it. I've brought whole fruit today. Surely that should put your mind at ease."

Talia was too exhausted to argue. She remained silent, hoping he would eventually grow tired of waiting and leave her alone.

Instead, the carriage door opened.

Edric stepped inside and held a silver tray before her.

"Forgive my persistence, Your Highness, but I'd prefer not to see this pilgrimage end with us escorting a coffin home. You cannot continue refusing every meal. Please, at least have some of this."

Talia raised her eyes towards him with an expression of pure contempt. Then her gaze shifted to the tray.

Several ripe red apples lay beside a handful of plums and a cluster of fresh green grapes. The fruit looked inviting, and her empty stomach gave a painful twist at the sight.

She stared at it for a long moment before speaking in a tired, suspicious voice.

"And what assurance do I have that you haven't coated the fruit with poison?"

Edric closed his eyes briefly, as though appealing to heaven for patience.

Any other man might have surrendered to her accusations by now. Edric, however, merely sighed, picked up one of the apples, and took a large bite.

After chewing and swallowing, he held the fruit towards her.

"There. I've eaten it myself. Will that satisfy Your Highness, or must I finish the entire tray before you'll believe me?"

Talia recoiled in disgust.

"Do you take me for some beggar who would eat what you've already bitten? Take that filthy thing away from me, you insolent cur!"

For a moment, Edric looked as though his remaining patience had finally deserted him. His jaw tightened, and his expression became so strained that Talia almost expected him to shout.

She turned her back on him before he could respond.

The truth was that she was desperately hungry. Her stomach ached from emptiness, and the sight of the fruit had made her mouth water. Yet the thought of eating something that had passed through another person's hands filled her with such revulsion that she could scarcely bear it.

Even if she forced herself to swallow a bite, she feared her stomach would immediately reject it.

"I've had quite enough of your foolishness," she muttered. "Leave me in peace."

Edric gave a humourless laugh.

"Does Your Highness know no other way to dismiss a person? I begin to think those are the only words you ever care to say to me."

His remark struck the last of her patience.

Talia pushed herself upright, determined to put an end to the conversation. The sudden movement sent a wave of dizziness through her, and for an instant the carriage seemed to tilt beneath her feet.

She gripped the edge of the seat until her vision steadied.

Edric watched her carefully, but she refused to acknowledge his concern. Once she could stand without swaying, she brushed past him and stepped outside.

The afternoon was drawing to a close. Above the plains, the deep blue sky had begun to fade into a muted orange, while the air carried the first welcome coolness of evening.

Talia paused beside the carriage, breathing slowly. Then she walked towards the quieter edge of the campsite, avoiding the curious glances of the soldiers.

She wanted nothing more than to find a place where she could sit undisturbed. Unfortunately, wandering too far beyond the camp would only invite the guards to follow her, claiming that they were obliged to ensure her safety.

After considering her surroundings, she noticed a small grove of trees behind the supply wagons.

It was far enough from the crowded tents to offer some peace, yet close enough for the sentries to keep her within sight. They would therefore have little reason to approach her.

Satisfied with the arrangement, Talia made her way towards the grove.

She found an old spruce tree and lowered herself carefully onto the ground, resting her back against its rough trunk. The evening breeze moved gently through the branches overhead, bringing with it the pleasant scent of earth and pine.

For the first time that day, she could breathe without feeling trapped.

She closed her eyes and allowed herself a few moments of rest.

How long she remained there, she could not say.

Eventually, the faint sound of laughter disturbed the silence.

Talia opened her eyes.

Someone else had entered the grove.

Through the spaces between the trees, she saw Ayla approaching at a leisurely pace, her expression bright and carefree.

Talia's face immediately hardened.

Of all the people she might have encountered, Ayla was the very last she wished to see. Her body was exhausted, her spirits were already in ruins, and she had neither the patience nor the strength to endure that cheerful expression.

She placed a hand against the tree and rose to leave.

Before she could take more than a step, however, another figure emerged from the shadows.

Talia stopped.

It was Varkas.

The very man whose safety had kept her awake throughout the previous night now stood before her, apparently untouched by the dangers she had imagined.

He was holding Toruk's reins loosely in one hand as he followed Ayla along the path.

But it was not his presence that held Talia motionless.

It was his expression.

Varkas looked entirely at ease. The familiar sternness had disappeared from his face, and there was a quiet gentleness in his eyes that Talia had never witnessed before.

She was so accustomed to his cold indifference and perpetually furrowed brow that the change seemed almost impossible.

Does he always look at Ayla that way?

The question rose unbidden in her mind.

Talia remained rooted to the spot, unable to turn away.

Ayla suddenly reached for Varkas's sleeve. She caught the fabric between her fingers, rose upon her toes, and lifted her face towards his.

Talia understood immediately what the young woman wanted.

Her breath caught painfully in her throat.

No. Please, don't.

She did not wish to see what would happen next. She knew that if she witnessed such a moment, the memory would remain with her for years, returning again and again no matter how desperately she tried to forget it.

Yet her eyes refused to obey her.

Perhaps some foolish part of her still hoped that Varkas would draw away. Perhaps she was waiting for him to look upon Ayla with the same cold displeasure he had always shown her.

But Varkas did neither.

Instead, he willingly lowered his head towards Ayla, answering her silent request without the slightest hesitation.

Talia felt something inside her give way.

Before she could see anything more, she turned and fled.

She hurried between the trees, desperate to escape before the image could become any clearer in her mind. Her heart was pounding, and she could scarcely see where she was going.

But she had barely reached the edge of the grove when a strong hand suddenly seized her arm.

Talia gasped and stopped short.

Gareth stood before her.

His cheeks were flushed from drink, and the faint smell of wine clung to him. His fingers tightened around her upper arm as he looked down at her with an expression of sharp curiosity.

Talia immediately lowered her head, hoping to conceal her face.

But she was too late.

Gareth had already noticed the tears gathering at the corners of her eyes.

His expression changed.

For a moment, he simply stared at her, as though something that had long puzzled him was finally beginning to make sense.

"Don't tell me..." he murmured, his voice filled with disbelief. "Could it possibly be that you...?"

A short, incredulous laugh escaped him.

Talia struggled to pull away, but Gareth held her firmly in place. The more desperately she tried to escape, the more closely he examined her expression.

His eyes moved over her face, taking in her trembling lips, her wounded gaze, and the tears she was fighting so hard to conceal.

Then his expression brightened with cruel amusement.

He threw back his head and burst into laughter.

"Well, well! So that's the truth of it!"
