Chapter 25

“I am always mindful of Her Highness’s well-being.”

Varkas spoke with such impeccable courtesy that Gares could find no fault with his words.

“I merely wished to assure Your Highness that the Princess is resting comfortably. There is no need for concern. I beg you not to misunderstand my intentions.”

Gares regarded him with irritation before turning sharply towards the banquet hall. There was little to be gained from arguing with a man as unyielding as stone. He would only succeed in making himself appear foolish.

Clicking his tongue, he entered the magnificent hall, whose lavish decorations rivalled those of a nobleman's residence.

“Your Imperial Highness, we are deeply honoured by your presence.”

As Gares approached the long dining table, covered with a spotless white cloth, the Abbot immediately rose to greet him.

“Pray, take this seat.”

Gares accepted the place offered to him and surveyed the feast. Evidently, the Abbot had no intention of observing the humble life of poverty so often preached by the clergy. The table was adorned with silver utensils, golden goblets, and splendid dishes seasoned with rare and costly spices.

Quite satisfied with the arrangements, Gares nodded towards the dozen priests gathered around the table.

“I thank you for your generous welcome. Please, be seated.”

At his command, the monks drew out their chairs and sat down almost in unison. Only Varkas remained standing behind the Crown Prince, silent and watchful as his personal guard.

Gares noticed several curious glances directed towards him. The priests appeared rather taken aback by the striking appearance of the Commander of the Imperial Guard.

His brows drew together.

Varkas had always attracted more attention than was necessary. Although Gares had long grown accustomed to it, he still found it disagreeable whenever the man's presence overshadowed his own.

Suppressing his displeasure, he raised his golden goblet.

“I offer my sincere gratitude to the Abbot for arranging such a splendid gathering.”

The priests immediately turned their attention towards him.

Gares paused, enjoying their respectful silence, before continuing in a measured voice.

“May this occasion prove meaningful to all who have gathered here, and may—”

His words suddenly failed him.

A figure resembling a golden apparition had appeared at the entrance to the hall.

For one dreadful instant, Gares thought Senevia herself had come to torment him.

His fingers tightened around the goblet as he recognised his half-sister.

Talia Roem Guirta stood beneath the brilliant chandeliers, dressed with such extravagant splendour that she might have commanded attention even at an imperial ball.

Apparently, she had never heard that modest attire was expected within the sacred grounds of a monastery.

Her gown was both ostentatious and scandalously revealing. The delicate fabric clung to her youthful figure, while the costly ornaments adorning her person glittered beneath the light.

Gares ground his teeth.

The monks looked utterly astonished. Several stared at her with their mouths slightly open, as though they had forgotten themselves entirely.

Unable to restrain his anger, Gares sprang to his feet.

“How dare you present yourself in such a manner in a place like this!”

He slammed his goblet upon the table, sending wine spilling over its rim. Without troubling to wipe his hands, he pointed accusingly at her.

“Did you not hear my warning to remain out of my sight?”

“Oh, but of course I heard it.”

Talia approached with an innocent smile and drew out the chair immediately beside him.

She seated herself as though her presence were the most natural thing in the world.

“How could I possibly stay away when my dear brother so plainly wished to see me? Since the great Crown Prince himself requested my presence, I even took the trouble to dress for the occasion.”

She smoothed a hand over her elegant skirts, displaying them with evident satisfaction.

Gares stared at her in disbelief.

“What absurd nonsense are you speaking?”

“Surely you have not forgotten my contrary disposition, Brother.”

Her clear cobalt eyes narrowed with mischievous delight.

“To send the Commander of the Imperial Guard himself to deliver such a warning! Could it mean anything other than that you were desperately longing to see your little sister?”

Her smile widened.

“Since Your Imperial Highness desired my company so earnestly, how could I possibly disappoint you?”

Gares summoned every remaining measure of self-restraint to prevent himself from striking her.

Yet Talia seemed determined to test him further.

“Ah, how delightful it is to see you again. Are you enjoying the evening, Brother?”

“I was,” he answered through clenched teeth, “until you arrived.”

Talia burst into laughter.

“Then I am pleased I made the effort to drag my weary self here.”

Gares clenched his jaw until it ached.

Whenever she laughed in that infuriating manner, he found himself struggling against the desire to destroy the beautiful face that mocked him so openly.

His shoulders trembled with suppressed fury.

“How much longer do you intend to behave like this?” he demanded in a low voice. “Are you determined to discover the limits of my patience?”

“How cruelly you speak to me…”

Talia leaned forward, resting her elbows upon the table.

The chandelier light fell across her slender shoulders and delicate collarbones, illuminating her pale skin with an almost ivory radiance.

The monks' eyes followed her movements with undisguised fascination.

That venomous little serpent.

Gares felt his disgust rising.

He could scarcely endure the sight of her deliberately charming the men around her.

As though she sensed his anger approaching its limit, Talia allowed a playful smile to curve her lips.

“I merely accepted an invitation. I cannot understand why you are so terribly agitated when I have done nothing wrong.”

She tilted her head with feigned innocence.

“Do you not think you are being rather unkind to your lovely youngest sister?”

Turning towards the Abbot, she regarded him as though seeking his agreement.

Basilis stiffened in embarrassment at being unexpectedly drawn into the quarrel.

His discomfort appeared to amuse her, for her eyes curved into a charming smile.

Gares could bear it no longer.

He seized her arm roughly.

“If you are so desperate for attention, then choose one of your attendants to share your bed! Cease making a spectacle of yourself before these holy men.”

His voice dropped into a savage warning.

“And if you dare bring disgrace upon the imperial family through some filthy scandal, I shall see to it that you never again have the opportunity to display that pretty face of yours.”

Talia's eyes flashed.

“And what, precisely, do you intend to do?”

She tilted her chin defiantly.

“Now that you have put the idea into my head, I am almost tempted to fulfil your expectations.”

That was the final insult.

Gares reached towards her slender throat, determined to silence her.

But before his hand could close around it, a heavy hand settled upon his shoulder.

“Your Highness.”

Gares turned sharply.

Varkas Raedgo Sierkhan stood beside him, looking down with his usual impassive expression.

His face betrayed not the slightest emotion, and yet, for one fleeting instant, Gares felt an inexplicable sense of danger.

It was an absurd feeling.

Varkas had never given him reason to believe he would raise a hand against him.

“Everyone is awaiting Your Highness's toast,” the knight said calmly.

His hand pressed firmly against Gares's shoulder, a silent warning not to yield to Talia's deliberate provocation.

Gares angrily brushed it aside.

His fingers trembled with the violence he had been forced to restrain.

He clenched his fists, concealing the movement, and fixed his gaze upon his half-sister's enchanting face.

She is provoking me deliberately.

I must not allow myself to be drawn into her schemes.

Repeating those words inwardly, he forced his anger beneath a mask of composure.

He released Talia's arm with a rough shove, reclaimed his goblet, and addressed the gathering in an exaggeratedly cheerful voice.

“We have wasted quite enough time upon this foolish disagreement. Come, let us enjoy our meal.”

He raised his glass.

“Once again, I offer my gratitude to the Abbot for arranging this splendid gathering. May this evening prove pleasant and meaningful to everyone present.”

The monks exchanged uneasy glances.

Their eyes moved between the Crown Prince, his illegitimate half-sister, and the imposing Commander standing behind them.

At last, they hesitantly raised their goblets.

Only Talia remained motionless, her arms folded and a mocking expression upon her face.

Gares felt his temper threatening to rise once more.

Before he could speak, however, Varkas bent slightly towards her.

“Your Highness, I must ask that you observe the ordinary courtesies expected of a guest.”

He gently unfolded her hand and placed a silver goblet within it.

Talia stiffened at the unexpected touch.

Her eyes flashed with hostility as she looked up at him.

Gares watched closely, fully expecting her to fling the goblet at Varkas.

After all, the two had quarrelled incessantly since childhood. Talia had always appeared particularly determined to make the knight's life miserable.

Yet, to his astonishment, she did nothing of the sort.

Though she continued to glare at him like an offended cat, she gradually straightened in her chair and composed herself.

Gares narrowed his eyes.

This was most unlike Talia Roem Guirta.

She was a girl who never knew when to retreat. Ordinarily, the slightest reprimand from Varkas would have driven her into another furious outburst.

Yet now, instead of resisting his instruction, she had quietly yielded.

Gares studied her with growing suspicion.

What is that girl plotting?
