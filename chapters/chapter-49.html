Chapter 49

Was there truly anything so unnatural about waking in the middle of the night? Why, then, did such an overwhelming sense of dread seize her?

Talia drew a trembling breath, struggling to calm the frantic beating of her heart. Yet the air seemed strangely thin, as though there were scarcely enough of it to fill her lungs. She tried to breathe more deeply, but her throat felt painfully constricted, and each attempt only made her growing panic worse.

Unable to endure the suffocating darkness any longer, she forced herself upright.

A sharp pain immediately tore through her splinted leg, but she scarcely cared. The desperate need for air overwhelmed every other sensation. Clutching at the carriage door, Talia nearly tumbled onto the ground before managing to steady herself. She staggered forward into the night, her lips parted as she struggled to draw breath.

Some distance ahead, a faint light flickered against the surrounding darkness.

The sight brought a small measure of relief to her troubled heart. Dragging her stiff, trembling leg behind her, Talia slowly made her way towards a large pavilion tent, where a cheerful fire burned brightly.

She had scarcely approached when a woman's voice drifted towards her, sharp and overflowing with contempt.

"It is nothing less than divine punishment."

Talia stopped.

Beside the blazing hearth sat a young maid, idly stirring the glowing embers with a long wooden stick. She appeared entirely at ease, speaking loudly and without the slightest concern that anyone might overhear her.

"Why, everyone knows what she has done!" the woman continued with growing excitement. "She dared to place a curse upon the First Princess. She even appeared at Her Highness's birthday celebration without an invitation and caused a dreadful disturbance!"

The maid's voice rose with righteous indignation, as though she were recounting some great injustice.

Standing silently beyond the reach of the firelight, Talia narrowed her eyes and studied the woman's face. She did not recognise her. Judging by her unfamiliar appearance, the servant likely belonged to the household of either Ayla or Gareth.

The woman lifted her cup, took a leisurely drink, and moistened her lips before eagerly continuing her gossip.

"Clearly, the heavens would not overlook such wickedness. The curse she intended for another has fallen upon her own head instead. I should say she has received precisely what she deserves!"

Another maid leaned forward, curiosity evident in her voice.

"I heard she might never walk properly again. Is there any truth to that?"

"Of course there is!" the first woman exclaimed. "One of the Second Princess's own attendants said the bones in her leg were crushed into countless pieces. Apparently, the healers spent hours putting them back together, just as one might try to mend a shattered earthen pot."

Talia flinched.

Her gaze instinctively fell towards her heavily bandaged leg, and for a moment, she could do nothing but stare at it.

Meanwhile, the maid leaned closer to her companions, lowering her voice to a conspiratorial whisper that was nevertheless loud enough to carry across the fire.

"And that is not even the worst of it. They say the scars are absolutely dreadful. The flesh was torn so terribly that not even healing magic could restore its former appearance. Imagine it! Her skin all twisted and puckered, covered in ugly red marks!"

"Well, at least we shall no longer have to endure that woman's arrogance," another servant remarked with satisfaction. "She has spent far too long parading about the palace as though she were its mistress."

"Indeed!" the first maid agreed eagerly. "Her pretty face and figure were the only things she ever had to boast of. Now that her body has been ruined, I wonder what she intends to be so proud of!"

A ripple of laughter spread through the gathering. The women exchanged delighted glances, thoroughly entertained by the misfortune of the princess they so freely ridiculed.

Talia remained motionless in the darkness.

Then, slowly, she began to move.

Dragging her injured leg across the uneven ground, she approached the woman whose back was turned towards her.

Several maids seated on the opposite side of the fire noticed the shadow emerging from the darkness. Their expressions changed at once. Colour drained from their faces, and their laughter died upon their lips.

But the woman who had been speaking so merrily remained entirely unaware of the danger approaching behind her.

"And her marriage prospects are surely finished as well!" she declared with a contemptuous laugh. "After all, what respectable noble family would willingly accept an illegitimate daughter who is also a crip—AAAH!"

Her sentence ended in a piercing shriek.

Talia had seized the thick braid gathered at the back of the woman's head and pulled it backwards with all the strength she could muster.

The maid cried out in pain, desperately reaching for the hands tangled in her hair. But Talia ignored her struggles and tightened her grip, forcing the woman's head back until their eyes finally met.

The instant the servant recognised her, every trace of colour vanished from her face.

Talia looked down at her with eyes as cold and lifeless as winter ice. Slowly, the corners of her lips curved into a faint, dreadful smile.

"Why have you fallen silent?" she asked softly. "Pray, do continue. I was finding your conversation most interesting."

"Y-Your Highness..."

The woman's eyes widened in terror. Her lips, which had been twisted into such a smug smile only moments before, now trembled uncontrollably.

Talia tilted her head.

"Whatever is the matter? Have you suddenly become frightened? Surely there is no reason to fear someone who is merely an illegitimate cripple."

The maid began to sob.

"P-Please forgive me, Your Highness! I... I have committed a terrible offence!"

"Have you indeed?" Talia replied coolly. "And what offence might that be?"

"I... I..."

The woman's chin quivered so violently that she could scarcely form a word.

"I spoke wickedly of Your Highness. I mocked you with my disgraceful tongue. P-Please, I beg you, show mercy and forgive me!"

Talia regarded her with utter contempt.

"When one commits a wrong, one must pay the price for it. Why, then, is mercy the very first thing you demand?"

The maid's face had turned a sickly shade of grey.

For several moments, Talia continued to stare at her trembling features. Then she slowly raised her eyes towards the other servants gathered around the fire.

The women immediately lowered their heads, shrinking beneath her gaze like frightened mice before a serpent.

Among them stood an Imperial Knight, his posture stiff and uncomfortable.

Talia fixed her eyes upon him and gave a sharp inclination of her chin.

"You there. Come forward."

The knight hesitated for a moment before reluctantly approaching her.

Without releasing the maid's hair, Talia pulled her head downwards, forcing the sobbing woman onto her knees before him.

"This servant has dared to insult a member of the Imperial Family," Talia declared coldly. "Such insolence cannot be permitted to pass without punishment. Draw your sword and carry out your duty."

The knight went rigid.

The maid's terrified gasps filled the silence that had settled over the gathering. For a long moment, the man merely stood there, looking down at the weeping servant. At last, he placed a clenched fist against his chest and bowed deeply.

"Your Highness, I beg you to reconsider," he said carefully. "We have already suffered grievous losses, and the camp is overwhelmed with preparations for the fallen and the care of the wounded. To carry out an execution at such a time would only create further unrest. I humbly ask that you restrain your anger and—"

"Are you refusing to obey me?"

Her voice was quiet, but the threat beneath it was unmistakable.

The knight pressed his lips tightly together and lowered his head even further.

He offered no reply.

For a moment, Talia simply stared at him. Then a hollow laugh escaped her lips, devoid of even the slightest trace of amusement.

Her expression twisted with sudden fury.

"Very well. If you lack the courage to do it, then I shall do it myself!"

She released the maid's braid with a violent shove, casting her aside as though she were nothing more than a worthless object.

Then Talia lunged towards the knight.

Startled by her sudden movement, the man instinctively reached out and caught her by the shoulders to prevent her from falling.

Talia's eyes blazed.

"How dare you lay your hands upon me!"

The knight immediately withdrew his hands as though he had been burned.

Seizing the opportunity, Talia reached for the sword hanging at his waist and caught hold of its hilt.

But the weapon was far heavier than she had anticipated.

She pulled desperately at the blade, attempting to draw it from its sheath. Yet her injured leg could scarcely support her own weight, much less allow her to struggle with a heavy longsword.

Before she could steady herself, her balance failed.

Talia fell heavily onto the hard earth.

For one terrible moment, the entire gathering fell silent.

No one moved.

No one spoke.

Clutching her ruined leg, which throbbed with fresh and unbearable pain, Talia slowly raised her head.

The knight stood frozen before her, while the surrounding maids stared down with rigid, expressionless faces.

And suddenly, a dreadful memory rose from the depths of her mind.

She remembered lying helpless upon the floor, surrounded by countless pairs of cold, indifferent eyes. She remembered the humiliation of being watched while she lay in her own sickness, unable to rise or defend herself.

The shame of that distant day returned with such force that it seemed to tear open an old wound.

Her chest tightened.

Her thoughts scattered.

No... Not again.

With trembling hands, Talia pressed her palms against the dirt and struggled to push herself upright.

She had to stand.

She had to rise before them, straighten her back, and remind these insolent servants that she was an imperial princess, not some pitiful creature to be stared at and despised.

But her leg would not obey her.

No matter how desperately she tried to move it, the injured limb remained heavy and useless beneath her.

She dragged herself forward, her fingers digging into the earth as she struggled to regain her footing.

Then she saw the maid.

The very woman who had mocked her so cruelly stood only a few paces away, staring at her with wide, vacant eyes.

She is watching me.

The thought struck Talia with unbearable force.

That woman... the woman who dared ridicule me... is watching me crawl upon the ground.

She could not endure it.

Something within her seemed to snap.

Without thinking, Talia reached towards the hearth and seized a burning piece of wood from the flames.

The scorching heat immediately bit into her palm, but she scarcely felt it. The pain of her wounded pride had become far more unbearable than any injury to her flesh.

With a sudden, furious movement, she swung the flaming branch towards the maid.

The burning wood struck the woman's face.

A dreadful scream tore through the night.

The maid staggered backwards, clutching at her injured face as her shrieks echoed across the camp.

But Talia was not satisfied.

Her eyes were wild, and her breathing came in harsh, uneven gasps. She raised the smouldering branch once more, preparing to strike again.

Before she could bring it down, however, a dark figure emerged swiftly from the night.

A powerful arm closed around her body, pulling her away from the trembling servant and holding her firmly in place.

Talia struggled violently.

She twisted and thrashed like a wounded animal, desperately trying to free herself from the hands restraining her.

Then a familiar scent reached her.

She froze.

Slowly, she lifted her head.

Varkas stood above her, holding her effortlessly against him with a single arm. His pale eyes looked down upon her with an expression so cold that she could find no warmth within them.

Talia stared at him, her chest rising and falling with ragged breaths.

Without saying a word, Varkas reached for the burning branch still clutched in her hand. He took it from her grasp and threw it far into the surrounding darkness.

Just as he had once taken away the dagger she had concealed, he had now deprived her of another weapon.

Again.

A bitter, suffocating resentment surged through Talia's chest.

This man had never once stood upon her side.

He would neither fight for her nor allow her to fight for herself.
