Chapter 14

“Your Highness, will you not go and speak with him?”

The maids standing beside Ayla could scarcely conceal their impatience as they watched her admire her fiancé from a distance. Each appeared eager for an opportunity to approach Varkas, and among them were several whose expressions betrayed an interest far warmer than ordinary admiration.

Ayla chose to overlook their improper fascination. She could afford such composure, for she knew perfectly well that Varkas would never bestow a second glance upon another woman.

Though, to be fair, he has never looked upon me with any particular passion either…

A faint, bitter smile touched her lips.

The truth was that Varkas scarcely seemed capable of such feelings.

Having entered the Imperial Palace at a tender age, he had been placed under the instruction of priests whose devotion to the Empire bordered upon fanaticism. Their severe teachings were intended to shape him into a perfectly obedient servant of the Imperial Throne, and the discipline they imposed had gradually stripped him of nearly every ordinary human emotion.

When Ayla's mother discovered the severity of the treatment endured by the young heir of House Sierkhan, she had struggled desperately to protect him. Yet by the time she intervened, the damage had already been done.

The boy had lost not only the ability to experience joy, anger, sorrow, and pleasure, but even the simplest desires which ought to belong naturally to every human being.

Ayla's expression grew troubled as she recalled their first meeting.

How terribly frightening he had seemed in those days, with eyes as empty as the discarded shell of an insect.

Varkas had resembled a beautiful doll fashioned from hardened wax. He spoke so little that it was unusual to hear more than a few words from him throughout an entire day. Unless someone instructed him to eat or rest, he would remain motionless, apparently indifferent to hunger and exhaustion alike.

It was as though the priests had suppressed his desires for so long that he had forgotten how to recognise them.

Compared with the boy she remembered, the man standing before her now appeared considerably more human.

Perhaps, with time, he may recover even further…

She regarded her fiancé with renewed hope. Countless times she had warned herself against expecting too much, yet whenever she looked upon him, her heart seemed determined to disregard every sensible resolution.

How could she prevent herself from longing for him?

He was the beautiful boy who had remained faithfully beside her unfortunate mother, and he had since grown into a man whose appearance and accomplishments were admired throughout the Roem Empire.

Ayla knew that countless young ladies had surrendered their hearts to him, only to suffer the disappointment of discovering that their affection would never be returned.

Yet she possessed an advantage which none of those women could claim.

However political the arrangement might be, she was to become his wife. In time, she would stand beside him as Grand Duchess, share his household, and perhaps bear the heir who would succeed him.

If I remain patient and offer him my affection throughout the years we shall spend together, surely even his frozen heart may one day begin to thaw.

Comforted by this fragile hope, Ayla finally approached him.

Varkas had been examining a warhorse near the outer wall. Perhaps sensing someone drawing near, he turned his head towards her.

The instant their eyes met, a chill passed through Ayla.

His expression was so utterly devoid of warmth that it seemed to mock every tender expectation she had entertained only moments before.

He regarded her briefly before returning his attention to the horse.

“Is there some matter requiring my attention, Your Highness?”

Ayla steadied herself and arranged a cheerful smile upon her lips.

“Nothing of particular importance. I merely wished to see whether the preparations for our journey were proceeding satisfactorily.”

“They are nearly complete.”

His reply was brief and composed. As he spoke, he ran one hand along the horse's thick neck, examining the animal with greater interest than he appeared to take in their conversation.

“I confess I am a little concerned,” Ayla continued. “The preparations have taken rather longer than we expected, and the weather grows warmer with every passing day. I fear the journey may prove exceedingly uncomfortable.”

“The delay is unavoidable. His Highness the Crown Prince has made certain alterations necessary.”

Ayla lowered her gaze with embarrassment.

“Indeed. My brother has been rather unreasonable, has he not?”

She glanced towards Varkas, hoping to discover whether he resented the inconvenience Gares had caused.

The Crown Prince had not been content merely to insist upon accompanying the party. He seemed determined to transport the entire contents of his residence along with him.

Dozens of personal attendants had been added to the travelling company, together with entertainers to relieve his boredom, a private cook, and even a tailor whose services he apparently considered indispensable.

Ayla could scarcely imagine how Varkas had endured such demands without once displaying his displeasure.

Yet he had accepted every alteration with the same untroubled composure he brought to all his duties.

“I am truly sorry that my brother has caused you so much unnecessary trouble,” she said, her expression becoming earnest.

“There is no reason for Your Highness to apologise. These matters would have required attention sooner or later.”

Varkas handed the horse's reins to a waiting stable hand before continuing.

“In truth, His Highness has conducted himself with rather more restraint than I anticipated. Considering that he is preparing to part from a sister whom he holds so dear, a certain degree of dissatisfaction is understandable.”

At these words, Ayla's expression clouded.

The anxiety she had attempted to suppress throughout the morning returned with renewed force.

She lifted her eyes towards the magnificent white fortress rising beyond the courtyard. The thought of leaving her brother alone within those walls filled her with sorrow.

The Imperial Palace held too many painful memories, and Gares had already endured more grief than she wished to remember.

Worse still, there remained within the palace a woman whom Ayla regarded as a dangerous enemy, one who seemed forever intent upon threatening her brother's position.

Gares possessed a proud and fiery disposition. Would he truly be able to withstand such a cunning adversary without anyone beside him to offer comfort or restraint?

Ayla tightened her fingers around the folds of her skirt.

“Varkas, might I ask something of you?”

He turned towards her.

“If it would not place too great a burden upon our household, I should very much like to visit the Imperial Palace from time to time after our marriage. Would you object to such an arrangement?”

For a moment, Varkas regarded her thoughtfully.

A faint crease appeared between his brows, and Ayla immediately felt the warmth rise to her cheeks.

She realised that her request might sound terribly childish.

Once she became Grand Duchess, she would be responsible for the management of the vast eastern territories and the affairs of the many retainers who served House Sierkhan.

Her duties as mistress of the Grand Duchy would naturally take precedence over her personal wishes.

Yet Varkas merely inclined his head.

“Provided the journey does not prove too exhausting for Your Highness, I see no reason why you should not visit whenever you please.”

Ayla looked up in surprise.

“Truly?”

“Certainly. Was not our marriage arranged, in the first place, to strengthen the position of His Highness the Crown Prince? Maintaining close relations with the Imperial Family is therefore entirely appropriate.”

Ayla's happiness faded a little.

To Varkas, their approaching marriage was evidently no more than a political arrangement, a duty to be fulfilled in accordance with the interests of the Empire.

But to her, it meant something far more precious.

For a brief moment, his words wounded her. Nevertheless, she concealed her disappointment beneath a grateful smile.

“Thank you. I am most relieved that you understand.”

Varkas acknowledged her gratitude with a slight nod before returning his attention to the horses.

Ayla suppressed a sigh.

Then, gathering her courage, she placed one hand upon his forearm.

“I know you have a great many matters requiring your attention, but might you spare me a few moments? There is something I should like to give you before our departure.”

Varkas regarded her with mild curiosity.

After a brief pause, he turned towards the young squire standing nearby and instructed him to lead the inspected horses back to the stables.

Having attended to this duty, he offered Ayla his arm and accompanied her away from the crowded courtyard.

The maids, displaying an unusual degree of discretion, remained behind.

For the first time that morning, Ayla found herself alone with her fiancé.

She rested her hand lightly upon his strong forearm as they followed a neatly maintained path towards the gardens of the Main Palace.

A gentle breeze stirred the leaves overhead and brushed pleasantly against their faces, offering welcome relief from the oppressive summer heat.

The gardens were flourishing beneath the brilliant sunlight. Flowerbeds carefully tended by the palace servants overflowed with blossoms of every colour, while the neatly trimmed shrubs displayed an abundance of deep emerald foliage.

It was a scene of remarkable beauty.

Yet as Ayla looked upon it, sorrow gradually settled over her features.

There had been a time when the sight of these gardens caused her almost unbearable pain. Every path and flowerbed seemed to remind her of her mother, and she could scarcely walk through the grounds without recalling the happiness which had once existed there.

But the years had passed.

The Imperial Palace, despite being filled with traces of Senevia, had slowly become part of her ordinary existence. Even the garden which had once held such painful memories was beginning to lose its power to awaken them.

And somehow, that discovery grieved her more than the memories themselves.

How dreadful it is that even the things we swear never to forget should grow faint with time.

“What was it that Your Highness wished to give me?”

Varkas's quiet question drew her from her thoughts.

Ayla turned towards him.

As a child, he had spent many hours in the garden which Bernadette had tended with such care. Ayla remembered how those peaceful surroundings had seemed to bring a little comfort to his troubled spirit.

She wondered suddenly whether he, too, missed the garden as it had once been.

Did he remember those days with affection? Did he ever long for the gentle presence which had helped him recover even a small measure of his humanity?

She searched his face for some indication of what he might be feeling.

But his expression remained perfectly still.

At length, Ayla abandoned the question with a quiet sigh and reached into her coat.

“I have made something for you.”

She withdrew a neatly folded handkerchief and held it out with both hands.

“I embroidered the crest of House Sierkhan upon it myself.”

Varkas lowered his gaze towards the small piece of cloth.

Ayla suddenly became conscious of how rapidly her heart was beating. Her mouth felt dry, and she began speaking with rather more enthusiasm than she had intended.

“It is an old custom, you see, for a lady to present her betrothed with a handkerchief worked by her own hands before he sets out upon a journey. I know it is rather unnecessary in our case, since we shall be travelling together, but I thought perhaps—”

“It is a most gracious gift.”

Varkas interrupted her nervous explanation and accepted the handkerchief.

Then, to her astonishment, the faintest smile appeared upon his lips.

Ayla felt her heart swell with happiness.

She was almost ashamed of how deeply such a small gesture affected her. A mere smile ought not to have been enough to leave her so thoroughly delighted.

Yet Varkas was a man who revealed so little of himself that even the slightest expression of warmth seemed an extraordinary treasure.

And this time, he had smiled at something she had made for him.

“I shall treasure it,” he said.

With quiet care, he fastened the embroidered handkerchief to the hilt of his sword.

Ayla watched the movement with shining eyes, unable to prevent a shy smile from spreading across her face.

For one brief and precious moment, she allowed herself to believe that perhaps her hopes were not entirely foolish.

Perhaps, given enough time, the distance between them might finally disappear.

But just as she was beginning to enjoy the sweetness of that thought, the sound of hurried footsteps reached them from somewhere along the garden path.

Someone was approaching.
